## Abstract

Problem F16 of Croft, Falconer, and Guy asks for the least worst-case length needed to join prescribed pairs of points by pairwise disjoint planar arcs, when the distance within each pair is at most one. The book suggests that the answer for two pairs is $\sqrt2$. We prove this exactly. The lower bound is a crossing argument in a square. The upper bound follows from a sharp ellipse lemma. As a consequence, the value proposed in the book for three pairs, $(\sqrt3+1)/2$, cannot be correct under the literal formulation, because the constants are nondecreasing in the number of pairs.

## Abstract

Problem F16 of Croft, Falconer, and Guy asks for the least worst-case
length needed to join prescribed pairs of points by pairwise disjoint planar
arcs, when the distance within each pair is at most one. The book suggests
that the answer for two pairs is2\sqrt{2}. We prove this exactly. The lower
bound is a crossing argument in a square. The upper bound follows from a
sharp ellipse lemma. As a consequence, the value proposed in the book for
three pairs,(3+1)/2(\sqrt{3}+1)/2, cannot be correct under the literal formulation,
because the constants are nondecreasing in the number of pairs.

## 3The three-pair value quoted in the book

LetCnC_{n}denote the corresponding constant fornnpairs. The sequence is
nondecreasing. Starting with any configuration ofnnpairs, add one more
pair in a small disk far from all existing terminals; its two points can be
joined inside that disk. Every admissible family for the enlarged
configuration restricts to an admissible family for the original one, so
the enlarged configuration has cost at least that of the original. Taking
suprema givesCn+1≥Cn.C_{n+1}\geq C_{n}.

In particular,C3≥C2=2.C_{3}\geq C_{2}=\sqrt{2}.

But3+12<2,\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}<\sqrt{2},

since(3+1)2=4+2​3<8(\sqrt{3}+1)^{2}=4+2\sqrt{3}<8. Thus the value(3+1)/2(\sqrt{3}+1)/2proposed forn=3n=3in F16 is incompatible with the exact
two-pair result under the stated definition, independently of the eventual
value ofC3C_{3}. DeterminingC3C_{3}remains a separate problem.
