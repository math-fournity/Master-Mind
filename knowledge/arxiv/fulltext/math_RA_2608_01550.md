## 1.Introduction

Quandles are algebraic structures with a nonassociative binary operation that satisfies axioms encoding the three Reidemeister moves of links in three-space. Although quandles originated from knot theory[21,24], they also enjoy applications in quantum algebra[1,10,13], algebraic geometry[31], and the theory of Riemannian symmetric spaces[23], among other areas of mathematics. In 1982, Joyce[21]and Matveev[24]independently introduced quandles and used them to construct complete invariants of non-split links up to orientation reversal. Of course, it is generally difficult to determine whether two quandles are isomorphic. This motivated a search for new properties and invariants of quandles themselves. We refer the reader to the articles[11,22,26]for introductions to the theory and its historical development.

Automorphisms of quandles, which reveal a lot about their internal structures, have been investigated in great detail in a series of papers[3,5,15]. By fusing ideas from combinatorial group theory into quandles, recent works[8,9]show that free quandles and link quandles are residually finite.

In an attempt to linearize the study of quandles, a theory of quandle rings analogous to the classical theory of group rings was proposed in[6], where several interconnections between quandles and their associated quandle rings were investigated, and an analogue of the group ring isomorphism problem for quandle rings was proposed. The work was developed further in a recent paper[14], where examples of nonisomorphic finite quandles with isomorphic quandle rings have been given.

The purpose of this paper is to develop the theory of quandle algebras of commutative quandles. Following[6], given a quandle (resp. rack)QQand a unital associative commutative ring𝕜\Bbbk, the quandle (resp. rack) algebra𝕜​[Q]\Bbbk[Q]is defined as the free𝕜\Bbbk-module generated byQQequipped with the nonassociative multiplication inherited fromQQ(see Section2).

As an analogue of Kaplansky’s unit conjecture in the study of group algebras, the authors of[7]proposed the study of idempotents in quandle algebras. They computed idempotents and𝕜\Bbbk-algebra automorphisms of quandle algebras for certain quandles of small orders, including all quandles of order33. In particular, they computed idempotents in the quandle algebras𝕜​[T]\Bbbk[\operatorname{T}],ℤ​[R3]\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{R}_{3}],ℤ​[R4]\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{R}_{4}], andℤ​[J3]\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{J}_{3}], whereT\operatorname{T}is any trivial quandle,Rn\operatorname{R}_{n}is a dihedral quandle of ordernn, andJ3\operatorname{J}_{3}is the 3-element quandle of Joyce[20].

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section2, we recall some basic definitions and examples from the theory of quandles and quandle algebras.

In Section3, we investigate idempotents in quandle rings of commutative quandles. In[10, Conj. 13.19], it was conjectured that ifQQis a finite Latin quandle, then the only idempotents ofℤ​[Q]\mathbb{Z}[Q]are the trivial idempotents. We verify this conjecture for all commutative ordered quandles; in fact, the statement holds over arbitrary integral domains𝕜\Bbbkwithchar⁡𝕜≠2\operatorname{char}\Bbbk\neq 2(Theorem3.8). Over such𝕜\Bbbk, we also show that that ifQQis commutative, then𝕜​[Q]\Bbbk[Q]contains no nontrivial idempotents of length22or33(Theorem3.6). We end this section by extending[16, Prop. 3.1]to all finite commutative quandles (Proposition3.11). This addresses[17, Conj. 3.10].

In Section4, we compute the automorphism groups of the quandle algebras of all finite trivial quandles and dihedral quandles of odd orders. More precisely, we prove thatAut⁡(𝕜​[Tn])≅Affn−1⁡(𝕜)\operatorname{Aut}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{T}_{n}])\cong\operatorname{Aff}_{n-1}(\Bbbk), whereTn\operatorname{T}_{n}is
the trivial quandle of ordernn(Theorem4.4). Also, we prove thatAut⁡(𝕜​[R2​n])≅Aff1⁡(SCn⁡(𝕜))⋊Aut⁡(𝕜​[Rn])\operatorname{Aut}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{R}_{2n}])\cong\operatorname{Aff}_{1}(\operatorname{SC}_{n}(\Bbbk))\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{R}_{n}])for all odd integersn≥1n\geq 1(Theorem4.13), whereSCn⁡(𝕜)\operatorname{SC}_{n}(\Bbbk)is the commutative ring of symmetric circulant matrices. Finally, we construct a canonical embeddingAut⁡(𝕜​[R2​n])↪GLn⁡(𝕜)×Aut⁡(𝕜​[Rn])\operatorname{Aut}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{R}_{2n}])\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\Bbbk)\times\operatorname{Aut}(\Bbbk[\operatorname{R}_{n}])over suitable rings𝕜\Bbbkfor each even integern≥2n\geq 2(Theorem4.15). This addresses[7, Prob. 6.5].

In Section5, we use a computer search to compute commutator widths of quandle rings𝔽q​[Rn]\mathbb{F}_{q}[\operatorname{R}_{n}](Theorem5.4) and𝔽q​[Q]\mathbb{F}_{q}[Q](Theorems5.5and5.6) for certain values ofnnandqq, whereQQis a noncommutative quandle of order at most77. In particular, we provide the first known examples of quandle algebras of commutator width greater than11.
