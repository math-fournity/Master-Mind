## Abstract

Using a method of Balková--Pelantová, we show that if ${\bf w}$ is a right-infinite word over a finite alphabet, then for each nonnegative integer $N$ there are at most $3(p_{\bf w}(N+1)-p_{\bf w}(N))+1$ distinct upper (and likewise lower and ordinary when they exist) frequencies for length-$(N+1)$ subwords of ${\bf w}$, where $p_{\bf w}(n)$ is the subword complexity function of $n$. In particular, this gives a uniform upper bound when ${\bf w}$ has linearly bounded subword complexity. We provide examples showing that whenever $f(n)$ is a weakly increasing function tending to infinity, there is a word ${\bf w}$ such that the number of subwords of length $n$ is $O(nf(n))$ and for which the limit supremum of the number of distinct upper frequencies of length-$N$ subwords of ${\bf w}$ as $N\to\infty$ is infinite.

## 1.Introduction

Let𝐰=u1​u2​u3​⋯{\bf w}=u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\cdotsbe a right-infinite word over a finite alphabetΣ\Sigma.

Frequencies often shed insight into the underlying structure of a word. For example, an infinite word𝐰{\bf w}is periodic if and only if there is some positive integerppsuch that each length-ppsubword of𝐰{\bf w}occurs with frequency1/p1/p. It is clear that ifp𝐰p_{\bf w}grows quickly withnnthen most length-nnsubwords cannot occur too frequently.

In addition, there are several fundamental questions about the complexity of the frequencies as real numbers, which often reveal how well behaved the word is. For example, in the case of automatic words, the frequencies of subwords (when they exist) are always rational[7]and the upper and lower frequencies are always rational[3]. In the case of morphic words, the frequencies, when they exist, are algebraic numbers (see Allouche and Shallit[1, Theorem 8.4.5]). Numbers with transcendental frequencies can exist. For example, the infinite binary word𝐰=u1​u2​u3​⋯{\bf w}=u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\cdotsin whichun=1u_{n}=1precisely when there is a positive integerjjsuch thatn=⌊j​π⌋n=\lfloor j\pi\rfloorhas the property that11occurs with frequency1/π1/\pi; however, words where subwords occur with non-algebraic frequencies are typically considered pathological.

The motivation for this paper arose from a well-known fact about Sturmian words.111A right-infinite binary word𝐰{\bf w}is Sturmian whenp𝐰​(n)=n+1p_{\bf w}(n)=n+1for alln≥0n\geq 0and this is the minimal subword complexity function for words that are not eventually periodic.It is an immediate consequence of the famous “three-gap theorem” of Sós, Surányi, and Świerczkowski[10,11,12]and the description of Sturmian words via irrational rotations (see, for example,[8, Chapter 2]) that ifddis a positive integer then there are at most three distinct values (which depend ondd) that can occur as frequencies of length-ddsubwords of a fixed Sturmian word. This result was extended to uniformly recurrent words by Boshernitzan[5](see also Balková–Pelantová[2]), who showed that the set of distinct frequencies of length-NNwords could be bounded in terms of the subword complexity function. We recall that the subword complexity of a right-infinite word islinearly boundedif there is a positive constantCCsuch thatp𝐰​(n)≤C​np_{\bf w}(n)\leq Cnfor allnnsufficiently large. Boshernitzan’s[5]result shows in particular that if𝐰{\bf w}is uniformly recurrent and has linearly bounded subword complexity then the number of frequencies of length-NNsubwords is uniformly bounded. We adapt a clever combinatorial argument of Balková–Pelantová[2]to extend this result to general words where we instead use upper and lower frequencies, as frequencies need not exist in a general word.
