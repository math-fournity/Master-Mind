## Abstract.

In this paper, we generalize the Steenbrink vanishing theorem for ample line bundles on complex projective varieties by extending it to big line bundles on compact complex spaces with multiplier ideal sheaves.

## Key words and phrases:vanishing theorems, big line bundles, singular Hermitian metrics, multiplier ideal sheaves, complex spaces.

## 1.Introduction

Vanishing theorems for cohomology groups are a central topic in complex geometry and powerful tools in algebraic geometry and several complex variables.
For example, the existence of global holomorphic sections obtained from cohomology vanishing has many applications.
The Kodaira vanishing theorem[Kod53]has since been generalized in various directions through numerous studies (see[AN54,Ram72,Nor78,Bog79,Bog80,Kaw82,Vie82,SJ85,Ste85,Nad89,HLWY23,Wat23,Wat26a,Wat26b,LMNWZ25,MQZ26]).
These developments have played an important role in a wide range of areas in complex geometry and have become indispensable in the classification theory of higher-dimensional projective algebraic varieties.

The Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing theorem[Kod53,AN54]asserts that ifXXis a compact complex manifold of dimensionnnandA⟶XA\longrightarrow Xis an ample line bundle, then

Hq​(X,ΩXp⊗A)=0H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}\otimes A)=0for anyp+q>np+q>n.

Extending the Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing theorem from ample to big line bundles is a natural problem.
As indicated by the vanishing theorems of Nadel, Bogomolov, and Kawamata–Viehweg described below, such an extension is highly significant.
Unlike the Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing theorem, which extends to bidegrees(p,q)(p,q)withp+q>np+q>n, the Nadel vanishing theorem[Nad89]provides an optimal extension of the Kodaira vanishing theorem from ample to big line bundles by twisting with multiplier ideal sheaves.
The Nadel vanishing theorem has a wide range of applications and plays an important role in complex geometry; it also implies the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem for nef and big line bundles[Kaw82,Vie82].
Furthermore, the Nadel vanishing theorem was recently extended to the bidegree(p,n)(p,n)case in[Wat23].

In summary, ifXXis projective andLLis big, equivalently, ifLLadmits a singular Hermitian metrichhwith a strictly positive curvature current (see[Dem90]), then

Hq​(X,ΩXn⊗L⊗ℐ​(h))=0H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{n}\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{I}(h))=0for anyq>0q>0[Nad89],

Hn​(X,ΩXp⊗L⊗ℐ​(h))=0H^{n}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{I}(h))=0for anyp>0p>0[Wat23].

The Nadel vanishing theorem in the first line can be extended to complex spaces; in particular, the projectivity assumption can be removed[Wat26b]by using the vanishing theorem for higher direct image sheaves.
The second vanishing result above is a version of the Bogomolov vanishing theorem[Bog79,Bog80]involving the multiplier ideal sheafℐ​(h)\mathscr{I}(h), and more recently, further generalizations of this result have been obtained in[LMNWZ25,Wat26a,MQZ26].
However, the above vanishing results cannot be extended to the same bidegrees as in the Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing theorem due to Ramanujam’s counterexample[Ram72](see also[Wat23, Remark 2.10]).
On the other hand, the Steenbrink vanishing theorem for ample line bundles is given as follows.

Althoughμ∗​A\mu^{*}Ais not necessarily ample, it is big. This suggests that logarithmic-type vanishing theorems for big line bundles may hold in the same bidegrees as the Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing theorem.
Therefore, we attempt to extend the ampleness assumption in the Steenbrink vanishing theorem to the case of big line bundles.

Logarithmic-type vanishing theorems are recalled below.
LetXXbe a projective manifold andDDbe a simple normal crossing divisor onXX. For an ample line bundleAAonXX, the vanishing result

## 2.3.Positivity of curvature currents and bigness on compact space

Singular positivity admits the following reformulation in terms of curvature currents.

## Proposition 2.6([Wat26b, Proposition 3.1]).

LetXXbe a complex space of pure dimension andL⟶XL\longrightarrow Xbe a holomorphic line bundle with a singular Hermitian metrichh.
We obtain the following relationship.
- •

LetXXbe a complex space andL⟶XL\longrightarrow Xbe a holomorphic line bundle.
We defineLLto bebigif the Kodaira-Iitaka dimension ofLLis maximal on each irreducible component. A compact complex spaceXXis said to beMoishezonif the algebraic dimension of each irreducible componentXjX_{j}is maximal (cf.[Moi66]). IfLLis big, thenXXis Moishezon, and every Moishezon space is bimeromorphic to a projective manifold (see[Moi66]).
Thus, since a compact Moishezon space contains sufficiently many curves, we define a line bundle to benefas usual in terms of the non-negativity of its intersection numbers.
Furthermore, nefness is preserved under pullbacks by resolution of singularities.

WhenXXis a projective manifold, Demailly proved that a line bundle is big (resp. pseudo-effective) if and only if it admits a singular positive (resp. singular semi-positive) Hermitian metric (see[Dem90]).
The notion of bigness defined above was later shown to admit the same characterization on compact complex manifolds without assuming projectivity (see[MM07, Theorem 2.3.30]), by combining Demailly’s approximation with the holomorphic Morse inequalities. However, when the space has singularities, it seems to be unknown whether bigness and singular positivity are equivalent.
