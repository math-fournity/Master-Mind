## Abstract

Let $X,X'$ be independent and identically distributed discrete real-valued random variables of finite Shannon entropy, and write $H(X)$ for the Shannon entropy of $X$. We prove that \[   \max\{H(X+X'),\,H(XX')\} \ge \frac87 H(X)-O(\log H(X)). \] This is the entropic analog of the celebrated sum-product phenomenon, and answers a question of Goh, which simply asked for a coefficient strictly larger than 1. An example by the author, Gavalakis, and Kontoyiannis showed the coefficient cannot exceed $\frac43$. Previous work by Gavalakis, Goh, and Kontoyiannis was able to prove a result of a weaker form, which could not translate to a coefficient strictly larger than 1 because of examples where the min-entropy is significantly smaller than the Shannon entropy. By splitting the distribution of $X$ into uniform pieces, which costs $O(\log H(X))$ entropy, we obviate this issue, establishing a coefficient of $\frac{10}{9}$. We augment this to $\frac87$ by adapting the work of Solymosi, which established the combinatorial sum-product phenomenon with coefficient $\frac43$ by bounding the multiplicative energy, to the entropy setting, again via a uniformization technique.

## 1.Introduction

LetA⊂ℝA\subset\mathbb{R}be a finite set of real numbers.
The sum-product phenomenon of Erdős and Szemerédi[8]asserts thatAAcannot be simultaneously close to an arithmetic progression and to a geometric progression, and so cannot have both its sumsetA+A={a1+a2:a1,a2∈A}A+A=\left\{a_{1}+a_{2}:a_{1},a_{2}\in A\right\}and its product setA⋅A={a1​a2:a1,a2∈A}A\cdot A=\left\{a_{1}a_{2}:a_{1},a_{2}\in A\right\}of size close to|A|\left\lvert A\right\rvert.
Formally, in 1983 they proved there exists some constantε>0\varepsilon>0such thatmax⁡{|A+A|,|A⋅A|}≥|A|1+ε−o​(1),\max\left\{\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert A\cdot A\right\rvert\right\}\geq\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{1+\varepsilon-o(1)},

whereo​(1)o(1)denotes a term that goes to 0 as|A|→∞\left\lvert A\right\rvert\to\infty.
The original result did not provide an explicit value forε\varepsilonand only holds for subsets of the integers, i.e.,A⊂ℤA\subset\mathbb{Z}, and there has been much subsequent work on proving this result for larger and largerε\varepsilon.
Two milestone results in this field were the work of Elekes[7]in 1997, who proved the result forε=14\varepsilon=\frac{1}{4}using the Szemerédi–Trotter incidence theorem[26,27], and the work of Solymosi[25]in 2009 who proved the result forε=13\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}, by specifically proving the following theorem.

## Theorem 1.1([25, Theorem 2.1]).

LetA⊂ℝA\subset\mathbb{R}be a finite set of positive reals. Then|A⋅A|​|A+A|2≥|A|44​⌈log⁡|A|⌉.\left\lvert A\cdot A\right\rvert\,\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert^{2}\geq\frac{\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{4}}{4\lceil\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert\rceil}.

In 2015, Konyagin and Shkredov[17]slightly improved upon Solymosi’sε=13\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}toε=13+120598\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{20598}, and the current published record is13+21167\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{1167}by Rudnev and Stevens[21], with subsequent preprints by Bloom[2]and then Cushman[4]improving this toε=13+2951\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{951}andε=13+104407\varepsilon=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{10}{4407}, respectively.

The trivial upper bound onε\varepsilonis 1, and Erdős and Szemerédi[8]in fact conjectured that the result is true forε=1\varepsilon=1, i.e., thatmax⁡{|A+A|,|A⋅A|}≥|A|2−o​(1).\max\left\{\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert,\left\lvert A\cdot A\right\rvert\right\}\geq\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2-o(1)}.

A recent breakthrough by Bloom, Sawin, Schildkraut, and Zhelezov[1]showed this sum-product conjecture is false overℝ\mathbb{R}; the conjecture is still open forA⊂ℤA\subset\mathbb{Z}.

Throughout,log\logdenotes the base-2 logarithm, and all entropies are in bits.
The natural logarithm will be denotedln\ln.
For a discrete random variableXXtaking values in a setAA, Shannon’s Asymptotic Equipartition Property (AEP) lets us heuristically interpret the entropyH​(X)H(X)as the log-cardinality of the “essential support” ofXX, the probabilistic analog oflog⁡|A|\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert.
Based on this heuristic correspondence, Ruzsa[24]in 2009 and Tao[28]in 2010 began exploring this connection between combinatorics and information theory in the context of sumset inequalities.
Interestingly, they found that if one took a sumset inequality, such as the classical Ruzsa triangle inequality[23]|A−C|≤|A−B|​|B−C||B|,\left\lvert A-C\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert A-B\right\rvert\left\lvert B-C\right\rvert}{\left\lvert B\right\rvert},

and replaced discrete subsets with independent discrete random variables and log-cardinalities with entropies, miraculously one would often obtain a legitimate new entropy inequality, such as the entropic Ruzsa triangle inequality[24](1.1)H​(X−Z)≤H​(X−Y)+H​(Y−Z)−H​(Y)H(X-Z)\leq H(X-Y)+H(Y-Z)-H(Y)

for independentXX,YY, andZZ.
This correspondence is rich and nontrivial, often with neither the combinatorial nor entropy version of these bounds implying each other.
We refer interested readers to[19]and references therein for a modern overview of this correspondence, including the differential entropy setting for continuous random variables.

Recently, we have also seen the field of combinatorics use entropy inequalities and results as the primary tool in the argument, so this dialogue has been fruitful in both directions.
Likely the most prominent example of entropy tools being used for sumset results is the recent work of Gowers, Green, Manners, and Tao[11,12], which proves a conjecture of Marton, widely known in the literature as thepolynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture, first in characteristic 2 and then for arbitrary additive groups with bounded torsion.

Bringing the sum-product phenomenon into this cardinality-entropy correspondence, Goh[10]asked whether an entropic sum-product phenomenon exists.

## 1.1.Proof summary and section overview

Gavalakis, Goh, and Kontoyiannis[9]apply a uniformization lemma of Vadhan[29]to split their distribution into a mixture of uniform distributions, the granularity of which is limited by the min-entropy.
Then they use a point-plane incidence bound of Rudnev[22]to prove lower bounds onH​(A​(B+C))H(A(B+C)), whereAA,BB, andCCare these uniform distributions.
Combined with upper bounds by Máthé and O’Regan[20]onH​(X​(Y+Z))H(X(Y+Z))in terms of the sum and product entropies, they are able to establish their sum-product results.

By first applying a dyadic decomposition to our random variable, which partitions its support into rough level sets of the probability mass function, and then applying the uniformization lemma, we avoid this limitation of the min-entropy, at a cost ofO​(log⁡H​(X))O(\log H(X)), which is negligible for the purposes of the sum-product phenomenon.
This causes our analogousAA,BB, andCCto potentially have very different sizes, something the original lower bound onH​(A​(B+C))H(A(B+C))is unable to handle, so we must generalize their arguments.
The previously stated upper bounds are actually incorrect, though the main results of Gavalakis, Goh, and Kontoyiannis[9]are salvageable (seeRemark6.3), so we also have to establish new, corrected upper bounds.
This adaptation by itself is able to prove the caseδ=19\delta=\frac{1}{9}via establishingTheorem1.7.

In the hopes of improving this all the way to the tightδ=13\delta=\frac{1}{3}, which was proven in the combinatorial setting by Solymosi[25], we studied their combinatorial argument bounding the multiplicative energy by sumset cardinalities.
While we were unsuccessful in fully transferring the argument to the entropic setting, this did enable us to proveTheorem1.6, our small-doubling linear entropic Elekes–Ruzsa theorem.
This protects against the weakness ofTheorem1.7at small doublings, improvingδ\deltato17\frac{1}{7}.

The dyadic uniformization technique is used in both parts of the argument, i.e., both small-doubling and large-doubling, and to do so we need the finite support assumption.
Fixing this costs essentially nothing at the level of our main theorems, as truncating our random variables to some large but finite support has negligible impact on their entropies, and we handle this at the end of our paper.
