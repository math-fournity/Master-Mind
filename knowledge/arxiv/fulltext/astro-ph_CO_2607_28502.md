## Abstract

Recent small-scale CMB data show a preference for larger scalar spectral index, $n_s$, when combined with DESI data. Monomial $α$-attractor T-models can be reconciled with the new observations if the power of the hyperbolic tangent function is $p\geq 6$, where $p$ is even. The supergravity construction of monomial T-models with $p>2$ relies on the assumption that lower powers remain negligible over the whole field range explored during inflation and reheating. What would be the consequences of going beyond the monomial formulation? As a first step in this direction, we consider the case of a binomial potential, given by the sum of quadratic and quartic terms. When the quartic coefficient, $c$, is $0< c+1/2 \ll 1$, we find that the new model displays non-universal behavior for $n_s$, leading to values as large as $0.965$ and with an $α$-dependence that is qualitatively different from that of monomial potentials. By solving the background dynamics during the first few e-folds of perturbative reheating we show that the quartic term might dominate before the quadratic one eventually takes over as the inflaton oscillations decrease in amplitude. This leads to a time-dependent equation of state, with $\bar w\sim 1/3$ initially before ultimately $\bar w\to 0$. Obtaining a quartic-dominated reheating stage lasting $\sim4$ e-folds requires a substantial hierarchy between the quartic and quadratic terms, $c\sim 10^5$ for $α\gtrsim 0.1$. Our study highlights that models beyond the monomial form can lead to non-trivial deviations of $n_s$ from the predictions of monomial potentials. Furthermore our results for $\bar w$ during reheating call into question the use of monomial models with large $p$; assuming that $\bar w$ is uniquely determined by the $p$-th power relies on substantial fine-tuning of the underlying supergravity potential.

## 1Introduction

Cosmological inflation is the leading paradigm to describe the very early universe[23].
Initially proposed as a solution to the horizon problem, quantum vacuum fluctuations generated during inflation also explain the origin of primordial perturbations observed in the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB).

The accelerated expansion of the background characterising inflation can be realised within the simplest models through the dynamics of a single, canonical scalar field, the inflaton, slowly rolling down its potential,V​(ϕ)V(\phi).
The landscape of single-field models of inflation is highly populated[25,26].
Amongst these, cosmologicalα\alpha-attractors stand out as particularly compelling potentials, see Ref.[22]and references therein.

## 3Reheating and its equation of state

In this section we study the post-inflationary dynamics of the binomial potential (1.4).
Our purpose is to determine the impact ofccon the equation of state parameter during the first few e-folds of perturbative reheating.

After the end of inflation, the homogeneous inflaton oscillates around the minimum of its potential and it decays into Standard Model particles, leaving behind a radiation dominated universe.
In this work we assume that the reheating process can be described perturbatively, i.e. the inflaton is weakly coupled to other fields.
By summarizing the effect of all decay channels in a total decay rateΓtot\Gamma_{\rm tot}, the homogeneous field obeysϕ¨+(3​H+Γtot)​ϕ˙+Vϕ=0.\ddot{\phi}+\left(3H+\Gamma_{\rm tot}\right)\dot{\phi}+V_{\phi}=0\;.(3.1)

At the beginning of reheating one typically hasH≫ΓtotH\gg\Gamma_{\rm tot}, and only whenHHdrops sufficiently belowΓtot\Gamma_{\rm tot}particle production becomes efficient.
Due to the weak coupling between the inflaton and other particles, it might takes many e-folds before this condition is realised.
As will be made clear below, any effect fromccis confined to the first few e-folds of reheating.
For this reason, we will assumeH≫ΓtotH\gg\Gamma_{\rm tot}in the following, simplify Eq. (3.1) accordingly, and assume that the inflaton dominates the total energy density and pressure, i.e.Ptot≈Pϕ,ρtot≈ρϕP_{\text{tot}}\approx P_{\phi},\,\rho_{\text{tot}}\approx\rho_{\phi}.

As done in Eq. (2.7), the reheating stage can be parametrised in terms of its duration,Δ​Nrh≡Nrh−Nend\Delta N_{\text{rh}}\equiv N_{\text{rh}}-N_{\text{end}}, and its average equation of state (EoS) parameterw¯≡1Δ​Nrh​∫NendNrhdN′​w​(N′),\bar{w}\equiv\frac{1}{\Delta N_{\text{rh}}}\int_{N_{\rm end}}^{N_{\rm rh}}\!\mathrm{d}N^{\prime}\,w(N^{\prime})\;,(3.2)

Because no explicit decay model is considered,Δ​Nrh\Delta N_{\text{rh}}is treated as a free parameter.
Nevertheless, as said above we will focus on the first few e-folds of reheating.
In this regime it is useful to introduce the average EoS parameter evaluated at an intermediate timeNend<N<NrhN_{\rm end}<N<N_{\text{rh}},w¯​(N)≡1N−Nend​∫NendNdN′​w​(N′).\bar{w}(N)\equiv\frac{1}{N-N_{\rm end}}\int_{N_{\rm end}}^{N}\mathrm{d}N^{\prime}\,w(N^{\prime})\;.(3.3)

## 4Discussion

In this work we considerα\alpha-attractor T-models beyond the usual monomial formulation (1.1).
By working with the binomial potential in Eq. (1.4) we investigate what effects might arise from the interaction between quadratic and quartic terms during inflation and reheating.
We assumec≥−1/2c\geq-1/2, which ensures monotonicity ofV​(ϕ)V(\phi)and avoids potential problems with the initial conditions.

We recover the large-scale predictions of monomial potentials withp=2p=2andp=4p=4whenc→0c\to 0andc→∞c\to\inftyrespectively.
Forc>0c>0we find thatnsn_{s}andrrvalues interpolate non-monotonically between the two asymptotic regimes.
Nevertheless, the deviation from monomial predictions fornsn_{s}is at most of𝒪​(10−3)\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})and theα\alpha-dependence is qualitatively unchanged.
We explain these numerical findings by showing that at leading order in the large-Δ​NCMB\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}expansion thensn_{s}predictions are universal, see Eq. (1.3) and Eq. (A.9).

On the other hand, for models withc≃−1/2c\simeq-1/2we find a larger variation ofnsn_{s}with respect to the monomial results, as well as a differentα\alpha-dependence.
In other words, the parametersccandα\alphaaffect thensn_{s}predictions much more than in the casec>0c>0, as confirmed by the large-Δ​NCMB\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}expansion in Eq. (A.25).
When0<s≡c+1/2≪10<s\equiv c+1/2\ll 1two new terms appear at𝒪​(Δ​NCMB0)\mathcal{O}(\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}^{0})and𝒪​(Δ​NCMB−1/2)\mathcal{O}(\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}^{-1/2}), which depend onssand (inversely) on powers ofα\alpha.
At leading order in the large-Δ​NCMB\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}expansion the predictions are therefore no longer universal.
Whenc=−1/2c=-1/2(s=0s=0) we find that the deviation with respect to the monomial behavior is due to the leading-order correction to Eq. (1.3) being of𝒪​(Δ​NCMB−3/2)\mathcal{O}(\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}^{-3/2}), instead of𝒪​(Δ​NCMB−2)\mathcal{O}(\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}^{-2})as usual.

We explore the interplay between the quadratic and quartic terms in Eq. (1.4) during the first few e-folds of perturbative reheating.
Ifccis large enough and/or the inflaton’s oscillations start off with a large enough amplitude, the quartic term initially dominates the dynamics before the quadratic one eventually takes over as the oscillations amplitude decreases.
We show that the binomial potential (1.4) might therefore lead to a time-dependent EoS during reheating, withw¯∼1/3\bar{w}\sim 1/3initially beforew¯→0\bar{w}\to 0.
Nevertheless, even a few e-folds of radiation-like reheating require a substantial hierarchy between the quartic and quadratic terms, e.g. we obtain 4 e-folds withw¯≥1/3\bar{w}\geq 1/3ifc∼105c\sim 10^{5}andα≳0.1\alpha\gtrsim 0.1.

Earlier works exist on the interplay between different potential terms during reheating and time-dependence ofw¯\bar{w}.
In Ref.[28]Turner considers the perturbative effect of higher-order powers in a polynomial potential.
In Ref.[27]Sahaet al.demonstrate for potentials which behave likeV​(ϕ)∝ϕpV(\phi)\propto\phi^{p}near the minimum that growing inhomogeneties and interactions with a lighter scalar field yield a time-dependent EoS approaching 1/3 after a few e-folds whenp≥3p\geq 3.
In Ref.[9]Cleryet al.point out that a bare mass term added to a quartic potential would lead tow¯\bar{w}transitioning from 1/3 to 0.
We provide here the first numerical study of a time-dependentw¯\bar{w}due to the interplay between a quadratic and a quartic term, where the latter is potentially much larger than the former.
Furthermore, this effect is discussed in the context of recent debates on the consistency of monomial T-models with SPA+BK+DESI measurements, see below.

The binomial model we consider represents the first instance of a more generic potential derived within supergravity, see Eq. (1.2).
Our results show that by working with a monomial T-model (1.1) withp=4p=4one might miss non-trivial behavior of the large-scale observables.
Furthermore, assuming thatw¯\bar{w}is uniquely determined by the quartic term for an extended period relies on the assumption of substantial tuning of the underlying supergravity potential (1.2).
For example, by extrapolating the linear fits in Fig.9, one finds that 10 e-folds of perturbative reheating withw¯≥1/3\bar{w}\geq 1/3requirec=109−1011c=10^{9}-10^{11}.

For monomial T-models,p=6p=6is the smallest value ofppleading to stiff reheating(w¯=1/2)(\bar{w}=1/2), and able to reconcile these potentials with current SPA+BK+DESI data ifΔ​Nrh≳24\Delta N_{\text{rh}}\gtrsim 24, see Figs. 1, 2 and 5 in Ref.[18].
For this reason, a logical next step would be to investigate the trinomial potential555See Refs.[10,17]for earlier studies of T-model polynomial potentials up to sixth order. Differently from Eq. (4.1), they include also odd powers of the hyperbolic tangent, and were introduced to implement an inflection point (Vϕ​ϕ​(ϕinfl)=0V_{\phi\phi}(\phi_{\text{infl}})=0), which can lead to non-attractor behavior and consequent enhancement of the power spectrum on short scales.V​(ϕ)=c2​tanh2⁡(ϕ6​α)+c4​tanh4⁡(ϕ6​α)+c6​tanh6⁡(ϕ6​α).V(\phi)=c_{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{6\alpha}}\right)+c_{4}\,\tanh^{4}\left(\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{6\alpha}}\right)+c_{6}\,\tanh^{6}\left(\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{6\alpha}}\right)\;.(4.1)

Does such a long reheating stage dominated by the sixth power require a large hierachy of parameters?
We expect our argument on the time-dependence ofw¯\bar{w}to apply to the potential in Eq. (4.1) as well, as it relies on the inevitable decrease of the inflaton’s oscillations amplitude.
Therefore we anticipate that only a very large hierarchy of the coefficients might decrease the tension with current large-scale data.
Nevertheless, as was the case for binomial models (1.4) withc≃−1/2c\simeq-1/2, there could be areas of the parameter space(α,c4,c6)(\alpha,\,c_{4},\,c_{6})which lead to non-universal behavior fornsn_{s}, potentially lifting it towards unity without the need for an extended phase of stiff reheating.
We leave this exploration for future work.
