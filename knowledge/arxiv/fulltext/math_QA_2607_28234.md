## Abstract

This paper studies iterated Hopf Ore extensions (IHOEs) over a field $\Bbbk$ of characteristic zero, a significant class of connected Hopf algebras with finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. We establish two fundamental structural properties for arbitrary IHOEs of $\Bbbk$. First, the class of IHOEs of $\Bbbk$ is closed under Hopf subalgebras and quotient Hopf algebras. Second, every one-sided coideal subalgebra of an IHOE of $\Bbbk$ is an iterated Ore extension of $\Bbbk$. These structural facts are built upon the thin replacement machinery for PBW generating systems originating from Kharchenko's work. We further prove a general inheritance theorem for PBW generating systems, which serves as a systematic perturbation method to produce PBW generating systems for subalgebras from those of the ambient algebra under mild hypotheses. Based on these theoretical advances, we develop an explicit combinatorial algorithm for classifying all one-sided coideal subalgebras of arbitrary IHOEs of $\Bbbk$. As practical illustrations, we explicitly classify all right coideal subalgebras of noncocommutative connected Hopf algebras of GK-dimension three.

## Abstract

Second, every one-sided coideal subalgebra of an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbkis an iterated Ore extension of𝕜\Bbbk. ## 1Introduction

The classification of Hopf algebras of finite Gelfand–Kirillov (GK) dimension represents a central, long-standing problem in Hopf algebra theory. Two major research programs have been advanced to tackle this classification problem: the lifting theory for pointed Hopf algebras pioneered by Andruskiewitsch and Schneider[4,5], and the homological classification framework developed by Brown, Goodearl and Zhang[10,16]. The majority of known classification theorems rely on restrictive hypotheses such as pointedness, smoothness or integrality, and exhaustive classifications remain essentially confined to Hopf algebras of GK-dimension at most two, whereas the higher-dimensional case remains widely uncharted. For further progress along these two directions, we refer the reader to the survey articles[1,2,11]and the references cited therein.

Considerable structural simplifications emerge when restricting attention toconnectedHopf algebras, a distinguished subclass within finite-GK-dimensional Hopf algebras. A Hopf algebra is calledconnectedif its coradical is one-dimensional. Standard examples include universal enveloping algebras of Lie algebras, coordinate rings of unipotent algebraic groups, and various combinatorial connected graded Hopf algebras, such as those of (quasi-)symmetric functions, permutations, and rooted trees. In particular, the latter family plays a foundational role in Connes and Kreimer’s rigorous formulation of quantum field renormalization[14]. While commutative and cocommutative connected Hopf algebras were thoroughly characterized in the 1960s[36,13], systematic structural investigations of their noncommutative noncocommutative counterparts have only blossomed over the past decade; see, for instance,[50,43,45,9,7,8,49,12,46,20].

Hopf Ore extensions serve as fundamental building blocks for constructing and investigating noncommutative, noncocommutative Hopf algebras of large GK-dimension. Iterated Hopf Ore extensions, first introduced in[9], constitute an iterative generalization of ordinary Hopf Ore extensions. By definition, annn-step Iterated Hopf Ore extension(nn-step IHOE) of a Hopf algebraRRis a Hopf algebraHHadmitting an ascending chain of Hopf subalgebrasR=H0⊂H1⊂⋯⊂Hn=H,R=H_{0}\subset H_{1}\subset\cdots\subset H_{n}=H,

where eachHiH_{i}is an Ore extension ofHi−1H_{i-1}for all1≤i≤n1\leq i\leq n. A fundamental result from[9, Theorem 3.2]ensures that everynn-step IHOE of𝕜\Bbbkis a connected Hopf algebra with GK-dimension preciselynn. This property enables the systematic construction of connected Hopf algebras of arbitrary finite GK-dimension. Low-dimensional classification results demonstrate the ubiquity of the IHOE structure: all connected Hopf algebras with GK-dimension≤4\leq 4are IHOEs of𝕜\Bbbk[50,43]. Hu extended this bound to GK-dimension≤6\leq 6in her doctoral work[19], without carrying out complete classification for such objects. Moreover, by[49, Theorem B], every finite-GK-dimensional connected graded Hopf algebra is an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbk. Nonetheless, the IHOE structure does not capture all finite-GK-dimensional connected Hopf algebras. Classic counterexamples include universal enveloping algebras of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras of dimension at least four, while more sophisticated noncocommutative counterexamples are constructed in[20].

It is well known that finite-GK-dimensional connected Hopf algebras are closed under taking Hopf subalgebras and quotient Hopf algebras. In this work, we establish the analogous closure property for the class of IHOEs of𝕜\Bbbk, which forms our first main theorem.

## Theorem 1.1(Theorem5.6, Theorem5.12).

Every Hopf subalgebra and every quotient Hopf algebra of an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbkis again an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbk.

Combining[49, Theorem B]and Theorem1.1, it follows that every Hopf subalgebra of a finite-GK-dimensional connected graded Hopf algebra is an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbk(Corollary5.8). For homogeneous substructures, a stronger relative result was established by Li and the second-named author in[31, Theorem B]: every finite-GK-dimensional connected graded Hopf algebra is an IHOE of each of its homogeneous Hopf subalgebras. These results raise a natural problem: ifHHis an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbk, isHHnecessarily an IHOE of each of its Hopf subalgebras? A closely related question in[31, Question C]asks whether every finite-GK-dimensional connected Hopf algebra is an IHOE of its primitive part, the universal enveloping algebra of its primitive space. The umbrella Hopf algebraUM​(2,2)\mathrm{UM}(2,2), constructed in[20]and revisited in Remark5.7, serves as a counterexample to both questions: it is an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbkbut not of primitive part[20, Corollary 4.5].

In addition, Hu proved that finite-GK-dimensional connected Hopf algebras are closed under Hopf algebra extensions[19]. Together with the subquotient closure of IHOEs established above, this motivates the following question concerning extension stability of the IHOE framework.

## Question 1.2.

For any exact sequence of Hopf algebras𝕜→K→H→J→𝕜,\Bbbk\rightarrow K\rightarrow H\rightarrow J\rightarrow\Bbbk,ifKKandJJare IHOEs of𝕜\Bbbk, isHHalso an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbk?

Beyond Hopf subalgebras and quotient Hopf algebras, one-sided coideal subalgebras represent another fundamental class of intermediate substructures that merit systematic study. Aleft(resp.right) coideal subalgebra of a Hopf algebraHHis a subalgebraA⊆HA\subseteq HsatisfyingΔH​(A)⊆H⊗A\Delta_{H}(A)\subseteq H\otimes A(resp.ΔH​(A)⊆A⊗H\Delta_{H}(A)\subseteq A\otimes H). Geometrically, such subalgebras correspond to quantum homogeneous spaces, and existing literature has mainly focused on their freeness, faithful flatness, and homological properties; see[34,42,33,26]. From a Lie-theoretic perspective, they serve as the core constituents of quantum symmetric pairs, a notion originally introduced by Letzter for quantized enveloping algebras[28]and further advanced by Wang and his collaborators[44]. In contrast to Hopf subalgebras, which are generally scarce for quantum groups, coideal subalgebras provide a rich collection of intermediate substructures[23,24,25,39,17]. For finite-GK-dimensional connected Hopf algebras, additional ring-theoretic and homological properties of coideal subalgebras have been obtained in[7,46]. In the present work, we establish a structure characterization of coideal subalgebras within arbitrary IHOEs of𝕜\Bbbkfrom the perspective of Ore extension theory, which forms our second main theorem.

## 5Iterated Hopf Ore extensions

Iterated Hopf Ore extensions (IHOEs) of𝕜\Bbbkform a fundamental class of connected Hopf algebras with finite GK-dimension. In this section, we revisit the definition of IHOEs and explore their intrinsic structure leveraging the full machinery of PBW generating systems and the thin replacement method established in previous sections. Our main result demonstrates that every Hopf subalgebra and every quotient Hopf algebra of an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbkis again an IHOE of𝕜\Bbbk. ## 6.4Some observations

## Remark 6.6.

Brown and Gilmartin classified the right coideal subalgebras ofB​(λ)B(\lambda); see[7, Proposition 3.7], though several details are omitted in their work. However, we discover an additional family of right coideal subalgebras with GK-dimension22in the caseλ=0\lambda=0, given by𝕜​[z2+a​z1]​[z3+b​z1+a2​z12;σ],a,b∈𝕜.\Bbbk[z_{2}+az_{1}]\bigl[z_{3}+bz_{1}+\tfrac{a}{2}z_{1}^{2};\sigma\bigr],\quad a,b\in\Bbbk.

This family corresponds to𝕀3′={2,3}\mathbb{I}_{3}^{\prime}=\{2,3\}.

## Remark 6.7.

From our classification results, one observes that each member of the two familiesA​(λ1,λ2,α)A(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\alpha)andB​(λ)B(\lambda)admits exactly one substantial Hopf subalgebra, namely the universal enveloping algebra of its primitive space, corresponding to the case𝕀3′={1,2}\mathbb{I}_{3}^{\prime}=\{1,2\}. By contrast, each such Hopf algebra possesses infinitely many substantial right coideal subalgebras. This supports the viewpoint that coideal subalgebras should be regarded as intermediate substructures beyond Hopf subalgebras in Hopf algebra theory, a perspective already adopted for quantum groups and mentioned in the paragraph preceding Theorem1.3in the Introduction.

## Remark 6.8.

The classification reveals that the parameter spaces of substantial right coideal subalgebras ofA​(λ1,λ2,0)A(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},0)(resp.B​(λ)B(\lambda)) behave drastically differently whenλ1≠λ2\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}versusλ1=λ2\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}(resp.λ≠0\lambda\neq 0versusλ=0\lambda=0). We expect a conceptual explanation for this phenomenon.
