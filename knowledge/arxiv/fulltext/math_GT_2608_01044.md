## Abstract.

We prove that any two quadrangulations of the same surface, i.e., decompositions of a surface into squares, can be transformed into one another by a finite sequence of two-dimensional local cubical Pachner moves and non-local subdivision transformations.

## 1.Introduction

It is well known that any two triangulations of anynn-manifold can be transformed into one another by a finite sequence of local Pachner moves ([4,8,10,11]). Each move replaces a subset ofnn-faces of an(n+1)(n+1)-dimensional simplex with the complementary subset of faces.

Analogously to triangulations, a cubulation is a presentation of a manifold as a result of gluing somenn-cubes along their(n−1)(n-1)-faces. The classification of 3-manifolds with low cubical complexity was carried out in[1,2,7]. Sometimes, cubulations are called cubications.

Similar to classical Pachner moves of triangulations, we can consider cubical Pachner moves for cubulations. Each such move replaces a set ofnn-faces of an(n+1)(n+1)-cube by the complementary set of faces. It is clear that these moves cannot transform one cubulation of annn-manifold into another, because the parity of cubes is conserved under cubical Pachner moves. Some characterisations of cubulation classes modulo cubical Pachner moves were studied in[5,6].

In the 2-dimensional case, when the manifolds are surfaces, cubulations are called quadrangulations. In this case, a complete set of moves has been found in[9]. It consists of two types of diagonal switches. These switches are not related to cubical Pachner moves. The results of[9]apply not to all surfaces, but only to closed ones, and furthermore only to so-called bipartite quadrangulations.

The main subject of this paper is to prove that any two quadrangulations of the same surface can be transformed into each other by a finite sequence of 2-dimensional cubical Pachner moves and one additional non-local transformation — subdivision.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we give formal definitions for cubulations and describe how to construct a cubulation of a manifold from any triangulation. We also describe cubical Pachner moves and the subdivision transformation. For the notation of moves, we follow the approach of[3]. All definitions and constructions apply to the generalnn-dimensional case, although the main result applies to the casen=2n=2.

In Section 3, we prove two intermediate lemmas and the main theorem.

## Lemma 2.

Let𝒞\mathcal{C}be a quadrangulation of the surfaceFF, and let𝒞′\mathcal{C}^{\prime}be the result of the subdivision of𝒞\mathcal{C}. Then there exists a triangulation𝒯\mathcal{T}ofFFsuch that𝒞′\mathcal{C}^{\prime}can be transformed intoβ​(𝒯)\beta(\mathcal{T})by a finite sequence of cubical Pachner moves of types(0,0)(0,0),(0,1)(0,1),(1,0)(1,0),(0,2)(0,2)and(2,0)(2,0).

## Proof.

Each group of four squares resulting from the subdivision of an original square of𝒞\mathcal{C}can be transformed into six squares that form a quadrangulation matching some triangulation: a move of type(1,0)(1,0)and then a move of type(0,0)(0,0)(in fig.7, this uses half of the transformations, from the central quadrangulation to either the initial or the final quadrangulation).
∎

## Theorem 1.

Let𝒞1\mathcal{C}_{1}and𝒞2\mathcal{C}_{2}be two quadrangulations of the surfaceFF. Then there is a finite sequence of subdivisions and de-subdivisions and cubical Pachner moves of types(0,0)(0,0),(0,1)(0,1),(1,0)(1,0),(0,2)(0,2)and(2,0)(2,0)that transform𝒞1\mathcal{C}_{1}into𝒞2\mathcal{C}_{2}.

## Proof.

The theorem follows from Lemmas1and2. Indeed, we start from the quadrangulation𝒞1\mathcal{C}_{1}, apply one subdivision, and then use the required cubical Pachner moves to obtain a quadrangulation𝒞′\mathcal{C}^{\prime}that matches some triangulation𝒯1\mathcal{T}_{1}(by Lemma2). We do the same with𝒞2\mathcal{C}_{2}and obtain a quadrangulation𝒞2′\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}that matches a triangulation𝒯2\mathcal{T}_{2}. Then, by Lemma1, we can transform𝒞′\mathcal{C}^{\prime}into𝒞2′\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}using cubical Pachner moves, after which we apply the reverse transformations from𝒞2′\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}back to𝒞2\mathcal{C}_{2}(the last step being a de-subdivision).
∎

## Remark 2.

To transform𝒞1\mathcal{C}_{1}into𝒞2\mathcal{C}_{2}, it is sufficient to apply only one subdivision and one de-subdivision.
