## Abstract

We show that every countable groupGGthat is mixed-identity-free (MIF) and contains a non-abelian free subgroup has the following property: the group ringK​GKGis primitive for any fieldKK. We also present a purely dynamical criterion that implies this result.

Our criterion recovers several of the existing results on primitivity, including those involving acylindrically hyperbolic groups. Furthermore, our criterion also applies (positively) to a plethora of new examples, such as Thompson-like groups, commensurator groups of hyperbolic groups, some Kac-Moody groups, and many more.

## 1Introduction

A ringRRis said to be (right) primitive if it has a faithful irreducible (right)RR-module. For many years, it was not known whether there exists a groupGGand a ringRRsuch that the group ringR​GRGis primitive[17, Problem 17]. However, Formanek and Snider provided the first example of such a group in[13], and they even showed that any group can be embedded in another groupGGsuch thatK​GKGis primitive for some fieldKK.

Since then, the study of primitivity in group rings has been an active area of research for several decades. A major milestone was achieved in 1978 through the work of Domanov[9], Farkas–Passman[10], and Roseblade[23,24], who established a complete characterization of the primitivity of group algebras of polycyclic-by-finite groups. More concretely, they proved that, for a polycyclic-by-finite groupGG, the group ringK​GKGis primitive if and only if the FC-center ofGGis trivial and the fieldKKis not absolute.

In the setting of non-noetherian groups, we can mention the results on non-elementary free products by Formanek[14], the results on amalgamated free products by Balogun[4], and those by Alexander-Nishinaka[2]. Very recently, using geometric methods, Solie established the primitivity of all group ringsK​GKGassociated with torsion-free (non-elementary) hyperbolic groups[26], and his result was extended to all (non-elementary) acylindrically hyperbolic groups without finite normal subgroups by Abbott-Dahmani[1].

Other interesting results on the primitivity of group rings can be found in the works of Nishinaka[19,20].

As we mentioned in the abstract, the goal of this article is to introduce a new algebraic criterion that implies the primitivity of group rings. This will be achieved using the concept of mixed-identity-free groups. We introduce the appropriate definition now.

## Definition 1.1.

A groupGGis mixed-identity-free (MIF) if, for everyn∈ℕn\in\mathbb{N}and every1≠w∈G∗𝔽n1\neq w\in G*\mathbb{F}_{n}, there is a homomorphismG∗𝔽n→GG*\mathbb{F}_{n}\to G, restricting to the identity onGG, that does not sendwwto11.

The main result of this article is the following:

## Theorem 1.2.

LetGGbe a MIF group that has a non-abelian free subgroup whose cardinality is the same as that ofGG. Then, ifRRis a domain with|R|≤|G||R|\leq|G|, the group ringR​GRGofGGoverRRis primitive. Moreover, the group algebraK​GKGis primitive for any fieldKK.

The class of countable groups that are MIF and contain a non-abelian free subgroup is vast, and it includes:
- (i)

all acylindrically hyperbolic groups without finite normal subgroups[16],
- (ii)

many linear groups[3],
- (iii)

the commensurator groups of non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic groups[5],
- (iv)

many notable groups of homeomorphisms of the Cantor set[6], such as the Higman–Thompson groupsGn,rG_{n,r}, the Brin-Thompson groupsn​VnV, Röver’s groupV​(Γ)V(\Gamma),
- (v)

all lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups[25], such as the Burger-Mozes group, the Amir-Lazarovich groups, some Kac-Moody groups, and
- (vi)

some other groups acting on trees[7,11].

In particular, since non-elementary acylindrically hyperbolic groups without finite normal subgroups satisfy the hypotheses of our main theorem, we recover the primitivity result of Abbott-Dahmani[1, Theorem 0.4]with an independent proof. Furthermore, since the class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups contains all non-elementary hyperbolic groups and all non-elementary free products[18,21], the same is true for the main primitivity results of Solie[26]and Formanek[14].

Furthermore, we note that a good number of the groups mentioned in the last paragraph were shown to be MIF by studying their dynamical properties. In light of this evidence, we also wish to propose a dynamical criterion for the primitivity of group rings. In this case, the relevant definitions are the following.

## Definition 1.3.

LetGGbe a group and letXXbe a Hausdorff space with#​X>2\#X>2. An action by homeomorphismsG↷XG\curvearrowright Xis said to be
- 1.

- 2.

With these definitions at hand, we now state our second criterion for primitivity.

## Theorem 1.4.

LetGGbe a countable group that admits a topologically free, extremely proximal actionG↷XG\curvearrowright X. Then, ifRRis a countable domain, the group ringR​GRGis primitive. Moreover, the group ringK​GKGis primitive for any fieldKK.

The point here is that a groupGGadmitting a topologically free, extremely proximal actionG↷XG\curvearrowright Xas above is forced to be MIF. This applies to all the relevant subclasses of groups studied in[1,7,11,27,5,25]222Note that some, if not most, of these actions are not explicitly studied as extremely proximal actions, but rather as actions with ‘(weak) north-south dynamics.’ The standard argument showing that they are extremely proximal can be found in[5, Lemma 2.4].. We provide a proof of this fact in Lemma2.4. It is a generalization of similar results proven by Ozawa[22]and Rybak[25].

Let us also mention that, due to their striking role in the theory ofC∗C^{*}-algebras[22,12], the study of extremely proximal actions is currently receiving a lot of attention, and it is likely that we will see new examples soon.
