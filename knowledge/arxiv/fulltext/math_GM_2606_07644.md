## Abstract

We develop a spectral theory of scale-invariant operators on the multiplicative half-line $(\mathbb{R}_+, dx/x)$. A symmetric kernel $M(x, y)$ satisfying $M(kx, ky) = k^{-a}M(x, y)$ necessarily factorizes as $(xy)^{-a/2}F(x/y)$, where the shape function $F$ depends only on the ratio of its arguments. The Mellin transform diagonalizes such operators: the generalized eigenfunctions are $ψ_ω(x) = x^{-a/2+iω}$, and the eigenvalues are the Mellin multiplier $\tilde{F}(ω)$. This structure reveals a fundamental decoupling of two exponents. The geometric exponent $a$, carried by the power-law envelope $(xy)^{-a/2}$, governs the matrix scaling under dilation. The spectral exponent $b$, measured from the eigenvalue decay of the finite-dimensional truncation, is an effective quantity determined by the shape of $\tilde{F}(ω)$. For the explicit kernel $F(t) = c ρ^{|\ln t|}$, the Mellin multiplier is a Lorentzian of width $σ= -\ln ρ$, not a power law -- so $b$ is generically distinct from $a$. This decoupling provides a precise mathematical characterization of multicriticality: the equality $a = b$ corresponds to a simple critical fixed point of the Renormalization Group, while $a \neq b$ signals the presence of multiple independent scaling dimensions. We prove that the discrete self-similarity condition forces eigenvector collapse on the lattice, motivating the continuum formulation. Finite-size corrections from lattice sampling are quantified numerically.

## 1Introduction

Scale invariance is a hallmark of systems at criticality[21,4,7]. In the Renormalization Group (RG) framework[27,10,26], a system at a critical fixed point is self-similar: coarse-graining by a factorkk—integrating out short-wavelength degrees of freedom and rescaling—leaves the essential structure unchanged up to a rescaling of coupling constants. When multiple independent scaling dimensions coexist, the system ismulticritical[25,28].

A natural spectral signature of criticality is a power-law distribution of eigenvalues in a correlation or coupling matrix. Such spectra arise in financial time series[17,12], random matrix theory[14,13], biological networks[15], and collective animal behavior[2]. A companion study[6]develops the physical framework for detecting criticality in multi-variable systems through matrix scale invariance.

This paper provides the mathematical foundations. We ask: what is the structure of a symmetric operator whose kernel satisfies the scaling relationM​(k​x,k​y)=k−a​M​(x,y)M(kx,ky)=k^{-a}M(x,y)? We show that such operators are completely characterized by a kernel factorization and diagonalized by the Mellin transform, yielding generalized eigenfunctionsx−a/2+i​ωx^{-a/2+i\omega}and a Mellin multiplier that plays the role of a continuous eigenvalue spectrum. This framework reveals a decoupling between the geometric exponentaa(from the kernel envelope) and any effective spectral exponentbb(from the eigenvalue decay of finite truncations), providing a rigorous foundation for the notion of multicriticality as the conditiona≠ba\neq b.

We also prove a discrete collapse theorem (Theorem15) showing that the natural self-similarity condition on the integer lattice forces all eigenvectors to be proportional, admitting at most rank-one structure. This necessitates the continuum formulation and clarifies the status of finite-dimensional matrix computations as lattice samplings of the underlying continuum operator.

The Mellin multiplier (15) is a Lorentzian, not a power law. ## 6Two Exponents and Multicriticality

## Remark 13(RG fixed-point condition).

In the RG framework[27,26,4,1,9], coarse-graining by a factorkkcorresponds to the dilation mapM​(x,y)↦M​(k​x,k​y)M(x,y)\mapsto M(kx,ky), and the RG map appropriate to a spectral rescaling isℛk​[M]=kb​M​(k​x,k​y)\mathcal{R}_{k}[M]=k^{b}\,M(kx,ky). The scaling operators of RG theory[4,1,20]correspond in this framework to the generalized eigenfunctionsψω\psi_{\omega}. The condition that all modes share the same geometric scaling (the mode-independence of the envelope exponenta/2a/2) is the content of the kernel factorization: the envelope(x​y)−a/2(xy)^{-a/2}is universal across all eigenmodes, and only the spectral weightF~​(ω)\widetilde{F}(\omega)distinguishes them. This separation of geometric universality from spectral content is the mathematical essence of criticality.

## 7The Discrete Lattice

## 7.1Eigenvector collapse

## Remark 16.

Theorem15is the multiplicative analogue of the well-known fact that Bloch wavesei​k​xe^{ikx}are not square-summable onℤ\mathbb{Z}. The self-similarity condition (16) demands that every eigenvector be a character of the multiplicative semigroup(ℕ,×)(\mathbb{N},\times), namelym↦m−a/2m\mapsto m^{-a/2}. Since the multiplicative group(ℝ+,×)(\mathbb{R}_{+},\times)has a single family of charactersxi​ωx^{i\omega}(parametrized continuously byω\omega), the lattice restriction collapses this continuous family to a single direction. The resolution is the same as for Bloch theory: pass to the continuum, where the eigenfunctions are generalized (distributional) and the spectrum is continuous.

## 7.3The discrete matrix as a continuum sampling

## 7.4Eigenvector universality and the limits of self-similarity as a diagnostic

The eigenvector collapse theorem and the continuum sampling picture together illuminate a subtlety in the numerical detection of scale invariance. In practice, one may attempt to test for eigenvector self-similarity by comparing the eigenvectors of a coarse-grained covariance matrixC(k)=B​C​BTC^{(k)}=B\,C\,B^{T}(whereBBis the block-averaging operator that groups variables into blocks of sizekk) with the block-averaged eigenvectorsv~i(k)=B​vi\tilde{v}_{i}^{(k)}=B\,v_{i}of the original matrixCC.

Numerical experiments reveal that the correlation betweenvi(k)v_{i}^{(k)}andv~i(k)\tilde{v}_{i}^{(k)}is extremely high—not only at the critical point, but throughout the parameter range[6]. This is the approximate, finite-dimensional manifestation of the eigenvector collapse established in Theorem15: the eigenvector geometry is generically preserved under coarse-graining because the envelopem−a/2m^{-a/2}is universal across all modes. Eigenvector self-similarity thus measures the smoothness of the covariance operator rather than scale invariance per se.

## Remark 19.

The Mellin framework makes the separation precise. The generalized eigenfunctionsψω​(x)=x−a/2+i​ω\psi_{\omega}(x)=x^{-a/2+i\omega}share a common power-law envelope foranyscale-invariant operator; only the Mellin multiplierF~​(ω)\widetilde{F}(\omega)distinguishes one operator from another. Criticality manifests not in the shape of the eigenfunctions but in the scale-free form ofF~​(ω)\widetilde{F}(\omega)—equivalently, in the power-law distribution of eigenvalues. This confirms that eigenvalue scaling, rather than eigenvector rescaling, is the robust diagnostic of critical organization, and that refined analyses of eigenspaces (such as subspace overlaps or participation ratios) are needed if one wishes to extract additional geometric information near criticality[6].

## 9Discussion and Outlook

We have developed a self-contained spectral theory of scale-invariant operators on the multiplicative half-line. The main results are:

(i)The kernel factorization theorem (Theorem4), which shows that every scale-invariant kernel separates into a universal power-law envelope(x​y)−a/2(xy)^{-a/2}and a shape functionF​(x/y)F(x/y).

(ii)The Mellin diagonalization (Theorem6), which identifies the generalized eigenfunctions asψω​(x)=x−a/2+i​ω\psi_{\omega}(x)=x^{-a/2+i\omega}and the eigenvalues as the Mellin multiplierF~​(ω)\widetilde{F}(\omega).

(iii)The eigenvector collapse theorem (Theorem15), which shows that discrete self-similarity is incompatible with a full spectral decomposition on the lattice, motivating the continuum formulation.

(iv)The decoupling of geometric and spectral exponents, which provides a precise mathematical characterization of multicriticality as the conditiona≠ba\neq b.

Several directions remain open. The generalization to mode-dependent scaling factorsγk​(n)\gamma_{k}(n)—where distinct eigenmodes carry independent geometric scaling dimensions—would accommodate systems with multiple coexisting fixed points. The relationship between the width parameterσ\sigmaof the Lorentzian multiplier and the effective spectral exponentbbof the finite truncation deserves a quantitative asymptotic analysis. Finally, the application of this framework to empirical correlation matrices—in financial[17,12], biological[15,24], neural[2], or physical[6]systems—could provide a practical diagnostic for proximity to criticality and a quantitative measure of multicriticality through the ratioa/ba/b.

## Remark 20(Connection to collective synchronization).

A deeper structural parallel may connect the mode-dependent generalization to the theory of collective synchronization[11,22]. In the Kuramoto model of globally coupled phase oscillators, the order parameter measuring phase coherence undergoes a continuous transition at a critical coupling strength, governed near criticality by a Landau expansion whose structure is that of a multicritical free energy. The natural frequenciesωi\omega_{i}of the oscillator population play the role of the mode-dependent scaling factorsγk​(n)\gamma_{k}(n): when all modes share a common geometric exponent—the kernel factorization condition of Theorem4—the system is analogous to a fully synchronized population with a single well-defined scaling dimension. The conditiona≠ba\neq bthen corresponds to partial synchronization, where distinct subpopulations of modes lock to different geometric scaling dimensions.

The Lorentzian plays a distinguished role in both frameworks. In the present theory, the Mellin multiplier (15) of the explicit kernel is a Lorentzian of widthσ=−ln⁡ρ\sigma=-\ln\rho. In the Kuramoto model, the exactly solvable case—the Ott–Antonsen reduction[16]—arises precisely when the frequency distributiong​(ω)g(\omega)is Lorentzian. This is not a superficial coincidence: in both settings, the Lorentzian is the Fourier (respectively Mellin) transform of an exponential decay, and its analytic structure in the complex plane is what permits exact dimensional reduction. A narrow Lorentzian (smallσ\sigma,ρ→1\rho\to 1) concentrates spectral weight nearω=0\omega=0and drivesa→ba\to b, analogous to a sharply peakedg​(ω)g(\omega)that promotes full synchronization.

The multicritical points known to arise in Kuramoto systems with bimodalg​(ω)g(\omega)—where the synchronization transition switches from continuous to discontinuous—suggest that systems with two competing geometric exponents may exhibit a richer phase structure than the simplea≠ba\neq bcriterion captures. In the language of the present framework, a bimodal distribution of scaling dimensionsa​(ω)a(\omega)would correspond to two coexisting fixed-point geometries, each attracting a subpopulation of Mellin modes. The mode-dependent generalization outlined above is positioned to explore this possibility.
