## Abstract

We study the Katz-Long-Moody (KLM) construction and classify exactly when the resulting braid group representations factor through Hecke algebras. We show that, except for one exceptional two-strand family, this property depends only on the eigenvalues of the free-group part and is independent of the construction parameter. We also give an exact criterion for when the resulting representations factor through Temperley-Lieb algebras.

## 1Introduction

Representations of the braid groupBnB_{n}in which the images of the standard
generatorsσ1,…,σn−1\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}satisfy a quadratic relation(ρ​(σi)−a)​(ρ​(σi)−b)=0(\rho(\sigma_{i})-a)(\rho(\sigma_{i})-b)=0,a,b∈𝒌×a,b\in\boldsymbol{k}^{\times},
occupy a special place in low-dimensional topology and mathematical physics:
they are precisely the representations that factor through the
Iwahori–Hecke algebra of typeAn−1A_{n-1}, and through them one obtains the
HOMFLY-PT invariants of links[14], as well as large families of
unitary braid representations relevant to topological quantum computation[22,7]. The prototype is the Burau representation[6], whose generators have the two eigenvalues11and−t-t.

There is a substantial topological literature on constructing Hecke
algebra representations. Lawrence realized Hecke algebra modules by
twisted homology and monodromy of configuration spaces[17,18], obtaining the representations attached to
two-row Young diagrams, and extended the construction to representations of the Iwahori–Hecke
algebra associated with arbitrary multi-row Young diagrams[19]. Bigelow
subsequently gave a homological construction of Iwahori–Hecke modules
and formulated a corresponding realization problem for the irreducible
ones[2]. Most directly relevant to the present paper,
Bigelow and Tian developed several generalizations of the Long–Moody
construction[3]: they extended it to subgroups ofBnB_{n}, recovering the Gassner representation of the pure braid group;
they introduced a Lawrence-type construction based on mixed braid
groupsBn,mB_{n,m}and proved that a quadratic Hecke relation satisfied by
the input representation ofBmB_{m}is inherited by the output[3, Theorem 5.4]; they observed a universal quadratic
relation for the Long–Moody matrices over the noncommutative group ringℤ​[Fn⋊Bn]\mathbb{Z}[F_{n}\rtimes B_{n}][3, Proposition 5.1]; and
they gave an explicit reduced version of the construction. Thus[3]already provides methods for producing Hecke
algebra representations by generalized Long–Moody procedures.
Functorial and categorical extensions of the Long–Moody construction
have been developed by Soulié[25].

The problem considered here is different and complementary. Starting
from the semidirect-product inputρ:Fn⋊Bn→GL​(V)\rho\colon F_{n}\rtimes B_{n}\to\mathrm{GL}(V)defined by the Artin action
(recalled in Section2), we ask forintrinsicconditions on the matricesgi:=ρ​(xi)g_{i}:=\rho(x_{i})andsi:=ρ​(σi)s_{i}:=\rho(\sigma_{i})under which the output of the Katz–Long–Moody construction is of Hecke
type. In particular, we do not assume that an auxiliary input
representation already satisfies a Hecke relation; our aim is to detect
the quadratic relation directly from the spectral and Jordan-theoretic
data of the given representation, and to track the effect of the
quotient defining the construction.

In[12], K. Hiroe and the author introduced the
Katz–Long–Moody (KLM) construction, which unifies the Long–Moody
construction of braid group representations[20]with Katz’s middle convolution algorithm[15,8,9]. Given a representationρ:Fn⋊Bn→GL​(V)\rho\colon F_{n}\rtimes B_{n}\to\mathrm{GL}(V)of the semidirect product of the

In the companion paper[23]the author showed that, upon

## 6Discussion

## Summary: why the KLM quotient produces Hecke
representations

The mechanism uncovered here can be summarized as follows. The
eigenvalue11ofgg, unavoidable for interesting inputs, is precisely
the obstruction to a Hecke relation: it contributes the eigenvalue pair{c,−c}\{c,-c\}to the intermediate representation (Theorem3.1). The KLM construction removes this obstruction
structurally rather than by hypothesis: the quotient by the invariant
subspaceKKabsorbs exactly these pairs, whileLLremoves, at
resonant parameters, a further copy ofspec⁡(si)\operatorname{spec}(s_{i})(Lemma5.2). Hecke algebra representations thus arise not
because the input avoids the eigenvalue11, but because the
construction itself disposes of its contribution — which is why the
classification of Theorem4.4is governed byspec⁡(g)∖{1}\operatorname{spec}(g)\setminus\{1\}alone.

## Iteration and Hecke-type input

Theorem5.4and Proposition5.8reduce the Hecke question for arbitrary input to concrete linear
algebra: the candidate parameters are constrained twist-independently;
the parameterλ\lambdacan delete only eigenvalues ofsis_{i}, throughLL(Lemma5.2), so that forn≥3n\geq 3it can never
create the two-distinct-root Hecke property in the scalar family
(Theorem4.4(iii)), while forn=2n=2resonance
can (Theorem4.4(iv)); whether resonance can
absorb a Jordan obstruction on the quotient for general input remains
open. The remaining
obstruction is the semisimplicity coupling at the spectral overlapsspec⁡(si)∩(−spec⁡(si​gi))\operatorname{spec}(s_{i})\cap\bigl(-\operatorname{spec}(s_{i}g_{i})\bigr), resolved by the
projection criterion at the intermediate level and by the containment
criterion of Remark3.7on the quotient. By Corollary5.7, in the twist-nontrivial regimeCi≠IC_{i}\neq I(withsis_{i}semisimple anddimK+dimL<n−1\dim K+\dim L<n-1) both Hecke parameters must already
occur inspec⁡(si)\operatorname{spec}(s_{i})(Corollary5.7), so the overlap
regimespec⁡(si)∩(−spec⁡(si​gi))≠∅\operatorname{spec}(s_{i})\cap\bigl(-\operatorname{spec}(s_{i}g_{i})\bigr)\neq\emptysetis
the typical one: the
input is then itself of Hecke type.

For input of Hecke type,spec⁡(si)={a,b}\operatorname{spec}(s_{i})=\{a,b\}, Corollary5.7shows that, wheneverK+LK+Lis too small to absorb
entire eigenvalues, the spectral condition reduces tospec⁡(si​gi)⊆{−a,−b}\operatorname{spec}(s_{i}g_{i})\subseteq\{-a,-b\}; the Hecke property itself is
then decided by the containment criterion of Theorem5.4. An inheritance result of this flavor is
known in a different setting: Bigelow and Tian proved that their
Lawrence-type construction, whose input is a representation ofBmB_{m}satisfying a quadratic Hecke relation, produces an output satisfying the
same relation[3, Theorem 5.4]. The problem below asks
for the analogous classification within the KLM construction, starting
from semidirect-product input. Classifying the pairs(si,gi)(s_{i},g_{i})with
this property, and determining whether the property can be sustained under
iteration of the KLM construction, appears to be a natural next step; we
note that for Burau-type input the listspec(si)⊎(n−1)⊎(−spec(sigi))\operatorname{spec}(s_{i})^{\uplus(n-1)}\uplus(-\operatorname{spec}(s_{i}g_{i}))of the intermediate
representation generically contains more than two values, and which of
them survive is governed byK+LK+L. A classical benchmark for cubic
behaviour is the Lawrence–Krammer–Bigelow representation[17,16,1], obtained from the
one-dimensional representationσ1↦t\sigma_{1}\mapsto tofB2B_{2}by a
generalized Long–Moody construction[3, Theorem 5.3]; after a rescaling and change of
parameters, Zinno identified it with the irreducible module of the
Birman–Murakami–Wenzl (BMW) algebra indexed by the one-row Young
diagram withn−2n-2boxes[30], so that its standard

was introduced independently by Birman–Wenzl and Murakami[5,21].
