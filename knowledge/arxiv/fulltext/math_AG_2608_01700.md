## Abstract

Given a Riemann surface Σ, let Γ {\subseteq} Mod(Σ) denote the monodromy subgroup of a family of complex curves homeomorphic to Σ, arising from a sufficently ample Lefschetz pencil. We establish that the group Γ acts with Zariski dense orbits or ergodically on certain character varieties for {π_1}(Σ). This answers a version of a Conjecture of Katzarkov, Pantev, and Simpson appearing in [KPS03].

## Theorem 1.4.

LetXXbe a smooth projective surface,DDa very ample divisor onXX. Letk≥7k\geq 7. LetΓ\Gammabe the monodromy group associated to the complete linear system|k​D|,|kD|,equivalently it is the monodromy group associated to a Lefschetz pencil in this complete linear system.

The assumption thatGGis semisimple is necessary, both Conjectures are false for reductive groups withGa​b≠0G^{ab}\neq 0. See subsection1.2for further details.

Our approach is completely different from that of[5]. They approach the problem of proving Zariski density by finding a specific representationρ\rhothat is fixed by a finite index subgroup of the mapping class group. They then prove that for thisρ\rho, the induced action of (a finite index subgroup of)Mod​(Σ)\textrm{Mod}(\Sigma)onTρ​𝒳G​(Σ)T_{\rho}\mathcal{X}_{G}(\Sigma)has Zariski dense orbits. This is enough to establish the result. This approach while quite interesting, is very dependent on finding finite subgroups with specified properties inside the ambient groupGG, which is why the argument only works forG​L2​n+1​(ℂ).GL_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C}).Our methods on the other hand are more reminiscent of the sewing methods involved in[6](along with methods developed in[2]).

Let us emphasize that the most interesting situation when Theorem1.4holds is whenπ1​X\pi_{1}Xis a large complicated group. Indeed, ifπ1​X=0\pi_{1}X=0, then the results of[2]imply thatΓ\Gammais of finite index in the mapping class group. The results of Pickrell-Xia in[6](and a little more work to pass fromGGto a maximal compact subgroup insideGG) would then imply Theorem1.4in that case.

However ifπ1​X\pi_{1}Xis a large group, thenΓ\Gammais very much of infinite index inMod​(Σ)\textrm{Mod}(\Sigma), and these groups can have interesting properties. This was explored by the author in[3], there one can see interesting relations between the groupsH1​(X,ℤ)H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})andΓ.\Gamma.

Finally we’d like to note that there are indeed somewhat large subgroups ofMod​(Σg)\textrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{g})that act with non Zariski dense orbits: Saadi[8]produces a filling family of multicurves, whose associated Dehn twists preserve a rational function.
