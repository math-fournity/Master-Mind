## Abstract.

Prudence is a stability property of risk functionals recently introduced by Wang and Zitikis and subsequently studied by Amarante and Liebrich. In this paper, we first establish general relationships between prudence and other stability properties, showing, in particular, that weak prudence and prudence coincide for a broad class of convex, law-invariant functionals. We then prove that prudence is preserved by cash-additive hulls of star-shaped functionals under a simple asymptotic condition, and by inf-convolutions of convex, cash-additive, law-invariant prudent functionals. Our results provide general methods for constructing prudent risk measures from existing prudent functionals.

## 1.Introduction

In practice, risk positions are often approximated by positions with simpler structures, under the assumption that the corresponding risk-measure values exhibit suitable approximation properties. Specifically, let𝒳{\mathcal{X}}be a collection of random variables representing loss positions, and letρ:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]be a risk functional. Let(Xn)n=1∞⊂𝒳(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{X}}andX∈𝒳X\in{\mathcal{X}}be such that(Xn)(X_{n})converges toXXin some sense. It is then desirable thatρ​(X)\rho(X)can be controlled by a suitable limit of the sequence(ρ​(Xn))(\rho(X_{n})). Properties of this type are sometimes referred to as stability properties.

In the recent literature, Wang and Zitikis[32]introduced the notion of prudence in their study of axiomatic characterizations of Expected Shortfall.
A functionalρ:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]is said to beprudentifρ​(X)≤limnρ​(Xn)whenever​Xn→a.s.X​and​limnρ​(Xn)​exists in​[−∞,∞].\rho(X)\leq\lim_{n}\rho(X_{n})\quad\text{whenever }X_{n}\xrightarrow{a.s.}X\text{ and }\lim_{n}\rho(X_{n})\text{ exists in }[-\infty,\infty].

The motivation is that, when a sequence(Xn)(X_{n})is used to approximateXX, the quantitylimnρ​(Xn)\lim_{n}\rho(X_{n})serves as an upper bound forρ​(X)\rho(X). Prudent risk measures can therefore serve useful regulatory purposes. The importance of prudence is further underscored by the fact that it was identified in[32]as one of the four axioms characterizing Expected Shortfall. Recently, Amarante and Liebrich[3]studied this property in depth for distortion risk measures and general risk functionals. In their analysis, they also observed connections between prudence and other stability properties. In particular, prudence is equivalent to the super Fatou property.

The super Fatou property was originally introduced by Gao and Munari[15]in the study of surplus-invariant risk measures. More generally, many stability properties can be formulated within a unified framework. Letη\etadenote a mode of convergence on𝒳{\mathcal{X}}. A common stability requirement is that(1.1)ρ​(X)≤lim infnρ​(Xn),whenever​Xn→𝜂X,\displaystyle\rho(X)\leq\liminf_{n}\rho(X_{n}),\quad\text{whenever }X_{n}\xrightarrow{\eta}X,

In other words,ρ\rhois required to be lower semicontinuous with respect toη\eta.

Whenη\etais the convergence induced by a topology on𝒳{\mathcal{X}}, for example the norm topology, this condition reduces to the usual notion of lower semicontinuity with respect to that topology. Whenη\etais dominated almost sure convergence in𝒳{\mathcal{X}}, that is,Xn→𝜂XX_{n}\xrightarrow{\eta}Xmeans thatXn⟶a.s.XX_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}Xand there existsY∈𝒳Y\in{\mathcal{X}}such that|Xn|≤Y\lvert X_{n}\rvert\leq Ya.s. for alln∈ℕn\in{\mathbb{N}}, it becomes theFatou propertyofρ\rho, which was introduced by Delbaen[11]in the study of dual representations of risk measures onL∞L^{\infty}to ensure that the dual elements in the representation can be chosen fromL1L^{1}.
This property has since been extensively studied in the literature, see, e.g., Chen et al.[9], Gao et al.[17], Gao et al.[18], Gao and Xanthos[19], Jouini et al.[21], Liebrich and Munari[26].

Whenη\etais almost sure convergence, the resulting property is called thesuper Fatou property.
Recall thatρ\rhois said to beweakly lower semicontinuousifη\etais convergence in distribution in (1.1). As observed by Gao, Munari and Xanthos[16, Remark 3.7], weak lower semicontinuity is equivalent to the super Fatou property for law-invariant functionals, owing to the classical Skorohod representation theorem. Hence, for law-invariant functionals, prudence, the super Fatou property, and weak lower semicontinuity are equivalent notions. This equivalence highlights the fundamental role of prudence among stability properties of risk functionals and naturally raises questions regarding its characterization and preservation.

Beyond the fact that Expected Shortfall is prudent,[16]raised the more general question of whether Haezendonck–Goovaerts risk measures are prudent. This question was answered affirmatively by Amarante and Liebrich[3]. Nevertheless, despite its close connections with several classical stability properties, relatively little is known about the structural properties of prudence and about operations that generate or preserve prudent risk measures.

The present paper contributes to this line of research from several perspectives.
Section 2 investigates the relationship between prudence and other stability properties. In particular, we establish the equivalence between weak prudence and prudence for convex, law-invariant functionals under a natural and mild condition. This result provides new insight into a question raised by Amarante and Liebrich[3]concerning the relationship between the two notions.

Section 3 studies the preservation of prudence under the cash-additive hull operation and establishes a broad and easily verifiable sufficient condition. The cash-additive hull is a standard tool for constructing cash-additive risk measures from more general functionals, and notable examples obtained in this way include Expected Shortfall and Haezendonck–Goovaerts risk measures. Consequently, our result provides a convenient method for constructing prudent cash-additive risk measures from prudent star-shaped functionals.

Finally, Section 4 proves that prudence is preserved by inf-convolutions of convex, cash-additive, law-invariant functionals. Since inf-convolution is a fundamental operation in capital allocation and risk sharing, this result shows that prudence is stable under a natural aggregation mechanism and identifies a broad class of prudent functionals that remain prudent under aggregation.

## 2.Stability properties of risk functionals

Let𝒳{\mathcal{X}}be a lattice ideal ofL0L^{0}.
Recall from[3]that a functionalρ:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]is said to beweakly prudentifρ​(X)≤limnρ​(Xn)\rho(X)\leq\lim_{n}\rho(X_{n})wheneverXn→a.s.XX_{n}\xrightarrow{a.s.}Xandlimnρ​(Xn)\lim_{n}\rho(X_{n})exists inℝ\mathbb{R}. Prudence requires the same inequality wheneverlimnρ​(Xn)\lim_{n}\rho(X_{n})exists in[−∞,∞][-\infty,\infty]. Thus, prudence is a priori stronger than weak prudence. A natural question is whether the two notions are equivalent under additional assumptions. In particular, Amarante and Liebrich[3, Sect. 3.2]asked whether weak prudence and prudence are equivalent for law-invariant functionals.
The main result of this section provides a broad sufficient condition for an affirmative answer.

## 3.Prudence of Cash-Additive Hulls

In this section,𝒳{\mathcal{X}}denotes a vector space overℝ\mathbb{R}, andΠ∈𝒳∖{0}\Pi\in{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{0\}is fixed. The vectorΠ\Piplays the role of a numéraire.

Star-shaped risk measures have attracted considerable attention in recent years; see, for example, Castagnoli et al.[8], Laeven et al.[23,24], Liebrich[25], Righi[30]. ## 4.Prudence of Inf-Convolutions

In this section, we show that prudence is preserved under inf-convolution for convex, cash-additive, law-invariant functionals.
Let𝒳{\mathcal{X}}be an r.i. space over a non-atomic probability space(Ω,ℱ,ℙ)(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{P}}).
For two proper functionalsρi:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho_{i}:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty],i=1,2i=1,2, their inf-convolutionρ1​□​ρ2:𝒳→[−∞,∞]\rho_{1}\square\rho_{2}:{\mathcal{X}}\to[-\infty,\infty]is defined byρ1​□​ρ2​(X):=inf{ρ1​(Y)+ρ2​(Z):X=Y+Z,Y∈𝒳,Z∈𝒳},∀X∈𝒳.\rho_{1}\square\rho_{2}(X):=\inf\{\rho_{1}(Y)+\rho_{2}(Z):X=Y+Z,\;Y\in{\mathcal{X}},\;Z\in{\mathcal{X}}\},\qquad\forall X\in{\mathcal{X}}.

When𝒳=Lp{\mathcal{X}}=L^{p}, Filipović and Svindland[14]showed that ifρ1,ρ2:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho_{1},\rho_{2}:{\mathcal{X}}\to(-\infty,\infty]are both convex, cash additive, law invariant, and have the Fatou property, thenρ1​□​ρ2:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho_{1}\square\rho_{2}:{\mathcal{X}}\to(-\infty,\infty]is convex, cash additive,
law invariant, and has the Fatou property. See also Acciaio[1], Chen, Gao and Xanthos[10], and Liu, Wang, and Wei[27]for further results on preservation of properties by inf-convolutions.

In particular, the result of Filipović and Svindland was extended in[10]fromLpL^{p}-spaces to arbitrary r.i. spaces, with the Fatou property replaced by the strong Fatou property. It was later shown in[31, Theorem 3.3]that, on every r.i. space other thanL1L^{1}, the Fatou property and the strong Fatou property coincide for convex, law-invariant functionals. Combined with theL1L^{1}case in[14], this yields that inf-convolutions of convex, cash-additive, law-invariant functionals on arbitrary r.i. spaces preserve the Fatou property and continue to take values in(−∞,∞](-\infty,\infty]. We now show that prudence is also preserved under inf-convolution for such functionals.

## Theorem 4.1.

Suppose that𝒳{\mathcal{X}}is an r.i. space. Letρi:𝒳→(−∞,∞]\rho_{i}:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]be proper, convex, cash-additive, law-invariant, prudent functionals,i=1,2i=1,2. Then their inf-convolutionρ1​□​ρ2\rho_{1}\square\rho_{2}is also prudent.
