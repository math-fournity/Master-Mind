## Abstract

Models of complex systems often have many parameters, yet are constrained by far fewer experimentally accessible observables: similar activity can emerge from coordinated parameter changes. We formalize these compatible parameter sets as \emph{viable parameter manifolds}: the inverse images of a system's target dynamical behaviors under a parameter-to-feature map. The relevant codimension is not the number of reported features, but the effective rank of that map at the target scale. Co-varying features lower the codimension, while poor conditioning, high curvature, or regime mixing degrade learnability. We train conditional score-based diffusion models on simulated parameter--feature pairs and use them as amortized samplers of prior-weighted viable sets. In the Lorenz system, scalar trajectory statistics generate thin viable sheets, and two-feature conditioning localizes a transition-adjacent corridor. In the Izhikevich neuron model, four firing descriptors lie close to a nearly two-dimensional family of features, and the learned inverse images reveal distinct regular and irregular compensation geometries. In a recent ODE reduction of finite spiking networks, the same framework reveals excitatory--inhibitory compensation, timescale--coupling tradeoffs, and input-dependent viable manifolds across 4--12 parameter dimensions. In this view, robustness, compensation, and hidden parameter dependencies are organized as inverse geometry, with diffusion models providing practical tools for sampling, visualizing, and interrogating that geometry.

## Significance Statement

Biological dynamical systems often admit many parameter combinations that produce the same function. We show that these non-unique inverse solutions form structured viable parameter manifolds whose dimension, tangent directions, curvature, and multimodality quantify how observables constrain the underlying dynamics. Conditional diffusion models learn these manifolds directly from simulation data in Lorenz, Izhikevich, and neuronal network models. The learned geometry reveals compensation laws, hidden parameter couplings, and the number of effective degrees of freedom that remain after the function is fixed. By turning robustness from a qualitative observation into a measurable geometric object, this framework provides a practical route to analyzing degeneracy and mechanism in complex biological models.

Keywords:diffusion models; viable parameter manifold; robustness; compensation geometry; computational neuroscience; dynamical systems

## 1Introduction

A central lesson from biological dynamics is that function can be reproducible even when the parameters that generate it are not. Neurons with different conductance combinations can fire in similar ways, networks with different synaptic couplings can produce comparable population activity, and systems biology models often preserve output while many microscopic parameters vary over wide ranges[1,2,3,4,5,6]. Across fields, this phenomenon appears as sloppiness, model-manifold compression, structural non-identifiability, dynamical compensation, and biological degeneracy[7,6,8,9,10,11,12,1]. This degeneracy is not merely a nuisance for parameter estimation. It is one mechanism by which biological systems remain robust, adaptable, and evolvable[13,14,15,2,5]. Yet most inverse analyses still report isolated parameter fits, local sensitivities, or low-dimensional sweeps, leaving the full shape of the compatible parameter set largely unseen.

We propose that the natural object of study is theviable parameter manifold:
the set of parameters that realizes a specified dynamical behavior. If𝜽∈Θ⊂ℝk\bm{\theta}\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}denotes model parameters andℐ​(𝜽)\mathcal{I}(\bm{\theta})denotes a low-dimensional vector of statistics extracted from simulated dynamics, then a target behavioryydefinesℳy={𝜽∈Θ:ℐ​(𝜽)=y}.\mathcal{M}_{y}=\{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta:\mathcal{I}(\bm{\theta})=y\}.(1)

Within a fixed dynamical regime, the statisticsℐ\mathcal{I}often vary smoothly with the parameters, soℳy\mathcal{M}_{y}is locally a level set. This simple observation changes the inverse problem. Instead of asking which parameter configuration is best, we study the viable parameter manifold as the inverse image of the parameter-to-feature map,ℳy=ℐ−1​(y)\mathcal{M}_{y}=\mathcal{I}^{-1}(y). Then we inquire the geometrical features ofℳy\mathcal{M}_{y}, namely the number of (local) dimensions, how it bends, and whether it splits into multiple mechanistic branches.

This geometric viewpoint gives direct scientific meaning to quantities that are otherwise treated as technical details. Intrinsic dimension estimates the number of effective degrees of freedom that remain after fixing a target behavior. Tangent spaces encode compensation laws, i.e., infinitesimal parameter co-variations that preserve the same output. Curvature measures how these laws change across the viable set. Nearby level sets show how changes in the desired behavior moves through parameter space. Disconnected components or irregular folds suggest that the chosen observables may be mixing distinct dynamical regimes, often near bifurcations or attractor transitions. Thus, the viable-manifold language used here gives an inverse-geometric way to connect parameter nonuniqueness to prior work on robustness, adaptability, and evolvability[13,14,15].

The computational challenge is that these manifolds are embedded in parameter spaces that are too large for exhaustive sweeps and too structured for pointwise optimization because of the curse of dimensionality. Conditional score-based diffusion models offer a practical way to uncover and to represent them. We train a diffusion model on simulated parameter–feature pairs and then condition the sampler on a target feature vector to generate parameters compatible with that target[16,17]. Recent theory helps explain why this is plausible: under suitable regularity assumptions, score-based diffusion can adapt to smooth low-dimensional manifold structure without explicitly estimating the manifold[18]. In our setting, diffusion is therefore used as an amortized sampler of inverse geometry rather than a black-box optimizer.

We develop this view across three dynamical systems of increasing complexity and biological relevance. In the Lorenz system[19], scalar trajectory statistics generate thin viable sheets and provide a controlled calibration of the geometry. In the Izhikevich neuron model[20,21], target firing statistics reveal parameter manifolds whose tangent directions express compensatory changes among excitability, reset, and adaptation parameters. In dsODE reductions of finite spiking networks[22], viable manifolds expose how excitatory–inhibitory balance extends beyond two-dimensional sweeps to hidden dependencies among synaptic strengths, timescales, connection probabilities, and external drives. Across these systems, the central message is the same: compatible parameters are not a cloud of arbitrary alternatives. They are organized geometric objects, and learning their geometry turns robustness from a qualitative observation into a measurable structure.

Throughout the main analysis, we make one explicit local regularity approximation. Under this approximation, the long-time statistics used as target features depend smoothly on𝜽\bm{\theta}, and a diffusion model conditioned onyyshould return samples close to a smooth level-set manifold. This assumption is deliberately local rather than global. When it fails, the failure is itself informative: split clouds, strong folding, abrupt changes in intrinsic dimension, or unstable local tangent spaces suggest that the target neighborhood crosses a bifurcation, mixes multiple attractor branches, or uses summaries that do not fully separate dynamical regimes.

## 2.3Izhikevich neurons: viable manifolds organize firing statistics beyond categorical classes

We chose these standard descriptors because they are both widely used observables in analyses of cortical and single-neuron spiking[24,25,26,27,28].

## 2.4dsODE synaptic manifolds extend E–I landscape geometry into network inverse problems

We next moved to dsODE, a deterministic reduction of finite-sized spiking neuronal networks (SNN). dsODE uses a discretized voltage-bin structure to represent the population voltage configuration of the SNN, and uses pending synaptic variables to describe the recurrent drives to neuron populations. In different sweeps of the parameter space, dsODE reveals and faithfully reproduces fixed points, one-beat and multi-beat oscillations, coexistence of multiple attractors, and the bifurcation organization of the underlying spiking network model[22]. These properties make dsODE an especially useful testbed for inverse problems: it remains mechanistically tied to SNN dynamics, yet is light enough to support the large simulation libraries needed for conditional generation. Without a reduction of this kind, broad parameter evaluations at the level of the full SNN would be computationally prohibitive, especially if one wishes to preserve the attractor and bifurcation structure that organizes viable solutions[30,22]. Xiao et al. showed that the viable firing-rate region is thin in this synaptic-strength landscape, so single-parameter perturbations of order1%1\%can already leave the viable set[30], while dsODE showed that varying these same couplings organizes a much richer set of dynamical regimes[22]. Following Xiao et al., we analyzed the four-dimensional synaptic box in theinhibition-plane coordinates(SI​E/SI​I,SE​I/SE​E)(S_{IE}/S_{II},S_{EI}/S_{EE}), whose product acts as a suppression index for E-cells. The corresponding viable set is therefore close to codimension-1 in the four-dimensional synaptic space and appears as hyperbolic viable strips in the inhibition plane, consistent with Fig. 1A ofXiao et al. We then moved to a more driven regime, where E and I mean rates are less tightly coupled and therefore provide two less dependent constraints (Fig.5A middle). The learned mean-conditioned families become intrinsically thinner, with participation-ratio dimensions between about 1 and 2.2, even though their projection onto the inhibition plane remains strip-like. Direct re-simulation confirms recovery of the target mean rates (Fig.5A right). In this sense, the same inhibitory-balance mechanism still organizes the projection, but the intrinsic dimension now reflects the number of firing rate directions that are genuinely independent.

This already suggests that once temporal heterogeneity (e.g., oscillations or bursting behavior) becomes appreciable, mean rates alone may not fully resolve the viable structure in this synaptic box.
As in the one-beat and two-beat dynamics reported previously[22,31], our simulations show that distinct oscillatory families can have similar long-time average E/I rates while differing strongly in temporal modulation amplitude. We therefore next conditioned on the standard deviations (std) of the excitatory and inhibitory rates. The resulting families in Fig.5B differ qualitatively from the mean-conditioned ones: they can split, becoming visibly multimodal in the same inhibition plane, even though direct re-simulation still validates the requested std targets. Parameter configurations located in different clusters correspond to 1-beat and 2-beat oscillatory dynamics, respectively (Fig.5B right).

We regard that irregularity as informative rather than as a failure of the generator. Conditional manifold learning is easiest when the inverse support varies smoothly with the target locally, but near bifurcations that support can fold or split because nearby parameters lie on different dynamical branches while possibly maintaining similar low-order summaries. Mean-conditioned sets therefore tend to remain close to the single compensation strip imposed by E–I balance, whereas std-conditioned sets are more branch-sensitive and can separate distinct dynamical regimes, e.g., one-beat from two-beat responses.

## 2.6Dimensionality and regularity determine both learnability and interpretation

Across all three systems, the same principle appears repeatedly: diffusion learns viable sets most effectively when the target-conditioned inverse family is both low-dimensional and regular at the tolerance scale. This is precisely where the method becomes computationally useful. Letℐ:Θ→ℝry\mathcal{I}:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}^{r_{y}}be the feature map, withΘ⊂ℝk\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}. In a bounded parameter box, anε\varepsilon-tube around such a set occupies only anO​(εry)O(\varepsilon^{r_{y}})fraction of the ambient volume. Therefore, unguided rejection or nearest-neighbor search pays a penalty in the normal directions before it ever resolves the tangential geometry.

By contrast, conditional diffusion amortizes the need for simulation in parameter space: one simulation library can be reused across many nearby target queries, and inference becomes direct proposal generation followed by re-simulation validation.
More importantly, the cost of building the library can itself be reduced by the regularity of the viable set and is therefore not fully described by a naive exponential-in-ddgrid law. Recent theory shows that score-based diffusion can adapt to smooth low-dimensional manifold structure without explicitly estimating the manifold. By contrast, observables aligned with the active normals of the parameter-to-feature map can contract clouds, separate nearby regimes, and regularize local geometry. ## 3Discussion

This work recasts inverse modeling in dynamical systems as the study of viable parameter manifolds. The typical parameter-fitting task answers one question:can the model reproduce the target?A viable manifold answers a deeper question:In how many mechanistically distinct ways can the model reproduce it and how must parameters co-vary to preserve it?Throughout this study, the compatible parameter sets were not featureless clouds but manifolds with geometry. The geometry matters not only for fitting but also for what a model can generalize, explain, and render interpretable, echoing the sloppy-model view that predictions are controlled by a small number of effective parameter combinations[7,6,33,8,9].

The three case studies play complementary roles. The Lorenz system shows the basic inverse geometry in a classical setting: fixing a scalar trajectory statistic cuts a three-dimensional parameter box into thin 2D sheets. In the Izhikevich model of single-neuron dynamics, four common firing descriptors co-vary strongly rather than acting as four independent constraints, so the inverse images remain extended manifolds in a four-dimensional parameter space. Their tangent directions describe compensatory changes among physiological parameters. The dsODE examples then move from single-neuron compensation to network homeostasis. Mean rates recover excitatory–inhibitory balance-like strips, rate fluctuations split branches that would merge when targeted on mean rates alone. Furthermore, higher-dimensional generations reveal tradeoffs among recurrent strength, filtering timescale, refractory effects, and external drive. In this sense, the learned parameter manifolds provide population-of-models descriptions of neuronal degeneracy and homeostatic compensation rather than opaque families of equally good fits[1,2,3,4,5,29].

A useful way to summarize these results is that degeneracy is not disorder. In biological models, many parameter combinations can produce the same function or behavior, but these combinations are constrained by the key features in the behavior. Intrinsic dimension measures the number of free local parameter directions. Tangent spaces identify the local compensation laws. The relative location of neighboring level sets shows how the function changes as parameters move (local coordinates of tangent spaces and normal directions shown in Fig.7C).
Curvature tells us when a simple linear compensation rule is only local, and component structure reveals alternative mechanisms or multiple regimes. In this paper these geometric quantities are treated as mechanistic observables extracted from the inverse problem, complementing the forward model-manifold view of sloppy systems[33,9,8].

Thus our results also clarify the true goal of target design. Imposing more feature coordinates is useful only when those coordinates add independent active information about the parameter-to-feature map. Conversely, a small number of well-chosen observables resolving the true geometry can sharply contract a viable set or separate dynamical branches if they are well aligned with directions along which the system dynamics actually changes.

This regular-manifold viewpoint is deliberately local. Yet parameter spaces of nonlinear systems are not globally smooth in this sense. Bifurcations, coexistence of attractors, transient chaos, and branch switching can match the same low-order statistics with different mechanisms[34,23]. In such cases, irregular diffusion outputs are strong indicators of regime mixing or nearby bifurcation structure, and should trigger targeted simulation, continuation, or component-wise analysis rather than being averaged into a single, smooth manifold.

The reader should note that conditional diffusion doesnotremove the need for simulation nor guarantee uniform sampling on a level set. Therefore our framework shouldnotbe treated as a universal solver for high-dimensional bifurcation discovery. It is valuable here because diffusion amortizes inverse queries and returns structured candidate sets that can be validated and analyzed geometrically. This perspective connects several traditions that are often separated. The sloppy/model-manifold literature studies forward images in prediction space, whereas identifiability and dynamical-compensation theory emphasize inverse non-uniqueness; viable manifolds unify these viewpoints as inverse geometric objects[33,9,10,32,11]. From dynamical systems, it inherits the idea that qualitative regimes organize parameter space. From inverse problems, it inherits the importance of pre-images and identifiability. From neuroscience, it inherits the problem of robustness and degeneracy in neurons and circuits. From modern generative modeling, it gains a scalable way to sample complicated embedded sets.

This synthesis suggests a broader program: building regime-aware atlases of viable manifolds, using active learning to refine transition-adjacent strata, and combining diffusion samplers with classical continuation methods to trace bifurcation surfaces when the smooth-manifold assumption fails. The natural next step is therefore regime-aware inverse geometry. Promising directions include component-wise manifold learning, active refinement of transition-adjacent strata, and diffusion-assisted continuation along codimension-1 boundaries.

The main conceptual conclusion is that robust biological function corresponds to a structured space of possibilities rather than to a privileged parameter configuration. Learning that space makes robustness measurable, compensation visible, and hidden dependencies testable. Viable parameter manifolds therefore turn inverse modeling from the search for one answer into the analysis of the space of answers. By connecting modern generative modeling to classical dynamical-systems ideas, this study establishes a technical language for a core principle: that complex dynamical systems maintain robust function amid parameter variability through structured, low-dimensional mechanisms that we can now extract and analyze.
