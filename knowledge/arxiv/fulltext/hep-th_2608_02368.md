## Abstract

We study subleading asymptotic charges of a U(1) gauge field coupled to a charged massless scalar field in Minkowski spacetime. We consider a phase space for which the scalar-field initial data on null infinity have compact support, while the gauge-field initial data admit an asymptotic expansion with inverse-power tails in retarded (advanced) time, which we refer to as initial-data tails. We identify three finite charges at the null infinities which are conserved under scattering. One is a renormalized version of the standard subleading soft charge. The second is a logarithmic charge which is the difference of the coefficients of the leading order tails at the future and past ends of null infinity. The third is a new charge that probes the average of the tail coefficients. We conjecture that the third charge is associated with a new class of large gauge transformations which diverge linearly in retarded time.

## 1Introduction

In recent years, the study of asymptotic symmetries and
charges in gauge theory has revealed a rich infrared structure even in
the simplest abelian modelsStrominger (2018). In
four-dimensional Maxwell theory, large gauge transformations acting
nontrivially on the celestial two-sphere organize an infinite set of conserved charges. Their Ward identities reproduce
soft photon theorems, and their fluxes encode electromagnetic
memory. The relations between soft theorems, asymptotic symmetries,
and memory were first uncovered in the context of gravityBondiet al.(1962); Sachs (1962); Strominger and Zhiboedov (2016); Heet al.(2015), but
have similar nontrivial realizations in QED and related abelian
theoriesHeet al.(2014); Kapecet al.(2017); Campiglia and Laddha (2015); He and Mitra (2019).

The general form of conservation laws associated with large gauge transformations in QED is as follows. The leading member of this tower is associated with Weinberg’s soft photon theoremWeinberg (1965), whose form is universal and exact to all orders in perturbation theory. soft theorem, and this suggests that, beyond the familiar leading and
subleading charges, there should be a corresponding family of
logarithmic charges sensitive to the early- and late-time tails of the

Our method of analysis is based on that of Ref.Campiglia and Laddha (2019a)and is as follows. ## 5Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we enlarged the phase space ofCampiglia and Laddha (2019a)to admit1/u1/utails in the free data onℐ±\mathscr{I}^{\pm}. Within this enlarged phase space, we derived three conserved charges extending the analysis ofCampiglia and Laddha (2019a)in two distinct directions. First, we generalized the logarithmic asymptotic charge to allow massless sources. Second, we uncovered a new matching condition across spatial infinity, leading to a new family of conserved charges.

Throughout this paper, we assumed, followingCampiglia and Laddha (2019a),
that the scalar field falls off at spatial infinity faster than any
power of the hyperbolic radial coordinateρ\rho. This excludes soft
scalar data at spatial infinity and ensures that the matter source is
asymptotically separated from the radiative Maxwell field, so that the
induced equations onℋ0\mathcal{H}^{0}reduce to the source-free
Maxwell’s equations. Under this assumption, the inclusion of massive or
fermionic matter should not qualitatively modify the analysis atℋ0\mathcal{H}^{0}. By contrast, extending the construction to
non-abelian gauge theories or gravity would be substantially more
subtle, since the asymptotic equations would then be intrinsically
self-interacting. There has been recent progress in related directionsCaponeet al.(2023); Agrawalet al.(2024), including gravitational tail and peeling matching relationsCompère and Robert (2026a)and an asymptotic derivation of the classical logarithmic soft graviton theoremBoschetti and Campiglia (2026). An especially important open
direction is to generalize the definition of the phase space used here
to allow configurations in which both the scalar and Maxwell fields
carry soft data at spatial infinity.

A natural question is whether the present construction extends to all subleading orders. At tree level, the subn-leading soft charge takes the schematic formCampiglia and Laddha (2019a)Qntree​[ϵ]∼∫d​u​d​Ω​ϵ​(x^)​un​∂uℒ​(DA​AA(0)​(u,x^)),Q_{n}^{\rm tree}[\epsilon]\sim\int\text{d}u\text{d}\Omega\,\epsilon(\hat{x})u^{n}\partial_{u}\mathcal{L}\!\left(D^{A}A_{A}^{(0)}(u,\hat{x})\right)~,(76)

whereℒ\mathcal{L}is a differential operator onS2S^{2}. If one allows power-law tailsDA​AA(0)∼u−kD^{A}A_{A}^{(0)}\sim u^{-k}, the naive charge becomes logarithmically divergent atn=kn=kand power-law divergent forn>kn>k. Extending the present analysis so as to regularize these divergences at arbitrarynnis thus a natural future direction.

Recent workSahoo and Sen (2019); Krishna and Sahoo (2023); Karanet al.(2025); Banerjeeet al.(2026)on loop-corrected soft photon theorems suggests a general pattern in which thenn-loop contribution enters at orderωn−1​(log⁡ω)n\omega^{n-1}(\log\omega)^{n}in the soft expansion. The corresponding free radiative data are then expected to involve polyhomogeneous tails, schematicallyu−k−1​(log⁡u)ku^{-k-1}\left(\log u\right)^{k}atℐ+\mathscr{I}^{+}. In particular, the𝒪​(log⁡ω)\mathcal{O}\left(\log\omega\right)soft theorem is associated with the1/u1/utail inA0B\overset{0}{A}_{B}and is one-loop exact, in the sense that higher-loop corrections first arise at parametrically more suppressed orders in the soft expansion. This in turn suggests that it would be worthwhile to enlarge the phase space further so as to admit such polyhomogeneous tails. A complementary enlargement allowing leading logarithmic divergences in
the null data, generated by advanced/retarded radiation with finite energy flux, is analyzed inBriceñoet al.(2026).

Finally, it would be interesting to understand the algebra generated
byQlnn​[ϵ]\overset{\ln}{Q}_{n}[\epsilon],Q′lnn​[ϵ]\overset{\ln}{Q^{\prime}}_{n}[\epsilon], andQn​[ϵ]Q_{n}[\epsilon]on the
enlarged phase space. A better understanding of this phase space,
together with a quantization of the corresponding charge algebra,
should lead to a sharper picture of the infrared structure of gauge
theories.
