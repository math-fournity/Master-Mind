## Abstract

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, and let $G$ be an algebraic $k$-group. We study finite abelian $k$-subgroups $A \subset G$ whose order is not divisible by the characteristic of $k$. This is a classical topic in the theory of algebraic groups going back to the work of Borel in the early 1960s. We sharpen previously known results on the structure of $A$. In particular, we show that there exists a maximal torus $T$ of $G$ such that the index $[A: (A \cap T)]$ divides the Grothendieck torsion index $t(G)$. We also show that there exists a maximal torus $T$ such that the quotient group $A/(A \cap T)$ is ``small'' in a suitable sense.   As applications of these results, we (i) give a positive answer to a question of Totaro for $G$-torsors over fields $k_r = k((t_1))((t_2)) \ldots ((t_r))$ of iterated Laurent series, (ii) prove a variant of the ``hypothèse optimiste'' of Tits about splitting fields of $E_8$-torsors, and (iii) show that certain torsors over $k_r$ cannot be split by the function field of a genus $1$ curve.

## 1.Introduction

Letkkbe an algebraically closed field, and letGGbe an algebraickk-group, that is, a group scheme of finite type overkk. In this paper we study the finite abeliankk-subgroups ofGG. A maximal torus ofGGcontains many abeliankk-subgroups, calledtoral.
We are interested in “how far” an arbitrary finite abeliankk-subgroupAAofGGis from being toral. This is a classical topic in the theory of algebraic groups, going back to the work of Borel[Bor61]. Borel studied the closely related setting whereGGis replaced by a connected compact Lie groupCC. A prime numberppis called a torsion prime ofCCif the integral cohomology ringH∗​(C,ℤ)H^{*}(C,\mathbb{Z})has non-trivialpp-torsion elements. Borel showed thatppis a torsion prime forCCif and only ifH∗​(B​C,ℤ)H^{*}(BC,\mathbb{Z})haspp-torsion if and only ifCChas a non-toral elementary abelianpp-group; see[Bor61, Theorem 4.5].

## 1.5.Splitting torsors by genus 1 curves

By a genus11curve over a fieldKK, we shall mean a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of geometric genus11overKK. We shall say that a Brauer classα∈Br⁡(K)\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(K)is split by a smooth integralKK-varietyYYifα\alphapulls back to zero inBr⁡(Y)\operatorname{Br}(Y). By a theorem of Grothendieck, this is equivalent toα\alphapulling back to zero inBr⁡(K​(Y))\operatorname{Br}(K(Y)). The following question was asked independently by Clark[Cla07]and Saltman[RV11].

## Question 1.6.

(i) Any genus11curveCCis a torsor over its Jacobian. Fixing an integerg⩾1g\geqslant 1, we can ask a similar question in the case whereCCis allowed to be a torsor over agg-dimensional abelian variety.

(ii) Recall that
a Brauer classα∈Br⁡(K)\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(K)is uniquely represented by aPGLn\operatorname{PGL}_{n}-torsorXXoverKK, wherennis the Schur index ofα\alpha. Moreover, the genus11curveCCsplitsα\alphaif and only if it splitsXX. It is thus natural to replace the Brauer classα\alphaby a torsor under some smooth affine connectedkk-groupGG, not necessarilyPGLn\operatorname{PGL}_{n}.

Note that (i) was already considered in[RS26], whereas (ii) was not. Combining (i) and (ii), we arrive at the following more general question.
