Several key developments were recently brought about using a number of constructions utilizing Macbeath regions. ## Abstract

The efficient representation of convex bodies in multi-dimensional spaces is a fundamental problem in computational geometry. Several key developments were recently brought about using a number of constructions utilizing Macbeath regions. In this paper, we present a novel intrinsic approach for approximate membership testing, where we carry out the entire development based on structures derived from the Hilbert metric associated with a convex bodyKKinℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}. First, we revisit the construction of economical Delone sets, deriving the size bound based on the notion of volume entropy. Second, we design a new query structure based on a simple covering by ellipsoids, where queries are answered by ray shooting. As an added bonus, the intrinsic viewpoint facilitates finger searching, where the query time can be bounded by the distance traveled in the Hilbert metric.

Keywords:Convex bodies, Hilbert geometry, Macbeath regions, Membership testing.

## 1Introduction

Imre Bárány famously quipped,“Everything interesting that can happen to a convex body happens near its boundary”[11]. Evidently, many works on the representation and approximation of convex bodies concern their boundaries. A classical result by Dudley[23]established that, for any convex bodyKKinℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}, it is possible to construct anε\varepsilon-approximating polytope under the Hausdorff distance withO​(1/ε(d−1)/2)O(1/\varepsilon^{(d-1)/2})facets. The facets defining the Dudley approximation are obtained using a uniform sample on an enclosing sphere, which is then projected onto the boundary∂K\partial Kyielding a collection of tangent hyperplanes. The same result was also achieved by the dual construction of Bronshteyn and Ivanov[16].

Much earlier than Dudley’s classical result, Hilbert defined a metric on theinteriorof a convex body[29]. The Hilbert metric (defined in Section2.1) has a number of nice properties. It is invariant under projective transformations, and when the body is a Euclidean ball, it generalizes the Cayley-Klein model of hyperbolic geometry. (See the handbook on Hilbert geometry by Papadopoulos and Troyanov[39].) This intrinsic point of view provides new insights into classical questions from convexity theory. Despite its natural appeal, there has been little work on the Hilbert geometry in the algorithm design community.

In this paper, we adopt the intrinsic point of view in revisiting the problem of polytope membership testing. The significance of this problem is highlighted by its critical role in several recent developments in geometric approximation algorithms (see, e.g.,[6,5,1,3]). Polytope membership queries, both exact and approximate, arise in many application areas, such as linear programming and ray-shooting queries[36,34,18,40], nearest neighbor searching and the computation of extreme points[21,19,2], collision detection[24], and machine learning[17].

It is well known that polytope membership is dual to answering halfspace emptiness queries. Fornnpoints inℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}, the fastest exact data structure with near-linear space ford>3d>3has a query time of roughlyO​(n1−1/⌊d/2⌋)O\big(n^{1-1/\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor}\big)[35], which is prohibitive for many applications. Hence, it is natural to consider the problem in an approximate setting.Figure 1:Approximate Polytope Membership (APM).

Letε\varepsilonbe a positive real parameter, and letdiam⁡(K)\operatorname{diam}(K)denoteKK’s diameter. Given a query pointq∈ℝdq\in\mathbb{R}^{d}, anε\varepsilon-approximate polytope membership query(ε\varepsilon-APM query) returns a negative result ifq∉Kq\notin K, a positive result ifq∈Kq\in Kand its distance from the boundary ofKKis greater thanε⋅diam⁡(K)\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{diam}(K), and it may return either result otherwise; see Figure1(a). (Such aninner approximationis a bit more convenient in our framework, but our approach can easily be adapted to answer APM queries with respect to anouter approximation.) Our objective is to build an efficient data structure that can answer such queries inO​(log⁡1ε)O(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})time with a storage matching the optimal bound by Dudley.

Recent progress onε\varepsilon-APM queries arose from the application of Macbeath regions[33]. In contrast to more traditional space partitioning techniques, such as quadtrees (see Figure1(b)), Macbeath regions provide shape-sensitive covers enabling for the first time an optimal approach withO​(log⁡1/ε)O(\log 1/\varepsilon)query time andO​(1/ε(d−1)/2)O(1/\varepsilon^{(d-1)/2})storage[6]. The data structure of[6]is based on a hierarchy of rings providing progressively better approximations of the boundary, where queries are answered by shooting rays from a center point and tracing intersections with the rings.

Later on, the incorporation of the Hilbert metric was explored in[1], allowing a more straightforward solution with the same optimality guarantees. The data structure of[1]disposes of the ray shooting employed in[6]in favor of a simple hierarchical covering of the entire convex body (see Figure1(c)). Intuitively, the hierarchical cover proposed in[1]is based on progressively finer approximations, where queries are answered by descending through the hierarchy, tracing smaller covers containing the query point. Notably, those covers were related in a precise sense to Delone sets in the Hilbert metric, enabled by the intimate relationship between Macbeath regions and metric balls in the Hilbert geometry. A Delone set consists of a set of points that have useful packing and covering properties with respect to metric balls. However, the great potential of the Hilbert geometry was not fully utilized. Mainly, critical proofs reverted to the machinery of Macbeath regions, hindering further insights on convex approximation.

In this paper, we combine the best of the two approaches[6,1]by presenting a streamlined and self-contained development of an optimal data structure forε\varepsilon-APM queries. The key idea is to replace the hierarchical layer-based structure with a purelyflatstructure based on covering the convex body with ellipsoids. As in[6], APM queries are answered by shooting a ray from the origin towards the query point, but in our structure ray-shooting involves simplywalkingthe ray through the ellipsoids of the cover, see Figure1(d). We effectively combine the ray shooting approach from[6]with the covering approach of[1].

We take the development a step further, obtaining a new result related to finger searching (see Lemma4.1). In the field of data structures, afinger searchemploys a pointer to a location within a data structure, called afinger, and the query time is sensitive to the distance between the location of the finger and the query point. (See Brodal’s survey on this topic[14].) As an application, suppose that rather than performing a single APM query, we are interested in performing a series ofmmqueries along a path,q1,…​qmq_{1},\ldots q_{m}, where each pointqi−1q_{i-1}is close to its successorqiq_{i}. Notably, this scenario is fundamental to random walks in convex bodies where multiplemove proposalswithin a given neighborhood are checked at each step[30,31]. While a sequence ofmmqueries could be answered in total timeO​(m​log⁡1ε)O(m\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})by querying each point individually, finger search provides a faster solution. Rather than starting each search from scratch, the search forqiq_{i}is initiated where the latest searchqi−1q_{i-1}left off. We show that our new APM data structure supports efficient finger searches, where the time taken to answer an APM query for a pointqiq_{i}given a finger for pointqi−1q_{i-1}isO​(1+dK​(qi−1,qi))O(1+d_{K}(q_{i-1},q_{i})), wheredKd_{K}denotes the Hilbert distance relative to the bodyKK. (See Section2.1for definitions.) We also show that if the query points lie within anε\varepsilon-erosion ofKK, the Hilbert distance between any two of them is never larger thanO​(log⁡1ε)O(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}). This implies that finger searching is never slower, and potentially much faster, than answering queries individually.

Together, our main result is summarized as follows.

## 5Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we consider the derivation of geometric query structures intrinsic to the natural metrics defined by the data at hand. Leveraging prior work on the approximation of convex polytopes, we present a self-contained development of Delone sets in the Hilbert metric associated with a convex bodyKKinℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}. The Delone set is realized by a covering of a scaled-down copy ofKKusing a collection of ellipsoids derived from Macbeath regions. We further simplify the proof of the size bound by sidestepping the economic cap cover construction, utilized crucially in prior work, and present a new proof based on the elegant comparison theorem of Bishop exploiting the relationship between different metrics. We proceed to demonstrate the application of the Delone set to the design of an efficient data structure for approximate polytope membership testing, with optimal query time and storage complexity. Queries are answered by shooting rays from the origin and navigating through the ellipsoidal cover. Notably, the intrinsic viewpoint enables finger searching, where the query time can be upper bounded by the distance traveled in the Hilbert metric. For future work, we anticipate further developments of geometric algorithms in the Hilbert metric and further applications of the Delone set construction.
