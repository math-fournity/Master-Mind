## Abstract

For a non-decreasing sequence $S=(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_r)$ of positive integers, an $S$-packing coloring of a graph $G$ is a partition of $V(G)$ into sets $A_1,\ldots,A_r$ such that any two distinct vertices in $A_i$ are at distance greater than $a_i$, for every $i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$. Gastineau and Togni [\emph{Discrete Math.} 339 (2016), 2461--2470] asked whether every subcubic graph, except the Petersen graph, is $(1,1,2,3)$-packing colorable. In this paper, we prove that every claw-free subcubic graph is $(1,1,2,3)$-packing colorable. Moreover, we show that every connected claw-free subcubic graph, except a single graph $\mathcal{H}$, is $(1,1,3,3,3)$-packing colorable, thereby confirming a conjecture of the first two authors. Both results are best possible. Our proofs rely on a structural framework based on the skeleton and core graphs of a claw-free subcubic graph, together with a Hall-type matching argument that reduces the construction of suitable $3$-packings to a matching problem in an auxiliary bipartite graph.

## 1Introduction

Packing coloring is a distance-constrained variant of graph coloring. Given a non-decreasing sequenceS=(a1,a2,…,ar)S=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})of positive integers, anSS-packing coloring of a graphGGis a partition ofV​(G)V(G)into setsA1,…,ArA_{1},\ldots,A_{r}such that any two distinct vertices inAiA_{i}are at distance greater thanaia_{i}, for everyi∈{1,…,r}i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}. WhenS=(1,2,…,r)S=(1,2,\ldots,r), this notion coincides with the classical packing coloring introduced by Goddardet al.[13], and the minimum value ofrrfor which such a coloring exists is called the packing chromatic number ofGG. Since its introduction, packing coloring and its generalization toSS-packing coloring have received considerable attention, especially for graphs of bounded maximum degree and, in particular, for subcubic graphs; see, for instance,[1,2,3,5,6,7,9,10,12,14,15,16,17,18,19,21,22,23,24,27,28,30]and the survey by Brešaret al.[4].

Among the numerous questions concerning packing colorings of subcubic graphs, one of the most influential was posed by Gastineau and Togni[12], who asked whether every subcubic graph, except the Petersen graph, admits a(1,1,2,3)(1,1,2,3)-packing coloring. This problem has stimulated a series of works devoted to improving the known packing colorings of subcubic graphs. In particular, Liu, Zhang, and Zhang[18]proved that every subcubic graph is(1,1,2,2,3)(1,1,2,2,3)-packing colorable, providing the first general result using only five colors. Later, El Zein and Mortada[9]showed that every non-regular subcubic graph is(1,1,2,2)(1,1,2,2)-packing colorable and, more generally, that every subcubic graph admits a(1,1,2,2,k)(1,1,2,2,k)-packing coloring for every integerk≥3k\geq 3, thereby extending and strengthening several previously known results. Most recently, Hou, Liu, and Wang[29]established that every connected subcubic graph, except the Petersen graph, is(1,1,2,2)(1,1,2,2)-packing colorable, completely settling the corresponding(1,1,2,2)(1,1,2,2)-packing coloring problem. Nevertheless, the original conjecture of Gastineau and Togni for the stronger sequence(1,1,2,3)(1,1,2,3)remains open. This naturally raises the question of whether the conjecture holds for important subclasses of subcubic graphs.

The present paper is motivated by this question. We investigate claw-free subcubic graphs, an important subclasse of subcubic graphs, and prove that every claw-free subcubic graph admits a(1,1,2,3)(1,1,2,3)-packing coloring. Thus, our result confirms the original conjectured behavior for this natural graph class. We also prove a stronger structural result by showing that every claw-free subcubic graph, with the exception of a single graphℋ\mathcal{H}(see Figure1), admits a(1,1,3,3,3)(1,1,3,3,3)-packing coloring. This confirms the conjecture proposed by the first two authors[11]. Both results are best possible, showing that the obtained packing colorings cannot, in general, be further improved.

Our proofs are based on a structural and combinatorial framework that reduces the original distance-coloring problem to a sequence of simpler selection problems. Starting from a claw-free subcubic graphGG, we first construct its skeletonSGS_{G}by suppressing the paths whose internal vertices do not belong to triangles. This operation removes structurally inessential vertices while preserving the distance relations needed for the packing conditions. The skeleton is naturally decomposed into triangle and diamond blocks. By contracting these blocks, we obtain the core graphCGC_{G}, which records how the local triangle structures ofGGinteract at a global level. In this way, the skeleton captures the relevant local distances, whereas the core captures the global organization of the graph.

The main advantage of this framework is that it transforms the construction of suitable33-packings into a matching problem. Given an independent setIIof the core graph, we define an auxiliary graphHIH_{I}on the vertices of the corresponding blocks of the skeleton. Two vertices are adjacent inHIH_{I}precisely when choosing them simultaneously would violate the33-packing condition. We prove that every component ofHIH_{I}has order at most three. We then construct a bipartite graphBIB_{I}whose left part represents the blocks indexed byII, and whose right part represents the components ofHIH_{I}. Selecting one suitable vertex from each block is therefore equivalent to finding a matching that saturates the left part ofBIB_{I}. Hall’s theorem guarantees such a matching and consequently produces a33-packing meeting every required block. Thus, the skeleton–core decomposition, together with the auxiliary conflict graph and the Hall-type matching argument, provides a systematic method for converting distance constraints into a tractable combinatorial selection problem.
Beyond the results established in this paper, we believe that the proposed skeleton–core framework provides a versatile approach for studying packing colorings and related distance-constrained coloring problems in claw-free graphs and other graph classes.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the main tools used in the proofs and introduce the skeleton and core graphs associated with a claw-free subcubic graph. In Section 3, we define the auxiliary graphs associated with an independent set of the core graph and use Hall’s theorem to construct a33-packing meeting every corresponding block of the skeleton. Section 4 is devoted to the proof that every claw-free subcubic graph is(1,1,2,3)(1,1,2,3)-packing colorable. In Section 5, we adapt the skeleton–core and matching framework to prove that every connected claw-free subcubic graph, exceptℋ\mathcal{H}, is(1,1,3,3,3)(1,1,3,3,3)-packing colorable. Finally, in Section 6, we discuss the sharpness of both results and propose a related open problem. ## 6Conclusion and open problems

We proved that every claw-free subcubic graph is(1,1,2,3)(1,1,2,3)-packing colorable. The result is sharp in the sense that there are infinitely many claw-free subcubic graphs that are neither(1,1,3,3)(1,1,3,3)- nor(1,2,2,3)(1,2,2,3)-packing colorable.

## Proposition 2.

The graph𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}(see Figure3) is neither(1,1,3,3)(1,1,3,3)- nor(1,2,2,3)(1,2,2,3)-packing colorable. Moreover, there are infinitely many connected claw-free subcubic graphs that contain𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}as a subgraph.

## Proof.

The graph𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}contains three vertex-disjoint triangles. Then, in any(1,1,3,3)(1,1,3,3)-packing coloring of𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}, there are at least three vertices colored by a color33. This is impossible since the diameter of𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}is33.

Suppose that𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}has a(1,2,2,3)(1,2,2,3)-packing coloring. Since the diameter of𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1}is33, then at most one vertex can be colored by33. If three of the vertices of the cycle of order66are colored by11, then no more vertices are colored by11. Otherwise, at most two vertices of the cycle of order66are colored by11, then at most five of the remaining vertices can be colored by the two colors22and the color11, a contradiction.

Is every connected claw-free subcubic graph, distinct fromℋ\mathcal{H},(1,1,3,3,4)(1,1,3,3,4)-packing colorable?

## Problem 2.

[12]Is every subcubic graph, except the Petersen graph,(1,1,2,3)(1,1,2,3)-packing colorable?

In another direction, Gastineau and Togni[12]asked whether every subcubic graph other than the Petersen graph is(1,2,2,2,2,2)(1,2,2,2,2,2)-packing colorable.
