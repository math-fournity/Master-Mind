## Abstract

How many knot types can be built from a fixed budget of straight sticks? We prove that the answer has factorial-scale growth, settling its order for the first time. No previously published general upper bound improves on the exponential-in-the-square estimate obtained from crossing-number enumeration; we replace it with a factorial-scale upper bound, which is optimal at the level of growth order. The proof turns polygonal self-intersection into a sparse real-algebraic chamber problem in only linearly many dimensions, while a complementary braid construction supplies factorially many distinct knots. The result creates a direct bridge between knot topology, real algebraic geometry, fewnomial structure, and permutation combinatorics.

## Abstract

How many knot types can be built from a fixed budget of straight sticks? We
prove that the answer has factorial-scale growth, settling its order for the
first time. No previously published general upper bound improves on the
exponential-in-the-square estimate obtained from crossing-number enumeration;
we replace it with a factorial-scale upper bound, which is optimal at the level
of growth order. The proof turns polygonal self-intersection into a sparse
real-algebraic chamber problem in only linearly many dimensions, while a
complementary braid construction supplies factorially many distinct knots.
The result creates a direct bridge between knot topology, real algebraic
geometry, fewnomial structure, and permutation combinatorics.

## 1Introduction

Letp1,…,pN∈ℝ3p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}be labeled vertices joined cyclically by straight
segments. The space of embeddedNN-gons is the complement of a
self-intersection discriminant. Its connected components are sometimes called
geometric knot types: two polygons in the same component are connected by an
isotopy throughNN-gons. Calvo computed this space forN=6,7N=6,7, showing in
particular that geometric equivalence is finer than ordinary topological knot
type[8]. The exact number of components is unknown in general.

The semialgebraic starting point is also already present in Calvo’s work:
for every pair of non–incident edges, the possible-intersection locus is
described there as a codimension-one cubic semialgebraic piece, with additional
inequalities selecting intersections of the segments rather than merely their
supporting lines. Thus neither the polygon-space discriminant nor its
semialgebraicity is claimed as new here. Our upper bound is a quantitative
extraction from that model: we divide out affine freedom and retain the
binomial dependence on the number of walls in a refined sign-condition
component estimate.

The purpose of this note is to isolate what can be proved by elementary
semialgebraic methods. The basic facts are:
- •

the discriminant is semialgebraic and has explicit low-degree chart
equations;
- •

knot type is constant on each chamber of the complement;
- •

every knot type with stick number at mostNNoccurs in at least one
chamber of positive measure; and
- •

determinant-related sign-condition bounds, with their binomial
factor retained after affine normalization, giveCN≤2​(A​N)3​N−12=N3​N+o​(N);C_{N}\leq 2(AN)^{3N-12}=N^{3N+o(N)};
- •

combined with the factorial lower bound implicit in earlier
arc-index work, the chamber estimate identifies the logarithmic growth

- •

The determinant form of the crossing walls is the reason that one expects a
substantially structured chamber-count problem rather than an arbitrary
arrangement of high-degree hypersurfaces.

A bound of some kind was already implicit: a generic projection of anNN-stick polygon has at mostN​(N−3)/2N(N-3)/2crossings, and classical
exponential enumeration by crossing number gives|𝒦N|≤exp⁡(O​(N2))|\mathcal{K}_{N}|\leq\exp(O(N^{2}))[12]. Our contribution is the

## Proposition 1(Chambers carry one knot type).

The ambient isotopy class of the cyclic polygon is constant on each connected
component ofXN∖ΔNX_{N}\setminus\Delta_{N}.

## Proof.

Connected semialgebraic sets are semialgebraically path-connected, so two
points of a chamber can be joined by a path inXN∖ΔNX_{N}\setminus\Delta_{N}. Such a path is a one-parameter family of embedded
polygonal curves with fixed cyclic combinatorics. For a sufficiently fine
subdivision of the parameter interval, consecutive polygons are close enough
that the straight-line interpolation of corresponding vertices remains
embedded. This gives an isotopy through embedded polygonal curves, and the
ambient isotopy extension theorem[10]gives ambient isotopy of
the endpoints.

## Remark 3(Upper bounds versus lower bounds).

The chamber-to-knot-type map is surjective onto𝒦N\mathcal{K}_{N}, but it is not
injective. Many chambers can carry the same isotopy class; this already occurs

## 9Fewnomial and determinant directions

The crossing equations have two special features. First, each wall is a
constant-support polynomial: the affine four-point determinantdet(pb−pa,pd−pc,pc−pa)\det(p_{b}-p_{a},p_{d}-p_{c},p_{c}-p_{a})has at most2424monomial terms. Second,
the family of all such walls is highly correlated: these are determinant walls
coming from one point configuration, not unrelated sparse cubics. This places
the problem between fewnomial real algebraic geometry and the combinatorics of
order types.

One possible route is determinant-combinatorial. The signs of all affine
four-point determinants are the chirotope data of the labeled configuration inℝ3\mathbb{R}^{3}. Classical work of Goodman–Pollack givesexp⁡(O​(N​log⁡N))\exp(O(N\log N))-type bounds for fixed-dimensional point configurations[13]. Alon’s refinement gives the fixed-dimensional
asymptotic order for configurations: for fixeddd, the number of labeled
order types ofNNpoints inℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}has the formNd2​N+o​(N)N^{d^{2}N+o(N)}

[3]. The same work givesNΘ​(N)N^{\Theta(N)}upper and lower
bounds for fixed-rank real matroids. Thus affine order types inℝ3\mathbb{R}^{3}live
at scaleN9​N+o​(N)N^{9N+o(N)}, while rank-four vector matroid data live atNΘ​(N)N^{\Theta(N)}. Our crossing discriminant uses only a structured subset of
the four-point determinant data, together with interval inequalities in
projection charts. A proof organized through order types may therefore give a
sharper version of Theorem2, and it would explain directly why
the relevant growth is of the formNO​(N)N^{O(N)}.

Gros–Ramirez Alfonsin’s strong geometry is the closest known reconstruction
result to this viewpoint[14]. It augments the ordinary affine
chirotope by a wedge chirotope encoding relative positions of spanned
hyperplanes, and proves that isomorphic strong geometries yield isotopic
generic polygonal knots. This is complementary to our argument: their data
classify isotopy type but are not accompanied by an asymptotic enumeration,
whereas our smaller wall family is used to count connected realization
chambers without claiming that its sign vector alone classifies a knot.

There is an important distinction between counting determinant sign data and
counting chambers. Order-type and oriented-matroid bounds count possible sign
patterns of minors. The chamber count asks for connected components of
realization sets for such sign patterns, after intersecting with the stick-knot
discriminant complement. Realization spaces of oriented matroids can be
topologically complicated by universality phenomena[20,23];
hence matroid enumeration alone does not automatically give the chamber count.
It does, however, give a natural intermediate invariant between the raw
semialgebraic arrangement and the knot type map.

A second route is genuinely fewnomial. Existing fewnomial theorems control
positive solutions of sparse systems and the topology of sparse hypersurfaces[16,6,5,7]. The chamber problem here asks for connected components of
sign conditions forO​(N2)O(N^{2})sparse determinant walls on the Stiefel manifold.
The useful theorem would keep the dependence on the common sparse support and
the determinant incidence structure, rather than replacing the equations by an
arbitrary family of bounded-degree polynomials.

## Question 1(Sparse determinant arrangement problem).

Can one prove a sign-condition component bound for the sparse determinant-wall
arrangement onSt3⁡(H)\operatorname{St}_{3}(H)that improves the exponent or leading constant in
theNO​(N)N^{O(N)}bound of Theorem2?

There is also a lower-bound version of the same problem. Fixing a regular
planar shadow, the over/under data are signs of height differences at its
crossings, hence are constrained by a finite system of linear inequalities in
the vertex heights. This gives a concrete feasibility problem for crossing
assignments. If one can construct shadows for which many feasible assignments
have distinct knot invariants, then the fewnomial/determinant chamber structure
would also become a knot production mechanism, not only an upper-bound
mechanism.
