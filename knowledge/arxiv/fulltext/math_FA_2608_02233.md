## Abstract

Let $X$ be a normed linear space, $Y$ a Banach space, $G\subset X$ an open set and $f:G \to Y$ a mapping. We show that the Fréchet derivative $f'$ of $f$ is of the first Baire class on the (possibly empty) set $D\subset G$ where it is defined.

## Theorem 3.1.

Suppose thatXXis a normed linear space,YYis a Banach space,G⊂XG\subset Xis an

For the proof we substantially use the notion of aσ\sigma-discrete family and its properties.

Recall that a
family of sets in a topological space is said to bediscreteif each
point of the space has a neighbourhood that meets at most one set of the family.
A family of sets is said to beσ\sigma-discreteif it is a union of countably
many subfamilies, each of which is discrete.

We use the following well-known facts.

- (i)

By[H], a family of sets is called abase for a functionfrom one topological space into
another if the preimage of any open set is a union of sets from the family.
A function is said to beσ\sigma-discrete if it has aσ\sigma-discrete base.

We need the following fact.

## Fact 3.3.

LetDDbe a metric space,ZZa Banach space and letφ:D→Z\varphi:D\to Zbe a function. Ifφ\varphiis of the first Borel class and, moreover, has aσ\sigma-discrete base, thenφ\varphiis also of the first Baire class.

This fact follows immediately from[F, Theorem 2]but also from[Rog, Theorem 1, Theorem 2 (b)]. Further relevant information
can be found in[V]and[Rog].

Another important ingredient of our proof is the following well-known fact.

## Fact 3.4.

LetDDbe a metric space,ZZa Banach spaces andfn:D→Zf_{n}:D\to Zfunctions of the first Baire class.
If the sequence{fn}\{f_{n}\}converges uniformly to a functionf:D→Zf:D\to Z, thenffis of the first Baire class
as well.

An essentially classical proof of this fact is sketched in[S], p. 984. It follows also from a more general[Rol, Lemma 1].
