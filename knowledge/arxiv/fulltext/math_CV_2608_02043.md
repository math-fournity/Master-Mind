## Abstract

We give an alternative proof of the negative answer to Erdős Problem 973. Specifically, for every $λ>0$ there exists an integer $N=N(λ)$ such that, for every integer $n\geq N$ and every $z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb{C}$ with $|z_j|\geq1$, \[ \max_{2\leq k\leq n+1}\left|\sum_{j=1}^n z_j^k\right|>\exp(-λn), \] even without the normalization $z_1=1$. The same conclusion was previously obtained by Luo, Yang, and Zhu. Our contribution is an alternative proof based on a stable-residual principle. Let $r_n$ be the infimum of \[ \frac{\|P'-P'(0)P\|_{H^2}}{\|P\|_{H^2}} \] over degree-$n$ polynomials $P$ with $P(0)=1$ and all zeros in the closed unit disk. We prove that there exist absolute constants $D>0$ and $N_0$ such that \[ r_n\geq\exp\left(-D n^{2/3}(\log(\mathrm{e}n))^{2/3}\right) \] for every $n\geq N_0$, and hence $r_n^{1/n}\to1$. An exact projected Newton identity transfers this result to exterior power sums. The residual estimate follows by normalizing at a boundary maximum, integrating a first-order ODE to separate the zeros, and excluding the resulting moment profile with a finite polynomial test.

## 8.4.Shared skeleton and exact priority statement

The two arguments are not unrelated. Both encode the exterior points
as zeros in𝔻¯\overline{\mathbb{D}}, and both use logarithmic derivatives to
extract information about those zeros. They diverge at the final
obstruction. Luo–Yang–Zhu use exterior overconvergence, a
normal-family limit, and a Cauchy transform. The present proof uses
maximum-point ODE separation and a finite polynomial test, without a
limiting measure or analytic continuation.
