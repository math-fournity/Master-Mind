# Trace maps on chiral Clifford algebras for the rank two fermionic vertex operator superalgebra

**arXiv ID**: 2608.02065v1
**Authors**: A. Zuevsky
**Published**: 2026-08-03
**Categories**: math.FA
**HTML URL**: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02065v1

## Abstract

For a holomorphic vector bundle $F$ of rank $r$ on a smooth Riemann surface $X$ we construct a trace map on the chiral homology of the chiral Clifford algebra $\CE$ attached to the purely odd bundle $E=Π(F\oplus F^\vee\otimesω_X)$. It is the chiral-algebraic realization of the rank two fermionic vertex operator superalgebra. The free-fermion (bc-type) conformal field theory built from a dual pair of odd fields $ β_i\in F$, $γ^j\in F^\vee\otimesω_X$. We give a complete construction of this vertex operator superalgebra, its associated vertex superalgebra bundle, and the isomorphism between the latter's chiral algebra and the chiral envelope $\CE$. Using the Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism together with Feynman diagrams we prove that the resulting trace map \[ \Trch : \bigl(\widetilde\sC^{\ch}(X,\CE)_{\sQ},\, \dch_{\CE}\bigr)\longrightarrow (\OBV,-\DBV) \] is a chain map satisfying a generalized quantum master equation and is a quasi-isomorphism, generalizing to the odd/Clifford setting the trace map on chiral Weyl algebras constructed by Gui for symplectic bosons. We establish existence, homotopy uniqueness, and functoriality (including explicit metric-independence up to chain homotopy) of the trace map, prove cyclicity of the relevant supertrace,   and verify nilpotency of $\DBV$, the graded Leibniz rule, $d^2=0$ for every differential introduced. As an application we compute the trace map on a modified affine current and on a modified energy-momentum tensor, recovering,   purely algebraically from the chiral chain complex, Fay's classical formulas for the variation of the fermionic (Ray-Singer) analytic torsion along the moduli of the bundle $F$ and along the moduli of the curve $X$.

## 1.Introduction

## 1.1.Background

Two-dimensional chiral conformal field theory admits a rigorous algebraic model through the chiral algebras of Beilinson and Drinfeld[4]. Costello[8,9]gave a geometric construction of the Witten genus[26]by applying Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) quantization[3]to theβ​γ\beta\gamma-system on an elliptic curve, and Gui-Li[17]recast this construction, together with its correlation functions, as a trace map on the chiral homology of the chiral Weyl algebra of the elliptic curve, resulting in the Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra of zero modes. Gui[16]extended this trace map from the elliptic curve to an arbitrary smooth Riemann surfaceXXand to an arbitrary holomorphic symplectic vector bundleEE, obtainingTr𝒜E:(𝒞~ch​(X,𝒜E)𝒬,d𝒜Ech)⟶(OBV,−ΔBV),\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}_{E}}:\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{ch}}(X,\mathcal{A}_{E})_{\mathcal{Q}},d^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\mathcal{A}_{E}}\bigr)\longrightarrow(O_{\mathrm{BV}},-\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}),

a chain-level realization of a deformed Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg (HKR) quasi-isomorphism, and used it to recover Fay’s formula[13]for the variation of analytic torsion along the moduli ofEEand along the moduli ofXX, purely from the chiral homology of𝒜E\mathcal{A}_{E}.

The chiral Weyl algebra𝒜E\mathcal{A}_{E}quantizes the symplectic bosons attached toEE, i.e., the free bosonic first-order system with Lagrangian∫X⟨ϕ,∂¯​ϕ⟩\int_{X}\langle\phi,\bar{\partial}\phi\rangle,ϕ∈Ω0,∙​(X,E)\phi\in\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,E). Its odd-parity counterpart - the free fermionic first-order system, whose local observables form a chiral Clifford algebra - governs theb​cbc-ghosts of two-dimensional gravity, the worldsheet fermions of the superstring, and, more generally, every chiral free-fermion vertex operator superalgebra (VOSA).
The initial idea of this paper was to give an account of the fermionic trace map, generalizing Gui’s construction from the chiral Weyl algebra to the chiral Clifford algebra𝒞E\mathcal{C}_{E}attached to a purely odd holomorphic bundleEEwith a symmetric𝒪X\mathcal{O}_{X}-pairingE⊗𝒪XE→ωXE\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}E\to\omega_{X}, and applying it to a single example (a modified affine current).
One would need to work directly with the chiral Clifford algebra, and consider
the vertex operator superalgebra it quantizes,
check the existence, uniqueness, and functoriality of the trace map, and verify the required algebraic identities (nilpotency of the BV operator, the chiral Wick theorem, the intertwining of differentials).
The present paper gives a complete, self-contained treatment. We work throughout with the bundleE:=Π​(F⊕F∨⊗𝒪XωX),E:=\Pi\bigl(F\oplus F^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{X}\bigr),(1.1)

forFFa holomorphic vector bundle of rankrronXX, equipped with its tautological symmetric pairing (Definition3.2). We call the resulting chiral-algebraic structure the rank two fermionic vertex operator superalgebra: its generating fields organize into two dual families,βi∈F\beta_{i}\in Fandγj∈F∨⊗ωX\gamma^{j}\in F^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X}(ii,j=1j=1,…\dots,rr), exactly as thebb- andcc-fields of ab​cbc-system. This is to be contrasted with the rank one (self-dual) free-fermion theories attached to a spin structureωX1/2\omega_{X}^{1/2}, in which a single family of fields is self-paired via a symmetric form onωX1/2⊕ωX1/2\omega_{X}^{1/2}\oplus\omega_{X}^{1/2}-type bundle. We comment on this dichotomy, which parallels the distinction in Gui’s paper[16]between the paired familiesEαE_{\alpha},α<1/2\alpha<1/2, and the self-dual, symplectically paired familyE1/2E_{1/2}occurring in the bosonic case, in Remark3.3. The rank two case is both the case occurring in every standard application (ghost systems, and the matter fermions of the NSR superstring away from the self-dual locus) and the case in which the full machinery of Lie∗-algebras, chiral envelopes and vertex algebra bundles of[4,11]applies without extra structure (no polarization or reality condition on a symplectic form is required, unlike the bosonic self-dual case).

## 1.3.Relations to the existing literature and new results

Here we organize the results above against five items of literature.

Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras and factorization algebras.The BV formalism[3]and its modern homotopical realization in the factorization algebras of Costello-Gwilliam[7]provide the language for treating renormalization and quantum master equations. What is new here relative to that literature is not the formalism itself, which we use as given, but its correct specialization to a purely odd classical phase space: the shifted symplectic pairing onℍ​(X,E)\mathbb{H}(X,E)is(−1)(-1)-shifted and symmetric on the underlying (unshifted) bundle rather than antisymmetric, and the resulting BV operatorΔBV\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}is built from anticommuting derivations. We verify (Proposition5.5) that this still produces
a well defined BV superalgebra, since the sign discrepancies between the symplectic and orthogonal cases are exactly compensated by fermion statistics.
This is a fact that is standard in the physics literature on BV quantization of fermionic gauge theories but that we have not found spelled out in the chiral-algebraic setting.

Chiral and vertex algebras.Beilinson-Drinfeld[4]and Frenkel-Ben-Zvi[11]treat chiral/vertex algebras built from arbitrary (super) vector bundles, and in particular the fermionic (b​cbc) vertex algebra bundle is implicit in[11, §5.2]as one member of a general family. What is new here is the explicit chiral-envelope versus vertex-bundle comparison isomorphism𝒰​(L♭)≃𝒱Fr\mathcal{U}(L^{\flat})\simeq\mathscr{V}_{F}^{\,r}carried out in full for the Clifford (odd, Pfaffian) case (AppendicesB-C), parallel to but independent of Gui’s bosonic comparison[16, §3.3, App. 7.1-7.2].
This comparison is what identifies𝒞E\mathcal{C}_{E}with a vertex operator superalgebra.

Clifford algebras.The finite-dimensional theory of Clifford algebras associated with a quadratic or symmetric bilinear form is classical (see, e.g.,[19]); our chiral Clifford algebra𝒞E\mathcal{C}_{E}is its sheaf-theoretic, chiral (OPE) analogue, in which the finite-dimensional Clifford relationei​ej+ej​ei=2​⟨ei,ej⟩e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=2\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangleis replaced by the operator product expansion regulated by the Szegő kernel. We verify explicitly (Lemma3.12) that Clifford multiplication is compatible with theℤ/2​ℤ\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}grading inherited fromEE, as requested for any such construction.

Analytic torsion.Ray-Singer analytic torsion[21]and the Quillen metric[20]on the determinant line bundle of∂¯F\bar{\partial}_{F}are classical invariants; Fay[13]and, in the family setting, Bismut-Freed[5]compute their variation along moduli using Szegő-kernel and heat-kernel methods, respectively. Gui[16]recovered Fay’s bosonic (symplectic boson) formula algebraically via the trace map on the chiral Weyl algebra. What is new here is the fermionic counterpart: we recover Fay’s fermionic variation formulas (both the bundle-moduli and the curve-moduli versions) from the chiral homology of𝒞E\mathcal{C}_{E}, and we make explicit, in Corollary8.10, the sign relating the two theories, which reflects the reciprocal (rather than equal) relation between the partition functionsZferm=det∂¯FZ_{\text{ferm}}=\det\bar{\partial}_{F}andZsympl. bos.=det(∂¯E)−1Z_{\text{sympl.\ bos.}}=\det(\bar{\partial}_{E})^{-1}.

Fermionic quantization in physics.b​cbc-ghost systems and the free-fermion sector of the NSR superstring are standard tools of string perturbation theory[12,10]; bosonization of chiral fermions on higher-genus Riemann surfaces is treated in[1]. Our trace map furnishes a mathematically rigorous, coordinate-free construction of the correlation functions of these theories on an arbitrary Riemann surface, at the chain level (before passing to cohomology/correlation functions), which is not available in the physics literature in this form.

Beyond these five points, this paper is supplying: a full notational and analytic-category preamble (§2); an explicit statement of the hypotheses under which the fermionic Szegő kernel exists and of its dependence on the auxiliary Hermitian metric (§6); an explicit sign-tracked verification of every algebraic identity claimed (the BV axioms,d2=0d^{2}=0for each differential introduced, the graded Leibniz rule, the cyclicity of the trace pairing - Lemma7.15); a discussion of existence, homotopical uniqueness and functoriality of the trace map (§7.3); a second example (the energy-momentum tensor/Beltrami insertion, §8.3) parallel to Gui’s[16, §6.2].

For ease of reference, we collect the principal new contributions of this paper in one place:
- (C1)

the rank two fermionic vertex operator superalgebraVFV_{F}and its associated bundle𝒱F\mathscr{V}_{F}, and the proof that𝒞E≃𝒱Fr\mathcal{C}_{E}\simeq\mathscr{V}_{F}^{r}(Theorem4.3, AppendicesB-C);

the corrected fermionic BV superalgebra(OBV,ΔBV)(O_{\mathrm{BV}},\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}), withΔBV2=0\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}^{2}=0and the graded Leibniz rule verified in full (Proposition5.5), including the identification of the field/antifield parity flip that a naive formula misses (Lemma5.1);

## Remark 3.3.

Let us compare rank one versus rank two.

## 9.1.The role of the vertex superalgebra bundle model

Beyond situating𝒞E\mathcal{C}_{E}within the standard family of chiral free-field theories, Theorem4.3plays an important role in the present paper.
