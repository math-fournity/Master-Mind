## Abstract

We identify properties of a Banach space $\mathcal{B}$ of analytic functions on the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ in the complex plane ensuring that a multiplication operator $M_ψ: \mathcal{B} \to\mathcal{B}$ is Fredholm if and only if its symbol $ψ$ is bounded away from $0$ near $\partial \mathbb{D}$. The properties we identify are shared by a wide variety of much-studied spaces, including the Hardy spaces $H^p(\mathbb{D})$, weighted Bergman spaces $A^p_ω(\mathbb{D})$, Hardy-Sobolev spaces $H^2_β(\mathbb{D})$, the spaces $S_j^p(\mathbb{D})$ of functions having $j$-th derivative in $H^p(\mathbb{D})$, and the disk algebra $A$. Thus, as a corollary, our work characterizes Fredholm multiplication operators on these spaces. In addition, we describe the closed, finite-codimensional subspaces of $\mathcal{B}$ that are invariant under $M_z: \mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{B}$ in terms of the zeros that the functions in such subspaces have in common. We discuss connections between these subspaces and the problem of characterizing the Fredholm multiplication operators on $\mathcal{B}$, and we prove that $M_z$ restricted to such a subspace is always cyclic, with a polynomial cyclic vector having degree equal to the codimension of the subspace.

## 5.Questions

We have shown that properties (i)–(vii) of Definition2.2are sufficient to ensure that a multiplication operator onℬ\mathcal{B}is Fredholm if and only if its symbol is bounded away from0near∂𝔻\partial\mathbb{D}(whereℬ\mathcal{B}is a Banach space of analytic functions on𝔻\mathbb{D}satisfying (i)–(vii)). To what extent are the properties of Definition2.2necessary for this characterization to be valid? Sheldon Axler has pointed out (private communication) that certain closed,MzM_{z}-invariant subspaces of Bergman spaces furnish examples relevant to this question. For instance, Theorem 6.1 of[17]shows that there exists a closed,MzM_{z}-invariant subspaceℳ\mathcal{M}ofAp​(𝔻)A^{p}(\mathbb{D})(p≥1p\geq 1), such thatdim(ℳ/(z​ℳ))=∞\dim(\mathcal{M}/(z\mathcal{M}))=\infty. Thus,Mz:ℳ→ℳM_{z}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}is not Fredholm onℳ\mathcal{M}yetψ​(z)=z\psi(z)=zis bounded away from0near∂𝔻\partial\mathbb{D}. It is not difficult to check thatℳ\mathcal{M}satisfies properties (ii), (iii), (v), and (vi) of Definition2.2(with (vi) holding vacuously); also, it is possible to show that (vii) holds:ℳ\mathcal{M}has boundary-extension type0in the sense thatMz−ζ:ℳ→ℳM_{z-\zeta}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}has dense range for eachζ∈∂𝔻\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}. Suppose thatBBis a Banach space of analytic functions on𝔻\mathbb{D}satisfying only (ii)–(v) of Definition2.2and thatψ\psiis a multiplier ofBB; by the proof of Theorem3.3(and the remark after it), the following implication is valid:(5.1)ψ​bounded away from​0​near​∂𝔻⟹Mψ:B→B​is Fredholm.\psi\ \text{bounded away from}\ 0\ \text{near}\ \partial\mathbb{D}\implies M_{\psi}:B\to B\ \text{is Fredholm.}

Thus, the exampleMz:ℳ→ℳM_{z}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}discussed above shows that the inclusion of (iv) in the list (ii)–(v) of properties ofBBis necessary for the implication (5.1) to hold.

To what extent can our work be generalized to multiplication operators on Banach spaces of analytic functions on planar domainsΩ\Omega? In particular, if (a) we replace𝔻\mathbb{D}in Definition2.2by a bounded planar domainΩ\Omegasuch that no connected component of∂Ω\partial\Omegais equal to a point, (b) we modify the spectral-radius assumption forMzM_{z}(e.g.Mz−λ​IM_{z}-\lambda Iis invertible whenλ∈ℂ∖Ω−\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega^{-}), and (c) we replaceℬ\mathcal{B}byℬ​(Ω)\mathcal{B}(\Omega), then are Fredholm multiplication operators onℬ​(Ω)\mathcal{B}(\Omega)precisely those whose symbols are bounded away from0near∂Ω\partial\Omega? The preceding question is motivated by Corollary 6 of[2], which shows that for such domainsΩ\Omega, a multiplication operator on the Bergman-ppspaceAp​(Ω)A^{p}(\Omega),p≥1p\geq 1, is Fredholm if and only if its symbol is bounded away from0near∂𝔻\partial\mathbb{D}.
