## Abstract

We prove that a symplectic $T^{n-1}$ action on a closed connected $2n$-dimensional symplectic manifold is Hamiltonian if and only if its orbits are contractible. This generalizes a result of Lalonde--McDuff--Polterovich on four-manifolds and theorems of McDuff and Kim on existence of fixed points. When the orbits are isotropic, we prove a stronger variant of this result, which implies non-extendability of certain symplectic circle actions on 6-manifolds to symplectic $T^2$ actions. Moreover, we prove that a symplectic $T^{n-1}$ action with isotropic orbits always splits into a maximal Hamiltonian action and a locally-free action. We end by posing several open questions on the topology of symplectic torus actions.

## 1.Introduction

## 1.1.Hamiltonian versus non-Hamiltonian

Let a torusTTact symplectically and faithfully on a closed connected symplectic manifold(M2​n,ω)(M^{2n},\omega). When the action is Hamiltonian, many properties of theTT-manifold can be read from its momentum map, its fixed point setMTM^{T}, and its isotropy data. In particular, one can read the fundamental group, the Betti numbers, the volumes of the reduced spaces, and information on the Chern class. Hence, it is natural to seek conditions under which a symplectic torus action must be Hamiltonian.

For a symplectic circle action, the existence of fixed points implies that the action is Hamiltonian under various assumptions:
Kähler manifolds (Frankel[22]), weak Lefschetz property
(Ono[64]), monotone symplectic manifolds
(Lupton–Oprea[58]), semi-free actions with finite fixed setMTM^{T}(Tolman–Weitsman[73]), and
symplectic four-manifolds (McDuff[62, Proposition 2]).
However, this is not true in general: McDuff[62]constructed a symplectic
non-Hamiltonian circle action on a six-dimensional manifold whose
fixed-point set is a nonempty union of 2-tori.
In[61, p. 151],
McDuff and Salamon asked the following question, often referred to asMcDuff’s Conjecture:
“Does there exist a non-Hamiltonian symplectic circle action with finite
fixed setMTM^{T}on a closed, connected symplectic manifold?”. This question
was resolved by Tolman[74], who constructed a
non-Hamiltonian symplectic circle action on a six-dimensional manifold,
with exactly3232fixed points (see also Jang–Tolman[40]).

In this paper, we are interested in a related question:contractibilityof theTT-orbits.
This is closely related to the orbit mapsρx:T→M\rho_{x}\colon T\to M,
given bya↦a⋅xa\mapsto a\cdot xforx∈Mx\in M,
being null-homotopic. BecauseMMis connected, any two orbit maps are homotopic,
so if there exist fixed points,
then all the orbit maps are null-homotopic.
Hence, for a Hamiltonian torus action
on a closed connected symplectic manifold, the orbit maps are null-homotopic.
For Hamiltonian circle actions, a vast generalization, proved using Floer theory,
is that the evaluation at a pointp∈Mp\in Mof a loop of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms
is null-homotopic (Lalonde–McDuff–Polterovich[51, Section 1.2]).

For non-Hamiltonian symplectic torus actions, the situation
is almost the opposite, as we now describe.
The action ofℝ2/ℤ2{\mathbb{R}}^{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}on itself by translations is free,
and its orbit maps are not null-homotopic.
On the Kodaira-Thurston symplectic 4-manifold,
there exists a free circle action whose orbits are null-homologous,
but its orbit maps are not null-homotopic.
In fact, in dimension four, the orbit maps of symplectic non-Hamiltonian
circle actions can never be null-homotopic, by a work of Lalonde-McDuff-Polterovich[51]. This extends to dimensions higher than four under various additional assumptions:
weak Lefschetz property (Allday–Oprea[3, Proposition 3.4]),
aspherical symplectic manifolds (Lupton–Oprea[58]),
symplectically aspherical symplectic manifolds
(Oprea–Walsh[65, Cor. 4.10]),
and spherically monotone symplectic manifolds
(Atallah[4]).
In contrast, the 6-dimensional examples of McDuff[62], Tolman[74], and Jang-Tolman[40]are non-Hamiltonian circle actions with fixed points, hence with null-homotopic orbit maps. See also Kotschick[50]for examples of free symplectic (non-Hamiltonian) circle actions with contractible orbits in every even dimension≥6\geq 6(which resolved a question of McDuff–Salamon[61, p. 152]).

Taking products of the above 6-dimensional examples with spheres, we obtain symplectic non-HamiltonianTn−2T^{n-2}actions on(M2​n,ω)(M^{2n},\omega)with null-homotopic orbit maps for everynn. In contrast, it follows from works of Duistermaat-Pelayo[19]and Benoist[9]that a symplectic non-HamiltonianTnT^{n}action on(M2​n,ω)(M^{2n},\omega)cannot have null-homotopic orbit maps (see AppendixB). Hence, the only missing case is symplecticTn−1T^{n-1}actions, which we resolve in this paper, thereby extending Lalonde-McDuff-Polterovich’s result[51]from dimension four to any dimension:

WhendimM=4\dim M=4anddimT=1\dim T=1, this is precisely
Lalonde-McDuff-Polterovich’s theorem[51]. Their argument in dimension four

## 1.3.Free symplectic circle actions

Next, we discuss free symplectic circle actions with contractible orbits.
Let(M,ω)(M,\omega)be a closed connected symplectic manifold,
equipped with a free symplectic circle action.
Following McDuff[62], if the de Rham cohomology
class[ω][\omega]is integral, then the circle action is generated by
a circle-valued momentum mapμ:M→ℝ/ℤ\mu\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.
Currently, the only known examples of free symplectic circle actions with
contractible orbits are given by Kotschick[50]and Tolman[74], and their reduced spaces
with respect to a circle-valued momentum map are symplectomorphic
to K3 surfaces.
It would be interesting to find new examples with different reduced spaces; see8.3and8.4.

InSection7, using long exact sequences of homotopy
groups together with a result of
Fernández–Gray–Morgan[20], we give a topological
characterization of those symplectic manifolds that can appear
as reduced spaces of free symplectic circle actions with contractible orbits
(Theorem7.3). This characterization is useful for
understanding wherenotto look for examples of free symplectic
circle actions with contractible orbits (Corollary7.4).
In particular, we deduce that aspherical symplectic manifolds, and
symplectic manifolds with second Betti number equal to one, cannot appear as
reduced spaces of free symplectic circle actions with contractible orbits
(Example7.6). See8.4for a related question on the possible second Betti numbers of reduced spaces of free symplectic circle action with contractible orbits.

## 3.Contractible orbits

This section is about contractibility of orbits of a torus action.
Here is an overview of the main results that we prove.
For smooth torus actions (without a symplectic form),
“the orbit maps are null-homotopic” is equivalent to “some orbits
are contractible” (Lemma3.1),
but does not imply “all orbits are contractible”
(Example3.3).
For locally-free torus actions with vanishing real Chern class,
the orbits are non-contractible (Lemma3.4).
For a Hamiltonian torus action, all orbits are contractible
(Lemma3.5).
For a symplectic torus action, if some orbit is non-isotropic,
then all orbits are non-contractible
(Lemma3.7).

For a related discussion
on the contractibility of orbits of smooth and Hamiltonian torus actions,
also see[65, Remark 1.8 and the paragraph after Corollary 6.8].

Let a torusTTact (faithfully) on a (nonempty, connected) manifoldMM.
