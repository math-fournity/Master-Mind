# Exact Finite-$N$ eRS Surface-Defect Indices and Giant-Graviton Corrections in $\mathcal N=4$ SYM

**arXiv ID**: 2608.01349v1
**Authors**: Ji-Seong Chae
**Published**: 2026-08-02
**Categories**: hep-th

## Abstract

We study the finite-$N$ superconformal index of four-dimensional $\mathcal N=4$ $U(N)$ Yang--Mills theory with antisymmetric elliptic Ruijsenaars--Schneider (eRS) insertions. The eRS insertions turn the uninserted index, which is an unweighted spectral sum, into a family of protected observables weighted by eRS eigenvalues and therefore retain finite-$N$ information not determined by the ordinary index alone On the codimension-one locus $t=q$, $p=uv$, the eRS operators become conjugate to free shifts, allowing us to rewrite the self-glued index as a bilateral free-fermion determinant. This gives exact finite-$N$ formulas for all antisymmetric ranks and makes the $u\leftrightarrow v$ structure manifest. On the boundary $v=0$, we determine the complete stable tower of pure $u^{kN}$ defect-resolved corrections, while the bilateral two-charge formula captures the reflected $v^{kN}$ sectors and mixed $u$--$v$ contributions, including the first mixed giant-graviton sector. We also develop the expansion away from the solvable locus through second order. Finally, we reproduce the ordinary one-giant coefficient from a maximal-D3 determinant and identify the additional microscopic input required to complete the bulk derivation of the defect correction.

## 1Introduction

The superconformal index (SCI) is a protected trace over short representations in
radial quantization and is invariant under continuous changes of the coupling.
Its general four-dimensional construction was developed in
refs.[1,2]; for𝒩=4\mathcal{N}=4super
Yang–Mills (SYM) theory, the same observable can be evaluated at weak coupling by a unitary
matrix integral and at largeNNby the Kaluza–Klein (KK) spectrum of
five-dimensional anti–de Sitter space (A​d​S5AdS_{5}) timesS5S^{5}.

Finite-rank corrections to this index have a natural D3-brane interpretation.
The original giant graviton is a spherical brane carrying angular momentum on
the internal sphere[3], while holomorphic-surface giants
and their quantization organize broader Bogomol’nyi–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) sectors[4,5]. Early finite-NNcomparisons of the
index with wrapped D3-branes were developed in
refs.[6,7]. The systematic giant-graviton
expansion was then formulated directly at the level of the index[8]and related to free-fermion and Fredholm expansions of
unitary matrix models[9]. Its multi-giant structure,
non-uniqueness beyond the first wrapping, and relation between boundary and
brane expansions were clarified in
refs.[10,11,12,13].
Independent bulk calculations reproduce the leading half-BPS giant
coefficient from collective-coordinate localization and semiclassical D3
fluctuations[14,15]. Refined Schur-sector
brane integrals, exact worldvolume coefficients and simple-sum expansions have
also been studied in
refs.[16,17,18].

Surface defects provide a second, complementary refinement. Certain BPS surface defects arise from residues of the four-dimensional index, and their insertion is represented by Ruijsenaars–Schneider-type difference operators[19]. Representation-labelled defects and theirqq-deformed Yang–Mills interpretation were developed in
ref.[20]; the full elliptic algebra was constructed in
ref.[21], where the fully antisymmetric operators were
identified with the elliptic Ruijsenaars–Schneider (eRS) Hamiltonians.
Microscopic descriptions in terms of coupled two-dimensional𝒩=(2,2)\mathcal{N}=(2,2)theories and M2-branes ending on M5-branes were given in
refs.[22,23]. The standard intersecting-D3
realization instead produces anA​d​S3×S1AdS_{3}\times S^{1}probe and a local𝒩=(4,4)\mathcal{N}=(4,4)intersection sector[24,25,26]. Giant-graviton
expansions with a half-BPS surface defect, Schur line defects and general
Wilson-line insertions have been investigated in
refs.[27,28,29].

A third ingredient is exact solvability. The ordinary𝒩=4\mathcal{N}=4Schur
index admits an exact free-fermion representation[30], while a two-parameter deformation can be expressed
in terms of ordinary Macdonald polynomials and supports finite-NNgiant
expansions[31]. For the full𝒩=4\mathcal{N}=4index, Ren and
Huang rewrote the self-glued matrix integral as a spectral sum over elliptic
Macdonald functions, which are simultaneous eigenfunctions of the commuting
eRS Hamiltonians[32]. In that representation, finite rank is
implemented by restricting the allowed generalized partitions, and the
elliptic eigenfunctions and spectral data are constructed perturbatively in
the elliptic nome. This provides the spectral starting point used here, but
does not perform the finite-NNspectral sum in closed form, include an
antisymmetric defect insertion in the self-glued trace, or reorganize the
finite-length tail into giant-graviton sectors.

Kim, Nedelin and Razamat studied a complementary spectral problem[33]. They obtained eRS eigenfunctions and eigenvalues from two
different gauge-theory constructions: four-dimensional class-SSindices
with surface defects, and the Nekrasov–Shatashvili limit of five-dimensional𝒩=1∗\mathcal{N}=1^{*}ramified-instanton partition functions. After imposing the
B-model quantization condition on the Coulomb-branch parameters, they matched
the two constructions for the ground and first excited states of theA1A_{1}model and for the ground state of theA2A_{2}model. Their analysis concerns
the extraction and comparison of particular eRS energy levels, rather than
the exact finite-NNself-glued𝒩=4\mathcal{N}=4index with an inserted eRS
operator. A recent operator formulation further relates unitary matrix
models, quantized symmetric functions and spin-chain Hilbert spaces[34], providing a broader framework for finite-rank spectral
structures.

The determinantal methods used below have related antecedents in Schur and
Macdonald processes, where difference operators evaluate observables of
partition measures[35,36,37]. We also use the
elliptic Frobenius determinant and the bilateral Kronecker expansion[38,39]. These identities supply
the relevant mathematical tools, but their application to the physical
self-glued index with antisymmetric eRS insertions is the additional step
taken here.

## 4Giant-graviton interpretation and surface-defect dressing

The exact boundary formulas suggest a bulk interpretation in terms of maximal giant gravitons coupled to the brane realization of the eRS surface defect. ## 5.2Limitations and outlook

The boundary analysis determines the finite-NNdefect expectation value,
the stable coefficientsckc_{k}andhkh_{k}, and their marked Fredholm
organization exactly. These are exact statements about the protected
boundary observable. They do not, however, determine a unique microscopic
decomposition of every coefficient into giant-graviton, defect, and
open-string contributions. The unresolved issue is not the functional agreement. What
remains to be derived is why the complete brane system produces precisely
this charge assignment and why its localization selects the required JK
chamber.

The M5–M5′–M2 configuration discussed above, or its
D3–D3′–KK5 duality frame, provides a possible endpoint geometry
for the defect brane. It has not yet been shown that this construction
reproduces the complete no-adjoint𝒩=(2,2)\mathcal{N}=(2,2)defect GLSM.
In particular, the precise two-dimensional matter content, the mechanism
removing the additional adjoint degree of freedom, and the localization
contour have not been derived directly from the ten-dimensional brane
configuration. The KK5 frame should therefore be regarded as a candidate
completion rather than an established microscopic derivation ofc1c_{1}.

Fork≥2k\geq 2, the boundary coefficientsckc_{k}andhkh_{k}remain exact,
but their bulk interpretation is less direct. The marked Fredholm
description shows that a single defect insertion is distributed over the
fullkk-hole configuration. It is therefore not equivalent to attaching
an independent copy of the rank-one factorc1c_{1}to each ofkkgiant
gravitons. Several microscopic sectors may contribute to the same boundary
coefficient, including nonabelian fluctuations of coincident giants, strings
stretched between different giants, and strings connecting the giant stack
to the defect brane. A physical derivation of the higher tower consequently
requires the coupled nonabelian worldvolume theory rather than a product of
independent rank-one determinants.

The defect expectation admits the corresponding second-order expansion aroundt=qt=q,
including the variation of both the measure and the inserted difference
operator. Thus, the present work establishes a controlled finite-NNperturbation theory through𝒪​(ϵ2)\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}), together with the
associated eRS recursion and large-NNconsistency check. The remaining
limitation is not the absence of any information away fromt=qt=q, but the
absence of an all-orders resummation or a new exact determinant valid at
finitet/q−1t/q-1. Beyond second order, increasingly high connected
correlators of the solvable ensemble enter, and the simple free-fermion
description is no longer expected to remain closed without further
structure.

There is also an important distinction between the exact finite-NNanswer and the coefficientwise stable tower. The functionsFFandHHcapture the stable families multiplyinguk​Nu^{kN}, but the complete
finite-NNindex also contains pureqNq^{N}contributions and mixed sectors
whoseqq-degree grows withNN. These terms are not errors or discarded

interpretation.
