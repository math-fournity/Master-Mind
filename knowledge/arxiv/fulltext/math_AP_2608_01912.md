## Abstract

For solutions to the Minkowski problem of the even dual curvature measures $\widetilde{C}_q$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, $n\ge 2$, we prove the non-uniqueness for $q>n$ and $n\ge 2$, and the uniqueness for $0<q<n$ and $n=2$. These results are governed by our established logarithmic Brunn-Minkowski inequality for dual quermassintegrals $\widetilde V_q$.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions to the dual Minkowski problem were both solved forq<0q<0by Zhao[38], and up to dilation forq=0q=0by Aleksandrov[1]. For0<q≤n0<q\leq n, only the existence of solutions to the problem in the symmetric case has been completely solved: the case0<q≤10<q\leq 1was proved in[21]; the case of integerq=2,…,n−1q=2,\ldots,n-1was proved by Zhao[37]; the case1<q<n1<q<nwas solved in[21]with a stronger sufficient condition and later completely solved by Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang, Zhang and Zhao[7]under the condition of Böröczky, Henk and Pollehn[3]; and the caseq=nq=nwas solved by Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang[6].

The uniqueness problem in the symmetric case for positive indices remains largelyopen. Forq=nq=n, it is the even log-Minkowski problem. Stancu treated the discrete planar case[32,33], while Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang determined the full planar case[5]. In higher dimensions, Saroglou proved the unconditional case[30]; Böröczky and Kalantzopoulos proved the case of convex bodies withnnindependent hyperplane symmetries[4]. Kolesnikov and Milman proved local uniqueness near the Euclidean ball[24], whereas Chen, Huang, Li and Liu proved global fixed-reference uniqueness when the reference body isC+2,αC^{2,\alpha}_{+}and Hausdorff-close to the ball[10]. Further global fixed-reference uniqueness results under curvature-pinching hypotheses were obtained by Milman[28]and by Ivaki and Milman[22].

For0<q<n0<q<n, general uniqueness remains open. For constant density, Chen, Huang and Zhao[11]proved that the Euclidean ball is the unique smooth uniformly convex origin-symmetric solution. For even densitiesCαC^{\alpha}-close to11, Hu[18]proved existence and uniqueness for0<q≤n−10<q\leq n-1and2≤n≤42\leq n\leq 4, and forn−4≤q≤n−1n-4\leq q\leq n-1andn>4n>4; Böröczky, Chen, Liu and Saroglou[8]extended this to every0<q<n0<q<n.
