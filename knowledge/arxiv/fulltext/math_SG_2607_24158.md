## Abstract

We classify the Namikawa--Weyl groups associated to symplectic quotient singularities $V/G$ when $G$ is a symplectic reflection group.   Our classification shows that every irreducible Weyl group can be realized as a factor of the Namikawa--Weyl group of $V/G$ for a suitable $G$.

## 1.Introduction

LetVVbe a finite-dimensional complex vector space endowed with a symplectic form and letG⩽Sp⁡(V)G\leqslant\operatorname{Sp}(V)be a finite group. The linear quotientV/G≔Spec⁡ℂ​[V]GV/G\coloneqq\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}is a symplectic quotient singularity[Bea00].
Aℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial terminalizationofV/GV/Gis a crepant, projective, birational morphismX→V/GX\to V/Gsuch thatXXisℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial with only terminal singularities.
Notice that a smoothℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial terminalization is exactly a projective symplectic resolution in the sense of[Bea00].

By a result of Namikawa[Nam15], aℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial terminalizationφ:X→V/G\varphi:X\to V/Gis arelative Mori dream spaceoverV/GV/G, see[HK00,Oht22]. This means that there are only finitely manyℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial terminalizations ofV/GV/Gup to isomorphism and that the relations between the different isomorphism classes are controlled by a wall-and-chamber structure in the vector space𝔠≔Pic⁡(X)⊗ℤℝ\mathfrak{c}\coloneqq\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}.
Namikawa’s key observation is that the walls in this wall-and-chamber structure in fact form a hyperplane arrangement in𝔠\mathfrak{c}, commonly called theNamikawa arrangement. Namikawa[Nam10]introduced, in addition, a certain real reflection group𝒲G⩽GL​(𝔠)\mathcal{W}_{G}\leqslant\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{c}), nowadays called theNamikawa–Weyl group, which permutes the hyperplanes in the Namikawa arrangement. The group𝒲G\mathcal{W}_{G}only depends onV/GV/Gand not on the morphismφ\varphi.

## Symplectic reflection groups

Recall that an elementg∈Gg\in Gis a symplectic reflection if the rank of1−g1-gis 2. The groupG⩽Sp⁡(V)G\leqslant\operatorname{Sp}(V)is said to be a symplectic reflection group if it is generated by the symplectic reflections it contains. By Verbitsky’s Theorem[Ver00], ifV/GV/Gadmits a symplectic resolution thenGGmust be a symplectic reflection group. We assume for the remainder of the article thatGGis a symplectic reflection group.

The purpose of this note is to explain how recent advances in our understanding of symplectic reflection groups[GRS25,RS26]allow us to explicitly compute the Namikawa–Weyl group for any symplectic reflection group.
There are several reasons why this is important.
Firstly, as explained above, knowing the Namikawa–Weyl group is an essential part of the ongoing programme to classify allℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial terminalizations of quotientsV/GV/Gby symplectic reflection groups.
In particular, if one wishes to count the number ofℚ\mathbb{Q}-factorial terminalizations admitted byV/GV/Gthen one needs to know the Namikawa–Weyl group; see[Bel16].
Secondly, for any given family of conic symplectic singularities, it is a rather subtle problem to decide which Weyl groups can be realised as the Namikawa–Weyl group of a singularity in the family.
For instance, it was shown by Wu[Wu23, Thm. 1.14]that the Namikawa–Weyl group of a Nakajima quiver variety can never have a factor of typeF4F_{4}.
For quotient singularities, however, it is a consequence of our classification result that every irreducible Weyl group appears as a factor of the Namikawa–Weyl group ofV/GV/Gfor a suitableGG.
More specifically:

## Theorem 1.1.

For an irreducible Weyl groupWW, there is a symplectically irreducible symplectic reflection groupGGsuch that the quotientV/GV/Ghas Namikawa–Weyl group of the formW×A1kW\times A_{1}^{k}for somek∈{0,1}k\in\{0,1\}.

Conversely, ifGGis a symplectically irreducible symplectic reflection group then the Namikawa–Weyl group ofV/GV/Gis of the formW×A1kW\times A_{1}^{k}, whereWWis an irreducible Weyl group and0⩽k⩽50\leqslant k\leqslant 5, except in the case whereGGis an exceptional complex reflection group of typeG5,G7,G10,G11,G18G_{5},G_{7},G_{10},G_{11},G_{18}orG19G_{19}.
In these cases, the Namikawa–Weyl group has at least two irreducible factors not of typeA1A_{1}.

The theorem follows from Propositions2.3,3.1,3.3and Tables2and5.

For a given irreducible Weyl groupWW, one can ask if there exists a symplectic reflection group whose Namikawa–Weyl group isWW.
Our classification shows that the answer is yes only for Weyl groups of simply laced type, of typeB2B_{2}, and of typeG2G_{2}.
The Weyl groupsWWof typeF4F_{4}andBmB_{m}form⩾3m\geqslant 3are only realizable as a Namikawa–Weyl group asW×A1W\times A_{1}.

Finally, we note that the Namikawa–Weyl group can be realised as a group of exotic automorphisms of the generalized Calogero–Moser space associated toGG. See Section2.3for details.
