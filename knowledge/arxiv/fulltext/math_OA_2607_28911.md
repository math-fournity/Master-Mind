## Abstract

Given a (not necessarily unital) complete operator system $S$ with a generating cone, there are some studies in literature on the operator system dual $S^\mathrm{d}$ of $S$, i.e., the dual matrix-ordered space $S^*$ equipped with a matrix norm that turns it into a dual operator system satisfying certain universal property.   In order to do further study on $S^\mathrm{d}$, one needs to consider the operator system predual construction.   More precisely, given a dual operator system $V$ with a generating cone, there is a matrix norm on the predual space $V_*$, that turns it into a complete operator system $V_\#$ satisfying certain universal property.   In this article, we show that $V\cong T^\mathrm{d}$ for a complete operator system $T$ if and only if $V$ satisfies a natural property called tightness; in this case, $V\cong (V_\#)^\mathrm{d}$.   Furthermore, we establish that if $S$ is tight, then $S\cong W_\#$ for a dual operator system $W$; in fact $S\cong (S^\mathrm{d})_\#$.   Since all unital complete operator systems and all $C^*$-algebra are tight, one sees that every unital complete operator system and every $C^*$-algebra is of the form $W_\#$, for a dual operator system $W$.

## 1.Introduction

The study of duality for operator systems began with the work[2]of Choi and Effros, in which it was shown that for every finite dimensional unital operator systemSS, one can find an Archimedean matrix order unit in the dual matrix ordered spaceS∗S^{*}, turning it into a unital operator system.

However, whenSSis an infinite dimensional unital operator system,S∗S^{*}may not have an order unit any more (see, e.g.,[14, Example 3.3(c)]).
Therefore, one needs to go beyond the unital case.

Werner began the study of not necessarily unital operator systems in[16,17]and gave a characterization of them in[16, Theorem 4.15].
This theory was applied by Han in[7]to study duality of infinite dimensional operator systems.
Moreover, based on[16,17], the first named author introduced and studied in[14]a duality framework for general complete operator systems.
In[11], this framework was further studied and was applied to the class of operator systems introduced by Connes and van Suijlekom in[3,4](see also[6]).
On the other hand, an equivalent form of not necessarily unital operator systems, in terms of noncommutative convex sets, was given recently by Kennedy, Kim and Manor in[9].
This work was then applied in[8]to study “duailzability” of operator systems in the sense of[14](see also[7]).

More precisely, for a complete operator systemSS(which is not necessarily unital), a certain “universal dual operator system envelope”,SdS^{\mathrm{d}}, ofS∗S^{*}, called theoperator system dual, was introduced in[14].
We say thatSS
- (1)

has agenerating coneif the linear span of the coneS+S_{+}equalsSS;
- (2)

isdualizableif the canonical map fromS∗S^{*}toSdS^{\mathrm{d}}is an operator space isomorphism.

It was shown in[11,14]that the constructionS↦SdS\mapsto S^{\mathrm{d}}induces a functor from the category of operator systems with generating cones (respectively, dualizable operator systems) to the category of dual operator systems with generating cones(respectively, dualizable dual operator systems), called thedual functor.

In order to study the duality of operator systems via the dual functor, it is necessary to consider its predual counterpart.
The aim of this article is to define and study “operator system preduals”.

Using the results in the article, it is recently obtained in[12]that the dual functor is an equivalence from the category of tight operator systems (see Definition3.8; this includes all unital operator systems and allC∗C^{*}-algebras) to the category of tight dual operator systems.

The following are two interesting questions concerning this duality framework:
- Q1).

Which dual operator system can be written in the formSdS^{\mathrm{d}}?
- Q2).

What is the relation between the dual operator system construction and the predual operator system construction?

The aim of this article is to give a study of these two questions.
We find that these two questions are both related to the notion of “tightness” for operator systems as will be defined in Definition3.8.
More precisely, for a dual operator systemVVand a complete operator systemSS, both with generating cones,
- (i).

ifV#V_{\#}is the “universal complete operator system envelope” of the predualV∗V_{*}ofVV, thenV#V_{\#}is always
tight (see Theorem3.9(a));
- (ii).

VVis tight if and only ifV≅(V#)dV\cong(V_{\#})^{\mathrm{d}}, which is equivalent toV≅TdV\cong T^{\mathrm{d}}for a complete operator systemTT(see Theorem3.10(b));
- (iii).

SSis tight if and only ifS≅(Sd)#S\cong(S^{\mathrm{d}})_{\#}(see Theorem3.9(b)).

Since unital complete operator systems are tight (see[14, Theorem 3.17(b)]), the last two statements above are applied in Corollary3.11to the case whenVVandSSare unital. In particular, every unital dual operator system can be written asTdT^{\mathrm{d}}for a complete operator systemTTwith a generating cone (see Corollary3.11(b)).
Moreover, Statement (iii) above also applies to the completion of the operator system associated with a tolerance relation (see Corollary3.2and3.12).

The above indicates certain duality between the constructionV↦V#V\mapsto V_{\#}and the constructionS↦SdS\mapsto S^{\mathrm{d}}.
The precise functorial connections between them will be studied in a further work (see[12]).
