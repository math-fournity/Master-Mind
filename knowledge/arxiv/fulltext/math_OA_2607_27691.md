## Abstract

We study the structure and regularity of higher rank graph $C^*$-algebras, with particular emphasis on their nuclear dimension. For a row-finite, locally convex $k$-graph $Λ$ with no sources, we characterise pure infiniteness of $C^*(Λ)$ in terms of generalised cycles, maximal tails, and strong aperiodicity, and we relate these conditions to topological dimension zero of the primitive ideal space and to the structure of gauge-invariant ideals. Our main application is that whenever $C^*(Λ)$ is purely infinite of topological dimension zero---in particular whenever its ideal lattice is finite---it is strongly purely infinite, $\mathcal O_\infty$-stable, and of nuclear dimension one, \emph{even when $C^*(Λ)$ is not simple}. This extends to the non-simple, higher-rank setting the nuclear-dimension-one computation known for simple UCT-Kirchberg $2$-graph algebras. Along the way we refine and correct several results in the existing graph $C^*$-algebra literature.

## 1.Introduction

Higher-rank graphC∗C^{*}-algebras were introduced by Kumjian and Pask[KP2]as a common generalisation of the Cuntz–Krieger algebras, the
graphC∗C^{*}-algebras of[KPR,KPRR,BPRS], and the rank-kkCuntz–Krieger
algebras arising from buildings. To akk-graphΛ\Lambda—a countable
category carrying a degree functord:Λ→ℕkd\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{N}^{k}with a unique
factorisation property—one associates the universalC∗C^{*}-algebraC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)generated by a Cuntz–KriegerΛ\Lambda-family. This class is
broad enough to contain irrational rotation algebras, Bunce–Deddens algebras
and a wide range of Kirchberg algebras[PRRS], yet combinatorial enough
that delicate structural properties can be read off the underlying graph.
Making that dictionary precise—between the combinatorics ofΛ\Lambdaand
the structure ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)—has been a central theme since[KP2,RSY1,KaP].

A basic dividing line in the structure theory of nuclearC∗C^{*}-algebras runs
between the stably finite and the purely infinite. Forsimplekk-graph
algebras, pure infiniteness is governed by aperiodicity and cofinality[S,PSS4]; in the non-simple setting, Evans and Sims[ES]introducedgeneralised cyclesand showed that their entrances control the presence
of infinite projections (see also[HS,PRRS]). Pure infiniteness, in the
sense of Kirchberg and Rørdam, is moreover the gateway to classification: a
separable, nuclear, strongly purely infinite algebra in the bootstrap class is𝒪∞\mathcal{O}_{\infty}-stable[KR], of nuclear dimension one[BGSW], and
hence classified by ideal-relatedK​KKK-theory. Our first aim is to give a clean,
graph-level characterisation of pure infiniteness for a row-finite, locally
convexkk-graph with no sources, and to situate it within the gauge-invariant
ideal theory of[KaP,PSS2].

Our second aim—and the point we most wish to stress—is the resulting
computation of thenuclear dimensionofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda). Nuclear
dimension, introduced by Winter and Zacharias, is the central regularity
invariant of the Elliott classification programme, and its finiteness is one of
the equivalent forms of the Toms–Winter regularity conjecture. We show
(Proposition7.4) that wheneverC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is purely
infinite of topological dimension zero—in particular wheneverH​(Λ)H(\Lambda)is
finite—it is strongly purely infinite and of nuclear dimension one,even whenC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is not simple. This extends to the higher-rank,
non-simple setting the nuclear-dimension-one computation obtained for simple
UCT-Kirchberg22-graph algebras by Ruiz, Sims and Sørensen[RSS]; the
value one comes from the theorem of Bosa, Gabe, Sims and White that separable,
nuclear,𝒪∞\mathcal{O}_{\infty}-stableC∗C^{*}-algebras have nuclear dimension one[BGSW](finiteness of the nuclear dimension of strongly purely infinite
algebras being due to Szabó[Sz]). What our combinatorial analysis
supplies is precisely the input those theorems need: a graph-level guarantee of
strong pure infiniteness, hence of𝒪∞\mathcal{O}_{\infty}-stability, for a large
class of non-simplekk-graph algebras.

Beyond pure infiniteness we study the regularity properties that make these
algebras tractable for classification: real rank zero, extremal richness in the
sense of Brown and Pedersen[BP,BP2], the chain conditions (Noetherian and
Artinian) on the ideal lattice, stable rank one, and𝒵\mathcal{Z}- and𝒪∞\mathcal{O}_{\infty}-stability. A recurring principle is that, once every ideal
ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is gauge invariant, each of these properties can be controlled
combinatorially through the latticeH​(Λ)H(\Lambda)of saturated hereditary subsets
ofΛ0\Lambda^{0}and the maximal tails ofΛ\Lambda.

## Techniques

Three tools underpin our arguments. The first is the gauge-invariant ideal
theory of[RSY1,KaP]: for a row-finite, locally convexkk-graph the mapH↦IHH\mapsto I_{H}is a lattice isomorphism from the saturated hereditary subsets
onto the gauge-invariant ideals, with quotients again of the formC∗​(Γ​(Λ∖H))C^{*}(\Gamma(\Lambda\setminus H))(Theorem2.11). The second is
strong aperiodicity: we show (Theorem3.7) that it is
equivalent both to every generalised cycle in each maximal tail having an
entrance and to every ideal ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)being gauge invariant, which
reduces the study of the entire ideal lattice to the combinatorics ofH​(Λ)H(\Lambda). The third is a systematic reduction to subquotients: inducting
along a maximal chain inH​(Λ)H(\Lambda), we assemble global properties ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)from those of the simpler algebrasC∗​(Γ​(Λ∖H))C^{*}(\Gamma(\Lambda\setminus H))(Theorems4.1and5.4). Into this scheme we feed
the external structural inputs of Kirchberg–Rørdam[KR],
Pasnicu–Rørdam[PR]and Brown–Pedersen[BP,BP2,BP3].

## Main results

Our first main result, Theorem3.9, characterises pure
infiniteness: for a row-finite, locally convexkk-graphΛ\Lambda, the algebraC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is purely infinite in the sense of Kirchberg–Rørdam if and only
if every vertex projection is properly infinite; equivalently, every generalised
cycle in each maximal tail has an entrance and each vertex of each maximal tail
is connected to by a generalised cycle; equivalently again,Λ\Lambdais
strongly aperiodic and each maximal-tail vertex is reached by a generalised
cycle. The equivalence of the entrance condition with strong aperiodicity, and
with the gauge invariance of every ideal, is Theorem3.7.

For real rank we prove (Theorem4.1) that ifΛ\Lambdais
strongly aperiodic, each maximal-tail vertex is reached by a generalised cycle,
andH​(Λ)H(\Lambda)is finite, thenC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is purely infinite of
topological dimension zero, and has real rank zero precisely when it isK0K_{0}-liftable; fork=2k=2the latter is the homological condition on the
connectivity matrices of[PSS2]. Combining this with the permanence of
extremal richness, such algebras are extremally rich
(Theorems5.2and5.3).
Separately, when every primitive quotient is AF,C∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)has stable
rank one (Theorem6.3). Section6records the
accompanying chain conditions: topological dimension zero ofPrim​(C∗​(Λ))\mathrm{Prim}(C^{*}(\Lambda))forces both the ascending and descending chain conditions
on ideals (Theorem6.2).

In Section7we show thatkk-graph algebras populate all
three branches of the finite-ideal trichotomy: a Noetherian separableC∗C^{*}-algebra is purely infinite, stably finite, or neither, and these classes
are mutually exclusive (Theorem7.1); we exhibit row-finite,
locally convexkk-graphs realising each branch. From this analysis we deduce
two absorption theorems. WhenC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is purely infinite and has
topological dimension zero—in particular wheneverH​(Λ)H(\Lambda)is
finite—it is strongly purely infinite and𝒪∞\mathcal{O}_{\infty}-stable,C∗​(Λ)≅C∗​(Λ)⊗𝒪∞C^{*}(\Lambda)\cong C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\infty}(Proposition7.4); and under the chain conditionsC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)is𝒵\mathcal{Z}-stable (Corollary7.7). In the
purely infinite, topological-dimension-zero case these algebras are moreover of
nuclear dimension one, even when they are not simple
(Proposition7.4).

In the course of these arguments we clarify and, where necessary, correct the
hypotheses of several results in the graph and higher-rank graph literature,
flagging each emendation at the point where it occurs.

## Comparison with earlier work of Pask, Sierakowski and Sims

The three most closely related antecedents are the papers[PSS2,PSS3,PSS4]of the present author with Sierakowski and Sims, and it is worth making the
relationship precise.

The paper[PSS3]treats thesimplecase: for akk-graph whoseC∗C^{*}-algebra is unital and simple it establishes a dichotomy between stable
finiteness and pure infiniteness, and gives necessary and sufficient
conditions, in terms of the underlyingkk-graph, for such an algebra to be
purely infinite. Theorem3.9is the non-simple counterpart:
for an arbitrary row-finite, locally convexkk-graph it characterises pure
infiniteness ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)through the generalised cycles and maximal tails
ofΛ\Lambda, the simple case of[PSS3]corresponding toH​(Λ)={∅,Λ0}H(\Lambda)=\{\emptyset,\Lambda^{0}\}.

The paper[PSS2]shows that strong aperiodicity is equivalent to
topological dimension zero ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda), and that aC∗C^{*}-algebra of
topological dimension zero is purely infinite precisely when all of its vertex
projections are properly infinite; for purely infinite22-graph algebras it
characterises real rank zero as topological dimension zero together with a
homological condition on the adjacency matrices, and it exhibits strongly
purely infinite22-graph algebras of topological dimension zero that fail to
have real rank zero. Theorem3.9sharpens the first of these
equivalences: since pure infiniteness already forces the entrance condition,
hence strong aperiodicity and thus topological dimension zero, the equivalence
between pure infiniteness and proper infiniteness of the vertex projections
holds with no separate dimension hypothesis, and is supplemented by graph-level
criteria valid for everykk. Theorem4.1complements the
real-rank results of[PSS2]in a different direction: it yields real rank
zero for allkk, and for algebras with mixed AF and purely infinite
subquotients, avoiding the22-graph adjacency-matrix condition, at the cost of
assumingH​(Λ)H(\Lambda)finite. The examples of[PSS2]show that this
reachability-and-finiteness hypothesis cannot simply be dropped.

Finally,[PSS4]computes the stable rank ofC∗​(Λ)C^{*}(\Lambda)forfinitekk-graphs that either contain no cycle with an entrance or are
cofinal, and determines exactly which finite locally convexkk-graphs give
unital stably finite algebras, the stably finite ones being direct sums of
matrix algebras over tori of dimension at mostkk. We work throughout with
row-finite, locally convexkk-graphs, so that infinite vertex sets are
permitted, and we use the local computations of[PSS4, Proposition 2.7, Theorem 2.5]as inputs. Our stable-rank statement
(Theorem6.3) is complementary rather than stronger: it
isolates the branch on which the stable rank equals one—namely when every
primitive quotient is AF—and does not reproduce the exact stable-rank
values, including those exceeding one and the value∞\inftyin the cofinal
purely infinite case, obtained in[PSS4].
