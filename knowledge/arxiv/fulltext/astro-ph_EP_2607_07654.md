## Extra Invariant in Magnetohydrodynamics of Planets and StarsAlexander M. BalkDepartment of Mathematics, University of Utah, Salt Lake City, UT 84112balk@math.utah.edu

## Abstract

The paper establishes extra invariant in magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) of rotating and stratified fluid layer. This invariant is conserved adiabatically, i.e. approximately over long time. The existence of the invariant is interesting by itself, as such invariants are extremely rare. However, in addition, this invariant appears to be connected to the famous dynamo phenomenon.

## IIntroduction

One of the biggest problems in MHD is to explain the dynamo phenomenon. It turns out that MHD possesses an adiabatic invariant, that appears to imply the dynamo.

First, let us discuss what kind of extra conservation precisely, we are talking about. If a physical system is weakly nonlinear, the main interactions are often three-wave resonances𝐤1=𝐤2+𝐤3,ω1=ω2+ω3,\displaystyle{\bf k}_{1}={\bf k}_{2}+{\bf k}_{3},\quad{\omega_{1}}={\omega_{2}}+{\omega_{3}},(1)

where𝐤{\bf k}is a wave vector andω=ω​(𝐤){\omega}=\omega({\bf k})is the frequency, determined by a dispersion relation [ωj=ω(𝐤j),j=1,2,3].\omega_{j}=\omega({\bf k}_{j}),j=1,2,3].The relations (1) correspond to the conservation of momentum and energy:𝐌=∫𝐤​N𝐤​𝑑𝐤,E=∫ω​(𝐤)​N𝐤​𝑑𝐤,\displaystyle{\bf M}=\int{\bf k}\;N_{\bf k}\;d{\bf k},\quad E=\int\omega({\bf k})\;N_{\bf k}\;d{\bf k},\quad(2)

whereN𝐤​(t)=ε𝐤/ω​(𝐤)N_{\bf k}(t)=\varepsilon_{\bf k}/\omega({\bf k})is a wave action spectrum (or the number of quasi-particles),ε𝐤​(t)\varepsilon_{\bf k}(t)is the energy spectrum.
A natural question: Does there exist another functionφ​(𝐤)\varphi({\bf k})— besides𝐤,ω​(𝐤){\bf k},\omega({\bf k})— which is also conserved in the interactions (1)φ1=φ2+φ3,[φj=φ​(𝐤j)]​?\displaystyle\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}+\varphi_{3},\quad[\varphi_{j}=\varphi({\bf k}_{j})]\;?(3)

(Zakharov & Schulman,1980,1988). The functionφ​(𝐤)\varphi({\bf k})is supposed to be linear independent of𝐤,ω{\bf k},\,\omega.
More precisely, does there exist a functionφ​(𝐤)\varphi({\bf k})so that equation (3) holds for any vectors𝐤1,𝐤2,𝐤3{\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3}bound by the resonance relations (1).

This question is similar(Ferapontov,1992)to the one asked byBoltzmann (1875,1876)about particle collisions in a gas: Does there exist some quantity, which is conserved in the main collisions. The latter are pair collisions; they are similar to four-wave interactions (two particles areinand two the same particles with new momenta areout).
If there were such quantity, Boltzmann’s kinetic equation would have an additional conservation law (besides the usual ones).Boltzmann (1875,1876)showed that no such quantity exists for classical gas.

Similar, if there existed functionφ\varphi— satisfying (3) — then the wave system would preserve integralI=∫φ​(𝐤)​N𝐤​𝑑𝐤\displaystyle I=\int\varphi({\bf k})\;N_{\bf k}\;d{\bf k}(4)

in addition to the momentum and energy. The integral (4) is exactly conserved in the three-wave resonance interactions, and therefore, by the wave kinetic equation. However,IIis not conserved in non-resonance interactions or in higher-order resonance interactions, that involve more than three waves. In the full weakly nonlinear dynamics, the integral (4) is conserved adiabatically, i.e. approximately over long time. Such integral (or just its kernelφ\varphi) is called “extra invariant”.

The wave systems with extra invariants are rare.
At present, I know onlytwo2-dimensional wave systems that have extra invariants: One is provided by the Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equation(Zakharov & Schulman,1980,1988). The other is the system of Rossby waves or drift waves in plasma(Balk et al.,1991; Balk,1991).

Let us stress that it is possible to find conservation laws in a nonlinear system by considering only linear dynamics.

The aim of the present paper is to establish extra invariant in MHD of a layer of conducting fluid on a celestial body (planet or star). There is equilibrium, when resting fluid is penetrated by a strong toroidal (zonal, parallel to the equator) magnetic field. We will see that waves on the background of this equilibrium possess extra invariant (4) with unusual kernelφ​(𝐤)=arctan⁡q+3​α​p𝗆​(q2+α​p2)−arctan⁡q−3​α​p𝗆​(q2+α​p2),\displaystyle\varphi({\bf k})=\arctan\frac{q+\sqrt{3\alpha}p}{{\sf m}(q^{2}+\alpha p^{2})}-\arctan\frac{q-\sqrt{3\alpha}p}{{\sf m}(q^{2}+\alpha p^{2})},\quad(5)

where𝐤=(p,q){\bf k}=(p,q)is a wave vector (k2=p2+q2k^{2}=p^{2}+q^{2}), and𝗆=f​Aβ​c,α=A2+c2c2,\displaystyle{\sf m}=\frac{fA}{\beta c},\qquad\alpha=\frac{A^{2}+c^{2}}{c^{2}},(6)

ffis the Coriolis parameter,β\betais its latitudinal derivative,AAis the Alfvén speed (based on the background toroidal magnetic field), andccis the gravity wave speed. The present paper considers dynamics on theβ\beta-plane.

This extra invariant is connected to the dynamo phenomenon.
In the second part (Section 3), we will see that the presence of the invariant (5) requres large-scale energy to accumulate in the toroidal magnetic field. This energy supply can maintain – and even increase — the background magnetic fieldAA. (This would happen on a longer time scale than the typical times of linearized dynamics.) Such feedback on the background magnetic field means dynamo action. However, the present paper does not study this feedback.

In the past(Balk,2014,2022), I considered a couple of specific situations where the Rossby wave extra invariant emerged. It turns out that a simple modification of the Rossby wave extra invariant can include general dynamics. Besides, the general consideration simplifies the exposition of the extra invariant and makes it more transparent.
In particular, we do not need the quasi-geostrophy (with or without magnetic forces). Neither we need to neglect the Alfvén speedAAin comparison to the gravity speedcc.

Let us consider waves on the background of resting fluid with strong toroidal magnetic field. The consideration is possible in two ways, producing the same result.

The magnethydrodynamics (MHD) of such layers can be studied using the system of “shallow water” MHD (SMHD) introduced byGilman (2000).

## IIIEnergy accumulation in the large-scale toroidal magnetic field

We will see now that the extra invariant (found in the previous section) constrains the energy flux through the scales: It requires the large scale energy to accumulate in toroidal magnetic field.

First, let us note an obvious fact: Any linear combination of invariants is an invariant.

In particular, the linear combination of the energy and thexx-componentP=x^⋅𝐌P={\hat{x}}\cdot{\bf M}of the momentum (2)F=P−β​𝗆2A2​E=∫p1+𝗆2​(q2+α​p2)​N𝐤​𝑑p​𝑑q\displaystyle F=P-\frac{\beta{\sf m}^{2}}{A^{2}}\,E=\int\frac{p}{1+{\sf m}^{2}(q^{2}+\alpha p^{2})}\;N_{\bf k}dp\,dq\quad(16)

is conserved. Let us call itenstrophy. The standard argument shows that the energy follows the direct cascade towards small scales, while the enstrophy follows the inverse cascade towards large scales.222Our energy and enstrophy are interchanged compared to the standard situation, because the dispersion relation (13) additionally contains factorq2+α​p2q^{2}+\alpha p^{2}in the numerator.

Nevertheless, the energy accumulates in large scales. Indeed, the enstrophy
carries with it the energy.
So, most of the energy — supplied by the source — flows to small scales and dissipates there. But a fraction of supplied energy has to go to large scales. If the source acts for a long time, the energy accumulates in the large scales.

Using the dimensional considerations, one can derive the Kolmogorov-type energy spectra for the turbulence of waves with dispersion relation (10). This spectrum has infrared divergence(Balk,2014), indicating the energy accumulation in large scales. It is actually typical across different physical systems that the large scales carry most of the energy.

## IVConclusion

We considered the MHD of conducting fluid layer on a celestial body (planet or star), when along with magnetic forces, the Coriolis and buoyancy forces are also important. The dynamics has simple equilibrium, when fluid is at rest, and magnetic field is toroidal and constant.
We studied the slowest waves on the background of this equilibrium.

We saw that these waves have extra invariant, conserved adiabatically. The invariant is given by the equations (4)-(5). Equivalently, the invariant is given by equations (19), (18), and (5).

We also saw that the presence of the extra invariant leads to the energy accumulation in the large-scale toroidal magnetic field. This energy influx can maintain — and even increase — the background toroidal magnetic field. Such feedback would constitute dynamo action. However, the detailed mechanism of this feedback is beyond the present paper. Also, the following important question is left out. The toroidal magnetic field has no signature outside the celestial body. It is unclear whether this toroidal field leads to the magnetic field noticable outside the planet or star.

The idea that the dynamo could be a weakly nonlinear phenomenon was suggested long time ago(e.g. Hide,1966; Braginsky,1967). The existence of the extra invariant supports this idea.
