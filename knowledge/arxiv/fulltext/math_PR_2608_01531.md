# Multilevel limits of spiked random matrix minors

## Abstract

We consider the largest eigenvalues of minors of the classical Gaussian random matrices with a finite rank spike in the critical BBP regime. We show that they converge to a multi-level interlacing particle system whose lowest level is the Airy$_β$ point process. Our construction and scaling limits extend in a natural way to non-classical $β>0$.

## 1.2Relation to other work

Some of the methods of our work are similar to the work of Bykhovskaya, Gorin and Sodin[9]. They consider theK=1K=1case and show that the largest eigenvalues ofG+AG+Ascale to the solutions of (1.4), giving an alternative characterization of the distributions found in[5]. In the case that𝔞i\mathfrak{a}_{i}is the Airy point process they dub the RHS of (1.4) the “Airy Green function.” The main focus of that paper is to the statistics applications of the BBP transition. In particular, the work[9]did investigate the possibilities of constructing a multi-level scaling limit as we do here. Indeed, their work finds the limit by a slightly different method: one uses rank one perturbation theory to write the eigenvalues ofG+AG+Ain terms ofGGinstead of the minor. Due to the aforementioned eigenvalue sticking phenomena, this leads to the same limits. At a more technical level, their proof of convergence is difference from ours, as they rely more heavily on meromorphicity properties of random matrix Stieltjes transforms. Additionally, they only consider the classicalβ\beta, as these are the most interesting cases for the statistics applications of their paper. The barrier to extensions of their work to generalβ>0\beta>0are adequate tightness properties of Gaussianβ\beta-ensemble. We derive these from the work[8].

The work[4]also considers the Airy Green function of[9]. They use it to find the distribution of eigenvector components of spiked random matrices in the critical BBP regime, under general finite rank perturbations. Their limit involves a derivative of the Airy Green function. They also handle the rank 1 critically spiked case for generalβ>0\beta>0. Part of their work relies on the convergence of the spiked eigenvalues of Bloemendal and Virág[5,6]which our work does not use as input.

Similar scaling limits have been derived in the context of the22-spin spherical SK model[18,17]and some of the methods here are similar to the methods there.

As we mentioned above, while we were preparing this paper, the aforementioned[20]appeared. It studies the processes we consider but for fixedNN, and focus on various asymptotic regimes asβ→∞\beta\to\infty.

The interlacing system we derive could be viewed as an edge analog of[19]that derives the bulk limit of the Hermiteβ\beta-corners process (i.e., withA=0A=0) as a bead process involving theSineβ\mathrm{Sine}_{\beta}process. In particular, they derive the version of (1.8) in the bulk (i.e., thejjth level of the system is derived from the(j−1)(j-1)st level via a similar Markovian structure involving a Stieltjes transform). Note that without a spike, the limiting system (1.8) is degenerate in the sense described above; thejjth level of particles just equals the(j−1)(j-1)st level. This is in contrast to the bulk setting which does not require a spike for non-degeneracy.
