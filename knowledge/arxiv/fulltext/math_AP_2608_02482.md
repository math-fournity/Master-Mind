## Abstract

Moreover, these explicit expressions may be decomposed into a kinetic-energy mismatch and an internal-energy mismatch from which we characterize agreement of Dafermos' entropy rate criterion with action rate criteria. ## Abstract

Moreover, these explicit expressions may be decomposed into a kinetic-energy mismatch and an internal-energy mismatch from which we characterize agreement of Dafermos’ entropy rate criterion with action rate criteria. ## 1Introduction

The compressible Euler equations in two or more spatial dimensions admit nonunique solutions of bounded entropy.
Specifically for Riemann initial data, the well-developed theory of
self-similar entropy solutions in one spatial dimension is in contrast with the infinitely many admissible
weak solutions in higher dimensions, constructed by convex integration[2,3,11].

This raises the basic question whether there is a selection criterion that ensures uniqueness. Dafermos’ entropy rate criterion[4]has long served as one such candidate. It prefers solutions whose mechanical energy
decreases at the greatest instantaneous rate. Recently, also related rate criteria based on the action were studied[1,5,9,10,14,15].

Action and entropy rate criteria both involve the same kinetic-energy density and compare solutions with the
same initial data, but differ in the sign of the thermodynamic contributions.
The purpose of the present paper is to make the relationship between the criteria explicit and readily computable
for convex integration solutions generated by general piecewise constant fan
subsolutions.

As an application, for the planar contact
discontinuity in[11]we find that both entropy and action rate criteria prefer the convex integration solutions to the classical11d self-similar solution. This aligns with the recent construction[15]of convex integration solutions that action rate criteria prefer to a contact-like solution. Correspondingly,[11]suggest that the non-uniqueness of “wild” solutions reflects the strong instability of the
one-dimensional Riemann solution with respect to genuine
multidimensional perturbations and may be indicative of a compressible Kelvin-Helmholtz instability and the onset of turbulence. If so, the “failure” of all available criteria to select the classical solution may reflect the physics.


The main structural result of this article is that the two
admissibility criteria possess a common reduction, which completely characterizes when the two
criteria agree and when they disagree: The kinetic-energy mismatch must dominate the internal-energy
mismatch. Specifically, they agree for the contact singularity from[11], while they disagree for the two-shock solutions in[9,10].
