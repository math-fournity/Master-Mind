## 1.Introduction

In[24], Watson introduced an invariantϰ​(K,Φ)\varkappa(K,\Phi)for a knotKKwith strong inversionΦ\Phi,
which is a finite dimensionalℤ\mathbb{Z}-graded vector space.
In particular,ϰ​(K,Φ)≅0\varkappa(K,\Phi)\cong 0if and only ifKKis the unknot.

Watson[23]proposed a conjecture that
a strongly invertible knotKKwith a strong inversionΦ\Phiis an L–space knot if and only if
the Watson invariantϰ​(K,Φ)\varkappa(K,\Phi)is supported in a single diagonal gradingδ=q−2​h\delta=q-2h.
For one of the implications of this conjecture,
Baker, Kegel, and McCoy[4]gave two counterexamples.
These are the knotst​09847t09847ando​9​_​30634o9\_30634in the SnapPy census; for details on the census, we refer to[10,11,15,1,5,6].
Interestingly, both share another exceptional property. Their formal semigroups[7,22,19]are closed under addition.
This observation leads to one more counterexample,o​10​_​143807o10\_143807[20].
At present, these three knots are the only strongly invertible L–space knots whose Watson invariant is known to be thick.

In addition, these knots are shown to admit no Khovanov thin surgery.
This means that filling along any non-trivial slope yields a33-manifold which can never be the double branched cover ofS3S^{3}over
a Khovanov thin knot or link.

The purpose of this article is to give the first infinite family of hyperbolic L–space knots having
a (unique) strong inversion whose associated Watson invariant is thick and admitting no Khovanov thin surgery.

## Theorem 1.1.

For eachn≥1n\geq 1, the knotKnK_{n}as defined above is a strongly invertible L-space knot with a unique strong inversionΦ\Phiand its Watson invariantϰ​(Kn,Φ)\varkappa(K_{n},\Phi)is thick.
More precisely, it is supported in at least twoδ\delta-gradings.

In fact, computer calculations suggest thatϰ​(Kn,Φ)\varkappa(K_{n},\Phi)has width exactly two. From the braid words, it is readily seen that eachKnK_{n}is strongly quasi-positive, from which we can read off that the Seifert genus distinguishes all knots in the family. Moreover, we can show that the knotsKnK_{n}have no Khovanov thin surgery.

## Theorem 1.2.

For eachn≥1n\geq 1, the knotKnK_{n}has no Khovanov thin surgery.

Note that Theorem1.1and Theorem1.2give further evidence for an affirmative answer to Question 1.4 from[4].

## 2.Strong inversions

We start by proving the first part of Theorem1.1.

## Lemma 2.1.

For eachn≥1n\geq 1, the knotKnK_{n}as defined above is a hyperbolic, strongly invertible L-space knot with a unique strong inversionΦ\Phi.

## Proof.

In[3]it was shown that the knotsKnK_{n}are L-space knots, by identifying an exceptional surgery to an L-space.

From the surgery description along the linkLLin Figure1, it follows directly that eachKnK_{n}is strongly invertible. Using SnapPy[9], we show that this is the only symmetry ofKnK_{n}.

First, we establish thatLL(which is calledL​12​n​1739L12n1739in the HTW census[12]) is hyperbolic and possesses a unique strong inversion. For that, we use SnapPy[9]to compute the symmetry group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms for the linkLL, and find that there exists exactly one non-trivial symmetry that extends to the link itself, which has to be the symmetry visible in Figure1. This unique strong inversion inherently survives the Dehn filling that yields the knotKnK_{n}.

By Thurston’s hyperbolic Dehn filling theorem[21], we know that any filling ofLLwith all slopes sufficiently large is again hyperbolic and has the same symmetry group asLL, and recent work of Futer–Purcell–Schleimer[13]gives a computable bound for that, cf.[5]. We compute this bound and check that forn≥9n\geq 9the slopes are sufficiently large, so that we conclude thatKnK_{n}has a unique strong inversion ifn≥9n\geq 9. For the finitely many small values ofnn, we build the knotKnK_{n}in SnapPy, verify that it is hyperbolic and that its symmetry group also has a unique strong inversion.

For the code and computations, we refer to[14].
