## Abstract.

We construct examples of totally symplectically aspherical Kähler manifolds with non-trivial second homotopy group in every even dimension greater than or equal to six. In dimension four, we construct examples of totallycc-symplectically aspherical near-symplectic manifolds with non-trivial second homotopy group.

## 1.Introduction

LetMMbe a closed smooth orientable2​n2n-manifold for somen≥1n\geq 1. A closed differential22-formω\omegaonMMis calledasphericalif∫S2f∗​ω=0\int_{S^{2}}f^{*}\omega=0

for each smooth mapf:S2→Mf\colon S^{2}\to M. In other words,ω\omegais an aspherical form if it evaluates trivially on each real spherical homology class ofMM, or equivalently, if the closed22-formπ∗​ω\pi^{*}\omegaonM~\widetilde{M}, pulled back along the universal Riemannian coveringπ:M~→M\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M, is exact.

Aspherical symplectic forms
were first introduced by Floer in his attempts to attack the Arnol’d conjecture[Flo89]. Indeed, the latter was proven true for mostsymplectically aspherical manifolds(i.e., closed manifolds equipped with aspherical symplectic forms), see[RO99]. It is also well-known that Floer homology, the Lusternik–Schnirelmann theory for Lagrangian intersections, and the theory ofJJ-holomorphic curves are simpler for symplectically aspherical manifolds, so naturally, thesymplectic asphericitycondition has appeared in a number of classical theorems in symplectic topology (see[KRT08]and the references therein).

Besides closed symplectic manifolds with vanishing second homotopy group that are symplectically aspherical for trivial reasons, there are many examples ofnon-trivialsymplectically aspherical manifolds (i.e., those with non-trivialπ2\pi_{2}); see[Gom98,IKRT04,FMS04,KRT07,DCDJ25,DCDJ26]for some examples and their other interesting features. However, it seems that the examples of non-trivialtotally symplectically aspherical manifolds(i.e., those with non-trivialπ2\pi_{2}on whicheverysymplectic form is aspherical) have not appeared in the literature. Of course, the topology and geometry of these manifolds are highly constrained; for instance, they cannot contain non-trivial real spherical homology classes (see[IKRT04,KRT08]for this, and Subsection2.Bfor other constraints).

Motivated by this circle of ideas, we investigate in this paper the problem of constructing explicit examples of totally symplectically aspherical manifolds. In this direction, we produce thefirstexamples ofnon-trivialtotally symplectically aspherical2​n2n-manifolds for eachn≥3n\geq 3.
Interestingly, our examples are smooth complex projective varieties.

Forn≥4n\geq 4, our examples arise as cyclic branched covers of abelian varieties. The construction of such examples crucially relies on the theory of abelian covers of Pardini–Tovena[PT95], and our proof of their non-trivial total symplectic asphericity in Section3uses classical tools from complex algebraic geometry and algebraic topology, such as the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem and the description of the homology of the fundamental group. For the details of this construction, we refer to Corollary3.3.

Forn=3n=3, our examples are somewhat different from the ones produced forn≥4n\geq 4; they are obtained as ample hypersurfaces in certain smooth projective44-folds. In particular, they are smooth projective33-folds. For the details of this construction, we refer to Corollary3.4.

Forn=2n=2, we are unable to produce symplectic examples! In particular, we leave open the question of whether smooth complex projective surfaces can be a source of such examples in dimension44. (Remarkably, if such projective surfaces exist, they must be of general type[DCDJ26].) This seems to be a very interesting problem in low-dimensional topology.

In view of our inability to construct low-dimensional symplectic examples, we investigate this circle of ideas in thenon-symplecticsetting. This is a natural direction, for over the past few decades, several notions of geometric structures on smooth manifolds that mimic or approximate symplectic structures have been introduced and studied, especially in dimension44. Such structures include, for instance,near-symplectic structures(i.e., closed 2-forms that are symplectic outside a codimension-33submanifold)[Tau99,Hon00,GK04,ADK05,Ver16]andcc-symplectic structures(i.e., closed 2-forms whose de Rham cohomology class cup to a top class)[LO95,AO06].
While these structures are quite interesting and have been well-studied (see also Section2), it seems thatnon-trivialasphericalcc-symplectic structures onnon-symplecticnear-symplectic manifolds have not received much attention in the literature.

In this direction, we produce here a family of explicit examples of non-trivialtotallycc-symplectically aspherical near-symplectic44-manifolds(i.e., closed near-symplectic44-manifolds with non-trivialπ2\pi_{2}on whicheverycc-symplectic form is aspherical). Our examples, described in Theorem4.1, which are neither complex nor symplectic, are
generalized connected sums involving thesymmetric squares of closed Riemann surfaces. The latter have been extensively studied in topology and geometry; see, for instance,[Mac62,ACGH85,BGZ02,OS04,DCDJ25].

Notably, the proofs of the desired properties of our examples in dimension44constitute the bulk of this paper and involve the use of several topological tools, from Whitney’s trick to spectral sequences (see Sections5and6). This hints at the difficulty of ensuring totalcc-symplectic asphericity on geometrically enriched closed44-manifolds in the presence of the second homotopy group.

In conclusion, the results of this paper show that the restrictive condition of beingtotallycc-symplectically aspherical is somewhat flexible, both topologically and geometrically, in the sense that it is independent of the vanishing of the second homotopy group and the existence of Kähler (resp. near-symplectic) structures in dimensions≥6\geq 6(resp.≥4\geq 4), much like the weaker condition of being merelycc-symplectically aspherical.

## Arrangement of the paper

We begin with a brief review of near-symplectic and asphericalcc-symplectic forms in Section2, where we also record some facts about the symmetric squares of closed Riemann surfaces. Then in Section3, which is mostly self-contained, we dive into the construction of totally symplectically aspherical manifolds in dimensions≥6\geq 6and state and prove our first main result (see Corollaries3.3and3.4). Section4contains the statement of our second main result in dimension44(see Theorem4.1) and a detailed discussion of our examples. Then, by analyzing sphere complements in the symmetric squares of surfaces, we assemble most of the ingredients required for the proof of our second main result in Section5, which is finally completed in Section6after studying the second homotopy group and real Hurewicz map of our44-manifolds. Finally, we conclude this paper in Section7by discussing the sharpness of our results.

## 2.A.Near-symplectic forms

LetMMbe a closed smooth oriented2​n2n-manifold for somen≥2n\geq 2. A closed differential22-formω\omegaonMMis callednear-symplecticif it is symplectic outside a codimension-33submanifold ofMMwhere it vanishes transversely, i.e., for everyp∈Mp\in M, eitherωpn>0\omega^{n}_{p}>0, orωpn−1=0\omega^{n-1}_{p}=0andωpn−2≠0\omega^{n-2}_{p}\neq 0at a codimension-33submanifold ofMM, see[ADK05,Ver16].

In dimension44, a near-symplectic form onMMexists if and only if there is a de Rham cohomology class whose square induces the given orientation ofMM, see[Tau99,Hon00]. A near-symplectic form determines asingular Lefschetz pencilhaving quadratic singularities along its11-dimensional zero set[ADK05,AK08]; this is analogous to Donaldson’s famous result in the symplectic setting[Don99]. In turn, a near-symplectic structure is implied by the existence of certain singular Lefschetz pencils and degree22(co)homology classes[ADK05]; this too is analogous to Gompf’s celebrated result in the symplectic setting[GS99]. We refer to[GK04,ET06]for other noteworthy results concerning near-symplectic44-manifolds.

## 3.B.Comments and consequences

While smooth ample hypersurfaces ofW3W^{3}are smooth projective symplectically aspherical surfaces with non-trivial second homotopy group, we do not know if they aretotallysymplectically aspherical!

## 3.5 Remark.

It is worth pointing out that Theorem3.1does not hold in lower dimensions. Indeed, forn=2n=2,[DCP24, Corollary 3.2]shows that some elements of the second homotopy group can be represented by smooth rational curves with respect to a different complex structure. (Notice how none of theXnX^{n}forn≥4n\geq 4considered here can support rational curves.) Forn=2n=2, this impliesIm​(h2)≠0\text{Im}(h_{2})\neq 0; see the proof of Corollary3.6below.

The following corollary highlights how the double-branched covers we considered in this section become, in a sense, more aspherical-like as the dimension increases.

## 3.6 Corollary.

LetXnX^{n}be the double-branched cover associated to a smooth ample divisorBn−1B^{n-1}in the linear system|2​L||2L|for an ample line bundleLLon an abelian varietyYnY^{n}. Forn≥4n\geq 4, any Kähler structure on the underlying smooth manifold given by the double-branched cover cannot support rational curves.

## Proof.
