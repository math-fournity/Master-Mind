# An $H^{-1}$ least-squares UnCut FEM on domains defined by a level set function

## Abstract

We propose a novel UnCut finite element method (FEM) for the Poisson and Stokes equations on domains with curved boundaries represented by a level set function. Like the $φ$-FEM, the method avoids numerical integration over cut subregions of boundary elements, but introduces a novel least-squares formulation that minimizes an $H^{-1}$ residual of the governing equations. This formulation ensures stability without requiring large stabilization parameters, thereby eliminating the need for user-tuned penalty parameters and improving the robustness of the computation. Optimal-order convergence of the UnCut FEM solutions is rigorously established in the $H^1$ norm for both the Poisson and Stokes equations, and numerical experiments are presented to support the theoretical analysis.

This difficulty has led to extensive research on unfitted finite element methods (FEMs) that do not require the computational mesh to fit the boundary. These methods employ a fixed background mesh, making them particularly attractive for problems involving intricate geometries or moving interfaces.

Unfitted finite element methods in the discontinuous Galerkin framework were first introduced in[18]and have since attracted considerable attention.
Over the past two decades, a wide range of unfitted finite element methods have been developed, most notably CutFEM[2,1,6,7,4,5]and XFEM[25,27,26].
For a comprehensive overview of recent developments, we refer to[5].
A central challenge in these methods is the so-called small cut cell problem, which arises from arbitrarily small or highly anisotropic intersections between the boundary and background mesh elements.
To address this issue, various stabilization strategies have been proposed, including ghost penalty techniques[24,2,16]and cell-aggregation or merging approaches[8,9,19,10], in which small cut cells are combined with neighboring elements to handle conditioning issues for higher-order elements.
Beyond steady-state problems, unfitted formulations have also been extended to time-dependent parabolic and Stokes equations[20,23,22,29]and fluid-structure interactions[3,15].
For Stokes problems, unfitted discretizations involve additional theoretical challenges beyond the small cut cell problem. A fundamental difficulty is establishing the inf-sup stability condition on geometrically unfitted meshes, which has been addressed in[17,28]. Another important consideration is the preservation of the divergence-free constraint.
In[21], the Scott–Vogelius element pair within the CutFEM framework
was shown to yield a nearly divergence-free velocity field.

Despite their flexibility and robustness, many unfitted FEMs remain challenging in practice. In particular, they require the accurate evaluation of integrals over the physical domainΩ\varOmega, which involves integration on curved cut cells, as illustrated in Figure1.

## 5Conclusion

We have introduced and analyzed a novel unfitted finite element method, called theH−1H^{-1}least-squares UnCut FEM, for the Poisson and Stokes equations on domains defined by a level set function. The proposed method inherits the implementation advantages of the existingϕ\phi-FEMs, while the design of the least-squares formulation eliminates the need for the stabilization parameters to be sufficiently large, thereby improving both robustness of computation and simplicity of implementation. The formulation in theH−1H^{-1}setting is the key to enabling the use ofC0C^{0}finite elements while retaining these favorable properties. Optimal-orderH1H^{1}-convergence of the velocity andL2L^{2}-convergence of the pressure (up to a constant) are rigorously established for the Stokes equations, together with optimal-orderH1H^{1}-convergence for the Poisson equation.

Several directions remain for future research, including the theoretical derivation of optimal-orderL2L^{2}-error estimates as well as extensions to Neumann/Robin boundary conditions and time-dependent problems.
