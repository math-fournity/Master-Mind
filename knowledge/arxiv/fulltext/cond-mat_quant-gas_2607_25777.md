## Abstract

We propose encoding a pseudo-spin-$1/2$ system in the ground ($v=0$) and first excited ($v=1$) vibrational states of polar molecules. Double microwave shielding simultaneously shields molecules in both states, suppressing two-body losses by orders of magnitude while strictly avoiding three-body recombination. The microwave dressing is state-dependent and results in highly tunable, long-range dipolar Ising exchange ($J_z$), density-density ($V$), and spin-density ($W$) interactions. These interaction length scales readily exceed the typical interparticle spacing, pushing the molecules deep into the strongly interacting regime. In bulk gases, this enables the exploration of itinerant quantum magnetism and quantum droplets with novel anisotropic spin textures; in optical lattices, it naturally realizes extended Hubbard and $t$-$J_z$ models, opening new directions in quantum simulation.

## Abstract

We propose encoding a pseudo-spin-1/21/2system in the ground (v=0v=0) and first excited (v=1v=1) vibrational states of polar molecules.
Double microwave shielding simultaneously shields molecules in both states, suppressing two-body losses by orders of magnitude while strictly avoiding three-body recombination.
The microwave dressing is state-dependent and results in highly tunable, long-range dipolar Ising exchange (JzJ_{z}), density-density (VV), and spin-density (WW) interactions.
These interaction length scales readily exceed the typical interparticle spacing, pushing the molecules deep into the strongly interacting regime.
In bulk gases, this enables the exploration of itinerant quantum magnetism and quantum droplets with novel anisotropic spin textures; in optical lattices, it naturally realizes extended Hubbard andtt-JzJ_{z}models, opening new directions in quantum simulation.

A central goal in quantum simulation is the realization of fundamental spin models[8,6,7,29], such as the Hubbard[25,32,42,34,23]andtt-JJmodels[35,48,9,73,59,79,78], which are believed to capture the essential physics underlying high-temperature superconductivity and magnetic ordering.
Ultracold atoms naturally realize the Hubbard model[8,6,7,29],
with tunnelingttcontrolled by the depth of an optical lattice,
and on-site contact interactionsUUtunable by magnetic Feshbach resonances[14].
Thett-JJmodel arises as the strongly interacting limitU≫tU\gg tof the Hubbard model[12],
where double occupancies occur only virtually and mediate effective off-site spin-spin interactions with strengthJ∝t2/UJ\propto t^{2}/U.
Consequently, these systems are limited to theJ<tJ<tpart of the parameter regime[35].
Furthermore, because the strongly interacting limit is achieved in deep lattices, bothttandJJare small, and the realization of low temperature compared to tunneling becomes a key bottleneck[80].

Ultracold polar molecules give access to strong long-range dipolar interactions[76,71],
offering a promising pathway to overcome these limitations by inducing strong off-site interactions directly, rather than by super-exchange[27,28].
Quantum simulation with polar molecules is pursued experimentally[81,57,15,58,54,47,56,19],
but achieving high phase-space densities and hence high lattice filling fractions has been challenging.
Remarkable progress has led to the realization of both Fermi degenerate gases[20,75,50,60]and Bose-Einstein condensates[5,62]of polar molecules.
Collisional shielding[38,37,1]plays a central role by
enabling efficient evaporative cooling[5], and collisional stability[82]with simultaneous control of molecular interactions[37,83].

Gorshkov and co-authors have shown that molecules dressed in combined microwave and dc electric fields realize a fully tunablett-JJHamiltonian extended by long-range density-density (VV) and density-spin (WW) interactions, referred to as thett-JJ-VV-WWmodel[27,28],
and realized recently[45,10].
However, this pioneering work was carried out before it was appreciated that even non-reactive polar molecules undergo loss by “sticky collisions”[52,3]and require active collisional shielding.
Mukherjee and co-authors have shown that microwave shielding of ultracold polar molecules is compatible with pseudo-spin encoding in the hyperfine degree of freedom[55].
This results in hyperfine-independent interactions, from which SU(NN)-symmetric exchange interactions can emerge, with rich phenomenology[26,70,69,84,31,65,68,77,74,11,33].
Yet, because these effective interactions arise indirectly through quantum statistics, they arise on prohibitively small energy scales.
Thus, a unified platform that simultaneously provides strong, directly tunable state-dependent interactions has yet to be realized.

Here, we propose to encode pseudo-spin in the ground (v=0v=0) and the first excited (v=1v=1) vibrational states of microwave-shielded polar molecules.
We show that this leads to a collisionally stable multi-component gas with long-range anisotropic dipolar interactions that are tunably spin-dependent.
Using bosonic NaCs as a highly relevant example, we show this setup provides broad tunability and access to the strongly interacting regime, while essentially eliminating collisional loss.

Although in this manuscript we have focused on bosonic NaCs, the proposed shielding framework applies equally to fermionic molecules.
Fermionic mixtures are expected to be at least as robust against collisional loss,
and the induced dipolar interactions are independent of quantum statistics.
The ability to induce strong, tunably state-dependent dipolar interactions therefore extends directly to multi-component Fermi degenerate gases of polar molecules.
Microwave shielding in single-component gases can already lead to unconventionalpp-wave superfluidity[2,18,44,21],
and the extension to tunable state-dependent interactions can be used to drive competing pairing mechanisms.

Our model can be extended by incorporating additional internal degrees of freedom.
The vibrational pseudo-spin can be combined withNNnuclear hyperfine states in the ground and excited manifolds, analogous to two-orbital SU(NN) magnetism in alkaline-earth atoms[26].
Under the conservation of vibrational excitations, this setup exhibits aU​(1)×SU​(N)×SU​(N)\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(N)\times\mathrm{SU}(N)symmetry, which can be extended to the fullSU​(2​N)\mathrm{SU}(2N)symmetry at the specific microwave parameters where the spin-dependent interactions vanish (Jz=W=0J_{z}=W=0). Additionally, our scheme is not limited to thev=0v=0andv=1v=1states and can be extended to higher vibrational manifolds (see AppendixD) to provide even more degrees of freedom.

In conclusion, we have proposed encoding pseudo-spin into the vibrational degree of freedom of ultracold polar molecules, where double microwave shielding eliminates collisional loss. This realizes long-range anisotropic dipolar interactions of the Ising exchange, density-density, and spin-density type that are all tunable by the microwave fields.
The detunings and Rabi frequencies of the two microwave fields provide flexible independent control over interactions in multiple states, unlike for atoms where all interactions are tuned by a single magnetic field[7,14].
The interaction length scales can exceed typical interparticle spacing in both optical lattices and bulk gases.

In bulk gases, this corresponds to the strongly interacting regime[82,61], where already in the single-component case the dipolar interaction drives formation of quantum droplets, as observed in[83], and competing phases are predicted[17]. Although our setup realizes a two-component pseudo-spin mixture rather than a spinor Bose-Einstein condensate[66,63,53], it offers a distinct mechanism for pattern formation. Specifically, the dipolar spin-density interaction found here favors an anisotropic spin texture in inhomogeneous gases and droplets—a phenomenon that in degenerate Bose gases cannot arise from exchange interactions alone[66].

In the lattice setting, the dipolar interactions realize long-range extended Hubbard models with tunably spin-dependent off-site interactions[41,30].
In the limit of hard-core repulsion that prevents double occupancy, this realizes an extension of thett-JJmodel[35,48]—a paradigmatic framework for strongly correlated electrons that hosts rich physics for Ising exchange[9,73,59,79,78].
In the extended model, long-range density-density interactions are predicted to enhance superfluid correlations[72],
and the Ising spin-spin and spin-density interactions can induce phase separation[49]or enhancedd-wave superfluidity[43].

Appendix C: Vibrationally inelastic processes—
To generate state-dependent interactions, the microwave detunings must be comparable to the rotation-vibration coupling constant (2​αe2\alpha_{e}). In this regime, the composition of the field-dressed states, which determines shielding efficiency and the induced dipolar interactions, is sensitive to the precise microwave parameters. Because of this sensitivity, it is nota prioriclear whether the two-body loss rates can be simultaneously suppressed for all collision pairs (|0⟩+|0⟩|0\rangle+|0\rangle,|0⟩+|1⟩|0\rangle+|1\rangleand|1⟩+|1⟩|1\rangle+|1\rangle), while ensuring the absence of the field-linked bound states that lead to three-body recombination.
