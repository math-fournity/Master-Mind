## Abstract

Quantizing high-dimensional vectors is fundamental to similarity search, distributed learning, and model compression. Feng, Indyk, Kapralov, Krachun, and Prokhorov established sharp guarantees for an unbiased dithered quantizer based on a randomized Hadamard transform [FIK+26]. Their $1/d$-scale inner-product estimator, however, uses a second randomized transform and residual quantization, increasing both communication and the leading constant in the proved bound. We show that this extra stage is unnecessary: pairwise-independent dithers across Hadamard coordinates suffice. The resulting unbiased single-stage estimator uses $b$ bits per coordinate and achieves \[ \mathbb{E}\!\left[   \left|\left\langle y,\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle\right|^2 \right] \leq \left(\frac{3π\sqrt{3}}{2}+o(1)\right) \frac{\lVert y\rVert_2^2}{d\,4^b}, \] as $b\to\infty$, with a dimension-free $o(1)$ term uniform over unit inputs and fixed queries. Compared with the two-stage construction of Feng et al., it eliminates the residual-stage $O(d)$-bit payload and reduces the leading upper-bound constant by a factor of approximately $5.93$.   The proof was first obtained using a fully automated Gemini-based agentic system developed internally at Google. The authors have verified the proof and edited it for clarity of presentation.

## 1Introduction

Vector quantization represents a high-dimensional vector by a short binary
string while retaining the geometric information needed by downstream tasks.
Atbbbits per coordinate, a vector quantizer consists of an encoder𝖤𝗇𝖼:ℝd→{0,1}b​d\mathsf{Enc}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{0,1\}^{bd}and a decoder𝖣𝖾𝖼:{0,1}b​d→ℝd\mathsf{Dec}:\{0,1\}^{bd}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}; on inputx∈ℝdx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}, it returns the reconstructionx^=𝖣𝖾𝖼​(𝖤𝗇𝖼​(x))\widehat{x}=\mathsf{Dec}(\mathsf{Enc}(x)). The encoder and decoder may use shared

distances, and inner products, while keeping the representation small. This
primitive is used in similarity search[CHA02,JDS11,DII+04,DCL08,GL24,GGX+25],

A widely used data-oblivious template has the form𝖤𝗇𝖼​(x)=quant⁡(R​x)\mathsf{Enc}(x)=\operatorname{quant}(Rx), whereRRis a random rotation matrix andquant⁡(⋅)\operatorname{quant}(\cdot)is often a scalar quantizer applied
coordinatewise[CHA02,DII+04,DCL08,AMC+24]. Broader
variants replace the scalar rule by structured vector codebooks[AIL+15,TCS+24]. The rotation distribution and the codebook are
chosen independently of the dataset, so no data-dependent training is required.
Moreover, the method is amenable to analysis because each coordinate ofR​xRxis
approximately normally distributed whenRRis a uniformly random rotation[FIK+26].
Variants of this template include DRIVE and EDEN for distributed mean estimation[VBP+21,VBP+22], RaBitQ for distance and inner-product
estimation[GL24,GGX+25], and TurboQuant[ZDH+26].

Despite these advantages, applying a dense random rotation matrixRRrequiresΘ​(d2)\Theta(d^{2})time, which can be prohibitively expensive in high dimensions. A
standard
way to reduce this cost is to use randomized Hadamard transforms[AC09,SAR06,WLR+08,KW11,HMT11]. Specifically,
one replacesRRwithH​DHD, whereHHis a normalized Hadamard matrix andDDis a diagonal matrix of independent random signs. For the Walsh–Hadamard

However, this speedup comes at an analytical cost:H​DHDis discrete, and each
coordinate ofH​D​xHDxis an input-dependent weighted sum of the random signs;

Feng, Indyk, Kapralov, Krachun, and Prokhorov[FIK+26]overcame this difficulty for vector distortion by

reconstruction is exactly unbiased and satisfies, uniformly over unit inputs,𝔼​∥x^−x∥22≤(π​32+o​(1))​4−b,\mathbb{E}\lVert\widehat{x}-x\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{2}+o(1)\right)4^{-b},

asb→∞b\to\infty. For fixed-query inner products, their separate construction
uses a closely related two-stage scheme: after an initial randomized Hadamard
quantization, it projects the reconstruction onto the unit ball and quantizes
the residual after a fresh randomized Hadamard transform. The residual stage
yields the desired1/d1/dscaling, but it also adds communication and enlarges
the leading constant in the proved bound.

In this work, we show that the residual stage is unnecessary. In the base
quantizer, one scalar dither is shared across all Hadamard coordinates, so the
coordinate errors can remain correlated even though each is centered. We
replace the shared dither by pairwise-independent dithers. Conditioned onDD, the errors
are then centered and pairwise independent, so every off-diagonal term in the
inner-product second moment vanishes. Thus pairwise independence suffices for
the desired second-moment bound.

## 1.1Related work

## Random rotations and coordinate-wise quantization.

Random projection followed by scalar quantization is classical in similarity
estimation and locality-sensitive hashing[CHA02,DII+04,DCL08]. In distributed mean estimation,
Suresh et al. combined a randomized Hadamard transform with stochastic
quantization[SYK+17], while DRIVE and EDEN developed rotation-based
schemes that include unbiased reconstruction and flexible rates[VBP+21,VBP+22]. Thus this template, including unbiased
variants, predates TurboQuant. In vector search, RaBitQ and its multi-bit
extension give rigorous distance and inner-product guarantees, including an
asymptotically optimal space–error tradeoff[GL24,GGX+25], consistent with the general compression bounds
of Alon and Klartag[AK17]. TurboQuant later combined a
dense-rotation MSE quantizer with residual quantization[ZDH+26]. Its relationship to DRIVE/EDEN and RaBitQ is discussed in[BBM+26a,GGX+26].

## Dithering.

Randomized offsets have a long history in quantization theory, where they are
used to control bias and the dependence of quantization noise on the input[SCH64,ZF92]. Dithered scalar grids have also been combined
with random projections for similarity and distance estimation[DII+04,DCL08]. Feng et al.[FIK+26]use one scalar
offset shared across all transformed coordinates. We leave their scalar
quantizer unchanged and modify only the dependence structure of the offsets.

## Fast structured rotations.

Randomized Hadamard transforms are central to fast embedding and randomized
linear-algebra methods[AC09,HMT11,KW11,TRO11].
Approximation of dense random rotations by one or more such transforms has
been studied for a single transform[CN22], two structured
blocks[ZM26], and multiple transforms with blockwise dependence
control[BKM+26b]. Direct single-transform quantization analyses
appear in distributed mean estimation[BVP+24]and, using
dithering, in the sharp high-rate result of Feng et al.[FIK+26].

## Relation to Feng et al.

Our scalar quantizer is exactly the unbiased Gaussian-companded construction
of Feng et al.[FIK+26]. We invoke their reconstruction map,
averaging identity, and scalar error estimates directly. Our new
ingredients are the pairwise-independent dither family, conditional covariance
diagonalization, and the weighted mixed-Rademacher estimate for the remaining
diagonal terms.
