## Abstract

The finite and infinite Fibonacci words are classical objects in combinatorics on words. Bio-inspired language operations provide a useful tool for studying how finite and infinite words can arise via local rewriting mechanisms. For example, the suffix square completion operation is known to generate the infinite Fibonacci word, as well as other infinite words such as the Thue--Morse word and the period-doubling word.   The prefix--suffix duplication operation produces a language of words formed by appending prefixes or suffixes of a word $w$ to the front or back of $w$ respectively, and Dumitran conjectured that Fibonacci words of the same index parity can be generated from one another by the bounded duplication length variant of this operation.   In this paper, we resolve and strengthen Dumitran's conjecture. We show that, for all $1 \leq p \leq n$, it is possible to generate the Fibonacci word $F_{2n}$ from $F_{2p}$, and $F_{2n+1}$ from $F_{2p+1}$, using only prefix duplications with a bound of $k \geq 3$. We furthermore show that this bound of $3$ is optimal, and we give an algorithm that produces a witness derivation in time linear in the length of the target Fibonacci word.

## 1Introduction

The well-studied sequence of finite Fibonacci words can be defined either by the two-symbol alphabet morphismϕ​(0)=01\phi(0)=01andϕ​(1)=0\phi(1)=0, or by the recurrenceFi=Fi−1​Fi−2F_{i}=F_{i-1}F_{i-2}with base casesF0=0F_{0}=0andF1=01F_{1}=01. By iterating either the morphism or the recurrence, we obtain the infinite Fibonacci word𝐅=0100101001001​…\mathbf{F}=0100101001001\dots.

It is known[7]that the infinite Fibonacci word can be generated by the bio-inspiredsuffix square completionoperation, which is defined bySSC⁡(w)={w​x∣w=w′​y​x​y​for some​x∈Σ+​and​w′,y∈Σ∗}.\operatorname{SSC}(w)=\{wx\mid w=w^{\prime}yxy\text{ for some }x\in\Sigma^{+}\text{ and }w^{\prime},y\in\Sigma^{*}\}.

Other well-known infinite words, such as the period-doubling word and the Thue–Morse word, can be generated by this same operation. Indeed, this gives rise to a fascinating observation: applying the suffix square completion operation once to a word necessarily generates a word that contains a square, yet iterated application of the suffix square completion operation can generate infinite words that avoid any word power of rational exponent strictly greater than22.

By contrast, the infinite Thue–Morse word cannot be generated by the bio-inspiredprefix–suffix duplicationoperationPSD⁡(w)\operatorname{PSD}(w), which produces a language of words formed by appending prefixes or suffixes of a wordwwto the front or back ofww, respectively. A similar result is not known for the infinite Fibonacci word, but by defining aboundedvariant of the prefix–suffix duplication operationPSDk⁡(w)\operatorname{PSD}_{k}(w), where appended prefixes and suffixes are constrained to have length at mostkk, it is possible that the Fibonacci word and other infinite words constructed via concatenation may be generated in this way.

Indeed, in his thesis[8], Dumitran states the following conjecture:

## Conjecture 1.

F2​n∈PSDk∗⁡(F2​p)F_{2n}\in\operatorname{PSD}_{k}^{*}(F_{2p})for any1≤p≤n1\leq p\leq nandk≥3k\geq 3, andF2​n+1∈PSDk∗⁡(F2​p+1)F_{2n+1}\in\operatorname{PSD}_{k}^{*}(F_{2p+1})for any1≤p≤n1\leq p\leq nandk≥5k\geq 5.

In other words, Dumitran conjectures that we may obtain the infinite Fibonacci word by iteratively applying the bounded prefix–suffix duplication operation, but we may only obtain even-indexed Fibonacci words from even-indexed Fibonacci words with a bounded length of at least33and likewise for odd indices with a bounded length of at least55.

In this paper, we resolve Dumitran’s conjecture in the positive, demonstrating that Fibonacci words of the same index parity can be obtained from one another using bounded prefix–suffix duplications. In fact, we prove a stronger result demonstrating that only prefix duplications are necessary, and where the lengths of each duplication are bounded byk≥3k\geq 3in both the even and odd cases. We furthermore show that the boundk≥3k\geq 3is optimal. Consequently, arbitrarily long prefixes of the infinite Fibonacci word can be obtained from shorter same-parity Fibonacci words via a finite number of prefix duplications.

## 3Proof of Dumitran’s Conjecture

## 4Algorithmic Implementation

The constructive nature of the proofs of Theorems9and10lends itself naturally to an algorithmic implementation. Each of the derivations used in those proofs can be expanded into a sequence of individual prefix duplication operations, and in doing so, the argument can be reformulated as a recursive procedure that outputs a validPDk∗\operatorname{PD}_{k}^{*}derivation from a seed Fibonacci wordFpF_{p}to a target Fibonacci wordFnF_{n}.

In this section, we present pseudocode for a procedureDerive(Algorithm1), which computes a derivation symbolically by recording the length of the prefix duplicated at each step, rather than constructing every intermediate word directly. Together with the seed Fibonacci wordFpF_{p}, the resulting sequence uniquely determines every word in the derivation.

In Algorithm1, the global sequenceSSstores the lengths of the prefixes to be duplicated. The instructionEmit(dd) appends an integerddtoSS, and this corresponds to duplicating the prefix of lengthddof the current word at the current step of the derivation. (Note that, at any point in the computation, the “current word” is understood to be the word obtained fromFpF_{p}by applying, in order, the prefix duplications whose lengths have currently been stored inSS.)

The procedureBuildEven(jj) implements the inductive construction from Lemma8. If the current word has the formF2​WF_{2}W, then the sequence given by this procedure transforms it intoF2​j​WF_{2j}W. The procedurePrependEven(jj) implements the consequence of Lemma8by first duplicating the leading prefixF2F_{2}and then callingBuildEven(jj) to transformF2F_{2}intoF2​jF_{2j}. Similarly, the procedurePrependOdd(jj) implements the claim from the proof of Theorem9by prependingF2​j+1F_{2j+1}to any word beginning withF2F_{2}.
