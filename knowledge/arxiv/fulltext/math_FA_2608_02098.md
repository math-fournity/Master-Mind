## Abstract

Let $C_n$ be the optimal constant with the following property. For every even, continuous, strictly positive density $f$ on $R^n$ and all origin-symmetric convex bodies $K,L\subset R^n$, the inequalities $$   \int_{K\capξ^\perp}f   \leq   \int_{L\capξ^\perp}f   \qquad\text{for all }ξ\in S^{n-1} $$ imply $\int_Kf\leq C_n\int_Lf$. In an earlier paper the authors proved that $C_n\leq\sqrt n$. In this paper, we prove the matching lower bound $C_n\geq c\sqrt n$. To simplify the exposition, we first give a complete one-scale construction, based on earlier work of Klartag and Koldobsky, which yields $C_n\geq c\sqrt{n/\log n}$. For the sharp result, we use the random-rounding construction of Klartag and Livshyts as a black box and combine it with a spherical-averaging support-separation argument.

Related isomorphic comparison theorems in which the two section
integrals are formed with different measures, together with applications to
slicing and distance inequalities, were obtained by Koldobsky, Paouris, and
Zvavitch[20]. Extensions to the case where the measures for sections

For Lebesgue measure, the isomorphic Busemann–Petty problem is directly
connected with Bourgain’s slicing problem. The formulation through central
hyperplane sections appears in[4, Section 2, especially Lemma 2 and the following remark],
while the comparison relevant here is due to Milman and Pajor[22, Section 5]. In particular, the isomorphic Busemann–Petty
constant for volume is controlled, up to an absolute factor, by the maximal
isotropic constant in the corresponding dimension. The assertion that the
isotropic constants of convex bodies are uniformly bounded in all dimensions
is an equivalent formulation of Bourgain’s slicing problem; see[22, Section 5]and[16, Section 1]. Following Chen’s
breakthrough[7, Theorem 1], the polylogarithmic estimate of
Klartag and Lehec[15, Theorem 1.1], and Guan’s bound[12, Theorem 1.1], Klartag and Lehec established this
dimension-free bound in[16, Theorem 1.2]; see also[2]for an alternative proof. Consequently, the
isomorphic Busemann–Petty constant for volume is bounded by an absolute
constant.

The related one-body slicing inequality for arbitrary measures
has a different dimensional behavior. Koldobsky proved its generalO​(n)O(\sqrt{n})upper bound[18, Theorem 1]; see also[19]for estimates in terms of the outer volume ratio and a different proof
of then\sqrt{n}estimate in[6].
Klartag and Koldobsky constructed examples yielding the lower boundc​n/log⁡log⁡nc\sqrt{n/\log\log n}[13, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]. Klartag and Livshyts subsequently
obtained the sharp lower boundc​nc\sqrt{n}by means of their random-rounding
construction[14, Theorem 1.1]. Thus, unlike the corresponding
problem for volume, the optimal constant in the slicing inequality for

## Remark 7.1.

The density in the sharp example cannot be log-concave. Indeed, every
log-concave density is, in particular,(−1/n)(-1/n)-concave. Therefore[21, Theorem 4](see also[26]and a more general inequality in[25]) bounds the
isomorphic Busemann–Petty constant for
even log-concave densities, up to an absolute factor, by the maximal
isotropic constant in dimensionnn. The dimension-free estimate for
isotropic constants proved in[16, Theorem 1.2]makes this restricted
isomorphic constant absolute. This is incompatible, for largenn, with
the ratio of ordern\sqrt{n}obtained here. We also note that, the two principal components offfhave deliberately separated supports.
The small Gaussian term in the proof of
Proposition4.3is used only to enforce strict
positivity.
