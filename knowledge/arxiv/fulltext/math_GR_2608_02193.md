## Abstract

This paper completely classifies when the pro-$p$ completion of an amalgamated product has bounded generation, assuming its starting vertex groups are already boundedly generated. We first prove that the pro-$p$ completion of any abstract amalgam always naturally forms a proper pro-$p$ amalgam. Within this framework, we show that bounded generation is extremely rare. Specifically, the completed group is boundedly generated if and only if one of the vertex groups completely collapses into the edge group (meaning it has an index of 1), or in the highly specific case where $p=2$ and both vertex groups have an index of exactly 2 over the edge intersection.Additionally, we develop a new set of criteria to determine when the original vertex and edge groups map into the completion without collapsing. Finally, we provide a cautionary counterexample: for any given prime $p$, we construct an amalgam whose pro-$p$ completion is boundedly generated, but its full profinite completion is not. This demonstrates that possessing bounded generation at a single prime does not guarantee that the full profinite group will share this property.

## 1.Introduction

A profinite groupGGis said to beboundedly generatedif there are elementsg1,…,gr∈Gg_{1},\dots,g_{r}\in Gsuch thatG=⟨g1⟩¯​⋯​⟨gr⟩¯.G=\overline{\langle g_{1}\rangle}\cdots\overline{\langle g_{r}\rangle}.

For an abstract group the analogous condition asks for a product of finitely many cyclic subgroups. Bounded generation occurs naturally in the structure theory of arithmetic groups, the congruence subgroup problem, and the study of finite quotients of linear groups; see, for example, Carter–Keller[2], Tavgen[13], Morris[6], Morgan–Rapinchuk–Sury[5], and Corvaja–Rapinchuk–Ren–Zannier[3]. In contrast with many higher-rank arithmetic examples, bounded generation is delicate in the presence of free products and amalgamations.

Ribes and Zalesskii asked for verifiable conditions ensuring bounded generation of the pro-ppcompletion of an abstract amalgamated productΓ=H1∗H0H2.\Gamma=H_{1}*_{H_{0}}H_{2}.

This is Question 9.5.6 in the second edition ofProfinite Groups[12, p. 391]. The present paper gives a fixed-prime classification within the boundedly generated-vertex class.

The neighboring full profinite problem, Question 9.5.5, is different: by Lemma2.3, it requires one bounded-width certificate valid simultaneously in all finite quotients. It enters here only as a point of comparison and through a fixed-prime separation example; it is not a second main problem of the paper.

The proper branch begins with the pro-22double-index-two case, in which the quotient by the edge isC2​∐C2≅ℤ2⋊C2.C_{2}\mathop{\amalg}C_{2}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rtimes C_{2}.

This is the unique nondegenerate positive profile in the proper pro-ppclassification. The main point of the paper is that abstract amalgams with boundedly generated vertex groups can be treated without first solving the intrinsic entrance problem: after passing to the actual closed vertex images in the pro-ppcompletion, every collapse is absorbed into a canonical proper residual amalgam. The finite-quotient criterion then detects all residual profiles outside dense folding and the pro-22double-index-two case.

A central obstacle is properness. IfG1​∐AG2G_{1}\mathop{\amalg}_{A}G_{2}denotes an amalgamated free profinite product, the canonical mapsGi→G1​∐AG2G_{i}\to G_{1}\mathop{\amalg}_{A}G_{2}are not automatically injective. Ribes emphasizes this point in the context of profinite Bass–Serre theory: properness is the condition that makes the vertex groups genuinely visible inside the profinite fundamental group[11, Chapter 6 and Section 15.1]. Ribes–Zalesskii make the same difficulty visible already in the theory of pro-𝒞\mathcal{C}topologies: the topology induced on a subgroup need not be its full pro-𝒞\mathcal{C}topology, and completion preserves inclusions precisely when the induced topology is the full one[12, Lemmas 3.1.4 and 3.2.6]. Thus, in this paper the main problem is divided into three layers:
- (i)

prove bounded generation once the relevant completion is a proper pro-ppamalgam;
- (ii)

give checkable criteria ensuring exact pro-ppentrance and properness when one wants to identify the completion with the intrinsic vertex completions;
- (iii)

remove the need for exact entrance in the bounded-generation test by passing to the closed residual vertex images insideΓ^p\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}and applying a finite bad-envelope criterion.

Within the boundedly generated-vertex scope of this paper, the third layer is the point at which the bounded-generation component of Question 9.5.6 becomes independent of a general intrinsic separability theorem.

It is important to distinguish two problems which are easily conflated. The first is the bounded-generation problem: decide whetherΓ^p\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}is boundedly generated. The second is the intrinsic entrance problem: decide, using only the original embeddingsK↪HiK\hookrightarrow H_{i}, whether the intrinsic completionsK^p\widehat{K}_{p}andHi^p\widehat{H_{i}}_{p}enterΓ^p\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}without kernel collapse, induced-topology loss, or extra intersection. The latter problem is a genuine separability problem in the sense of Ribes–Zalesskii’s pro-𝒞\mathcal{C}topology. It was a natural first route to Question 9.5.6, but it is not necessary for the boundedly generated-vertex residual criterion proved here. The residual properization theorem below bypasses it by replacing the intrinsic completions with the actual closed vertex imagesVi≤Γ^pV_{i}\leq\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}and the actual residual edgeV1∩V2V_{1}\cap V_{2}.

The intrinsic entrance problem arose naturally from the strategy of forcing the abstract amalgam into the proper pro-ppBass–Serre model. The residual properization theorem shows that this strategy is not necessary for the bounded-generation classification under the boundedly generated-vertex hypothesis: the test can be made directly on the residual vertex images insideΓ^p\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}. The unrestricted intrinsic separability problem remains a separate problem in profinite Bass–Serre theory.

## 6.Properness-entry criteria

The preceding section shows that the bounded-generation analysis is essentially complete once the pro-ppcompletion has entered the proper Bass–Serre framework. This section is devoted to the separate entrance problem: how to verify that an abstract amalgam actually enters that framework. The result is a criterion package with five levels:
- (i)

the sharppp-embeddedness criterion, which is equivalent to exact pro-ppentrance for the natural completion maps;
- (ii)

thepp-virtual retract criterion, which is often the quickest way to verify vertexpp-embeddedness;
- (iii)

nilpotent and nilpotent-by-ppvertex-group criteria, where the edge inclusionK↪HiK\hookrightarrow H_{i}is automatic;
- (iv)

cofinal strong finitepp-envelope systems, which turn the properness question into finitepp-group envelope checks;
- (v)

the Higman–Aschenbrenner–Friedl finite-amalgam criteria, which turn those finite envelope checks into compatible centralpp-filtration tests, and in the normal finitepp-extension case into relative lower exponent-ppseries tests.

Thus properness is not ignored in the paper. It is treated by several verifiable criteria, while a fully general separability/properness theorem for arbitrary abstract amalgams is left outside the scope. The key notion behind the package is that an inclusion of abstract groups should induce the full pro-pptopology on the subgroup.

## 8.The full profinite limitation

The pro-ppclassification is prime-specific. For the full profinite completion, bounded generation is equivalent to the existence of one fixed finite family of elements whose cyclic images multiply to every finite quotient, by Lemma2.3. Separate bounded-generation statements for the groupsΓ^p\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}do not supply such a uniform family across all primes and all finite quotients.

This is the precise point at which fixed-prime information becomes insufficient. The residual profile theorem controls finitepp-quotients for one prime at a time, whereas a full profinite criterion would have to impose one uniform bounded-width condition across all finite quotient profiles.

The arithmetic construction in TheoremTheorem 3makes the separation concrete. For every primeppit gives an amalgamΓ=H∗KH\Gamma=H*_{K}Hsuch thatΓ^p≅H^pis boundedly generated,Γ^is not boundedly generated.\widehat{\Gamma}_{p}\cong\widehat{H}_{p}\quad\text{is boundedly generated,}\qquad\widehat{\Gamma}\quad\text{is not boundedly generated.}

Thus bounded generation of the pro-ppcompletion at one prescribed prime does not imply bounded generation of the full profinite completion. The construction does not assert the existence of a single group whose pro-qqcompletions are boundedly generated for every primeqqwhile its full profinite completion is not boundedly generated. What the full problem requires is the single uniform finite-quotient certificate described in Lemma2.3. This is the additional difficulty in the neighboring Question 9.5.5.

## 9.The boundedly generated-vertex fixed-prime classification and the full profinite boundary

Fix a primepp. Within the class of abstract amalgams with boundedly generated vertex groups, TheoremTheorem 2gives the paper’s residual-profile classification, equivalently its finite-pp-quotient-envelope classification, relevant to the pro-ppbounded-generation form of Ribes–Zalesskii Question 9.5.6. Its detailed proof is given by the residual profile dichotomy and the finite bad-envelope criterion in Theorems4.13and4.12.

The proper pro-ppclassification in TheoremTheorem 1, proved in Theorem3.6, is the structural input for the nondegenerate branch. TheoremTheorem 3realizes the other positive mechanism: residual dense folding in a genuinely non-proper arithmetic family. Together these results identify both positive mechanisms appearing in TheoremTheorem 2.

The intrinsic entrance and separability problem is separate and stronger. Sections6and7study when the original embeddingsK↪HiK\hookrightarrow H_{i}allow the intrinsic completions to enter without kernel collapse, induced-topology loss, or extra intersection. Within the boundedly generated-vertex, fixed-prime scope above, the tools developed there—pp-embeddedness,pp-ECEP, finite-amalgam filtration criteria, and joint actions—are not additional bounded-generation cases left open by the residual classification: the unconditional residual properization absorbs every such entrance failure into the actual residual edgeV1∩V2V_{1}\cap V_{2}. No classification outside that vertex class is asserted.

The full profinite boundary is genuinely different. Full profinite bounded generation requires uniform finite-quotient control, whereas TheoremTheorem 2fixes one prime. The arithmetic separation theorem shows that a boundedly generated pro-ppcompletion may coexist with a non-boundedly generated full profinite completion. Accordingly, no solution of Question 9.5.5 is claimed here: the paper isolates the additional uniformity that any such solution would have to supply.
