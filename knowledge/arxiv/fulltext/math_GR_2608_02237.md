## Abstract

A group word $w$ is said to be strongly concise in a class $\mathcal{C}$ of profinite groups if, for every group $G \in \mathcal{C}$ such that $w$ takes less than $2^{\aleph_0}$ values in $G$, the verbal subgroup $w(G)$ is finite. Using the notion of polynomial mappings introduced by Passi, we establish that every group word is strongly concise in the class of profinite metabelian groups. With this new approach, we also give an alternative proof for the fact that every group word is strongly concise in the class of profinite nilpotent groups.

## 1.Introduction

Agroup wordwwinkkvariables is an element of the free groupFkF_{k}, and substituting variables for elements of a given groupGG, defines a mapw:Gk→Gw\colon G^{k}\rightarrow G. Verbal problems study the relation between the image of the map, the so-called set ofww-valuesw​{G}={w​(g1,…,gk)∣gi∈G},w\{G\}=\{w(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\mid g_{i}\in G\}\,,

and theverbal subgroupw​(G)w(G)ofGGgenerated byw​{G}w\{G\}. (In the context of topological groups,w​(G)w(G)denotes theclosedsubgroup generated byw​{G}w\{G\}.)

The wordwwis said to beconcisein the class of groups𝒞\mathcal{C}, if for everyGGin𝒞\mathcal{C},w​(G)w(G)is finite wheneverw​{G}w\{G\}is finite. For instance, a classical theorem of Schur[21]says that the commutator wordγ2​(x,y)=[x,y]\gamma_{2}(x,y)=[x,y]is concise in the class of all groups, and many other families of words are known to be concise. This conclusion fails for words in general: there are words and groups wherew​{G}w\{G\}is finite, but, nevertheless,w​(G)w(G)is infinite, see[15].

The property of conciseness has been generalised in several directions, we refer to[2,3,8,27]. One such generalisation is that ofstrong concisenessin the setting of profinite groups, introduced in[5]and subsequently extended in[4,6,10,11,16]. A wordwwis strongly concise in a class of profinite groups𝒞\mathcal{C}, if for everyGGin𝒞\mathcal{C},w​(G)w(G)is finite whenever|w​{G}|<2ℵ0|w\{G\}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}. As a strengthened version of the classical conjecture that every word is concise in the class of profinite groups, see[22], it is conjectured that every word is actually strongly concise in the class of profinite groups, see[4].

The so-calledstrong conciseness conjecturehas been confirmed for the classes of virtually nilpotent profinite groups[7,4], analytic groups over general pro-ppdomains[26], or linear profinite groups[11]. In most of these cases, one shows that|w​{G}|<2ℵ0|w\{G\}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}already forcesw​{G}w\{G\}to be finite, so the result follows from the known conciseness of linear groups[18], general analytic groups[26], or virtually abelian-by-nilpotent groups[25]. Notwithstanding, there is yet another class ofabstractgroups where every word is concise, but the strong version of the problem is still open: by a theorem of Turner-Smith[25], every word is concise in an abelian-by-nilpotent group, but strong conciseness is unknown for their profinite counterparts, compare with[11, Question 1.7].

In this manuscript, we present the following case:

## Theorem 1.1.

Every word is strongly concise in the class of profinite metabelian
groups.

Although it is straightforward that every word is strongly concise in the class of abelian groups (see Lemma2.1), strong conciseness does not behave well under group extensions, and the considerations that enter into its proof are new. Indeed, the main idea is proving that when a word map between profinite spaces is polynomial in the sense of Passi (see Section4for the precise definition), then its image is either finite or at least continuum sized, see Proposition4.1.

This approach differs from the previous study of word problems in the class of abelian-by-nilpotent groups (e.g.[23]or[11, Theorem 1.4]), which rely heavily on the fact that for a finitely generated nilpotent groupNN, the group ringℤ​[N]\mathbb{Z}[N]is Noetherian. This framework, however, is not available in general.

It can be easily concluded from[17]that word maps in nilpotent groups are polynomial, which, as a by-product, gives an alternative proof for the strong conciseness of words in the class of nilpotent profinite groups, due to Detomi, Klopsch and Shumyatsky.

## Theorem 1.2([4, Theorem 1.2]).

Every word is strongly concise in the class of profinite nilpotent groups.

However, for metabelian groups, not all word maps are polynomial, as we show in Example4.3, but they are for commutator words whose image is central, see Theorem4.4. This is enough for proving Theorem1.1.
