## Abstract

The Dittert conjecture states that the Dittert functional on nonnegative $n\times n$ matrices whose entries sum to $n$ is uniquely maximized by the uniform matrix. We prove the conjecture in dimension $4$. More precisely, let $K_4$ be the simplex of nonnegative $4\times4$ real matrices whose entries sum to $4$, let $U_4$ be the uniform matrix, and let $φ$ denote the Dittert functional. We establish $\frac{61}{32}-φ(A)\geq \frac{1}{52}\lVert A-U_4\rVert_F^2$ for every $A\in K_4$. Consequently, $U_4$ is the unique maximizer of $φ$ on $K_4$. The proof reduces to certifying the nonnegativity of a structured quartic polynomial in sixteen variables on a simplex. We construct an exact rational constrained sum-of-squares certificate using an agent-guided symbolic-numeric procedure that combines template selection with sequential rational recovery. The main SOS consists of $152$ positively weighted rational squares, while each of the $136$ smaller SOS blocks consists of $16$ such squares. Exact $LDL^{\mathsf{T}}$ decompositions certify positivity, and exact coefficient comparison over $\mathbb{Q}$ verifies the complete polynomial identity. The resulting exact certificate is formally verified using the Lean proof assistant.

## 1Introduction

The permanent is a classical matrix invariant in linear algebra and
combinatorics. Its extremal behavior on nonnegative matrices includes the
van der Waerden theorem for doubly stochastic matrices. The Dittert
conjecture asks for an analogous extremal characterization on the larger
simplex obtained by fixing only the sum of all matrix entries: it asserts that
the uniform matrix uniquely maximizes a functional formed from the products
of the row sums, the products of the column sums, and the permanent.

The conjecture was recorded as Conjecture 28 in Minc’s survey[10]; Hajek proposed a related generalized permanent inequality
motivated by a multiaccess communication problem[5,1].
The casesn=2n=2andn=3n=3were proved by Sinkhorn and Hwang,
respectively[15,6,7]. Cheon and Wanless obtained
further restrictions on possible maximizers[1]. More recently, Udayan and Somasundaram proved
that every maximizing
matrix in the four-dimensional Dittert problem is fully indecomposable[17].
Pang presented a proof of the conjecture
forn≥17n\geq 17in a recent arXiv preprint[12]. In this paper, we prove the complete
dimension-44statement.

For the statements below, letK4K_{4}denote the simplex of nonnegative4×44\times 4matrices whose entries sum to44.
ForA∈K4A\in K_{4}, letri​(A)r_{i}(A)andcj​(A)c_{j}(A)denote its row and column

whereper⁡(A)\operatorname{per}(A)denotes the permanent ofAA. We writeD​HnDH_{n}for the Dittert conjecture in dimensionnn. Our first main
result is the following.

Moreover,ϕ​(A)=6132⟺A=U4.\phi(A)=\frac{61}{32}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A=U_{4}.

The proof of Theorem1.2reduces to establishing
the nonnegativity of a structured
quartic polynomial in sixteen variables on a simplex,
which has affine dimension1515. Semidefinite programming provides an effective
numerical approach to this problem. However, the resulting SOS
certificate is only approximate, owing both to round-off errors in its
floating-point coefficients and to finite-precision errors in the SDP
solver, and therefore does not by itself constitute an exact proof.

Building on the symbolic–numeric exact SOS recovery methods of[14,8], we use numerical
semidefinite optimization to obtain numerical SOS representations
and exact rational recovery to reconstruct a certificate. However, the present setting
differs from the standard recovery problem in two essential respects. The
equality point corresponding toU4U_{4}forces every Gram matrix appearing in the semidefinite programming problem to be
singular, and the constrained certificate couples many Gram blocks through
a polynomial multiplier of a linear equality. Consequently, the existing
recovery techniques are not directly applicable in their standard form.

To overcome these difficulties, we develop an agent-guided procedure for
constructing the certificate. The agent first searches a prescribed
hierarchy of constrained SOS templates and identifies a numerically feasible
one. It then controls a sequential exact recovery procedure, that is, the smaller Gram blocks
are recovered and fixed first, after which the reduced main matrix is
corrected so that it remains positive definite and the remaining residual is
an exact multiple of the simplex equality. The main SOS consists of152152positively weighted rational squares, and each of the136136small SOS
blocks consists of1616such squares. All positivity claims are certified
by exactL​D​L𝖳LDL^{\mathsf{T}}decompositions, and the complete polynomial
identity is verified independently overℚ\mathbb{Q}and subsequently
formalized in Lean. Thus the floating-point SDP computation is used only to
discover candidate certificate data.

## 4Agent-Guided Construction of an Exact Constrained SOS Certificate

Symbolic–numeric methods can recover exact rational SOS decompositions from
numerical Gram representations; see[14,8]. The present problem faces
two difficulties. First, equality atU4U_{4}forces every Gram matrix in
the desired certificate to be singular, so the standard rational recovery methods must be adapted to handle singular positive semidefinite Gram matrices. Second, the certificate couples
many Gram blocks with a free polynomial multiplier of the linear equalityh=0h=0. The blocks cannot in general be rationalized independently, since
changing one collection of blocks alters the exact coefficient equations for
the remaining ones.

We therefore use an agent-guided procedure with two stages. The agent

prescribed hierarchy.

recomputes the numerical main block. Once both conditions hold, exact

## Remark 5.1.

The numerical SDP solver, the agent-guided search procedure, and the
symbolic recovery code described in
Section4are not included in the trusted
computing base. They are used only to generate rational certificate data,
whose correctness is re-checked within Lean.

The formal verification relies on Lean’s evaluation mechanisms for the
finite certificate checks and polynomial normalization. These mechanisms,
including the use ofnative_decideandimplemented_by, are included in the trusted computing base.
