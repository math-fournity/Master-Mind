## Abstract.

We prove that every holomorphic differential form of non-top degree
on a locally symmetric variety with Baily-Borel boundary of codimension>1>1is square-integrable with respect to the invariant metric.
This generalizes a theorem of Weissauer in the Siegel modular case.

## 1.Introduction

Let𝒟\mathcal{D}be a Hermitian symmetric domain andΓ\Gammabe an arithmetic subgroup ofAut​(𝒟){\rm Aut}(\mathcal{D}),
which we may assume to be neat without loss of generality for our purpose.
The quotientX=𝒟/ΓX=\mathcal{D}/\Gammahas the structure of a smooth quasi-projective variety.
We assume that the boundary of the Baily-Borel compactification ofXXhas codimension>1>1.
The invariant Hermitian metric on𝒟\mathcal{D}induces
a natural complete Kähler metric onXX.
In this note we prove the following.

## Theorem 1.1.

Every holomorphic differential form of degree<dimX<\dim XonXXis square-integrable with respect to the invariant metric.

In the case of Siegel modular varieties,
this was proved by Weissauer ([10]Satz 7)
as a consequence of his classification of square-integrable Siegel modular forms.
In other (higher-dimensional) cases, Theorem1.1seems to be new.

Experience shows, at least in typical cases, that
holomorphic differential forms of non-top degree correspond to
vector-valued modular forms whose weight is so “small” that
the automorphic representations they generate are no longer holomorphic discrete series.
Theorem1.1can be interpreted as saying that
modular forms of such a small weight (of specific type) are always square-integrable.
It ensures that the theory of discrete automorphic representations can be used to
fully capture holomorphic differential forms.
By contrast, in the case of top degree,
square-integrability is equivalent to cuspidality (Remark2.4),
and canonical forms with this property generate holomorphic discrete series.

The proof of Theorem1.1does not involve estimate of theL2L^{2}-norm, however.
Instead, by making use of the mixed Hodge structure and theL2L^{2}-cohomology ofXX,
Theorem1.1is derived as a simple consequence of the Pommerening extension theorem[6].

I would like to thank Shuji Horinaga for asking this question in the orthogonal case.

## Remark 2.3.

The above argument shows that
Theorem1.1is actually equivalent to the Pommerening extension theorem.
In the Siegel modular case, this means that
Weissauer’s square-integrability theorem ([10]Satz 7)
is equivalent to the extension theorem of Freitag and Pommerening[1].

## Remark 2.4.

In the casek=nk=n,
Lemma2.2says that we haveH(2)0​(KX)=Fn​Wn​Hn​(X)=H0​(KX¯)H^{0}_{(2)}(K_{X})=F^{n}W_{n}H^{n}(X)=H^{0}(K_{\overline{X}})

we recover the well-known fact that
a canonical form (or a modular form of canonical weight) onXXis
square-integrable if and only if it is a cusp form.
Since the canonical weight belongs to the discrete series,
