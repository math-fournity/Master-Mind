## Abstract

A two-level analytical framework is presented for modeling random walk transport of messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) molecules along neuronal microtubules from soma to synapses. Motivated by empirical observations of mRNA cargo motion, the transport within a dendrite is modeled by a persistent telegraph process with pauses. Theoretical expressions for the probability of traversing the dendrite and the mean time for such travel are derived for different and equal probabilities of persistence. These results are used for the construction of a semi-Markov model of motion of mRNA cargo within the whole neuron. The semi-Markov model provides the probabilities of absorption at a given synapse and corresponding mean first-passage times (MFPTs) from the soma, where mRNA is transcribed. The theoretical expressions, together with experimentally obtained parameter values, are used to calculate MFPTs for neurons with empirically reconstructed morphology. The model predicts that when retrograde persistence is stronger, the MFPT to each synapse is effectively the same. Otherwise, when the persistence is more pronounced in the anterograde direction, the transport in the neuron resembles the motion along a single dendrite -- nearly linear dependence of MFPT on the distance between soma and synapse. These findings are theoretically justified when the lengths of dendrites are considerably longer than the distance traversed during a typical run.

MRNA trafficking along microtubules is major mechanism for synaptic plasticity and memory formation[40,31,13,25,11,16], by delivering code for production of proteins at specific locations, known as mRNA localization[24,10]. The localization is needed to assure that activation is enabled only in presence of certain triggers. Experimental studies of mRNA transport along microtubules in the last few decades have uncovered its random nature that differs from pure diffusion[27,28,21,18,12]. A recent well documented study of the random transport of mRNA along dendritic microtubules[39]made substantial progress by identifying the following features: 1. The cargo can move in either direction for random, nearly exponentially distributed period (running phase) with speed that is approximately constant; 2. After each run there is a phase of resting which has also exponentially distributed duration; 3. After each pause, the cargo can move in the same direction as previously with probability slightly exceeding 0.5 – there is some persistence. Despite the wealth of experimental observations on the random motion of mRNA cargo, a theoretical model that incorporates them into a framework for studying the long-range transport from soma to synapses is still lacking.

In this Letter, we develop an experimentally motivated two-level analytical framework, that bridges microscopic cargo dynamics and long-range transport in dendritic trees. At the microscopic scale, a Persistent Telegraph Process with Pauses (PTPP)[33,36,8,34]is used to model the motion within single dendrite. The model extends the classical Continuous-Time Random Walk (CTRW) framework[35]by incorporating persistence[20,41]and four internal states[29,22,42,44]. For simplicity, once the mRNA cargo reaches the end of a dendrite, the framework assumes that each outgoing dendrite is chosen with equal probability. At the whole neuron-tree-level, transport is described by a coarse-grained semi-Markov model. The resulting traversal and return probabilities for mRNA cargo along each dendrite determine the transition probabilities in the respective semi-Markov chain. The corresponding conditional Mean First-Passage Times (MFPTs) define the semi-Markov sojourn times. The states in the semi-Markov chain are identified with the soma, branching nodes and synapses, while dendrites represent the links in the respective dendritic tree network. The transition probabilities determine the matrix of the embedded Markov chain, while the sojourn times which depend on the pair of nodes (start and end), makes a need for the most general form of the memoryless processes – semi-Markov process[38,30]. The model predicts two distinct regimes of asymptotic behavior for sufficiently long dendrites. In the first one, when the cargo prefers retrograde direction, the transport time is nearly equal to all synapses, regardless on theis distance from the soma. In the opposite case, if anterograde motion is more pronounced, the transport to synapses seems like happening on single dendrite instead of a tree. These findings are found to be present in different neurons which morphology was taken from the public repository NeuroMorpho.org[3].

Conclusions–We developed a two-level analytical framework for studying mRNA transport along microtubules throughout neuronal dendritic trees. At the microscopic level, PTPP provides a natural description of experimentally observed run-and-pause dynamics of mRNA cargo along individual dendrites. At the whole-neuron level, the mRNA transport is represented as a semi-Markov random walk on the dendritic tree, where dendrites constitute the network links. One key finding related to the motion along a single dendrite is that stronger persistence does not necessarily imply faster transport. The PTPP model predicts faster transport, on average, in the direction with smaller persistence, for certain parameter values. Another important finding is the existence of two distinct regimes for the end-to-end transport of mRNA cargo for dendritic lengths typically obtained in experiments. When the anterograde persistence is stronger, transport through the whole neuron tree resembles the transport along a single dendrite. Conversely, when the retrograde direction is favored, the conditional MFPT from the soma to a synapse becomes nearly independent of the distance between them.

The framework can be additionally refined through additional experimental data and further modification of the proposed model. Possible refinements could be related to the neuronal geometry, or to the influence of molecular regulators on mobility of the cargo. The model we have developed disregards that mRNA is subject to degradation, which can be quantified by its half-life[37]. It would be thus interesting to compare the average transport time of mRNA to synaptic regions with its half-life.

Although motivated by intracellular mRNA transport, the presented framework applies to a much broader class of stochastic transport problems on filamentous and network-like structures. Cellular transport of other molecules and organelles[39], protein sliding on DNA[7], transport of molecules in channels and pores[5]are examples where the proposed framework may prove useful.
