## Abstract

We show that there exists a constant $C > 0$ such that for any $n \geq 1$ and any convex body $K \subset \mathbf{R}^n$, \[ 1 \leq \inf_{T \in \mathrm{GL}(n)} \, \frac{M^\ast(TK)}{\mathrm{vr}(TK)}   \leq C\sqrt{\log(\mathrm{e} n)}, \] where $M^\ast$ denotes the spherical mean width and $\mathrm{vr}(\cdot)$ denotes the volume radius. The righthand side is attained, up to universal constants, by the crosspolytope and the regular $n$-simplex. Analogously, we show that, up to universal constants, the logarithm of the Euclidean covering number is maximized over convex bodies $K \subset \mathbf{R}^n$ by the simplex and crosspolytope. Our proof makes use of Eldan's stochastic localization.

## 1.1.History and additional remarks

## 1.1.1.Covering numbers

For symmetric convex bodies, covering numbers are precisely the geometric counterpart of entropy numbers of operators between Banach spaces, which are fundamental quantities in operator and approximation theory; see[27,12]and the references therein. For instance, Schütt’s result (2) is a direct translation from the original formulation for entropy numbers.

A breakthrough by V. Milman[23]was the realization that by fixing the Euclidean structure in dimensionnn, the entropy numbers for all convex bodies are the same. Milman’s M-ellipsoid theorem asserts that for every symmetric convex bodyK⊂𝐑nK\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}there exists an ellipsoidEEof equal volume, withlog⁡N​(K,E)≤c​n\log{N(K,E)}\leq cn[23]. This also extends to the non-symmetric case; see[24].Theorem1.1, withr=vr⁡(B1n)r=\operatorname{vr}(B_{1}^{n}), corresponds to V. Milman’s relation:i.e.,the position used in our result is an M-position (in the sense of V. Milman).

Milman’s position is a central tool in the local theory of Banach spaces and in asymptotic convex geometry. Among its principal consequences are Milman’s reverse Brunn–Minkowski inequality and the Bourgain–Milman “reverse Santaló” inequality[11]. Pisier considerably strengthened Milman’s theorem by proving the existence of regular ellipsoids. More precisely, for everyα∈(0,2)\alpha\in(0,2)and every symmetric convex bodyKK, there is an ellipsoidEαE_{\alpha}such that(7)max⁡{log⁡N​(K,t​Eα),log⁡N​(K∘,t​Eα∘)}≤Cα​ntα,t>0.\max\left\{\log{N(K,tE_{\alpha})},\log{N(K^{\circ},tE_{\alpha}^{\circ})}\right\}\leq C_{\alpha}\frac{n}{t^{\alpha}},\ t>0.

See[30, Chapter 7, Theorem 7.13]. This is known as Pisier’sα\alpha-regular M-position. In the above setting, asα\alphatends to22,CαC_{\alpha}tends to infinity. Thus,Theorem1.1introduces the necessary logarithmic correction inttwhen only one of the two covering numbers is considered. The non-symmetric analogue of (7) is still an open problem[38,9].

Artstein, Milman, and Szarek settled the Hilbertian case of Pietsch’s long-standing duality of entropy conjecture[1, Theorem 1]. Their result implies that whenKKis symmetric andEEis an ellipsoid, the covering numbers satisfylog⁡N​(K,E)≃log⁡N​(E∘,c​K∘)\log{N(K,E)}\simeq\log{N(E^{\circ},cK^{\circ})}. Hence,Theorem1.1has an immediate dual counterpart.

Finally, we mention K. Ball’s observation[5]that, assuming the hyperplane conjecture, the isotropic position of a convex body is an M-position. The recent breakthrough paper of Bizeul–Klartag[9]confirms that isotropic position is, up to polylogarithmic factors, a22-regular Pisier M-position.

## 1.1.2.Mean width of convex bodies

The mean width of a convex body is a fundamental geometric parameter with applications in asymptotic convex geometry, the local theory of Banach spaces, the geometry of numbers, high-dimensional probability, random matrix theory, empirical-process theory, compressed sensing, high-dimensional statistics, and convex inverse problems; see, for example,[3,37,31].

In probability theory, it frequently appears in its Gaussian normalization: the Gaussian mean width of a bounded setKKisw​(K)=𝐄​supx∈K⟨G,x⟩=𝐄​hK​(G)w(K)=\mathbf{E}\sup_{x\in K}\langle G,x\rangle=\mathbf{E}h_{K}(G). Note thatn​M∗​(K)≃w​(K)\sqrt{n}\,M^{\ast}(K)\simeq w(K).
Hence, these parameters are equivalent to the supremum of a canonical Gaussian process. Beginning with the work of Dudley, Sudakov, and Fernique, a close connection between expected suprema of processes and the metric properties (in particular, the metric entropy) of the index set was established. This culminated in Talagrand’s majorizing measures theorem[35,36]: the Gaussian width is entirely determined, up to universal constants, by the metric structure of the process.

A parallel development took place in the local theory of Banach spaces, initiated primarily by Maurey and Pisier. For a Banach spaceXX, itsKK-convexity constant is the normK​(X)=‖RadX‖K(X)=\|{\rm{Rad}}_{X}\|of the Rademacher projection (see the survey[22]). Pisier’s fundamental theorem (see[29]) states that boundedKK-convexity is equivalent to the absence of uniformly embedded copies ofℓ1m\ell_{1}^{m}. Figiel and Tomczak–Jaegermann introduced theℓ\ell-norm and established its fundamental connection with mean width andKK-convexity[16]. Their result implies that, for every symmetric convex bodyK⊂𝐑nK\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}, there existsT∈GL​(n)T\in\mathrm{GL}(n)such that(8)M​(T​K)​M∗​(T​K)≤C​K​(XK),M(TK)\,M^{\ast}(TK)\leq CK(X_{K}),

## 1.1.4.Positions of convex bodies

For the reverse isoperimetric inequalities mentioned above, one wants to find the linear transformation that minimizes the particular quantity associated with the convex body of interest. This leads to the notion of “positions” of convex bodies. As explained in[17], building on results dating back to F. John in 1948, these positions typically lead to “isotropic” conditions. In[17], the authors provide conditions for minimizing thekkth quermassintegral. Notably, weare notworking with these positions, and in fact, they can differ. The position we use was introduced by Bobkov[10]and was shown to be a Milman position. It is known thatTheorems1.1and1.3donothold in the minimal mean width position. Markessinis–Saroglou–Paouris[21]provide an example of a convex body that is in the minimal mean width position but is far from Milman’s position.

## 1.2.Ideas in the proof

We make use of an idea originating with Eldan–Lehec[15]and later presented in an optimized form by Bizeul–Klartag[9]. The idea is to use the stochastic localization process to obtain comparison theorems for isotropic log-concave random vectors.

Letψn\psi_{n}denote the KLS constant in dimensionnn. The main result in[9]shows that, by working with the uniform measure on the convex bodyK∘K^{\circ}, one obtains(9)n​M∗​(K)≤C​ψn​log⁡(e​n).\sqrt{n}\,M^{\ast}(K)\leq C\,\psi_{n}\,\sqrt{\log(\mathrm{e}n)}.
