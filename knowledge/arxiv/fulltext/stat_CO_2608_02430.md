## 1Introduction

Sampling from a probability densityπ∝e−V\pi\propto e^{-V}inℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}is a fundamental problem in a variety of fields.
A popular family of methods to produce these samples, known as MCMC algorithms, consists in designing a stochastic process which is ergodic with respect toπ\pi, and simulating it for a long enough time.
One of the most fundamental choices is theLangevin dynamics (LD): given a (possibly) random initializationμ∈𝒫2​(ℝd)\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)and a standarddd-dimensional Brownian motion(Bt)t(B_{t})_{t}, it is given by the solution of the SDEX0∼μ,d​Xt=−∇V​(Xt)​d​t+2​d​Bt.X_{0}\sim\mu,\quad dX_{t}=-\nabla V(X_{t})dt+\sqrt{2}dB_{t}.(1)

It is well-known that, under mild assumptions onVV, this stochastic process is ergodic with respect toπ\pi, making it a natural candidate for an MCMC algorithm (see[Pav14]). Its practical implementation, however, requires considering a numerical scheme for (1), such as the Euler–Maruyama method, which leads to theunadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA), described by the easily implemented recursionX^0∼μ,X^k+1=X^k−h​∇V​(X^k)+2​(B(k+1)​h−Bk​h),\hat{X}_{0}\sim\mu,\quad\hat{X}_{k+1}=\hat{X}_{k}-h\nabla V(\hat{X}_{k})+\sqrt{2}\left(B_{(k+1)h}-B_{kh}\right),(2)

for a small step-sizeh>0h>0.

Unfortunately, the above scheme is biased, which means that its limiting stationary distributionπ^\hat{\pi}is in general different fromπ\pi; this leads to a trade-off in the choice of the step-size, with smaller values ofhhleading to a smaller error in the bias, but to a higher required number of iterations of (2) in order to converge toπ^\hat{\pi}.

## Literature overview

Assumption1.1is classical in the study of the quantitative guarantees of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (see[Dal17a,DM17]and the book[Che26]for a more complete overview). Theoretical guarantees in Wasserstein distance under this assumption were first provided in[DM19,Dal17], which establish the boundW2​(π,π^)≲κ​h​dW_{2}(\pi,\hat{\pi})\lesssim\kappa\sqrt{hd}. This bound was later improved toW2​(π,π^)≲κ​h​dW_{2}(\pi,\hat{\pi})\lesssim\sqrt{\kappa hd}in[DMM19].
Linear dependence inhhfor the asymptotic bias was only established under additional assumptions on the third derivatives ofVV, but with a worse dependence on the dimension cf.[DM19,DK19]. Subsequently, the dimension dependence was improved, but only under even stronger assumptions on the third derivatives ofVV(see[LZT22,DE24,DK26]).
Besides Wasserstein distance, convergence guarantees have also been provided in other metrics, such as total variation, cf.[Dal17a,DM17], and relative entropy, see[VW23,Che+25], which also relaxes the log-concavity assumption to appropriate functional inequalities. Additionally, recent work[Che+26]showed under suitable sparsity assumptions that dimension-free guarantees (up to logarithmic factors) can be obtained, but this crucially relies on replacing the22-Wasserstein distance with anℓ∞\ell^{\infty}-version.
