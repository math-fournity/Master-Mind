## Abstract

For a Boolean communication matrix $M$, let $D(M)$ denote its deterministic communication complexity and let $r(M):={\mathrm{rank}}_{\mathbb{R}}(M)$. The log-rank conjecture asks whether $D(M)$ is polynomial in $\log r(M)$. The best known general upper bound, due to Sudakov and Tomon'25, is $D(M)=O(\sqrt{r(M)})$.   On the lower-bound side, G{ö}{ö}s, Pitassi, and Watson'18 constructed explicit matrices satisfying $D(M)=Ω((\log r(M))^2/(\log\log r(M))^2)$. We improve the lower bound to $D(M)=Ω((\log r(M))^2/\log\log r(M))$.   Our construction revisits their pointer function over its original non-Boolean alphabet and lifts it with an alphabet-valued Index gadget, via the multicolor simulation theorem stated by Roughgarden and Weinstein'16. Compared with the quantitatively explicit GPW bound, the alphabet-preserving lift removes one factor of $\log\log r$. We also give a self-contained proof of the multicolor simulation theorem in the parameter regime required by the construction.

Communication complexity asks how much two separated parties must communicate to evaluate a function of their joint input. ## Upper bounds

The central structural question is whether low rank forces a large monochromatic rectangle. Letα​(s)\alpha(s)be such that every rank-at-most-ssBoolean matrix contains a monochromatic rectangle occupying at least a2−α​(s)2^{-\alpha(s)}fraction of all entries. Nisan and Wigderson[20]showed how to convert such a rectangle theorem into a complete protocol; in a convenient dyadic form their reduction gives𝒞​(r)=O​((log⁡r)2+∑i=0⌈log⁡r⌉α​(⌈r/2i⌉)).\mathcal{C}(r)=O((\log r)^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log r\rceil}\alpha(\lceil r/2^{i}\rceil)).(1)

This reduction, rather than a new numerical record at the time, became the organizing principle for subsequent upper bounds.

Early progress came through graph-theoretic versions of the conjecture. Work of Kotlov and Lovász[13]controlled the number of distinct neighborhoods in a low-rank graph, and Kotlov[14]obtained the constant-factor improvement𝒞​(r)≤log⁡(4/3)​r+O​(1)\mathcal{C}(r)\leq\log(4/3)r+O(1), wherelog⁡(4/3)≈0.415\log(4/3)\approx 0.415. This remained the best unconditional general estimate before the first genuinely sublinear bound. An additive-combinatorial route was developed by Ben-Sasson, Lovett, and Ron-Zewi[6]: assuming the characteristic-two Polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture, they proved𝒞​(r)=O​(r/log⁡r)\mathcal{C}(r)=O(r/\log r). The required additive-combinatorial conjecture is now a theorem, following the resolution of Marton’s conjecture by Gowers, Green, Manners, and Tao[7]; the resulting bound is nevertheless superseded by the stronger unconditional results below.

A decisive change came from discrepancy. Building on the factorization-norm and discrepancy theory of Linial, Mendelson, Schechtman, and Shraibman[15], Lovett[16]proved that every rank-rrBoolean matrix has a monochromatic rectangle of relative area2−O​(r​log⁡r)2^{-O(\sqrt{r}\log r)}. Combining this with Eq. (1) yields𝒞​(r)=O​(r​log⁡r)\mathcal{C}(r)=O(\sqrt{r}\log r), the first unconditionalo​(r)o(r)bound.

The current record is due to Sudakov and Tomon[27]. If anm×nm\times nBoolean matrix has rank at mostrrand densityp≤1/2p\leq 1/2, they establish the sparsity-sensitive discrepancy estimatedisc⁡(M)≥c​m​n​min⁡{p,p/r}\operatorname{disc}(M)\geq cmn\min\{p,\sqrt{p}/\sqrt{r}\}. A density-decrement iteration then produces an all-zero or all-one submatrix with at leastm​2−O​(r)m2^{-O(\sqrt{r})}rows andn​2−O​(r)n2^{-O(\sqrt{r})}columns.

## Lower bounds

The trivial rank argument givesD​(M)≥log⁡r​(M)D(M)\geq\log r(M), and improving this separation has its own substantial history. The graph formulation asks how large the chromatic number of a graph can be compared with the real rank of its adjacency matrix. Alon and Seymour[1]first disproved the most optimistic rank-coloring inequality by a finite example. Razborov[22]then constructed an asymptotic family withχ(G)=Ω(rank(AG)4/3)\chi(G)=\Omega(\operatorname{rank}(A_{G})^{4/3}), which translates into only a constant-factor improvement over the elementary logarithmic communication lower bound. Raz and Spieker[24]obtained the first superlinear-in-log⁡r\log rseparation; in a rank-only statement, their construction givesD​(M)=Ω​(log⁡r​log⁡log⁡log⁡r)D(M)=\Omega(\log r\log\log\log r).

Nisan and Wigderson[20]gave an explicit family for whichD​(M)=Ω​((log⁡r)log2⁡3)D(M)=\Omega((\log r)^{\log_{2}3}), wherelog2⁡3≈1.585\log_{2}3\approx 1.585. They also recorded Kushilevitz’s unpublished six-variable base construction, which improves the exponent tolog3⁡6≈1.631\log_{3}6\approx 1.631.
O’Donnell et al.[21]subsequently realized this construction in the XOR/Fourier-sparsity setting. These examples are important evidence that any valid log-rank exponent must exceed one, but they remain far below the best upper bound.

Before the present work, the strongest general separation with an explicitly computed polyloglog loss was due to Göös, Pitassi, and Watson[10]. Their lifted pointer-function construction yields, for an explicit family,D​(M)=Ω​((log⁡r)2/(log⁡log⁡r)2).D(M)=\Omega({(\log r)^{2}}/{(\log\log r)^{2}}).(3)

The polylogarithmic loss is worth displaying: statements of the result are often abbreviated as a near-quadratic lower bound. Their work also produced a nearly quadratic separation between deterministic communication and one-sided rectangle partition number. A separation of the same near-quadratic order also follows from the construction of Balodis, Ben-David, Göös, Jain, and Kothari[3]based on unambiguous DNFs; Hambardzumyan, Lovett, and Shirley[11]record it, with the polyloglog factors suppressed, as the largest known separation. The polyloglog loss is not computed in[3]; in SectionAwe trace their chain as written and show that it yields explicit matrices satisfyingD​(M)=Ω​((log⁡r)2/(log⁡log⁡r)7)D(M)=\Omega((\log r)^{2}/(\log\log r)^{7}). Eq. (3) therefore remains the sharpest previously known bound.

Combining Eq. (2) and Eq. (3), the best previously known general bounds were thereforeΩ​((log⁡r)2/(log⁡log⁡r)2)≤𝒞​(r)≤O​(r).{\Omega}((\log r)^{2}/(\log\log r)^{2})\;\leq\;\mathcal{C}(r)\;\leq\;O(\sqrt{r}).

The lower bound is polylogarithmic inrr, while the upper bound is exponential in the log-rank. In particular, no unconditionalo​(r)o(\sqrt{r})upper bound is currently known, and no superpolynomial-in-log⁡r\log rlower bound is known.

## Comparison with the GPW parameters.

The error-correcting-code Booleanization used by GPW, followed by Boolean Index lifting, gives the denominator(log⁡log⁡r)2(\log\log r)^{2}in Eq. (3); see[10, Section 4].
