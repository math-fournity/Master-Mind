## Abstract

{We study the module structure of the polynomial ring, localized at the discriminant, over the ring of differential operators on the ring of invariants of the imprimitive complex reflection group $G(r,p,n)$, describing its simple components with explicit generators given by the higher Specht polynomials of Ariki--Terasoma--Yamada. The proof rests on a Jacobian lemma computing the discriminant of $G(r,p,n)$, combined with a double-centralizer argument. As particular cases ($r=2$, $p=2$ or $p=1$) we recover, and considerably shorten, the known decomposition theorems for the real reflection groups $W(D_n)$ and $W(B_n)$; we also treat $G(r,r,n)$ and $G(r,1,n)$ explicitly, with worked examples ($D_2$, $D_3$, $B_2$) and their central idempotents. Finally, applying the Galois descent equivalence of categories of Nonkané to $G(r,p,n)$ for the first time gives a second, generator-free description of the simple summands as twisted invariants.}

## 6Concluding remarks and perspectives

We have given a uniform construction of the simple𝒟~Y\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}-module summands of the polynomial ring
localized at the discriminant, for the entire family of imprimitive
complex reflection groupsG​(r,p,n)G(r,p,n), resting on two ingredients –
the Jacobian lemma of Subsection3.1and the
double-centralizer argument of Subsection3.2– that
replace the ad hoc, group-specific constructions used in earlier
treatments of special cases (symmetric groups, and our own starting
pointW​(Dn)W(D_{n})[9]). Specializing this single argument recovers, and
considerably shortens, the decomposition theorems for the real
reflection groupsW​(Dn)W(D_{n})andW​(Bn)W(B_{n}), and a Galois descent
equivalence of categories (Theorem3.14) provides a
second, generator-free description of the same summands. We close by
indicating some directions this leaves open.

## Exceptional complex reflection groups.

The Shephard–Todd
classification consists of the infinite familyG​(r,p,n)G(r,p,n)together
with3434exceptional groups. The double-centralizer argument of
Subsection3.2is entirely general and applies
verbatim to any complex reflection group; what is specific toG​(r,p,n)G(r,p,n)is the Jacobian lemma (Lemma3.1)
and the combinatorial substitute for higher Specht polynomials, both
tied to the monomial wreath-product structure. Obtaining analogous
discriminant formulas and explicit bases for the exceptional groups
would need to be developed case by case.

## Connection to rational Cherednik algebras.

The ring𝒟~Y\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}studied here is closely related to the
spherical subalgebra of the rational Cherednik algebraHc​(W)H_{c}(W)att≠0t\neq 0,c=0c=0: atc=0c=0the Dunkl operators ofHc​(W)H_{c}(W)degenerate
to ordinary partial derivatives, and the spherical subalgebrae​H0​(W)​eeH_{0}(W)erecovers (an algebraic avatar of) the invariant
differential operator ringD​(V)WD(V)^{W}, closely related to𝒟~Y\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}. Under this identification, our
decomposition theorem should correspond to the decomposition of the
polynomial representation ofH0​(W)H_{0}(W)into simple modules in category𝒪\mathcal{O}atc=0c=0. Making this correspondence precise, and
comparing our explicit generators to the standard modulesΔ0​(λ)\Delta_{0}(\lambda), would connect the present combinatorial
construction to the representation theory of Cherednik algebras –
and might in turn suggest how our construction should deform asccmoves away from0.

## Physical motivation revisited.

The original version of
this circle of ideas, forW​(Dn)W(D_{n}), was motivated by the rational
quantum Olshanetsky–Perelomov system; the present, purely algebraic
treatment does not use this structure. Reintroducing Dunkl–Opdam
operators for the whole familyG​(r,p,n)G(r,p,n)– and, in particular,
working out the associated integrable system forW​(Bn)W(B_{n}), now
treated on equal footing withW​(Dn)W(D_{n})in
Section5– is a natural next step.

## Completing the remaining explicit examples.

We gave fully
explicit generators for every simple summand in the casesD2D_{2},D3D_{3}, andG​(r,1,2)G(r,1,2); extending this toD4D_{4}, or to a general
closed-form recipe for the generators attached to shapes mixing both
components ofλ\lambda– rather than the case-by-case constructions
(the reflection representation and the gradient of the discriminant)
used in Subsection4.2– remains open.

## A categorical refinement.

Corollary3.21identifies the summands of𝒪~X\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X}with a
subcategory of𝒟~Y\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{Y}-modules trivialized by the
discriminant cover (Theorem3.14); it would be
interesting to know whether the equivalence of
