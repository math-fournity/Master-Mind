## Zusammenfassung.

In [Publ. Mat. 67 (2023), no. 1, 173–198], G. Navarro asked whether the character field of an odd-degree character of a finite group is generated by a single character value. We show by explicit examples that the answer is negative in general, for non-solvable as well as solvable groups.

## 1.Introduction

For a finite groupGGand a complex characterχ\chiofGGthecharacter fieldℚ​(χ)\mathbb{Q}(\chi)is the field extension ofℚ\mathbb{Q}generated by the values ofχ\chi:(1.1)ℚ​(χ)≔ℚ​({χ​(g)∣g∈G}).\mathbb{Q}(\chi)\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}(\{\chi(g)\mid g\in G\})\;.

Since character values are always sums of roots of unity inℂ\mathbb{C}, the character field will be contained in a cyclotomic fieldℚ​(ζn)\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}). Here,ζn\zeta_{n}denotes a primitivenn-th root of unity. Theconductorc​(χ)c(\chi)ofχ\chiis defined to be the smallestnnsuch thatℚ​(χ)\mathbb{Q}(\chi)is contained inℚ​(ζn)\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}). One says thatχ\chiis2-rationalifc​(χ)c(\chi)is odd.


In[undefi, Problem 7.3], G. Navarro asked the following question:

LetGGbe a finite group, and letχ∈Irr⁡(G)\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G). Ifχ​(1)\chi(1)is
odd orχ\chiis 2-rational, is it true that there isg∈Gg\in Gsuch thatℚ​(χ)=ℚ​(χ​(g))\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}(\chi(g))?

Navarro explains that this question arose in the context of[undefj, Conjecture]and explicitly says “Perhaps there is an easy explanation or a counterexample.” The question is also mentioned in the recent paper[undeff], adding “[…] as far as we know, this remains open”. The question is located in the landscape around Feit’s conjecture[undefe].


Below, I will provide explicit examples of odd-degree characters where the answer is negative. In fact, I have found many such examples, including non-solvable and solvable groups, and characters which are both odd and 2-rational. The question is still interesting nonetheless, and I will take the opportunity here to collect cases, where the question is already known to have a positive answer. This also makes clearer where to look for negative examples.

With OSCAR or GAP—seeAfor the full OSCAR code, GAP is similar—one computes that forχ=α⊠λ\chi=\alpha\boxtimes\lambdawe haveℚ​(χ)=ℚ​(ζ12)=ℚ​(−3,i)\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3},i), so(3.6)[ℚ(χ):ℚ]=4.[\mathbb{Q}(\chi)\colon\mathbb{Q}]=4\;.

But the inspection of the minimal polynomials of the character values shows that they have degree at most two, so(3.7)[ℚ(χ(g)):ℚ]≤2.[\mathbb{Q}(\chi(g))\colon\mathbb{Q}]\leq 2\;.

Hence,(3.8)ℚ​(χ​(g))≠ℚ​(χ)\mathbb{Q}(\chi(g))\neq\mathbb{Q}(\chi)

for allg∈Gg\in G. This means thatFS⁡(χ)\operatorname{FS}(\chi)does not hold, and sinceχ\chiis of odd degree, it follows thatFS2⁡(G×C4)\operatorname{FS}_{2}(G\times C_{4})doesnothold. I have checked thatCS⁡(G×C4)\operatorname{CS}(G\times C_{4})holds, however.

For theC4C_{4}-construction I have so far searched through 2,325 groups from the 2,397 of the GAP Character Table Library and found 148 eligible characters (odd degree or 2-rational) for whichFS⁡(χ)\operatorname{FS}(\chi)does not hold—so, counterexamples are abundant, and this just concerns products withC4C_{4}: the Galois idea works more generally by taking a direct product ofGGwithCq2C_{q^{2}}, a faithful characterλ\lambdaofCq2C_{q^{2}}and an appropriate choice ofα\alpha. Then one gets a Galois group of the character field ofχ=α⊠λ\chi=\alpha\boxtimes\lambdaof the formCq×CqC_{q}\times C_{q}and a similar “threat” as forC4C_{4}arises. There may be a smaller counterexample using theC4C_{4}-construction—I did not pursue a systematic search.


The counter-example above is non-solvable. The solvable situation is more restrictive for theC4C_{4}-construction because by[undefj, Theorem 2.8]the characterα\alphamust necessarily be non-real. But there is also a solvable counterexample with theC9C_{9}-construction, producing a Galois groupC3×C3C_{3}\times C_{3}. The first I (well, ChatGPT but it started with a quotient of this one where theC9C_{9}factor is less clearly visible) found starts withGGbeing theSmallGroup(189,5). This group is metacyclic: it isC63⋊3C3C_{63}\rtimes_{3}C_{3}in the notation of[undefd]. In particular,GGis solvable. I note thatGGitself satisfiesFS2⁡(G)\operatorname{FS}_{2}(G). But now we do theC9C_{9}-construction. Letα\alphabe one of the 12 degree-3 faithful characters (it does not matter which one). Letλ\lambdabe a faithful character ofC9C_{9}(it does not matter which one). Considerχ=α⊠λ\chi=\alpha\boxtimes\lambda. This is a degree-3 irreducible character ofG×C9G\times C_{9}. With OSCAR (seeAfor the code) one computes the following. The character field ofχ\chiisℚ​(ζ63)\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{63}), so[ℚ(χ):ℚ]=36[\mathbb{Q}(\chi):\mathbb{Q}]=36. Moreover,c​(χ)=63c(\chi)=63is odd and thusχ\chiis also 2-rational. But the fields generated by the individual character values all have degree at most 12, soFS⁡(χ)\operatorname{FS}(\chi)does not hold and thusFS2⁡(G×C9)\operatorname{FS}_{2}(G\times C_{9})does not hold.


The conductor versionCS⁡(χ)\operatorname{CS}(\chi)and Feit’s conjectureF⁡(χ)\operatorname{F}(\chi)are immune to this direct product construction. This situation can be avoided by just considering simple groups, and hereFS2⁡(G)\operatorname{FS}_{2}(G)is still an open problem it seems, although it is a drastic fix. In general, one could introduce the invariant(3.9)v​(χ)≔min⁡{r∣ℚ​(χ)=ℚ​(χ​(g1),…,χ​(gr))}.v(\chi)\coloneqq\min\{r\mid\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}(\chi(g_{1}),\ldots,\chi(g_{r}))\}\;.

I believe one can iterate theC4C_{4}-construction to yield arbitrarily highv​(χ)v(\chi)but I have not thoroughly checked this.
