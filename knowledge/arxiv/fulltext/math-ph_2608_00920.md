## Abstract

Torquato and Stillinger conjectured an exponential improvement of Minkowski's classical lower bound on the maximal density of sphere packings in high-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^d$ using a pair-correlation-function optimization framework. Conditional on their realizability conjecture, we show that a simple family of hyperuniform pair correlation functions yields polynomial improvements over Minkowski's lower bound of the form $φ_{\mathrm{max}} \gtrsim d^β2^{-d}$ for every fixed $β>1$ in sufficiently high dimensions. As the polynomial exponent is allowed to increase with dimension, this family continuously approaches the previously conjectured exponential improvement. We further derive the same exponential asymptotic rate independently from the Cohn--Elkies dual linear programming upper bound formulation, demonstrating that its radial objective test functions cannot asymptotically exclude packings with the Torquato--Stillinger density scalings. The agreement between these alternative approaches provides new evidence that exceptionally dense disordered sphere packings may exist in high dimensions and strengthens the case for the Torquato--Stillinger conjectural lower bound.

## 1Introduction

Sphere-packing problems remain a vibrant area of research, with far-reaching applications throughout the physical, mathematical, and biological sciences[28,27,26,11,19,3]. In physics, disordered sphere packings in dimensionsddgreater than three provide valuable models for understanding liquids, metastable states, and glasses[18,6,17,16,5,4], while also offering insight into the behavior of disordered phases in lower-dimensional systems. More broadly, the large-dimensional limit has emerged as a powerful theoretical framework, much as it has in other areas of physics, including the study of certain aspects of quantum gravity[1,14].
The quest to determine the densest sphere packings in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces is of fundamental interest to both mathematicians and physicists.
The mathematical study of the densest sphere packings across spatial dimensions has long been a central theme in discrete geometry[9,18], with deep connections to number theory[9,13], cryptography[9], and coding theory[21,9]. Beyond its intrinsic mathematical significance, this problem has profound practical implications: the optimal transmission of digital information over a noisy communication channel is mathematically equivalent to finding the densest sphere packing in a sufficiently high-dimensional Euclidean space[21,22,9]. The resulting error-correcting codes underpin modern digital communication and data storage technologies, including compact discs, cellular networks, and the Internet.

Our focus in this paper is the sphere packing problem, which asks for the densest packing of spheres indd-dimensional Euclidean spaceℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}at number densityρ\rho(number of sphere centers per unit volume), i.e., what fraction ofℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}can be covered by balls that do not intersect except along their boundaries?

## 3Alternative Proof of the Torquato-Stillinger Exponential
Asymptotic Form

Samorodnitsky used Delsarte’s linear programming approach to derive bounds on the maximal sizeA​(n,𝒟)A(n,{\cal D})of a binary error-correcting

Motivated by these results, we derive a lower bound on the Cohn–Elkies LP upper bound that has the same asymptotic form as the Torquato–Stillinger conjectured exponential improvement of Minkowski’s lower bound.
The proof can physically be interpreted as a type of “diffraction-limit” argument. A Cohn–Elkies test function is a positive-definite radial certificate that must become nonpositive outside the hard-core radius. Its low Fourier modes (small wavenumbers), however, are too smooth and, after radial weighting, too monotonic to produce the required sign change. Consequently, the sign change must be generated by the high Fourier modes (large wavenumbers), which create a compensating negative dip near contact. In high dimensions, however, the decay of the Bessel functions strongly suppresses the contribution of these high modes, making them increasingly inefficient. As a result, the necessary high-wavenumber correction prevents the value of the Cohn–Elkies certificate from decaying faster than the Torquato–Stillinger exponential scale.

## 4Discussion and conclusions

Conditional on the Torquato–Stillinger realizability conjecture, we have obtained a family of conjectural lower bounds on the maximal packing fraction of the formϕmax≳e−c​dβ​2−d\phi_{\max}\gtrsim e^{-c}d^{\beta}2^{-d}for every fixedβ>1\beta>1andc>1c>1in sufficiently high dimensions. These bounds arise from a single family of hyperuniform pair correlation functions, Eq. (25), in which the contact weightZ=dβZ=d^{\beta}determines the polynomial enhancement while the gapσ−1=c/d\sigma-1=c/dpreserves it up to a dimension-independent factor. Thus, the different polynomial improvements do not require unrelated choices ofg2​(r)g_{2}(r), but are generated by varying a single parameter within one construction.

The conjectural nature of these polynomial improvements is essential: the two-point conditionsg2​(r)≥0g_{2}(r)\geq 0andS​(k)≥0S(k)\geq 0do not by themselves establish the existence of a sphere packing. Our analysis shows that the structure-factor constraint requiresβ<βc​(d)\beta<\beta_{c}(d), whereβc​(d)\beta_{c}(d)diverges with dimension. Consequently, every fixed exponentβ>1\beta>1eventually satisfies the two-point conditions. Conditional on the Torquato–Stillinger realizability conjecture, these pair statistics then correspond to sphere packings. Ifβ\betais instead allowed to increase withddtowardβc​(d)\beta_{c}(d), the resulting densities recover the previously conjectured Torquato–Stillinger exponential asymptotic form, including its subexponential correction up to a multiplicative constant.

Independently of any realizability assumption, we have shown that the radial Cohn–Elkies linear programming formulation yields the same exponential asymptotic rate. Theorem1establishes that every admissible radial dual certificate satisfies a lower bound whose asymptotic decay coincides with the Torquato–Stillinger exponential scaling. Physically, this reflects the fact that the high-wavenumber Fourier modes needed to enforce the hard-core sign change become increasingly ineffective as the dimension grows. Thus, the radial Cohn–Elkies program cannot asymptotically exclude packings with the Torquato–Stillinger density scaling. The agreement between these two independent approaches—the pair-correlation framework and the dual linear programming formulation—provides strong evidence that the Torquato–Stillinger exponential scaling captures a fundamental asymptotic limit.

These results lend further support to the possibility that the densest sufficiently high-dimensional sphere packings are disordered and increasingly characterized by pair correlations alone, consistent with the decorrelation principle. Nevertheless, the principal open question remains unchanged: whether the proposed pair correlation functions are realizable as sphere packings. Constructing such packings—numerically or analytically—and establishing realizability beyond the two-point conditions would elevate the conjectural polynomial and exponential improvements presented here to rigorous lower bounds, thereby resolving one of the central outstanding questions in the theory of high-dimensional sphere packings.
