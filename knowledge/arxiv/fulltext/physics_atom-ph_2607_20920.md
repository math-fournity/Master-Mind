## Abstract

We present a high-precision measurement of the reduced electric-dipole matrix element $\langle P_{1/2}\|r\|S_{1/2}\rangle$ in $^{138}\mathrm{Ba}^+$. By comparing off-resonant scattering rates with dispersive Stark-shift measurements, we determine the matrix element to be $3.322\,7(12)$, corresponding to a $P_{1/2}$ excited-state radiative lifetime of $7.866\,3(56)$~ns. Combining our experimental results with a prior model for the differential scalar polarizability $Δα_0(ω)$, we extract the static value $Δα_0(0) = -73.09(12)$a.u. This determination directly improves the evaluation of the blackbody radiation (BBR) shift, minimizing a prominent systematic limitation in room-temperature $\mathrm{Ba}^+$ optical clock error budgets. These results provide a stringent benchmark for atomic-structure calculations and advance high-precision applications in quantum metrology and tests of fundamental physics.

## IIntroduction

Singly ionized barium (Ba+\mathrm{Ba}^{+}) is a premier system for precision spectroscopy and the validation of relativistic atomic structure calculations. Featuring a single valence electron outside a closed shell, its electronic structure allows highly accurate,ab initiomany-body theoretical treatments while remaining sensitive to core electron-correlation and relativistic effectsSafronovaet al.(2004); Guet and Johnson (1991); Gopakumaret al.(2002); Iskrenova-Tchoukova and Safronova (2008); Dzuba and Flambaum (2011). Beyond testing atomic theory,Ba+\mathrm{Ba}^{+}is a vital platform for optical frequency standardsShermanet al.(2005); Arnoldet al.(2020), quantum information processingDietrichet al.(2010); Inleket al.(2017); Huculet al.(2017); Bruzewiczet al.(2019); Lowet al.(2025), and low-energy tests of the Standard Model, such as atomic parity nonconservationFortson (1993); Dzuba and Flambaum (2011). It is also valuable for modeling clock transition polarizabilitiesJayjonget al.(2026). Ultimately, the precision limits of these applications depend on minimizing uncertainties in the underlying electric-dipole matrix elements.

One of the most precise experimental determinations of electric-dipole transition strengths was made inBa+\mathrm{Ba}^{+}from analyses of Rydberg fine-structure measurements in neutral and singly ionized barium, from which the relevant dipole matrix elements were extractedWoodset al.(2009,2010). Later, a complementary high-precision method was demonstrated inCa+\mathrm{Ca}^{+}, where a dipole matrix element was measured using a single trapped ion by combining ac-Stark shift measurements with photon-scattering rate measurementsHettrichet al.(2015). Given the close similarities in the electronic structure ofCa+\mathrm{Ca}^{+}andBa+\mathrm{Ba}^{+}, this methodology can be extended to theBa+\mathrm{Ba}^{+}system with only minor modifications. As we will show, the larger branching fraction of decays fromP1/2P_{1/2}toD3/2D_{3/2}allows a more straightforward implementation in which many systematics are more heavily suppressed.

In this work, we present a high-precision measurement of the reduced electric-dipole matrix element⟨P1/2∥r∥S1/2⟩\langle P_{1/2}\|r\|S_{1/2}\rangleinBa+138{}^{138}\mathrm{Ba}^{+}using a combined analysis of off-resonant scattering rates and Stark-shifts induced by a probe detuned from theS1/2−P1/2S_{1/2}-P_{1/2}transition. Our measured value of3.322 7​(12)​e​a03.322\,7(12)\,ea_{0}for⟨P1/2∥r∥S1/2⟩\langle P_{1/2}\|r\|S_{1/2}\rangleprovides a rigorous, high-precision challenge to existing experimental benchmarksJayjonget al.(2026); Woodset al.(2010); Kastberget al.(1993); Davidsonet al.(1992); Gallagher (1967)and represents a stringent test for theoretical calculationsRobertset al.(2023); Sahooet al.(2009); Dzuba and Flambaum (2011); Iskrenova-Tchoukova and Safronova (2008); Gopakumaret al.(2002); Guet and Johnson (1991). In conjunction with previous workArnoldet al.(2019); Zhanget al.(2020); Jayjonget al.(2026), this single matrix element allows updates to lifetimes of theP1/2P_{1/2}andP3/2P_{3/2}levels, the associated matrix elements, and the differential scalar polarizabilityΔ​α0​(ω)\Delta\alpha_{0}(\omega)model obtained inJayjonget al.(2026).

## III.3Measurements

Measurements of Stark shifts and scattering rates are carried out in an interleaved fashion. Each cycle begins with Stark-shift (Δs\Delta_{s}) measurements on the|S1/2,m=±1/2⟩−|D5/2,m′=±1/2⟩\lvert S_{1/2},m=\pm 1/2\rangle-\lvert D_{5/2},m^{\prime}=\pm 1/2\rangleclock transitions. Each Zeeman line is probed on either side of its resonance with and without the probe beam present, giving eight measurements in total. Subsequently, scattering-rate (γs\gamma_{s}) measurements are performed at two durations,t0=800​μ​st_{0}=800\,\mu\mathrm{s}andt1=4​mst_{1}=4\,\mathrm{ms}, chosen to yield ground-state populations of approximately0.80.8and0.30.3, respectively. The two scattering rate measurements are performed starting with population pumped into|S1/2,−1/2⟩\lvert S_{1/2},-1/2\rangleand repeated with population pumped to|S1/2,1/2⟩\lvert S_{1/2},1/2\rangle, to check for any dependence on population distribution. These four scattering measurements are repeated three times within the cycle. Each cycle is repeated 100 times before updating a servo that tracks the Stark shifts and magnetic field.

## III.4Systematics

Agreement between the individually averaged results and the straight-line fit to the uncorrected data is a mathematical requirement when uncertainties are equal. Agreement with the average of the corrected data reflects the fact that other systematic shifts are fractionally<2×10−5<2\times 10^{-5}.
