## Abstract

A conservation-law-guided classification framework is developed for the execution of coarse-graining operations in quantum field theory. Coarse-graining produces a selection at the level of Feynman diagrams. Diagrams with nonzero momentum transfer are suppressed by oscillatory factors, zero-momentum-transfer ladder diagrams can accumulate through geometric series resummation to produce a spectral pole, and single-bubble topologies contribute only to the continuum. Conservation laws govern this classification. For operators protected by a Ward identity, the matrix element at zero momentum is guaranteed to be nonvanishing. For operators protected by BRST symmetry, the Slavnov-Taylor identities provide no mandatory suppression. The two cases differ in the strength of the algebraic guarantee. For unprotected operators the injection term vanishes and the spectral function remains continuous. The sign of the single-bubble contribution is determined by spin statistics. A positive sign leads to amplificative feedback in the ladder resummation, a negative sign leads to suppressive feedback. These two attributes, the nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign of the single-bubble contribution, are the defining criteria of the classification. As a direct application, a general classification of local operators is established within the emergence framework and verified on eight physical channels and three known solvable systems. The framework indicates which emergence paths are possible for each operator; whether the critical condition is reached is left for independent nonperturbative computation. The logical structure of this classification is parallel to the strategy used in deriving fluid equations from molecular kinetic theory in classical statistical physics.

## 1Introduction

How microscopic degrees of freedom produce macroscopic collective behaviour through coarse-graining is a question that runs through statistical physics, fluid dynamics, condensed matter physics and quantum field theory.
In classical statistical physics the rigorous derivation of the Navier–Stokes equations from Boltzmann’s molecular kinetics has been completed in recent work, which established the macroscopic fluid equations as the inevitable consequence of microscopic molecular collisions in the thermodynamic limitDenget al.(2025).
In cosmology the effective field theory of large-scale structure shows how symmetry principles and coarse-graining govern the nonlinear clustering of dark matter.
Short-range physics is absorbed into a finite set of transport coefficients, yielding high-precision predictions for the observed galaxy distributionIvanov (2026).
In condensed matter physics the topological order of the fractional quantum Hall effect, the pseudogap phase of high-temperature superconductors and non-Fermi-liquid behaviour all involve long-range correlations whose structure is constrained by conservation lawsMcGreevy (2023).
In quantum gravity, Sakharov’s induced gravity, Jacobson’s thermodynamic gravity and Verlinde’s entropic force proposal have approached gravity as an emergent phenomenon from the perspectives of quantum fluctuations, thermodynamic relations and the holographic principle respectivelySakharov (1968); Jacobson (1995); Verlinde (2011).
More recently the development of generalised global symmetries has provided a formal framework in which symmetry protection constrains the correlation functions of operators at a fundamental levelGaiottoet al.(2015).

What these developments share is a common logical structure.
The emergence of long-range correlations is constrained by conservation laws and the kinematic structure of coarse-graining, while microscopic details affect only non-universal transport coefficients.
This logic has not yet been systematically implemented in the nonperturbative sector of quantum field theory.
The main tools currently available for nonperturbative problems have each achieved important successes but share a structural limitation.

Lattice gauge theory discretises spacetime onto a finite lattice and evaluates the path integral by Monte Carlo methods.
It has been successful for the QCD mass spectrum, hadron structure and quark confinement, but it is constrained by fermion doubling, the sign problem at finite density and computational cost.
Lattice results by themselves do not reveal which diagrammatic channels are responsible for the structures seen in spectral functions.
The functional renormalisation group follows the evolution of the effective action with the coarse-graining scale through the Wetterich equation.
It can track spectral functions, effective potentials and critical behaviour, and is widely applied to critical phenomena, quantum gravity and strongly correlated systems.
It does not, however, automatically identify which diagram classes accumulate under coarse-graining and which are suppressed, and its results depend on the choice of regulator.
Recent advances in spectral functional renormalisation group methodsHoraket al.(2024)have made it possible to compute spectral functions of quantum field theories directly, though the regulator dependence and the lack of an analytic classification remain.
Holographic duality maps strongly coupled gauge theories to weakly coupled gravitational theories via the AdS/CFT correspondence, providing direct access to the strong coupling regime.
It applies, however, only to systems that possess a gravitational dual and is not directly applicable to the Standard Model itself.
Dyson–Schwinger equations start from the quantum equations of motion and solve for Green’s functions with nonperturbative truncations.
They can handle mass generation and chiral symmetry breaking but depend on the choice of truncation scheme.

The common limitation of these four tools is that they yield quantitative results without supplying an analytic criterion that determines, before any numerical computation, which operators and which interaction channels possess the potential to develop long-range correlations.
This judgement ordinarily relies on complete numerical computations whose outcome itself depends on the regulator scheme, computational resources and model choices.

The idea that conservation laws select physically admissible subsets of Feynman diagrams has a precedent in perturbative gauge theory.
Boos and Ohl constructed minimal gauge-invariant classes of tree-level diagrams, known as groves, by requiring that a subset of diagrams satisfy the Ward identities by themselvesBoos and Ohl (1999).
The classification developed here applies the same underlying logic of conservation laws as a selection criterion to a different physical setting.
Instead of ensuring gauge invariance at tree level, the criterion determines which operators can develop long-range correlations under the nonperturbative operation of coarse-graining.

In a previous workGuo (2026)the author established coarse-graining as a dynamical principle for emergent gravity and proposed a spectral criterion, namely whether the spin-2 spectral density of the energy–momentum tensor commutator develops an isolated pole at zero momentum.
That work proved that a smooth spectral function cannot be a fixed point of the coarse-graining flow, identified the delta function as the only admissible non-smooth configuration, and invoked Weinberg’s low-energy theorem to lock the low-energy effective theory uniquely to the Einstein–Hilbert form.
It did not, however, systematically address how the coarse-graining operation is executed at the level of Feynman diagrams, how different operators behave differently under coarse-graining, or whether the critical condition required by the spectral criterion is satisfied by Standard Model dynamics.
A second workGuo and Bai (2026)tested the operator distinction implied by the emergence framework in the electroweak sector.
Taking the ultraviolet boundary conditionλ​(MP)=0\lambda(M_{P})=0for the unprotected Higgs quartic coupling, two-loop renormalisation group evolution yielded a Higgs mass consistent with the measured value within theoretical uncertainties.

The present paper develops a conservation-law-guided classification framework that supplies an analytic screening mechanism.
Without relying on perturbative expansions or the strength of couplings, and using only conservation laws and the kinematic structure of Feynman diagrams, it determines whether an arbitrary operator can develop long-range correlations under coarse-graining.
The framework makes the coarse-graining operation of Ref.Guo (2026)precise at the diagrammatic level.
Whether the momentum transfer vanishes separates diagrams that can accumulate from those that are suppressed by oscillatory factors.
Whether the diagram topology is a ladder separates diagrams capable of producing a spectral pole from those that merely contribute to the continuum.
Whether the operator is protected by a conservation law separates operators with a nonvanishing injection term from those whose injection term vanishes.
These criteria introduce no new physical hypotheses.
They systematise, algorithmise and make general the diagram classification ideas contained in Appendix E of Ref.Guo (2026).

The classification logic is structurally similar to the Landau paradigm in condensed matterBhardwajet al.(2024).
In that framework, symmetry dictates which excitations can emerge at low energies.
Here symmetry plays the same role.
Ward identities and BRST invariance determine which operators can accumulate spectral weight under coarse-graining.
The CGC framework is the QFT analogue of the Landau classification for long-range correlationsChen (2025).

The execution of coarse-graining involves several interrelated aspects.
At the diagrammatic level it is necessary to clarify which Feynman diagrams make cumulative contributions to the slow-mode effective action after fast modes are integrated out, which are suppressed by fast-mode oscillations, and whether the selection criteria depend on the order of perturbation theory or the size of couplings.
At the operator level different operators display sharply different behaviour under coarse-graining.
Whether an operator can generate long-range correlations, and why, is traceable to the conservation properties of the operator and the spin statistics of the fields to which it couples.
A further distinction is drawn between dynamical ladder accumulation and topological zero-mode mechanisms.
The framework analyses the former; the latter, where relevant, is identified as a separate pathway.
The classification framework developed here systematically answers these two questions.
It is orthogonal to the standard RG classification by scaling dimension; it addresses pole accumulation rather than operator growth under scale change.
A complementary question concerns verification, namely how the analytic classification can be cross-checked against independent nonperturbative numerical computations.
This task is carried out by a separate functional renormalisation group calculation.
The classification framework indicates which emergence paths are possible.
The FRG computation directly evaluates the propagator and determines whether a pole is present.
Together the analytic classification and the numerical verification form a complete chain from analytic discrimination to numerical confirmation.
The CGC framework is designed as a pre-screening tool.
It asks which operators are possible candidates for dynamical emergence.
It does not replace the FRG or lattice computations that determine whether the critical condition is reached.
CGC answers possibility; the FRG answers actuality.
The framework also identifies situations where the dynamical pathway is closed by topological obstruction, as with the scalar glueballG2G^{2}.
In such cases, the framework does not provide the topological analysis itself; it flags the obstruction as a separate condition that must be checked independently.

## 2Three Criteria of Coarse-Graining

## 2.1What Coarse-Graining Does at the Level of Feynman Diagrams

The coarse-graining flow of the spectral density derived in Ref.Guo (2026)consists of two terms.
A dilution term suppresses spectral weight at all nonzero momenta through the exponential window.
An injection term deposits the spectral weight of the integrated fast modes back into the surviving slow modes.
The two terms are not independent.
Their combined effect is equivalent to a set of selection rules operating directly on the Feynman diagrams that constitute the spectral function.
The dilution term governs the suppression of diagrams whose fast internal lines carry a momentum transfer that oscillates under the coarse-graining window.
The injection term governs the redistribution of spectral weight through diagrams whose topology permits a cumulative build up over successive coarse-graining steps.
The present section makes this correspondence precise.
The two criteria developed below, one kinematic and one graph theoretic, map the dilution and injection terms onto explicit diagrammatic selection rules.

Coarse-graining integrates out the fast modes with an exponential window functionWσ​(p)=exp⁡(−p2​σ2/2)W_{\sigma}(p)=\exp(-p^{2}\sigma^{2}/2).
This operation is irreversible.
In quantum field theory the fast and slow modes are coupled by interactions, and their degrees of freedom are correlated in the path integral.
Integrating out the fast modes amounts to taking the partial trace over the fast Hilbert space.
The reduced density matrix of the slow modes is obtained.
Because the entanglement between the fast and slow modes is removed, the reduced density matrix turns from a pure state into a mixed state, and its von Neumann entropy increases.
At the level of Feynman diagrams the effect of fast-mode integration is characterised by two criteria.
The two criteria are independent of each other, respectively kinematic and graph-theoretic, and do not rely on the specific form of the interactions or on the strength of the couplings.

The first criterion concerns whether the momentum transfer vanishes.
Consider a Feynman diagram containing internal fast lines and external slow legs.
The net momentum transferqqfrom the fast internal lines to the slow external legs is a purely kinematic quantity, determined by the conservation of energy and momentum at each vertex.
Whenq≠0q\neq 0the integration over the fast modes produces an oscillatory factorei​q⋅xe^{iq\cdot x}.
In the infrared limit of the coarse-graining flow the coarse-graining scaleσ\sigmabecomes macroscopic, and this oscillatory factor is exponentially suppressed by the integration over spacetime within the coarse-graining window, vanishing in the limit.
Diagrams withq≠0q\neq 0therefore give a negligible net contribution in the infrared.
They do not participate in the formation of a spectral pole but instead feed the noise kernelη​(t,x)\eta(t,x)in the Langevin equation (4) of the order parameter.
Whenq=0q=0the fast modes enter the vertices that connect to the slow external legs in a back-to-back configuration, meaning that the fast momenta appear in pairs of+k+kand−k-k, with zero net momentum transfer.
This is the characteristic momentum routing of ladder diagrams.
In this case there is no oscillatory factor, and the contribution of the diagram can accumulate over successive coarse-graining steps.
This classification is a standard result of Fourier analysis and is mathematically rigorous.
It does not depend on the order of perturbation theory or on the size of the couplings.
It depends only on the momentum routing of the diagram, and the momentum routing is itself fixed solely by energy–momentum conservation.

The second criterion concerns diagram topology.
Among the diagrams withq=0q=0a further distinction must be made according to topological structure.
The simplest are single-bubble diagrams, which contain a single closed fast loop and no irreducible internal insertions.
The contribution of a single-bubble diagram to the spectral function remains smooth in the infrared limit, producing only a continuumρcont​(s)\rho_{\text{cont}}(s)and no isolated pole.
Another important class consists of ladder diagrams.
A ladder diagram is built from several independent bubbles connected in a chain by irreducible verticesVV.
The irreducible vertexVVdoes not itself contain fast propagators.
It is an effective four-operator interaction among the slow modes, generated by the second-order effect of integrating out the fast modes at an earlier coarse-graining step.

The momentum transfer criterion and the topology criterion address two distinct questions.
The first determines which diagrams survive the oscillatory suppression of coarse-graining.
The second identifies, among the survivors, the specific class of diagrams capable of producing a spectral pole through geometric series resummation.
Neither criterion, however, addresses a further question.
The selection rules derived so far assume that the vertices coupling the fast shell modes to the slow external legs have nonvanishing matrix elements at zero momentum transfer.
If those matrix elements were to vanish, no amount of kinematic survival or topological accumulation would produce an injection term, and no spectral weight would flow.
Whether the relevant matrix elements are nonvanishing is decided by a property that lies outside the kinematic and graph theoretic analysis.
This property is the protection of the operator by a conservation law.
The analysis of this third criterion follows.

Together the three criteria form a complete decision tree.
The momentum transfer classification removes the diagrams that cannot accumulate.
The topology classification singles out the ladder diagrams that can produce a pole.
The conservation law check decides whether those ladder diagrams have nonvanishing matrix elements, thereby determining whether an injection term exists to drive the accumulation.

## 2.2How Conservation Laws Guarantee a Nonvanishing Injection Term

Two complementary arguments are given.
One is based on the dynamical mechanism of critical slowing down under coarse-graining, the other on a rigorous proof using the algebraic properties of the Noether current.
The two arguments lead independently to the same conclusion.

## 2.2.1Dynamical mechanism

ReferenceGuo (2026)established coarse-graining as a dynamical principle and derived the Langevin dynamics of the order parameter.

The premise for this transmission is that the bare spectral function of the operator contains no cutoff-dependent divergences.
If the bare spectral function still contains uncancelled power-law divergences or logarithmic divergences, these cutoff-dependent terms absorb the zero-frequency divergence through the same coupling, leaving the spectral function continuous at zero momentum.
Whether an operator is protected by a conservation law therefore directly determines whether the zero-frequency divergence can reach the spectral function freely.
For protected operators the Ward identities have already eliminated all power-law divergences from physical correlation functions, and the zero-frequency divergence becomes the only singularity that cannot be cancelled.
For unprotected operators the surviving cutoff-dependent divergences absorb the zero-frequency divergence.

## 2.2.2Algebraic argument

The injection termΔσ\Delta_{\sigma}in the coarse-graining flow is the spectral weight deposited into the slow modes after the fast modes are integrated out.
At the perturbative level the nonvanishing of the injection term relies on the Seeley–DeWitt coefficienta2a_{2}of the heat kernel expansionAvramidi (2015), which assumes that nonperturbative effects do not cancel this coefficient.
The present work establishes the nonvanishing of the injection term as a rigorous consequence of conservation laws, without relying on perturbative estimates.

For the energy–momentum tensorTμ​νT_{\mu\nu}, the conserved Noether current of spacetime translations, a standard corollary of the Ward identity statesWeinberg (1995)that the matrix element of a conserved Noether currentJμJ_{\mu}at zero momentum equals the matrix element of the conserved chargeQQbetween the same initial and final states,⟨f|Jμ​(0)|i⟩|q=0∝⟨f|Q|i⟩.\langle f|J_{\mu}(0)|i\rangle\big|_{q=0}\propto\langle f|Q|i\rangle.(5)

For the energy–momentum tensor the conserved charge is the total energy–momentumPμP_{\mu}.
Any particle state that carries energy and momentum satisfies⟨p|Pμ|p⟩=pμ≠0\langle p|P_{\mu}|p\rangle=p_{\mu}\neq 0.
Hence the matrix element of the energy–momentum tensor at zero momentum is nonvanishing as an algebraic fact, independent of the order of perturbation theory, the size of the couplings, or the details of nonperturbative corrections.

The gauge field strengthFμ​νaF_{\mu\nu}^{a}is protected by BRST symmetry.
It is BRST closed,sB​F=0s_{B}F=0, and its matrix elements are protected by the Slavnov–Taylor identities.
BRST closure guarantees that the correlation functions of gauge-invariant operators do not depend on the gauge fixing.
For the two-point function ofFμ​νaF_{\mu\nu}^{a}the Slavnov–Taylor identity does not force the matrix element to vanish atq=0q=0.
The structural logic is parallel to that of the energy–momentum tensor.
In both cases the conservation law eliminates the mechanism that would otherwise force the matrix element to vanish at zero momentum.
The protected status of an operator consists precisely in this elimination.
ForTμ​νT_{\mu\nu}, the conserved charge on single particle states supplies a direct argument for a nonvanishing matrix element.
For the gauge field strength no strictly analogous charge argument is available.
Its protected status rests on the same structural ground as that of the energy–momentum tensor.
The Slavnov–Taylor identity provides no mandatory suppression at zero momentum transfer.
Both operators belong to the protected class, though the proof of nonvanishing is stronger for the energy–momentum tensor.

The injection term is regenerated at every step of the coarse-graining process.
Because it originates from the shell of fast modes that is integrated out at the current coarse-graining scale, and every such shell contains the same matrix elements of the protected operator, the injection term is nonvanishing at every step.
The injection term therefore acts as a nonvanishing source that continuously deposits spectral weight throughout the entire coarse-graining flow, not merely at some particular scale.

For protected operators the matrix elements of the zero-momentum-transfer ladder diagrams are nonvanishing, and the injection term continuously injects spectral weight.
For unprotected operators the injection term vanishes, and the spectral function remains continuous in the infrared limit.
The injection term is nonvanishing not because perturbation theory suggests it might be, but because the algebraic properties of the conserved Noether current require it to be.
The result is no longer an estimate.
It follows from the algebra.

The dynamical mechanism and the algebraic argument, though logically independent, converge to the same physical conclusion for complementary reasons.
The dynamical mechanism reveals the physical origin of the nonvanishing injection term.
Critical slowing down generates a zero-frequency singularity that cannot be absorbed for protected operators, because all divergences that could absorb it have already been eliminated by the Ward identities.
The algebraic argument supplies a proof for specific operators that does not depend on the details of the critical slowing down dynamics.
For the energy–momentum tensor, the algebraic argument provides a rigorous demonstration of the nonvanishing based on the relation between the Noether current and the conserved charge.
For the gauge field strength, the algebraic argument establishes that the Ward identity provides no mandatory suppression at zero momentum, placing the operator in the same protected class.
The two arguments are not redundant.
The first explains why the classification works, and the second proves that it must.

The fluctuations of an ordinary composite operator are gradually diluted by coarse-graining and eventually fade away.
The fluctuations of the energy–momentum tensor are different.
Their spectral weight is protected by the Ward identity.
It cannot disappear.
It can only be redistributed across momentum space.
It is this forced redistribution that makes it possible for spectral weight ultimately to condense into an isolated pole at zero momentum.

## 2.3Reliability of the Ladder Approximation

The ladder approximation is a tool for the quantitative resummation step.
The classification conclusions do not depend on it.
The nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}follow from algebraic and group-theoretic arguments that do not involve any resummation topology.
The FRG computation determines the critical scale.
It solves the full Wetterich equation and automatically includes all diagrammatic topologies.
It does not require a separate completeness proof for the ladder approximation.

The following three arguments provide supporting evidence for the ladder approximation as a reasonable starting point for the FRG computation.

## 2.3.1Guarantee from conservation laws

The conservation laws provide a guarantee that does not rely on the ladder approximation.
The nonvanishing of the injection term is an algebraic consequence of the conservation laws.
Even if non-ladder diagrams produce additional contributions, they can only shift the quantitative position of the critical condition, not the qualitative conclusion that the injection term is nonvanishing.
Whether an operator possesses emergence potential does not require the ladder approximation to be valid.
No matter whether crossed diagrams, vertex corrections or other multi-loop topologies are suppressed, the Ward identity or BRST symmetry guarantees the nonvanishing of the injection term.
If the ladder approximation were quantitatively imprecise, that would merely correct the critical scale, without altering the judgement that the operator has emergence potential.

## 2.3.2Spectral sparseness of the emergence window

The spectral sparseness of the emergence window supplies an independent physical basis for the ladder approximation.
When the coarse-graining window contains only a single effectively active field mode, the topological distinction between crossed diagrams and ladder diagrams loses its meaning.
A crossed diagram requires at least two independent active modes to build its crossed momentum routing.
When only one mode is active, the topological structures of crossed and ladder diagrams degenerate into different representations of the same diagram.
The independent contributions of vertex correction diagrams and other multi-loop topologies are likewise restricted to a single momentum configuration.
Any diagram built on a single active mode has its possible structures severely compressed.
The ladder approximation is therefore not an approximation in this regime.
It is exact when the mode count is exactly one, and highly accurate when the active modes form a small, sparse set.
This conclusion does not rely on a large-NNexpansion or on any suppression parameter, but is a rigid consequence of counting the modes of the internal space that lie within the emergence window.

Whether this spectral sparseness condition is realised depends on the spectrum of the internal space at the relevant coarse-graining scale.
In the regime in which the emergence mechanism operates, the internal space is highly curved and its nonzero spectral gaps are large compared with the coarse-graining scale.
With the Litim regulatorLitim (2001), a sharp step function used widely in FRG practice for its optimal convergence in the derivative expansion, only modes whose eigenvalue lies belowk2k^{2}contribute to the trace densityPawlowski and Reichert (2020).
Under these conditions the only mode that satisfies this condition is the scalarJ=0J=0zero mode, whose eigenvalue is zero and therefore always lies below the cutoff, together with the nearby modes that remain within the window.
Using the exponential windowexp⁡(−p2​σ2/2)\exp(-p^{2}\sigma^{2}/2)of Ref.Guo (2026)instead gives every mode an exponentially small contribution rather than a sharp cutoff, but the scalar zero mode continues to dominate under any reasonable regulator.
The quantitative mode counting for the specific internal space of the emergence framework is performed in a separate geometric analysis; the present argument requires only that the active mode set be sparse, a condition that is verified for that spectrum.

A completeness theorem covering all possible non-ladder diagram classes is addressable within the FRG, where the functional resummation automatically captures all diagram topologies.
The current verification covers the known relevant classes, and the crossed and ladder topologies have been shown to degenerate on a sparse spectrum.
The FRG treatment subsumes the completeness question: the critical scale is determined by the full Wetterich equation, which is not restricted to any particular diagram class.

## 2.3.3Colour factor suppression

Colour factor suppression operates in the multi-mode region.
At the equilibrium configuration, where the number of active modes is about nine, the ratio of crossed diagrams to ladder diagrams lies between roughly0.10.1and0.50.5.
In the perturbative regime the group theory ofSU​(3)\mathrm{SU}(3)gives a colour factor suppression for crossed ladders,R=1/(2​CA2)≈1/18R=1/(2C_{A}^{2})\approx 1/18.
Emergence, however, takes place inside the window whereNeff=1N_{\text{eff}}=1, not in the multi-mode region.
The colour factor suppression in the perturbative regime is auxiliary.
It shows that even in regions without the protection of spectral sparseness the contribution of crossed diagrams remains at a moderate level of suppression.

## 2.4Systematic Execution Steps

The conservation-law-guided classification framework is implemented as a six-step pipeline.

(1) Given a target composite operator, all connected irreducible Feynman diagrams are generated at the one-loop level.
The generation can be performed using the built-in field type enumerator, which automatically determines the coupled field types from the conservation property of the operator and then produces for each field type aq=0q=0bubble and aq≠0q\neq 0variant, or it can call an external tool such asQgrafNogueira (1993)for cross-checking.
For the four core operators of the emergence framework the expected numbers of diagrams are six for the energy–momentum tensor, four for the gauge field strength, two for the fermion bilinear and four for the Higgs quartic coupling.

(2) Each generated diagram is classified by its net momentum transferqqfrom the fast internal lines to the slow external legs.
Diagrams withq=0q=0proceed to the next step; diagrams withq≠0q\neq 0are labelled as suppressed and feed the Langevin noise kernel.
This is a purely kinematic judgement that does not depend on the specific form of the interactions.
For the energy–momentum tensor one expects threeq=0q=0diagrams and threeq≠0q\neq 0diagrams; for the gauge field strength, two of each; for the fermion bilinear, one of each; for the Higgs quartic coupling, two of each.

(3) Theq=0q=0diagrams are sorted by topology into three classes.
Single-bubble diagrams, which contain only one closed loop without any irreducible insertion, are labelled as the continuum class and contribute only to the continuum.
Ladder diagrams, in which several bubbles are connected in a chain by irreducible vertices, are labelled as the accumulation class and can produce aδ\deltapole through geometric series resummation.
Non-standard topologies such as crossed ladders and vertex corrections are labelled as the pending class.
At the one-loop level allq=0q=0diagrams are single-bubble diagrams; ladder diagrams are multi-loop structures generated automatically by the resummation module.

(4) A conservation law check determines whether the ladder diagrams in the accumulation class have nonvanishing matrix elements.
Protected operators, such as the energy–momentum tensor (protected by the Ward identity) and the gauge field strength (protected by BRST symmetry), have a nonvanishing injection term and therefore possess emergence potential.
Unprotected operators, such as the fermion bilinear and the Higgs quartic coupling, have a vanishing injection term and no emergence potential.

(5) For the accumulation class diagrams that have passed the conservation law check, the geometric series resummation is performed and the critical condition1−λeff​Π0​(0)=01-\lambda_{\text{eff}}\Pi_{0}(0)=0, equivalentlyV⋅Π0=4/27V\cdot\Pi_{0}=4/27, is output.
Whether the effective coupling indeed reaches the critical value is a dynamical question that must be answered by FRG or DSE.

(6) The results are cross-validated against an independent FRG computation.
The classification results are transmitted to the FRG engine through a standardised JSON interface.
The FRG engine solves the complete Wetterich equation on the discrete spectrum of a compact internal space and returns the spectral function and the pole information.
The cross-validation module compares the outputs of the two independent computational paths, namely the classification path and the direct continuum path, and produces a consistency report on the sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}, the nonvanishing of the injection term and the existence of a pole.
The pipeline runs automatically from operator specification to final classification verdict.

## 3Operator Classification

The distinct critical behaviour exhibited by the energy–momentum tensor and the Higgs quartic coupling under coarse-graining has its origin in symmetry.
The former is the conserved Noether current of spacetime translations.
Its vacuum correlation functions are protected by Ward identities, power-law divergences cancel automatically within commutators, and the zero-frequency divergence produced by critical slowing-down propagates freely to the spectral function, accumulating as an isolated pole at zero momentum.
The latter has no such protection.
Its logarithmic divergence survives in physical correlation functions, the zero-frequency divergence is absorbed, and the spectral function stays continuous at the critical point.

A local operator is called a protected operator if its vacuum matrix elements are protected by conservation laws or Ward identities, so that cutoff-dependent divergences cancel automatically in physical correlation functions and infrared observables are independent of the ultraviolet regularisation scheme.
A local operator is called an unprotected operator if its vacuum expectation values enjoy no such protection, are sensitive to cutoff-dependent divergences, and its ultraviolet boundary values depend on the regularisation scheme.
These two definitions are exhaustive.
Every local operator is either protected by Ward identities or it is not.
There is no intermediate case.

## 3.1Basic Principles

Under coarse-graining different operators display sharply different behaviour.
The origin of this difference lies not in the details of perturbative calculations but in two definite algebraic attributes of the operator.
These two attributes are direct consequences of the diagrammatic analysis carried out in Section2.
Their physical meaning can be illustrated concretely by comparing how Feynman diagrams contribute to the coarse-graining flow for different operators.

The first attribute is whether the injection term is nonvanishing.
The injection termΔσ\Delta_{\sigma}is the spectral weight deposited into the slow modes after the fast modes are integrated out.
For a protected operator, the algebraic relation between the Noether current and the conserved charge guarantees that this term is nonvanishing at every coarse-graining step.
It acts as a persistent source that drives the accumulation of spectral weight toward zero momentum.
For an unprotected operator, no such guarantee exists, and the spectral function can only be diluted as coarse-graining proceeds.

The second attribute is the sign of the single-bubble contributionΠ0\Pi_{0}.
Even when the injection term is nonvanishing, whether the injected spectral weight can be amplified into a pole depends on the sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}.
This quantity is the contribution of the simplest closed fast-mode loop, the single-bubble diagram, evaluated at zero momentum.
Its sign is determined entirely by the spin statistics of the fields propagating in the loop.
Bosonic fields give a positive contribution, fermionic fields give a negative contribution.
The sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}controls the direction of the self-consistent feedback in the ladder resummation.
WhenΠ0\Pi_{0}is positive, each rung of the ladder enhances the effective coupling, and the geometric series drives the dressed polarisation toward a pole.
WhenΠ0\Pi_{0}is negative, each rung suppresses the coupling, and the series remains convergent.
A positiveΠ0\Pi_{0}combined with a nonvanishing injection term is therefore the sufficient condition for dynamical emergence.

## 3.2Symmetry Origin of the Criteria

The two classification criteria, the nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}, are not empirical or numerical properties.
Both follow from symmetry, the first from the continuous symmetries of the Standard Model that give rise to Ward identities, the second from the Lorentz representation of the fields that contribute to the single-bubble diagram.

## Nonvanishing injection term.

The injection term is the matrix element of the operator at zero momentum transfer.
A nonvanishing value is guaranteed when the operator is the Noether current of a continuous symmetry.
The Ward identity∂μJμ=0\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0implies, by a standard argument, that⟨f|Jμ​(0)|i⟩|q=0∝⟨f|Q|i⟩\langle f|J^{\mu}(0)|i\rangle|_{q=0}\propto\langle f|Q|i\rangle, whereQQis the conserved charge.
ForTμ​νT_{\mu\nu}, this charge is the total energy–momentumPμP_{\mu}; for a conserved vector currentJμJ^{\mu}, it is the corresponding global charge.
The matrix element is nonvanishing for any state carrying the corresponding quantum number.

For gauge-invariant operators such asFμ​νa​Fa​μ​νF_{\mu\nu}^{a}F^{a\mu\nu}, BRST symmetry provides a different kind of algebraic control.
The Slavnov–Taylor identities ensure that the matrix element is not forced to vanish by gauge invariance.
This is weaker than the Noether current case.
There is no conserved charge whose expectation value supplies a direct proof of nonvanishing.
What the Slavnov–Taylor identities do supply is the absence of a mandatory suppression.
Throughout this paper, the term protected for BRST-invariant operators is used in this restricted sense.

Operators with no continuous symmetry protection have no such algebraic constraint.
Their injection term may be zero, or scheme-dependent; in neither case can it serve as a stable seed for ladder accumulation.

## Sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}.

The sign of the single-bubble contribution is determined by spin statistics, which follows from the representation theory of the Lorentz group.
Bosonic fields give positive contributions; fermionic fields give negative contributions.
The sign is an algebraic fact, independent of coupling strengths, regularisation schemes, or perturbative orders.

The classification therefore rests on two independent symmetry inputs, namely the Ward/BRST structure that guarantees the injection term and the Lorentz representation that fixes the sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}.
Both are rigid because both follow from group theory, not from dynamical approximations.

## Protection is necessary but not sufficient.

The classification identifies operators that satisfy both criteria as candidates for dynamical emergence.
It does not guarantee that emergence occurs; the critical coupling must still be reached.
Conversely, an operator may satisfy both criteria yet be blocked by topological considerations, as withG2G^{2}, whose scalar Laplacian has a vanishing analytic index on compact spaces.
The symmetry criteria are necessary conditions; the sufficiency depends on additional dynamical or topological conditions.

## Scope of the symmetry analysis.

This section has considered the continuous symmetries of the Standard Model that give rise to Ward identities, namely spacetime translations, BRST symmetry, and global continuous symmetries.
Discrete symmetries such asCC,PP, andC​PCPdo not yield Ward identities and therefore do not protect operators in the sense used here.
Generalised global symmetries, while relevant to the broader structure of quantum field theory, are not needed for the classification of the fundamental local operators considered in this paper.
The symmetries analysed here are the full set required for the classification; no other continuous symmetries of the Standard Model act on the local operators under consideration.

## 3.3Protected Operators

Protected operators are those whose zero-momentum-transfer ladder matrix elements are forced to be nonvanishing by conservation laws under coarse-graining.
Three protection sources exist.

The Ward identity originates from conserved Noether currents of spacetime translations.
The energy–momentum tensor satisfies∂μTμ​ν=0\partial_{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0.
Its zero-momentum matrix element equals the matrix element of the total energy–momentumPμP_{\mu}, which is nonvanishing for any particle state that carries energy and momentum.
The BRST symmetry protects the gauge field strengthFμ​νaF_{\mu\nu}^{a}.
It is BRST closed and its matrix elements are protected by the Slavnov–Taylor identities.
The structural logic is identical to that of the energy–momentum tensor.
The conservation law eliminates the mechanism that would otherwise force the matrix element to vanish at zero momentum.
Noether’s theorem provides protection for conserved currents arising from global continuous symmetries.
Gauge currentsJμ​aJ^{\mu a}are protected by covariant conservation,Dμ​Jμ​a=0D_{\mu}J^{\mu a}=0, which operates through the same structural logic as the ordinary Ward identity.

The proof for the energy–momentum tensor is based on the algebraic fact that the matrix element of a Noether current at zero momentum equals the matrix element of the conserved charge (5), which supplies a direct argument for nonvanishing.
The proof for the gauge field strength is based on the same structural logic.
The Ward identity provides no mandatory suppression at zero momentum transfer, a weaker but still algebraic guarantee.
Zero-momentum-transfer ladder diagrams can accumulate because the injection term acts as a nonvanishing source that deposits spectral weight at every coarse-graining step.
The spectral function may develop an isolated pole under coarse-graining.
Whether this potential is realised depends on the dynamics of the system under the full coarse-graining flow.

## 3.4Unprotected Operators

Unprotected operators are those whose zero-momentum-transfer ladder matrix elements have no conservation law protection.

The injection term of an unprotected operator lacks the algebraic constraint that forces it to be nonvanishing.
For a protected operator the Ward identity or Slavnov–Taylor identity supplies an algebraic relation between the matrix element atq=0q=0and an independently known quantity, namely the conserved charge forTμ​νT_{\mu\nu}or a BRST-invariant observable forFμ​νaF_{\mu\nu}^{a}, and this relation directly implies that the matrix element cannot vanish.
For an unprotected operator no such relation exists.
The matrix element atq=0q=0is not constrained by any symmetry, and there is no algebraic reason for it to be nonvanishing.
The classification framework operates under two premises.
First, coarse-graining criticality excludes fine-tuning.
Second, physical observables must be scheme-independent.
Given these premises, the natural assignment for an unprotected operator is a vanishing injection term.
This is not a mathematical theorem.
An unprotected operator could in principle have a non-zero injection term through dynamical effects.
The framework does not prove impossibility.
It systematically identifies which operators are not protected and therefore lack a guaranteed nonvanishing injection term.
The zero injection classification should be understood as the natural assignment under the stated premises.
Neutrino mass operators, whether of Dirac typeL¯​ϕ~​νR\bar{L}\tilde{\phi}\nu_{R}or Majorana type(L​ϕ~)​(ϕ~T​Lc)/Λ(L\tilde{\phi})(\tilde{\phi}^{T}L^{c})/\Lambda, fall into the unprotected class.
Neither carries a conserved-current Ward identity.
Their injection terms vanish.
They cannot drive dynamical emergence through ladder resummation.

Without a nonvanishing injection term, no source exists to deposit spectral weight continuously into the slow modes.
The spectral function remains continuous under coarse-graining, and no isolated pole develops.
The ultraviolet boundary value of an unprotected operator is therefore not determined by coarse-graining dynamics; it must be specified externally within a regularisation scheme.
This is the subject of the following subsection.

## 3.5Scheme Selection

Unprotected operators do not accumulate zero modes.
Their ultraviolet boundary values are therefore not determined by coarse-graining dynamics.
The correct boundary condition can only be formulated within a subtraction scheme that removes the divergences.

Two classes of unprotected operators must be distinguished.

Take the Higgs mass parameterm2m^{2}as an example of a positive mass-dimension operator.
In a scheme that retains power-law divergences, the one-loop flow equation contains a term proportional tok2k^{2}Hooft and Veltman (1972).

For unprotected operators the divergences survive in physical correlation functions; the correct boundary condition can only be formulated within a subtraction scheme.

## 4Irreversibility of Coarse-Graining and the RG Time

In the derivation of the Navier–Stokes equations from Boltzmann’s molecular kinetics, microscopic collision diagrams are classified by their wave number.
Diagram classes not protected by conservation laws are suppressed in the thermodynamic limit.
Those that are protected survive, and their resummation yields the macroscopic fluid equationsDenget al.(2025).
The classification framework developed here follows the same sequence of classification, selection and resummation.
Feynman diagrams are classified by whether the momentum transfer vanishes.
Those withq=0q=0can accumulate and those withq≠0q\neq 0are suppressed.
The nonvanishing of the injection term for protected operators is an algebraic consequence of the conservation laws.
The resummation of ladder diagrams for protected operators necessarily produces a massless spectral pole.

## 4.1Irreversibility of Coarse-Graining

The classification framework established in Sections2and3determines whether a given operator possesses the potential for emergence, and along which path that emergence would proceed.
The framework is static in the sense that it evaluates the attributes of an operator at a given coarse-graining scale.
Whether the potential it identifies is realised depends on a further condition.
The effective coupling that drives the ladder resummation must reach the critical value required for pole formation.
This condition is dynamical.
It can only be assessed by following the evolution of the system under the full coarse-graining flow.
This section examines that dynamics.
The irreversibility of coarse-graining supplies an arrow of time, the RG time, which drives scalar condensation and defines a characteristic window within which the critical condition can be met.

ReferenceGuo (2026)established coarse-graining as the single dynamical principle governing the emergence framework.
Coarse-graining is irreversible.
Integrating out the fast modes removes their entanglement with the slow modes, turning the reduced density matrix of the slow modes from a pure state into a mixed state.
Information is permanently lost.
The irreversibility manifests itself as a definite direction of the coarse-graining flow, from the ultraviolet toward the infrared, from high energies toward low energies.
The RG timet=ln⁡(k/Λ)t=\ln(k/\Lambda)inherits this irreversibility.
It flows only in one direction, and the information carried by the integrated fast modes cannot be recovered.
No additional hypothesis is required.
The directedness of the RG time is an intrinsic property of the Wilsonian renormalisation groupWilson and Kogut (1974).

This directedness of the RG time has a direct consequence for the emergence framework.
The coarse-graining flow drives the system from the ultraviolet toward the infrared along a preferred direction.
If the effective potential of a scalar field possesses a negative curvature at the ultraviolet scale, the RG flow will gradually reshape it.
As more fast modes are integrated out, the shape of the effective potential evolves.
Under appropriate conditions the curvature can change sign, and a scalar field that initially had no vacuum expectation value can acquire one.
The irreversibility of coarse-graining is the dynamical origin of this process.
No external time coordinate needs to be introduced.

In the effective field theory of large-scale structure in cosmology, the bias functional that describes galaxy formation is non-local in time.
Galaxy formation takes a Hubble time, comparable to the time scale on which the Universe evolves, and the bias functional therefore depends on the entire formation history.
In perturbation theory, however, the fields at each order evolve with a simple time dependence, and the time non-locality collapses to a set of constant coefficientsIvanov (2026).
The same logic applies to scalar condensation driven by the RG flow.
Even if the complete real-time evolution involves non-local memory effects, under the coarse-graining flow of the RG time the microscopic history is expected to manifest itself as local and computable contributions at the macroscopic scale.

## 4.2Scalar Condensation Driven by the RG Flow

The operator classification established in Section3identifies which operators possess the potential for dynamical emergence.
For a protected operator with a positive single-bubble contribution, the ladder resummation can produce a spectral pole.
Whether this potential is realised depends on the dynamics of the system under the RG flow.
Scalar condensation provides a concrete setting in which the RG flow drives the system through the critical region where the conditions for emergence can be met.

The mechanism described above can be made quantitative within the functional renormalisation group.
Consider a scalar field whose effective potential at the ultraviolet scale has a negative squared mass.
The Wetterich equationWetterich (1993)governs the evolution of the effective potential as the RG scalekkis lowered.
At each step the trace density, summed over the spectrum of the theory, deforms the potential.
As modes are progressively integrated out, the effective potential changes shape.
If the parameters are such that the curvature at the origin switches sign at some critical scale, the scalar field acquires a nonvanishing vacuum expectation value.
The trajectory of the vacuum expectation value as a function of the RG scale is a pure FRG output.
It contains no phenomenological parameters.

The accuracy of this approximation is controlled by the anomalous dimension of the scalar field.
When the anomalous dimension is small compared to the relative change of the vacuum expectation value over the relevant range of scales, the local potential approximation is reliable.
A quantitative analysis of this condition is left for independent nonperturbative computation.

## 5.2Cross-Validation Logic

The cross-validation between the classification framework and the FRG computation employs a three-category logic.

The cross-validation also isolates the scheme dependence of the two approaches.
The analytic conclusions of the classification framework, namely the nonvanishing of the injection term, the sign ofΠ0\Pi_{0}, and the presence or absence of emergence potential, are scheme-independent results.
They do not rely on the choice of regulator or on the strength of couplings.
The FRG computation, performed with a specific regulator on a specific discrete spectrum, independently confirms these conclusions.
The agreement between the two is a direct numerical confirmation of the analytic classification.

The FRG equilibrium flow integrated over the discrete spectrum of the compact internal space gives an enhancement of the effective coupling of order unity.
The spectrum contains no hidden amplification.
This result is a negative finding.
It eliminates the alternative hypothesis that emergence might originate from some concealed property of the spectrum.
Had the FRG flow itself produced a large enhancement, the source of emergence could not have been precisely located.
The source of emergence is cleanly isolated to a single mechanism, the Dyson self-consistent resummation.
The absence of enhancement in the FRG flow acquires a clear physical meaning within the CGC analytic framework.
This indicates that the emergence mechanism is isolated to the ladder resummation, not to the spectrum itself.

The classification rests on three diagrammatic principles derived directly from the coarse-graining operation: momentum-transfer suppression, ladder-topology accumulation, and conservation-law protection.
Together with two rigid attributes of the operator—the nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign of the single-bubble contribution—these define the classification.

The classification criteria establish whether a given operator possesses the potential for emergence.
Whether this potential is realised in the actual dynamics of the Standard Model is a separate question, one that requires quantitative nonperturbative computation.
The classification framework and the functional renormalisation group perform complementary roles.
The classification framework answers whether emergence is possible and along which path.
The FRG answers whether the critical condition is reached.

The quantityΠ0\Pi_{0}is computed along two independent code paths, the diagrammatic pipeline and the direct continuum computation, which agree on the classification-relevant sign structure for every channel (Section5.1).
For the energy–momentum tensor channel the classification returns a verdict that dynamical emergence is possible.
The injection term is nonvanishing and the single-bubble contribution, when activated by geometric curvature, is positive.
The critical conditionV⋅Π0=4/27V\cdot\Pi_{0}=4/27must still be reached.

Two distinct pathways to long-range correlations have emerged from this analysis.
The first is dynamical, proceeding through ladder resummation of composite operators withΠ0>0\Pi_{0}>0.
The second is topological, proceeding through zero modes guaranteed by index theorems on compact spaces.
The CGC framework addresses only the first pathway.
ForF2F^{2}, the dynamical pathway is closed (Π0<0\Pi_{0}<0), but the topological pathway, if realised on a suitable compact space, would provide the gauge zero modes independently of ladder resummation.

The impossibility of ladder accumulation does not imply the absence of gauge interactions.
Gauge bosons exist by virtue of gauge symmetry, independently of the CGC mechanism.

The cross-validation described above establishes the consistency between the analytic classification and the numerical computation at the level of static operator properties.
The framework contains a dynamical dimension.
The irreversibility of coarse-graining supplies the arrow of time.
The RG time inherits this irreversibility and drives the condensation of scalar fields whose effective potential at the ultraviolet scale possesses a negative curvature.
The trajectory of the vacuum expectation value as a function of the RG scale defines an emergence window.
Inside this window the dynamical conditions for pole formation are met.
Once the window is crossed, the pole forms irreversibly and is protected by the conservation laws that guarantee the nonvanishing of the injection term.
The RG time structure extends the correspondence between the emergence framework and classical statistical physics from the spatial momentum level to the temporal level.

The verification on eight channels, summarised in Section5.1, covers all logical possibilities of the classification with no counterexample.
The framework has also passed benchmark tests in three known solvable physical systems.
In the large-NNO(NN) model the classification returns a verdict of dynamical emergence for the conserved Noether current, consistently with the rigorous proof that the spectral function necessarily develops a pole.
In QCD the fermion bilinear is correctly classified as unprotected, producing no false positive for an operator whose chiral symmetry breaking is driven by the NJL mechanism rather than by spectral weight accumulation.
In the free-field limit the sign of the single-bubble contribution reproduces the spin statistics rule.

The large-NNO(NN) model, QCD chiral symmetry breaking and the free-field limit share none of the geometric details of the internal space that drive the emergence mechanism, yet the framework gives correct classifications in all three cases.
This suggests that the core strategy, classifying Feynman diagrams by their momentum transfer and topology and then using conservation laws to determine which classes accumulate, is a general analytic tool for coarse-graining problems not merely a specific instrument of the emergence framework.
Extensions to other areas, such as the fractional quantum Hall effect or strongly correlated electron systems, rely on the same diagrammatic logic, though the specific forms of the conservation laws and the relevant operators differ from one physical setting to another.

The internal consistency checks confirm that the diagram counts, the momentum transfer classification, the topology classification and the conservation law verdicts all agree with the analytic results of Appendix E of Ref.Guo (2026).
The cross-validation with two independent nonperturbative methods, the FRG and the Dyson–Schwinger equationAlkofer and von Smekal (2001), confirms the value ofΠ0\Pi_{0}and its sign structure.
Whether the TT propagator develops a massless pole is a dynamical question addressed in a companion paper.
The entire chain of reasoning proceeds from the conservation laws, through the operator classification and the RG time dynamics, to the localisation of the emergence window and the independent verification of the pole.
No step relies on perturbative expansions or phenomenological parameters.

The physical consequence of theTμ​νT_{\mu\nu}classification, namely that the low-energy effective theory is uniquely Einstein gravity if the critical condition is reached, has been established independently through a uniqueness argument based on Weinberg’s theoremWeinberg (1964).
The classification framework identifies the candidate operators; the theorem fixes the outcome.

A specific contribution of the CGC framework is the identification of topological obstruction as a distinct mechanism that can block zero-mode accumulation even when the dynamical conditions are satisfied.
The scalar glueballG2G^{2}is the example.
Its single-bubble contribution is positive and its injection term is nonvanishing, yet it does not accumulate zero modes because its kinetic operator has a vanishing analytic index on compact spaces.

Several directions remain open.

The CGC framework has a well-defined scope.
It classifies composite operators by the algebraic properties of the symmetries that protect them.
It does not apply to kinetic terms in the Lagrangian, to four-point contact interactions, or to topological terms whose two-point functions are contact terms.

The ladder approximation at the classification stage is used only for the qualitative judgement of whether a pole can form.

The FRG computation is independent of the ladder approximation and does not require a prior completeness proof.
The extension of the framework to finite temperature, finite density and non-equilibrium systems is another direction for future work.
The connection between the Euclidean FRG and the Schwinger–Keldysh real-time FRG formalism, needed for a rigorous treatment of the real-time non-equilibrium dynamics, is left for future work.

The framework developed in this paper is not restricted to the emergence context.
It supplies a general analytic strategy that can be applied to any quantum field theory problem involving coarse-graining and the emergence of long-range correlations.
A structurally parallel logic has been rigorously established in classical statistical physicsDenget al.(2025).
The present work brings this logic into quantum field theory.

## A.4Hermiticity restriction

The spectral representation (8) applies in its standard form to Hermitian operatorsStreater and Wightman (1964); Weinberg (1995).

All fundamental operators in the Standard Model are Hermitian.
The hermiticity restriction does not affect the main applications of this paper.

## A.5Scope of applicability

The transmission mechanism described above assumes that the bare spectral functionρ𝒪​(p2)\rho_{\mathcal{O}}(p^{2})contains no power-law divergences.
For protected operators this condition is guaranteed by Ward identities: conservation laws or BRST symmetry force the divergences to cancel in physical correlation functions, and the bare spectral function satisfies the required regularity conditions.
For unprotected operators, power-law divergences survive in physical correlation functions.
The zero-frequency divergence produced by critical slowing-down is absorbed by these cutoff-dependent divergences, and the spectral function remains continuous at the zero mode.

Section2.2and Section3together form a complete logical chain.
The dynamical mechanism establishes the universal mathematical route by which critical slowing-down induces zero-mode accumulation, together with the conditions under which it applies.
The operator classification confirms that protected operators satisfy those conditions, making zero-mode accumulation inevitable, and that unprotected operators do not, making it impossible.
