## Abstract

The goal of this paper is to consider symplectic singularities of the moduli space of $G$-Higgs bundles over a compact Riemann surface with genus at least $2$. Furthermore, when $G$ is semisimple, we also study its symplectic resolution.

## 1Introduction

LetGGbe a complex connected reductive group. Let us recall that aGG-Higgs bundle on a compact Riemann surfaceΣ\Sigmais a pair(P,Φ)(P,\Phi), wherePPis a principal holomorphicGG-bundle onΣ\SigmaandΦ∈H0​(Σ,P​(𝔤)⊗K)\Phi\in H^{0}(\Sigma,P(\mathfrak{g})\otimes K), whereKKis the canonical bundle onΣ\Sigma. Note thatΦ\Phiis called the Higgs field. The notions of stable, semistable and polystableGG-Higgs bundles are introduced in[10, Definition 2.9]. Note that this is an equivalent definition to semistableGG-Higgs bundles in[18].
Roughly speaking, aGG-Higgs bundle(P,Φ)(P,\Phi)is polystable if it is semistable and the structure group ofPPcan be reduced to a subgroup, and this gives a stable pair, and this corresponds to the process of looking at a polystable vector bundle as a direct sum of stable vector bundles of the same slope.

In this paper, we consider
the moduli space ofGG-Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes over a compact Riemann surface with genusg≥2g\geq 2denoted byMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G). It is well-known that there is a corresponding Betti moduli space (which is also called character variety) denoted byMB​(G)M_{B}(G). When we study the symplectic geometry ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)andMB​(G)M_{B}(G), we have the following natural question:

## Conjecture 1.1.

A moduli spaceMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)admits a symplectic resolution if and only if a moduli spaceMB​(G)M_{B}(G)admits a symplectic resolution.

The parabolic version of this conjecture is also suggested in[16, Conjecture 7.14].
From this conjecture, we consider theGG-Higgs bundle side corresponding to the following result:

## Theorem 1.2.

[13, Theorem 7.1 and Corollary 7.3]Let us assume thatg>1g>1.
WhenGGis semisimple and its Dynkin diagram has noA1A_{1}-component org≥3g\geq 3, then the irreducible component ofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)containing the trivial representation does not admit a symplectic resolution.

The reductive version of this result is discussed in[13, §7.4].

## 1.1Main result

It is known when the Betti moduli space over a compact Riemann surface admits a symplectic resolution or not in[3,13,17]. Using this result, we give the following evidence for Conjecture1.1.

## Theorem 1.3.

(Theorem3.1)
Recall thatg≥2g\geq 2.
Let us assume thatGGis semisimple and the Dynkin diagram ofGGhas noA1A_{1}-component org≥3g\geq 3. Then the irreducible component of moduli spaceMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)corresponding to the identity component ofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)does not admit a symplectic resolution.

The connected reductive group case is considered in Corollary3.5.
Note that the cases of typeAAgroups are already
well-studied in[14,20].

## Theorem 1.4.

[14,20]The following hold true:
- 1.

Wheng≥1g\geq 1andn≥2n\geq 2, the moduli spaceMD​o​lv​c​(GLn)M_{Dol}^{vc}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})has symplectic singularities.
- 2.

They admit projective symplectic resolutions exactly in the casesg=1g=1or(g,n)=(2,2)(g,n)=(2,2).

## Remark 1.5.

It is natural to consider a symplectic resolution ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)wheng=1g=1. In fact, it is easy to check some cases, for example, whenGGisSO2​n+1\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}orSp2​n\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}, we can check that the identity component ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)admits a symplectic resolution (via the Hilbert scheme) using[8, Theorem 4.34].
However, we would like to answer Conjecture1.1over every almost simple group and every component ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)(orMD​o​l​(G)M_{Dol}(G)), so we focus ong≥2g\geq 2case in this paper.

## 2Symplectic singularities

Let us prove the existence of symplectic singularities ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)using the formal neighbourhood ofMB​(G)M_{B}(G). Note that this result is proved in[6, Proposition 6.28]for an arbitrary reductive group; their proof relies on a codimension estimate forMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)with the work of Faltings[7].
In addition, this is also stated in[13, Following paragraph of Theorem G]without proof.

## Remark 2.1.

In fact, Proposition2.2and Theorem2.3are well-known from[13,6].
To be self-contained, we write a brief proof here again.
Note that the proof of the following proposition will be used to prove our main result.

## Proposition 2.2.

Recall thatg≥2g\geq 2. Let us assume thatGGis semisimple, and eitherGGcontains no simple factor of rank11org≥3g\geq 3.
The codimension of the singular locus ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)is at least44.

## Proof.

Let us recall that there are canonical isomorphisms between the formal neighbourhoods ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)andMB​(G)M_{B}(G)from[18, Theorem 10.6].
So we prove this proposition usingMB​(G)M_{B}(G). Recall that from the work of Simpson, the formal neighbourhood of a pointppofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)is the formal completion of its tangent coneC//CG(p)C/\!\!/C_{G}(p), cf.[18, Proposition 10.5]. Note that[13, Theorem G (4)]implies that the codimension of the singular locus of the formal neighbourhood ofppatMB​(G)M_{B}(G)is at least44using[19,Tag 033A]and the fact that if the codimension of the singular locus of a varietyXXis at leastmm(i.e., Serre’s condition(Rm−1)(R_{m-1})), then its local rings also satisfy the condition(Rm−1)(R_{m-1}).

Let us recall that the codimension of the singular locus of the formal neighbourhood ofp~∈MD​o​lv​c​(G)\tilde{p}\in M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)is equal to the codimension of the singular locus of the formal neighbourhood of the corresponding point ofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)(since the formal neighbourhood isomorphism preserves the singular locus). Then since the codimension of the singular locus of the formal neighbourhood of every point inMB​(G)M_{B}(G)is at least44, so the codimension of the singular locus of the formal neighbourhood of every point inMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)is at least44. This implies that the codimension of the singular locus ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)is at least44from[19,Tag 0353]and[19,Tag 00MC].
∎

## Theorem 2.3.

Recall thatg≥2g\geq 2. Let us assume thatGGis semisimple, and eitherGGcontains no simple factor of rank11org≥3g\geq 3.
The moduli spaceMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)has symplectic singularities.

## Proof.

Using the Isosingularity theorem (which shows that formal neighbourhoods ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)andMB​(G)M_{B}(G)are isomorphic, cf.[18, Theorem 10.6]), we can check thatMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)is normal with[17, Theorem A]. (Note that the same method is used whenG=GLnG=\mathrm{GL}_{n}in[20, Theorem 4.6].) Furthermore, this is quasi-projective, for example[11].

To get the result, we show thatMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)has generically non-degenerate Poisson structure, and then by applying Flenner’s theorem (since the codimension of the singular locus is at least44), we can finish the proof.
From[12, Page 705]or[1], the smooth locus ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)has a hyperkähler structure, and so a holomorphic symplectic structure. Then following the proof of[20, Theorem 4.6](sinceMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)is equidimensional) and using Proposition2.2, we can check that the smooth locus ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)admits a symplectic structure.111This is also studied in[4, §4].Furthermore, we can check thatMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)has generically non-degenerate Poisson structure with the fact that the codimension of the singular locus is at least44from Proposition2.2again. So, by applying Flenner’s theorem, we can prove thatMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)has symplectic singularities.
∎

## 3Symplectic resolution

From the existence of symplectic singularities, the natural step is to consider its symplectic resolution.
Our goal is to prove the following.

## Theorem 3.1.

Recall thatg≥2g\geq 2.
Let us assume thatGGis semisimple and the Dynkin diagram ofGGhas noA1A_{1}-component org≥3g\geq 3. Then the irreducible component of moduli spaceMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)corresponding to the identity component ofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)does not admit a symplectic resolution.

## Remark 3.2.

Recall that wheng=2g=2andGGis a direct product ofSL2{\rm SL}_{2}’s, then the corresponding moduli space admits a symplectic resolution from[14, Theorem 1.1].

Before starting the proof of this result, we need to introduce the following lemma and a property of a cone.

## Lemma 3.3.

Let us assume the same condition as in Theorem3.1.
The formal completion of the tangent cone of the trivial representation (denoted byt​r​i​vtriv) ofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)does not admit a symplectic resolution.

## Proof.

Note that the formal neighbourhood oft​r​i​vtrivis well-studied in[13], and we use a proof of Proposition2.2whenp=t​r​i​vp=trivand soCG​(p)=GC_{G}(p)=G. Recall again the formal neighbourhood oft​r​i​vtrivofMB​(G)M_{B}(G)is the formal completion of its tangent coneC//GC/\!\!/G. From[13, §7],CCisNVN_{V}forV=g​𝔤V=g\mathfrak{g}.

From a property of a conical symplectic variety, if the formal completion ofNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gat the vertex admits a symplectic resolution, thenNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gglobally admits a symplectic resolution from the proof of[2, Lemma 6.16].
Conversely, ifNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gglobally admits a symplectic resolution, then the formal completion ofC//GC/\!\!/Gat the vertex admits a symplectic resolution by restriction.
Therefore, in the case of cone,NV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gadmits a symplectic resolution if and only if the formal completion ofNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gat the vertex admits a symplectic resolution.
Therefore, our problem is reduced to check thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gdoes not admit a symplectic resolution.

Note thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gis singular from[13, §7.2](if it is smooth, then obviously it admits a symplectic resolution). Let us prove this by showing thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gis factorial and has terminal singularities.
From,[13, Theorem 4.7]we directly get thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gis factorial.
We can check thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Ghas terminal singularities by showing that it has symplectic singularities and the codimension of its singular locus is at least44, cf.[15, Corollary 1]. We have thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Ghas symplectic singularities from[13, Theorem F]. So if the codimension of the singular locus ofNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gis at least44, then we can get thatNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Ghas terminal singularities. We have that the codimension of the singular locus of the formal neighbourhood is at least44from[13, Theorem G (4)]and the proof of2.2.
Then[19,Tag 0353]gives that𝒪NV⁣/⁣/G,t​r​i​v\mathcal{O}_{N_{V}/\!\!/G,triv}has(R3)(R_{3})property.
With the same logic in[2, Proof of Proposition 6.16], we have thatℂ[NV//G]\mathbb{C}[N_{V}/\!\!/G]is anℕ\mathbb{N}-graded, connected algebra. Then we have thatℂ×\mathbb{C}^{\times}-action onNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gcontracts tot​r​i​vtriv(cf.[5, §2]), and this gives that theNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gitself satisfy(R3)(R_{3})property.
Therefore, this gives that the codimension of the singular locus ofNV//GN_{V}/\!\!/Gis at least44. Recall that a singular factorial variety with terminal singularities does not admit a symplectic resolution, cf.[9, Corollary 1.3]or[2, Proof of Theorem 6.13].
Therefore, we are done.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem3.1.

## Proof of Theorem3.1.

For contradiction, let us assume that the corresponding component ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)admits a symplectic resolution, then any formal neighbourhood of every point of the component ofMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)also admits a symplectic resolution, cf.[20, §4.1 (B)].

Let(Y,y)(Y,y)be the formal neighbourhood inMD​o​lv​c​(G)M_{Dol}^{vc}(G)which corresponds to the formal completion of(T​Ct​r​i​v​(MB​(G)),t​r​i​v)(TC_{triv}(M_{B}(G)),triv).
Then since(Y,y)(Y,y)admits a symplectic resolution, this induces a symplectic resolution on the corresponding formal completion.
However, we already know that the formal completion of(T​Ct​r​i​v​(MB​(G)),t​r​i​v)(TC_{triv}(M_{B}(G)),triv)does not admit a symplectic resolution from Lemma3.3. Then this gives a contradiction, and therefore, we are done.
∎
