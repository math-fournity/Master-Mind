## Abstract

Engineered Complete Intersections (ECI's) are a class of sparse polynomial systems frequently arising in a number of contexts, both in pure mathematics (e.g. enumerative geometry) and applications (e.g. chemical reaction networks). Based on theoretical results given by the first author, we give several contributions. First we give a new effective technique to tropicalize such systems by generalizing the classical notion of mixed subdivisions introduced by Huber and Sturmfels (1995) to ECI's with the particular goal to efficiently count solutions of square systems of equations in ECI form. We further design a tropical homotopy continuation algorithm for computing such mixed subdivisions, inspired by Jensen (2016), Malajovich (2017) and Daisey and Ren (2024). Our techniques can be used to numerically solve such systems by coupling them with the algorithms introduced by Helminck, Henriksson and Ren (2024). Finally, we give an algorithm to compute Newton polytopes of eliminants of ECI's. This gives a new way to compute, for example, Newton polytopes of so-called $A$-discriminants. Coupled with evaluation-interpolation paradigms our algorithm gives an efficient approach to compute such eliminants. We implemented our algorithms in the form of a software package which we use to demonstrate their practical feasibility on a range of examples.

## Abstract

Engineered Complete Intersections(ECI’s) are a class of
sparse polynomial systems frequently arising in a number of
contexts, both in pure mathematics (e.g. enumerative geometry) and
applications (e.g. chemical reaction networks).

Based on the theoretical results given in[Est24,Est25], we give several
contributions. First we give a new effective technique to
tropicalize such systems by generalizing the classical notion of
mixed subdivisions[HS95]to ECI’s with the particular
goal to efficiently count solutions of square systems of equations
in ECI form. We further design atropical homotopy
continuationalgorithm for computing such mixed subdivisions,
inspired by[Jen16,Mal17,DR24]. Our techniques can be
used to numerically solve such systems by coupling them with the
algorithms introduced in[HHR24]. Finally, we give an
algorithm to compute Newton polytopes of eliminants of ECI’s. This
gives a new way to compute, for example, Newton polytopes of
so-calledAA-discriminants. Coupled with evaluation-interpolation
paradigms our algorithm gives an efficient approach to compute such
eliminants.

We implemented our algorithms in the form of a software package
which we use to demonstrate their practical feasibility on a range of
examples.

## Engineered Complete Intersections.

Anengineered complete intersection(ECI) is a class of
polynomial systems constructed as follows: LetA⊂ℤnA\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}be a finite
multiset and letV⊂ℂk×AV\subset\mathbb{C}^{k\times A}be a matrix withk≤nk\leq nwhose columns are indexed byAAand whose rank is equal tokk. Then the associated ECI is the parametric Laurent
polynomial systemfi:=∑a∈Aca​vi,a​𝐱a=0;i=1,…,k,f_{i}:=\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}v_{i,a}\mathbf{x}^{a}=0;\;i=1,\ldots,k,

where thecac_{a},a∈Aa\in A, are symbolic coefficients and𝐱\mathbf{x}is a set ofnnvariables.

In words, such an ECI arises by pluging the monomial functions with
indeterminate coefficientsca​𝐱ac_{a}\mathbf{x}^{a}into the system of linear
equations with matrixVV. In more invariant terms, it is the
intersection of a general coset of a given subtorus in the algebraic
torus(ℂ∗)A(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{A}with a given vector subspace ofℂA\mathbb{C}^{A}.

ECI’s encode parametric polynomial systems where each equation has
coefficients that linearly depend on the remaining equations in a
fixed way for generic choices of coefficients. For this reason,
0-dimensional ECI’s are often referred to asvertically
parametrized systemsin the literature.

Prominent examples of ECI’s are given by systems of critical or
singular points: For example, fixing a setA⊂ℤnA\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}andnnvariables𝐱:={x1,…,xn}\mathbf{x}:=\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right\}as well asfA:=∑a∈Aca​𝐱a,f_{A}:=\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}\mathbf{x}^{a},

the parametric system whose solutions are the
critical points of the projection of the vanishing locus offAf_{A}in
the torus(ℂ∗)n(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}to thex1x_{1}-axis is an ECI, as this class of
systems is given byfA=x2​∂x2fA=…=xn​∂xnfA=0.f_{A}=x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}f_{A}=\ldots=x_{n}\partial_{x_{n}}f_{A}=0.

Notably, ECI’s also appear
as polynomial systems of equations encoding steady states of dynamical
systems coming from so-calledchemical reaction networks, see
e.g.[Con+17,Dic20].

There are many other examples of algebraic varieties and systems of
equations of special interest arising from an ECI, see
e.g.[Est24,Est25,Sel25].

## Contributions and Related Work.

Engineered complete intersections are studied from a theoretical point
of view by the first author of this paper in[Est24,Est25]. More precisely,[Est24,Est25]studied ECI’s from the
point of view oftropical geometry(see
e.g.[MS21,Mik06]for an introduction to this
subject). Our contribution is essentially a set of algorithms that can
be used to compute tropical data associated to ECI’s.

While tropicalizations of polynomial ideals are in general difficult
to compute (see e.g.[Bog+07]),
there are more favorable cases: WhenFFis agenericsystem
of Laurent polynomials with fixed Newton polytopes, its
tropicalization depends only on those Newton polytopes, see
e.g.[ST08,FS97]. WhenFFhas as many equations
as variables, and thus finitely many solutions, and its
coefficients are power series of a parameter (or specialized to any
other valued field) its tropicalization is given by a certain
subdivision of the involved Newton polytopes, called amixed
subdivision, and encodes in particular the number of solutions ofFF, given as themixed volumeof the involved Newton polytopes
by the BKK theorem[Ber75,Kou76,Kho95]. These subdivisions, together with an algorithm to
compute them, were introduced in the celebrated work[HS95]and can be used in particular to numerically
solve such a systemFFusinghomotopy methods[SVW05]in an optimized way. Note that such
systemsFFare particular instances of ECI’s.

In[Est24], it was shown
that the polyhedral framework established by[Ber75,Kou76,Kho95]could be generalized to ECI’s by
replacing the implied Newton polytopes withconewise linear
functions(depending only onAAandVV, and actually moreover
only on the matroid defined by the columns ofVV). The
tropicalization of a generic instance of the ECI given byVVandAAthen depends only on these conewise linear functions and can be
constructed using the theory established in[Est12]. This generalizes the
setting above in the sense that thesupport functionof a
polytope is a conewise linear function and, for a generic Laurent
polynomial system with fixed Newton polytopes, the results of[Est24]then specialize to
the results mentioned above.

When the ECI given byVVandA⊂ℤnA\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}is zero-dimensional (in the
sense thatVVconsists ofnnlinearly independent rows) then the
root count of a generic instantiation of this ECI coincides with themixed volumeof the associated conewise linear functions, a
quantity introduced in[Est12].

Our first contribution is to transport the notion of a mixed
subdivision established in[HS95]for collections of
polytopes to ECI’s. We furthermore give an algorithm to compute such
mixed subdivisions of ECI’s. Importantly, these mixed subdivisions can
again be used to numerically solve generic instances of0-dimensional ECI’s in an optimized way, using the techniques
introduced in[HHR24], and the sum
of the volumes of all the polyhedra contained in this subdivision
coincides with mixed volume of the underlying ECI.

Our algorithm is a tropical version of the homotopy techniques used in
numerical algebraic geometry mentioned above. Suchtropical
homotopyalgorithms were proposed in[Jen16,Mal17]to compute mixed subdivisions
of polytopes, our algorithm can therefore be seen as a generalization
of these techniques to ECI’s. As the algorithms given in[Jen16,Mal17], our algorithm works
essentially by tracking a mixed subdivision along a linear path through
an ambient space of dimension equal to the size ofAA. Each time
this path crosses a facet of a so-calledmixed cell cone, the
mixed subdivision is modified computationally.

In[HHR24], another effective
technique to compute the tropicalization of an ECI was proposed: The
authors showed that it is given by the projection of thestable intersectionof a tropical linear space with a
collection of tropicalizations of hypersurfaces defined by
binomials. The authors note that a direct implementation of the
computation of the tropicalization of an ECI may be challenging, as
the tropical linear space to be computed may be larger than the final
result. Another result based on tropicalizing linear spaces was
given recently in[Fel+26]which in
particular yields an algorithm to compute root counts of generic
instances of ECI’s. This work also gives various bounds on the number
of real solutions of instances of ECI’s.

In order to circumvent the direct computation of a tropical linear
space, a tropical homotopy algorithm is given in[DR24,Dai25]which is able to treat
stable intersections of tropical linear spaces and hypersurfaces and
thus extends beyond the case of ECI’s. This homotopy algorithm relies
theoretically on subdivisions of so-calledmatroid polytopeswhich become rapidly infeasible to handle computationally. As
mentioned in[Dai25], this has
the consequence that the mixed cell cones mentioned above cannot be
computed with directly in the context of this technique and that
instead one has to work computationally withchains of flatsof
matroids which provide a certain encoding of tropical linear spaces[[, see e.g. Theorem 4.2.6
in]]maclaganIntroductionTropicalGeometry2021. We note that no
experimental evaluation of the resulting homotopy algorithm is given
in[DR24,Dai25].

As mentioned, our algorithm instead relies on the conewise linear
functions associated to an ECI constructed in[Est24,Est25]. Each constituent
equationfif_{i}of the given ECI gives one such conewise linear
function and is constructed essentially by cancelling terms infif_{i}usingscalarmultiples of the equationsf1,…,fi−1f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1}. This methodological core results in an algorithm
which is able to manipulate the involved mixed cell cones directly and
thus gives a relatively simple procedure to change the considered
mixed subdivision when a facet of a mixed cell cone is crossed, close
to the one given in[Jen16]. We
evaluate our algorithm experimentally, showing that it is able to
handle many ECI’s of practical interest with relative ease, for
example ECI’s coming from the theory of chemical reaction networks.

Furthermore, a much more general class of polynomial systems
(callednondegenerate upon cancellation, see Section 3 in[Est25]) can be tropicalized
similarly by cancelling terms infif_{i}usingmonomialmultiples of the equationsf1,…,fi−1f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1}. Our algorithmic techniques
thus potentially generalize to this setting as well.

Our algorithm also allows us to make effective thereal
patchworkingtechniques for ECI’s introduced in[Est25]which extend the
classical tropical patchworking techniques given in[Stu94,Vir82]. Combined
with out algorithm, these techniques allow one to algorithmically find
the number of real solutions of an ECI depending on a single parameter
for a sufficiently large parameter value. This combination requires
one to count the number of real solutions of certain polynomial
systems whose supports are vertices of mutually transversal
simplices. We show how to do this algorithmically by reducing to
solving a linear system over the finite field with two elements. As
an application, we construct algorithmically a degree 4 hypersurface
in 3 variables such that all cusp singularities of its discriminant curve
are real, seeFigure˜1.Figure 1:This is the discriminant curve of the projection of a torus into the planeℝy,z2{\mathbb{R}}_{y,z}^{2}, with cusp and self-intersection singular points in blue and red. If the torus is given by a general polynomial equationf​(x,y,z)=0f(x,y,z)=0, the cusps are defined by the ECIf=fx=fx​x=0f=f_{x}=f_{xx}=0.

Our second contribution concerns the following problem: Given an ECI(V,A)(V,A)withA⊂ℤn×ℤkA\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}for integerskkandnnandV∈ℂ(n+1)×AV\in\mathbb{C}^{(n+1)\times A}of rankn+1n+1, the projection of the algebraic
set defined by a generic instance of(V,A)(V,A)to(ℂ∗)k(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}is a
hypersurface𝐕​(g)\mathbf{V}(g)which one may wish to compute. Examples of
particular cases of this problem are given by the computation of
certain discriminants, in particular so-calledAA-discriminants[GKZ94], the computation of critical values of projection
maps[[, see e.g. the introduction
of]]esterovEngineeredCompleteIntersections2025 and the computation
ofminimal polynomialsof dynamical systems with polynomial
right hand sides[MP25].

The Newton polytope ofggis independent of the chosen generic
specialization of(V,A)(V,A), depending only on the tropicalization of(V,A)(V,A), and describing this Newton polytope in terms of the conewise
linear functions associated to(V,A)(V,A)was done from a theoretical
point of view in[Est25].
We give here an algorithm to compute theNewton polytopeofggfrom the data(V,A)(V,A), based on this theoretical description. Knowing
the Newton polytope ofggcan be used to computeggitself, for example
with an evaluation-interpolation approach, or by avoiding a direct elimination
computation using Gröbner bases with an approach similar to the so-called
FGLM algorithm[Fau+93].

When the tropicalization of an algebraic set is known, and its
eliminant is a hypersurface, general techniques exist to compute the
Newton polytope of this hypersurface from the given tropicalization,
see e.g.[ST08,RST25]. In the particular case of
polynomial systems with fixed Newton polytopes and generic
coefficients, the sought Newton polytope is the so-calledmixed
fiber polytopeof the input Newton polytopes[EK08,McM04]. A dedicated algorithm for
computing mixed fiber polytopes was given in[MM25]and therein shown to be much
faster than the more general purpose tropical elimination techniques
mentioned above in the case considered. This algorithm worked by
giving an effective formula which, from a certain mixed subdivision,
extracts a vertex for the sought Newton polytope. Using this formula
one can avoid the potentially costly computation of the complete
tropicalization of the input system. The algorithm we give works along
a similar formula which we prove for the more general case of ECI’s.
We evaluate our algorithm experimentally, showing that it is able to
compute Newton polytopes ofAA-discriminants in times similar or
faster to the techniques dedicated to this case described in[RST25].

Both of our algorithms are implemented in a software package
accompanying this paper, written in the programming languagejulia[Bez+17], and available at

https://github.com/RafaelDavidMohr/MCISubdivisions.jl.

We finally mention that all of our algorithmic techniques actually
work for so-calledmatroid complete intersections(MCI), a
notion introduced in[Est24,Est25]to combinatorially
abstract ECI’s in the same way as matroids combinatorially abstract
complements of hyperplane arrangements.

from[Est24,Est25]in order to state our

to the tropicalization of a positive dimensional MCI are given as

from[Est25]and show how

Section˜4concerns the elimination problem for ECI’s described
above, again in the more general context of MCI’s, and gives an
effective formula for vertices of the sought Newton polytope in terms

can be combined with the technique introduced in[Hug06]in order to compute the full
Newton polytope of the sought eliminant.
