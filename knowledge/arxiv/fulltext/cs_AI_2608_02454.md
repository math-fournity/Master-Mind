## Abstract

Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) is one of the most widely adopted languages for specifying temporal extended objectives in AI, with applications ranging from reactive synthesis to stochastic planning in Markov decision processes and reinforcement learning. Traditionally, solving any of these problems requires translating the LTL specification to a nondeterministic automata on infinite words and then determinizing it, a step that is notoriously difficult in theory and in practice. Recent work has introduced LTLf+, which lifts the finite-trace logic LTLf to infinite traces. LTLf+ has the same expressive power as LTL, yet it retains most of the crucial advantages of its base logic LTLf. Most reasoning in LTLf+ rests on finite automata on finite words, for which we have not only a canonical minimal representation but also an efficient determinization procedure. In this work we present the first translation from LTL to LTLf+. We first normalize an LTL formula into the syntactic reactivity fragment of the Manna-Pnueli hierarchy, to create the general fragment-based shape of LTLf+. We then present linear translations for each individual component of that fragment. As a consequence of this translation, the expanding body of techniques developed for LTLf+ now becomes available to many AI problems currently formulated in LTL. We further show that this comes at no asymptotic cost, as the pipeline from LTL to automaton via LTLf+ remains doubly exponential.

## 1Introduction

Linear Temporal Logic (LTL)(Pnueli1977)is one of the most widely adopted specification languages for reasoning about the temporal behavior of dynamic systems.
It plays a central role in model checking(Baier and Katoen2008), reactive synthesis(Pnueli and Rosner1989), supervisory control(Ehlerset al.2017), and AI planning for temporally extended goals(Bacchus and Kabanza1998; De Giacomo and Rubin2018; Camachoet al.2019).
In robotics,LTLspecifications express complex mission objectives such as persistent surveillance and conditional responses to environmental events(Fainekoset al.2009).
In business process management,LTLcaptures compliance constraints and procedural requirements(Maggiet al.2011).
Across all these domains,LTLprovides a natural and declarative way to specifywhata system should do, without prescribinghow.
Despite its wide applicability, the computational cost of strategic reasoning tasks, such asLTLsynthesis, remains a barrier.
The traditional pipeline requires translating anLTLformula into a nondeterministic Büchi automaton on infinite words and determinizing it.
But the determinization step is notoriously difficult both in theory and in practice(Vardi2007), and has long limited the scalability ofLTL-based synthesis methods.

To sidestep determinization difficulties, state-of-the-artLTLsynthesis tools exploit a normal form that decomposes the formula into a Boolean combination of simpler formulas(Esparzaet al.2024).
Each component can be translated into a deterministic Büchi or co-Büchi automaton, and their Boolean combination yields directly a deterministic Emerson-Lei automaton(Emerson and Lei1987), on which synthesis is then solved.
This normal form is based on Manna and Pnueli’s safety-progress hierarchy(Manna and Pnueli1990), which distinguishes six basic classes of temporal properties: safety (nothing bad ever happens), guarantee (something good eventually happens), their boolean combinations, called obligations, recurrence (something good happens infinitely often), persistence (something bad happens only finitely often), and their boolean combinations, called reactivity.
While the normalization is worst-case exponential, it is now used by the best-performingLTLsynthesis tools, from𝖲𝗍𝗋𝗂𝗑\mathsf{Strix}(Meyeret al.2018)to𝖲𝖾𝗆𝖬𝖫\mathsf{SemML}(Kretínskýet al.2025,2026), which have dominated theLTLsynthesis tracks of SYNTCOMP in the recent years.

Recently a different route has emerged in AI, around the logicLTLf+(Aminofet al.2025).LTLf+builds on the finite-trace logicLTLf(De Giacomo and Vardi2013,2015), which interprets the syntax ofLTLover finite traces. NotablyLTLfformulas can be effectively translated into deterministic finite automata (DFAs).
Furthermore, in contrast to their infinite-word counterparts, DFAs can be efficiently minimized.
As a resultLTLfsynthesis is reaching the performance and scalability of model checking(Duret-Lutzet al.2025), which in turn is an industrial reality(26;27).

LTLf+liftsLTLfto infinite traces, again through the Manna-Pnueli hierarchy.
It places one of four prefix quantifiers over anLTLfformula, requiring it to hold on some prefix (∃\exists, guarantee), on every prefix (∀\forall, safety), on infinitely many prefixes (∀∃\forall\exists, recurrence), or on all but finitely many prefixes (∃∀\exists\forall, persistence).
These quantifiers match exactly the corresponding classes of the Manna-Pnueli hierarchy, with obligations and reactivity captured by their Boolean combinations.LTLf+has the full expressive power ofLTL, yet its reasoning problems are mostly solved on DFAs built from the finite-traceLTLfsubformulas.

## Contribution.

In this paper, we show thatLTL, once put in normal form(Esparzaet al.2024), can be translated intoLTLf+inlinear time.

We thereby close a gap in(Aminofet al.2025)by lifting the existential result to a constructive one.
Further, we pave the way for a new synthesis pipeline, that first convertsLTLtoLTLf+and then continues through theLTLf+synthesis pipeline.
In particular, this allows for DFA-based techniques (e.g. automaton minimization which previously was off limits forLTL) to be applied to problems stated inLTL.
More generally, we establishLTLf+as a practical intermediate language for arbitraryLTLspecifications, thereby enabling the growing ecosystem of DFA-based techniques developed for LTLf to be applied beyond finite-horizon specifications.
We thereby enable future algorithms developed forLTLf+to be applied to the full expressive power ofLTLwithout additional asymptotic cost.

Each temporal operator is a fixpoint. The operatorsF,U, andMareoperatorswith theleastfixpoint (μ\mu) that require a witness in a finite time. The operatorsG,W, andRaregreatestfixpoint (ν\nu) operators

## 3Normalization toΔ2\Delta_{2}Normal Form

It noted above everyLTLformula can be translated to an equivalent formula that belongs toΔ2\Delta_{2}(Lichtensteinet al.1985; Changet al.1992).
The original result, however, uses automata as intermediate representations and incurs a non-elementary blow-up. Recently, a pure formula rewriting system, which is only single exponential and much more effective, was proposed byEsparzaet al.(2024). As it plays a central role in our translation, we provide a brief account of this normalization procedure in this section.

## Intuition.

The normalization classifies the traces of the formula-to-be-normalized by thelimit-behaviorthey exhibit.
A limit-behavior is a pair(M,N)(M,N)of sets of limit formulas, whereMMrecords which least fixpoint subformulas are satisfied infinitely often, andNNwhich greatest fixpoint subformulas will eventually be satisfied forever.
For every limit-behavior, we can rewrite the original formula assuming the current limit-behavior holds.
In particular, we can simplify arbitrary nesting of greatest and least fixpoint operators down to a single alternation (i.e. aΣ2\Sigma_{2}or aΠ2\Pi_{2}formula) when assuming a certain limit-behavior.
The normalized formula is then a disjunction over all limit-behaviors.
Each disjunct contains i) the simplified formula under the current limit-behavior and ii) a check that the current limit-behavior indeed holds.
Crucially, these checks fall into the classesΣ2\Sigma_{2}orΠ2\Pi_{2}which overall yields a Boolean combination of onlyΣ2\Sigma_{2}orΠ2\Pi_{2}formulas, or in other words, a syntacticΔ2\Delta_{2}formula.

## Synthesis with Normal Form.

TheΔ2\Delta_{2}normal form is the standard starting point for reactive synthesis fromLTL(Meyeret al.2018; Kretínskýet al.2025,2026).
To construct a deterministic automaton, we first translate the individual components (i.e. theΣ2\Sigma_{2}bodies and the limit behavior formulae) for each disjunct of the normalization in Theorem4into deterministic Büchi and co-Büchi automata using the simple constructions fromEsparzaet al.(2020). This yields a boolean combination of deterministic Büchi and co-Büchi, or equivalently, a deterministic Emerson Lei automaton, where the state space is the synchronous product and the acceptance condition is the exact same boolean combination of the individual Büchi and co Büchi acceptances. As there are exponentially many deterministic Büchi and co-Büchi automata and each of them is doubly exponential in the original formulas size, the Emerson Lei automaton has(22O​(|φ|))2O​(|φ|)=22O​(|φ|)\big(2^{2^{O(|\varphi|)}}\big)^{2^{O(|\varphi|)}}=2^{2^{O(|\varphi|)}}states. Intuitively, the exponentially many factors are accumulating inside the inner exponent rather than adding a further exponentiation. This means that, even though the normalization yields an exponentially large formula, converting that formula to a deterministic automaton does not become triply exponential. Instead, the normalization allows us to obtain a theoretically optimal and practically tractable LTL-to-deterministic-automaton translation that powers the state of the art synthesis tools Strix(Meyeret al.2018)and SemML(Kretínskýet al.2025,2026).

## 4TranslatingLTLtoLTLf+

The four prefix quantifiers ofLTLf+correspond exactly to the classes of the safety-progress hierarchy ofManna and Pnueli (1990).
The existential quantifier∃\existsexpresses guarantee properties (Σ1\Sigma_{1}).
The universal quantifier∀\forallexpresses safety properties (Π1\Pi_{1}).
Further, the alternating quantifiers∀∃\forall\existsexpress recurrence properties (Π2\Pi_{2}) and the symmetric combination∃∀\exists\forallexpresses persistence properties (Σ2\Sigma_{2})(Aminofet al.2025).
Thus, by design, everyLTLf+formula is a boolean combination of formulae of these 4 fragments.

Consequently, when translating from arbitrary LTL formulas to LTLf+, we first use the normalization of Theorem4to create the general shape of a boolean combination of the 4 fragments.
Recall that this yields a boolean combination of three types of formulas, namely:
- 1.

Π2\Pi_{2}-formulas of the formGF​(σ1)\textsf{G}\textsf{F}(\sigma_{1}), withσ1∈Σ1\sigma_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}
- 2.

Σ2\Sigma_{2}-formulas of the formFG​(π1)\textsf{F}\textsf{G}(\pi_{1}), withπ1∈Π1\pi_{1}\in\Pi_{1}
- 3.

generalΣ2\Sigma_{2}formulas.

where the former two are the checks of the guessed limit behaviors and the latter are the resultingΣ2\Sigma_{2}bodies assuming the respective limit behavior holds.
To complete the translation, it suffices to translate each type of formula to its corresponding LTLf+ fragment.
In the remainder of this paper, we present exactly these translations.

## 8Conclusion

In this paper we showed thatLTLformulas, once in normal form, can be translated intoLTLf+in linear time.
Given anLTLformulaφ\varphi, its normal form is worst-case of size2O​(|φ|)2^{O(|\varphi|)}.
This exponential blow-up, however, does not reflect in a higher complexity of translating LTL to deterministic automata, which remains worst-case in 2EXPTIME (the problem is 2EXPTIME-complete).
In fact, the simplicity of handling the single components makes it the best option for solvingLTLsynthesis and is largely adopted by the state-of-the art synthesis tools(Kretínskýet al.2025,2026; Meyeret al.2018).
Our translation makes the DFA-based technology developed forLTLfavailable toLTLsynthesis.
In particular, all intermediate DFAs can be minimized, which can drastically speed up synthesis in practice.
Furthermore, beyond synthesis, our translation affects all areas which requireLTLto be translated to deterministic (or limit deterministic)
automata.
This includes Markov decision processes(De Giacomoet al.2025), stochastic planning(Weinhuberet al.2026), and reinforcement learning(Hahnet al.2020; Jackermeier and Abate2025; Abateet al.2026).
We thereby establish LTLf+ as a practical intermediate language for arbitrary LTL specifications in a variety of contexts.

We leave the practical realization of this route to future work.
As a first step, we have already implemented the full translation on top of the𝖲𝖯𝖮𝖳\mathsf{SPOT}library(Duret-Lutzet al.2022).
The implementation goes beyond the plain construction and includes several optimizations.
In particular, for the widely used obligation fragment(Duret-Lutzet al.2026)it skips both the
normalization and the tight completion and directly returns obligation formulas
ofLTLf+.

## Appendix ARelated Work

## Normal forms for the safety-progress hierarchy.

The hierarchy originates withLichtensteinet al.(1985), was described in detail and named byManna and Pnueli (1990), and is surveyed byPiterman and Pnueli (2018).
In particular, every formula ofLTLwith past operators is equivalent to a formula⋀i(GF​(φi)∨FG​(ψi))\bigwedge_{i}\big(\textsf{G}\textsf{F}(\varphi_{i})\lor\textsf{F}\textsf{G}(\psi_{i})\big)whereφi\varphi_{i}andψi\psi_{i}contain only past operators(Lichtensteinet al.1985; Manna and Pnueli1990).Changet al.(1992)carried the characterization over to future-onlyLTLand stated the correspondingΔ2\Delta_{2}normalization theorem.
The classical procedures behind these results incur a non-elementary blow-up, translating formulas into counter-free automata and star-free expressions and back.
The elementary alternatives construct deterministicω\omega-automata as an intermediate step and are therefore at least doubly exponential.
We refer toEsparzaet al.(2024)for a detailed description.
TheΔ2\Delta_{2}-normalization ofEsparzaet al.(2024)that our translation builds on is instead direct and purely syntactic, and incurs only a single-exponential blow-up.
Together with the unifiedLTL-to-ω\omega-automata translation ofEsparzaet al.(2020), it has renewed interest in the hierarchy(Aminofet al.2025).

## LTLto deterministic automata.

TranslatingLTLto deterministicω\omega-automata is doubly exponential, and this is tight(Kupferman and Rosenberg2010).
Moreover, the classical route through Büchi automata and Safra-style determinisation is notoriously difficult(Vardi2007).
Deterministic Emerson-Lei automata(Emerson and Lei1987)carry a Muller acceptance condition that is expressed symbolically as a Boolean formula, which makes it possible to shift complexity from the state space to the acceptance condition.
In particular,Müller and Sickert (2017)translate the (co-)safety and fairness fragments ofLTLdirectly, delegate subformulas outside these fragments to external translators, and compose the parts by a product.

## Finite-trace logics andLTLf+.

LTLfinterprets temporal specifications over finite traces, where the automaton counterpart is the DFA(De Giacomo and Vardi2013,2015), and supports a family of scalable synthesis tools(Zhuet al.2017; Bansalet al.2020; De Giacomo and Favorito2021; Duret-Lutzet al.2025).
Its pure-past variant PPLTL sees the trace backwards and admits DFAs of single-exponential size(De Giacomoet al.2020).LTLf+and PPLTL+ lift this machinery to infinite traces through prefix quantification(Aminofet al.2025), with synthesis through Emerson-Lei games and Manna-Pnueli games(Hausmannet al.2025), symbolic algorithms for the obligation fragment(De Giacomoet al.2026), andLTLf+objectives in MDPs(De Giacomoet al.2025; Weinhuberet al.2026).Aminofet al.(2025)prove thatLTLf+captures all ofLTLbut do not provide a translation.
Our translation supplies the missing step and connects the normalization with the prefix quantifiers.
Our tight completion builds on classical prefix characterizations of safety and co-safety(Kupferman and Vardi2001; Cimattiet al.2023).
