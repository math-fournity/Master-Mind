## A torsion-free non-sofic groupFrancesco Fournier-Facio

## Abstract

OpenAI announced the existence of a non-sofic group: the unit group of the binary Leavitt algebra. We exhibit a different source of examples (relying on the same technical criterion) which includes torsion-free groups.

## 1Introduction

On 1 August 2026, OpenAI announced solutions to 10 major open problems in mathematics. In group theory, the target was the problem of the existence of a non-sofic group111OpenAI calls this the “soficity conjecture”[Ope26], but I do not think it was ever conjectured that all groups are sofic, on the contrary, already from the first instance of the name[Wei00]it was believed that there should be a non-sofic group., attributed to Gromov[Gro99]and Weiss[Wei00]. We refer the reader to[CSC23, Chapter 7]for the relevant definitions.

## Theorem 1.1([Ope26]).

There exists a non-sofic group.

The key technical ingredient in the proof is the following, which crucially builds on work of Kun[Kun16]and Kun–Thom[KT19]222Contrary to the claim in the OpenAI announcement on 1 August[Ope26]that there has been “no progress on the main result for at least a decade”. This claim has been redacted on 3 August..

## Proposition 1.2([Ope26, Proposition 2.3]).

Suppose thatG=⟨Γ,t1,…,tm⟩G=\langle\Gamma,t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\ranglewhereti​Γ​ti−1⊂Γt_{i}\Gamma t_{i}^{-1}\subset\Gammafor allii. This proposition is then applied withJJbeing Thompson’s groupVV(which is not LEF, being finitely presented and simple), andGGandΓ\Gammabeing groups of elementary matrices over the binary Leavitt algebraRR. This is a clever choice, because the behaviour in the proposition is very natural for Thompson-like groups, but Thompson-like groups almost never have property(T)\mathrm{(T)}. On the other hand, elementary matrices overRRdo, by work of Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain[EJZ10], becauseRRis a finitely generated ring, and they can be seen as living in the same world asVV, acting on binary strings; this connection has been exploited before, see e.g.[Par11,BSr16,HO17].

In an effort to better understand the solution, and the role that the various parts of the proof play, I explain here how to construct a non-sofic group avoiding Leavitt algebras entirely, while still using Proposition1.2. I do not claim that this proof is easier, better, or more elementary, although it is more natural to me, in that it only uses things that I already knew on 30 July (unlike the connection between Leavitt algebras and Thompson groups, which I learned about by reading the first draft). It is a rather standard argument in small cancellation theory[Osi10,Hul16], which to me indicates that Proposition1.2is the key novelty in[Ope26]. As it often happens with these things, the novelty is not so much in the proof (which is still clever and technical), as in the statement: experts aware of[KT19]would probably have been able to prove Proposition1.2, but they might not have thought of this statement. There are myriad possible statements strengthening[KT19], many of which are surely false, and many of which might be true but have no application. AI models are not inconvenienced by constraints of time and resources, so they get to throw things at the wall and see what sticks.

In the next section I present this alternative construction, which buys some new flexibility:

## Theorem 1.3.

There exists a finitely presented torsion-free non-sofic group.

It is easy to see that for a Leavitt algebraRRlike the one used in[Ope26], the multiplicative groupR×R^{\times}(hence any group of elementary matrices overRR[KT26, Corollary 4.4]) always has torsion444Thanks to Johan Öinert for pointing out that this holds regardless of the finiteness of the base field.. Let us also note that the existence of a non-sofic group immediately implies the existence of a finitely presented one, because non-soficity is an open property in the space of marked groups[CSC23, Proposition 7.5.10], and so[Ope26]already yields a finitely presented non-sofic group. But this argument does not specialise to torsion-free groups, since torsion-freeness is not an open property; here we directly construct a finitely presented example.

## 2The construction

We will use the following input groups:
- •

A universal finitely presented torsion-free groupUU, that is, a finitely presented torsion-free group that contains a copy of every finitely presented torsion-free group[Hig61,BS08,Chi14];
- •

A finitely presented simple torsion-free groupSS, e.g. a Burger–Mozes[BM97]or Hyde–Lodha group[HL25];
- •

A torsion-free hyperbolic property(T)\mathrm{(T)}groupHH, e.g. a random group at suitable density[Żuk03].
