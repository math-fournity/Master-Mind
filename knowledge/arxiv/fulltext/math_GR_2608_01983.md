## Abstract

We prove that the Diophantine problem, i.e. the problem of solving equations with coefficients, in one-relator groups and one-relation monoids is undecidable in general. Specifically, we show that for all $d \geq 2$, the Diophantine problem for the Baumslag-Gersten group $\mathcal{B}_d = \operatorname{BG}(1,d)$ and for a closely related one-relation monoid $\mathcal{M}_d$ are both undecidable. Both results are obtained by an encoding of a recent undecidability result due to Rybalov.

## Abstract.

We prove that the Diophantine problem, i.e. the problem of solving equations with coefficients, in one-relator groups and one-relation monoids is undecidable in general. Specifically, we show that for alld≥2d\geq 2, the Diophantine problem for the Baumslag–Gersten groupℬd=BG⁡(1,d)\mathcal{B}_{d}=\operatorname{BG}(1,d)and for a closely related one-relation monoidℳd\mathcal{M}_{d}are both undecidable. Both results are obtained by an encoding of a recent undecidability result due to Rybalov.The author is supported by KIAS Individual Grant HP094701 at Korea Institute for Advanced Study, and by the Mid-Career Researcher Program (RS-2023-00278510) through the National Research Foundation of the Government of Korea.

One of the central lessons of combinatorial group and semigroup theory in the 20th century is that undecidability is ubiquitous for finitely presented groups and monoids. These developments began in 1947 with the undecidability of the word problem for finitely presented monoids by Markov[27]and Post[41], and continued into the 1950s with the undecidability of the word problem for finitely presented groups by Novikov[34]and Boone[7]. The aforementioned central tenet is highlighted most spectacularly by the mid-1950s Adian–Rabin Theorem[1,42,38], whose content can be summarized in the adage “it is impossible to algorithmically detect most reasonable properties of groups just from a presentation”.

An unexpected exception to this adage appeared in the 1970s and early 1980s in the study ofequationsin groups and monoids. For a group or monoidSS, the algorithmic problem of deciding whether or not a system of equations, including coefficients inSS, has a solution is known as theDiophantine problemforSS. This topic has a rich history, and many natural problems, studied independently, are included within it. For example, the famousconjugacyproblem for a groupGGasks for an algorithmic way to determine if two elementsg,h∈Gg,h\in Gare conjugate or not. This is the same problem as trying to find solutions to the equationX​g​X−1=hXgX^{-1}=h, whereXXis some variable. In free groups, the conjugacy problem is easily decidable, but even the case of understanding the solutions of a single equation – possibly with multiple variables – turned out to be rather challenging, with some initial probes in the 1960s by e.g. Appel[3]and Lyndon & Schützenberger[22]. Equations over free monoids (often calledword equations) saw similar probes, e.g. by Hmelevskii[18]and Lentin[20].

The name “Diophantine” for the problem of solvability of equations in groups and monoids stems from its analogy with Hilbert’s 10th Problem (H10), i.e. the algorithmic problem of determining if a given polynomial has a solution overℤ\mathbb{Z}or not. The analogy is not only surface-level, as best illustrated by the work of Matiyasevich, who investigated connecting H10 with word equations[28]. In particular, he proved that if H10 had a positive solution, then the problem of word equations with “length constraints” would be decidable, thus opening up a route for proving undecidability of H10. It is a curiosity of mathematical development that Matiyasevich[29](building on work by Davis, Putnam, and Robinson[12]) would famously only a few years later prove by a completely different route that H10 has a negative solution.

It was thus a rarity of decidability when Makanin[25]proved in 1977 that the Diophantine problem isdecidablein free monoids. Soon thereafter, he proved the analogous result also for free groups[26]. This surprising decidability has led to the development of a large body of work on equations in free groups and monoids. The free group case was made more tractable by Razborov[43], who introduced what are now known asMakanin–Razborov diagrams. These diagrams, which fall outside the scope of this article, now form a central part of the theory of algebraic geometry over free groups. There has also been recent interest in the language-theoretic properties of solutions to equations in free groups, see[10].

By contrast, even in the case of adding a single defining relation, equations have been much less forthcoming. The conjugacy problem for one-relator groups is a classic problem, with the first major result by B. B. Newman[33], who solved the conjugacy problem in all one-relator groups with torsion. This solution relied on his “Spelling Theorem”, which in modern language has as a key consequence that all one-relator groups with torsion are hyperbolic (see[35]for a historical background). Much later, Dahmani & Guirardel[11]proved that the Diophantine problem is decidable in all hyperbolic groups, and hence also in all one-relator groups with torsion. This, along with a handful of other techniques, including the decidability of the Diophantine problem for all right-angled Artin groups[14], can be exploited to give decidability also for some torsion-free one-relator groups[37].

In spite of these positive results, even the conjugacy problem for torsion-free one-relator groups remains one of the central open problems in the area. Since the conjugacy problem is one of the smallest special cases of the Diophantine problem, this therefore points in the direction of the Diophantine problem for one-relator groups being very difficult. Likewise, the Diophantine problem for one-relation monoids has seen some study, e.g. by Garreta & Gray[16], who obtained a number of reduction results indicating the difficulty of the problem. There also exists a “word-hyperbolic” monoid (defined language-theoretically) with an undecidable Diophantine problem[39], showing that a direct analogue of the aforementioned positive result by Dahmani & Guirardel for hyperbolic groups does not exist.

In this article, we will show that the above difficulty of the Diophantine problem for one-relator groups and one-relation monoids is not illusory. Specifically, we will show that both of the problems are undecidable in general, beginning with:

## Theorem A.

The Diophantine problem for one-relator groups is undecidable in general.

More specifically, we will show that for everyd≥2d\geq 2, the Diophantine problem for theBaumslag–Gersten groupℬd=Gp​⟨p,t∣(t​p​t−1)​p​(t​p​t−1)−1=pd⟩\mathcal{B}_{d}=\textnormal{Gp}\langle p,t\mid(tpt^{-1})p(tpt^{-1})^{-1}=p^{d}\rangle(ℬd\mathcal{B}_{d})

is undecidable. The proof goes by constructing an effective Diophantine encoding of a structure𝒩d\mathcal{N}_{d}insideℬd\mathcal{B}_{d}using equations that detect centralizers and positive powers. The Diophantine problem for𝒩2\mathcal{N}_{2}is undecidable by a recent theorem due to Rybalov[45], and his proof is easily generalizable to alld≥2d\geq 2(see Theorem1.2). This yields the undecidability insideℬd\mathcal{B}_{d}.

The Baumslag–Gersten groupsℬd\mathcal{B}_{d}do not admit a one-relation monoid presentation (as they are not positive one-relator groups[4,40]), but we can nevertheless exploit closely related presentations to find an encoding of the Diophantine problem for𝒩d\mathcal{N}_{d}into that of one-relation monoids. This yields the following.

## Theorem B.

The Diophantine problem for one-relation monoids is undecidable in general.

More specifically, we will show that for everyd≥2d\geq 2, the Diophantine problem for the monoidℳd=Mon​⟨a,t∣t​a2​t=(a​t)d​t​a⟩\mathcal{M}_{d}=\textnormal{Mon}\langle a,t\mid ta^{2}t=(at)^{d}ta\rangle(ℳd\mathcal{M}_{d})

The group defined by the group presentation with the same relation asℳd\mathcal{M}_{d}is isomorphic toℬd\mathcal{B}_{d}, andℳd\mathcal{M}_{d}embeds, by a theorem due to Adian (see §1.6), into that group. We emphasize that there isa priorino direct implication of any kind between TheoremAand TheoremB; their only commonality is the reduction to Rybalov’s undecidability result.

Finally, we note that our results say nothing in the way of undecidability of the conjugacy problem. We remark that Beese[6]solved the conjugacy problem inℬ2\mathcal{B}_{2}(cf. also[15]).

## Remark.

A few days before the finalization of the present article, a preprint by Gray & Levine[17]appeared on the arXiv, where they show undecidability of the Diophantine problem forℬp\mathcal{B}_{p}in the special case thatp≥2p\geq 2is prime. Their method is via reduction to another undecidability result, due to Denef[13]. They do not provide a one-relation monoid with undecidable Diophantine problem, and indeed ask for one as an open problem (their Question 4.1), which our TheoremBthus answers.
