## Abstract

We study the complexity of computing the Threshold dimension of a hypothesis class and its variant, the Extended threshold dimension. For the latter, we prove that it is both NP-hard and co-NP-hard, which (partially) answers an open question of Dmitriev et al. (SODA 2026). Furthermore, by relating the problem to a variant of Maximum Balanced Biclique, we prove strong hardness of approximation for both dimensions, including in the parameterized setting.   As an intermediate result, we also prove hardness (of approximation) results for computing the ladder index and the semi-ladder index (Fabianski et al., STACS 2019), which have recently been used in the design of fixed-parameter tractable algorithms.

## 1Introduction

For a finite space𝒳\mathcal{X}, a hypothesis classℋ⊆{0,1}𝒳\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{X}}is a set of hypothesesh:𝒳→{0,1}h:\mathcal{X}\to\{0,1\}. Roughly speaking, itsThreshold dimensionis the number of thresholds that can be formed from restrictions ofℋ\mathcal{H}. This can be formalized as follows.

## Definition 1(Threshold DimensionShelah (1978); Hodges (1997)).

TheThreshold dimensionof a hypothesis classℋ⊆{0,1}𝒳\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{X}}, denoted byThD​(ℋ)\text{ThD}\left(\mathcal{H}\right), is defined as the largest non-negative integerddsuch that there existsx1,…,xd∈𝒳x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in\mathcal{X}andt0,…,td∈ℋt_{0},\dots,t_{d}\in\mathcal{H}whereti​(xj)=1t_{i}(x_{j})=1if and only ifj≤ij\leq i.

Suchx1,…,xd,t0,…,tdx_{1},\dots,x_{d},t_{0},\dots,t_{d}is referred to as thewitnessofThD​(ℋ)\text{ThD}\left(\mathcal{H}\right).

Threshold dimension is closely related to theLittlestone dimension, which characterizes the optimal mistake bound in online learningLittlestone (1988). In particular, both dimensions are within an exponential factor of each other. More recently, the Threshold dimension has also been used for characterization of PAC learning with (approximate) differential privacyAlonet al.(2019).

Dmitriev et al.Dmitrievet al.(2026)initiated a study of online learningwith replayswhere, in each round, the adversary can choose to reveal either the correct label or the label resulting from an output hypothesis from a previous round. Here mistakes are only counted in the former case. Remarkably, they show that the mistake bound can be characterized by a variant of Threshold dimension, called theExtended threshold dimension. To define this, we recall the definition of closure andff-representation.

## 1.1Our Results

While the computational complexity of other dimensions, such as VC and Littlestone dimensions, have been thoroughly investigatedSchaefer (1999,2000); Mossel and Umans (2002); Papadimitriou and Yannakakis (1996); Frances and Litman (1998); Manurangsi and Rubinstein (2017); Manurangsi (2023); Foucaudet al.(2025), the computational complexity of the Threshold dimension and its variants remains largely unexplored. In this work, we initiate the study on this topic and prove several hardness results.

## Threshold Dimension.

To formally study the computational hardness of computing Threshold dimension, we define the following decision problem111Throughout this work, we assume that the hypothesis class is encoded as a binary matrix{0,1}ℋ×𝒳\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{X}}.:

Problem:Threshold-DimensionInput:A finite space𝒳\mathcal{X}, a hypothesis classℋ⊆{0,1}𝒳\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{X}}, and a target integerk≥0k\geq 0.Question:IsThD​(ℋ)≥k\text{ThD}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\geq k?

Note that this problem is clearly in NP, as the witness (inDefinition1) can be efficiently verified.
Our first result is to show the NP-hardness of this problem:

## Theorem 1.

Threshold-Dimensionis NP-complete.

In certain scenarios, it might be sufficient toapproximatelycompute the threshold dimension. Unfortunately, we show strong hardness of approximation results, under the Gap Exponential Time Hypothesis (Gap-ETH)222Gap-ETHDinur (2016); Manurangsi and Raghavendra (2017)postulates that, for some constantδ>0\delta>0, there is no2o​(n)2^{o(n)}-time algorithm that can distinguish between a satisfiable 3CNF formula and one which is not even(1−δ)(1-\delta)-satisfiable. ## Ladder and Semi-Ladder Indices.

Perhaps interestingly, our hardness results are shown via viewing the problems from a graph-theoretic perspective. In particular, it turns out that Threshold dimension of a classℋ\mathcal{H}and its closureℋ¯\overline{\mathcal{H}}are closely related to the notion of Ladder and Semi-Ladder indices from the parameterized algorithm literatureFabianskiet al.(2019), which we define below.

## Definition 5(Ladder IndexFabianskiet al.(2019)).

We refer to sucha1,…,aℓ,b1,…,bℓa_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}as aladder of orderℓ\ellofGG.

## Definition 6(Semi-Ladder IndexFabianskiet al.(2019)).

Asemi-ladder indexof a bipartite graphG=(L,R,E)G=(L,R,E), denoted bySLI​(G)\text{SLI}\left(G\right), is the largest non-negative integerℓ\ellsuch that there existsa1,…,aℓ∈La_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\in Landb1,…,bℓ∈Rb_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}\in Rthat satisfy the following:
- •

(ai,bi)∉E(a_{i},b_{i})\notin Efor alli∈[ℓ]i\in[\ell], and,
- •

(ai,bj)∈E(a_{i},b_{j})\in Efor alli,j∈[ℓ]i,j\in[\ell]such thati>ji>j.

We refer to sucha1,…,aℓ,b1,…,bℓa_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}as asemi-ladder of orderℓ\ellofGG.

We note that, for any bipartite graphGG,SLI​(G)≥LI​(G)\text{SLI}\left(G\right)\geq\text{LI}\left(G\right). This is simply because any ladder of orderℓ\ellis also a semi-ladder of orderℓ\ell. We can now define the associated computational problems as follows.

Problem:Semi-Ladder-IndexInput:A bipartite graphG=(L,R,E)G=(L,R,E)and a target integerk≥0k\geq 0.Question:IsSLI​(G)≥k\text{SLI}\left(G\right)\geq k?

It is obvious that these problems are in NP. We show that they are NP-hard:

## 6Conclusion and Open Questions

In this work, we show computational hardness (of approximation) for the problem of computing the threshold dimension, the extended threshold dimension, the ladder index and the semi-ladder index. Given that the problem of computing the extended threshold dimension is both NP-hard and co-NP-hard, it remains an interesting question to prove a completeness result (e.g. with respect to the classΠ2\Pi_{2}) for this problem. Another interesting direction is to improve the hardness of approximation factor to|𝒳|1−o​(1)|\mathcal{X}|^{1-o(1)}and|ℋ|1−o​(1)|\mathcal{H}|^{1-o(1)}for (Extended) Threshold dimension, or ton1−o​(1)n^{1-o(1)}for (Semi-)Ladder Index. Whilen1−o​(1)n^{1-o(1)}-factor inapproximability for Maximum Balanced Biclique is knownBhangaleet al.(2016); Manurangsi (2017b), these hardness results arenotin the form ofGap-Balanced-Bicliqueused in our reduction. Therefore, we cannot directly apply our reduction to these results.

## C.2Polynomial-Time Algorithm

While the existence of an intersection-closed representation is not always guaranteed, finding one (when it exists) is computationally tractable, contrasting the hardness of computing the Extended Threshold dimension we proved earlier.

## Theorem 16.
