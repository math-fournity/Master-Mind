## Abstract

In [AS21], Axiotis and Sviridenko conjectured that the linear dependence on the restricted condition number in sparse convex optimization cannot be improved by a polynomial-time algorithm. We establish their conjectured lower bound for least-squares objectives, conditional on the randomized exact-volume Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis in the weighted regular-graph formulation of Raghavendra, Steurer, and Tulsiani [RST12]. Concretely, for every fixed $γ\in(0,1]$, there is no randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, with probability at least $2/3$, returns a vector $x$ such that, writing $s=\lVert x\rVert_0$, \[   \lVert Ax-b\rVert_2^2   \leq   \min_{\lVert z\rVert_0\leq k}\lVert Az-b\rVert_2^2+\varepsilon   \quad\text{and}\quad   s=O\!\left(k\,κ_{s+k}^{\,1-γ}\right), \] where $κ_r$ is the restricted condition number at sparsity level $r$. The result holds even on rational instances with $A$ of full column rank.   The proof was first obtained using a fully automated Gemini-based agentic system developed internally at Google. The authors have verified the proof and edited it for clarity of presentation.

## 1Introduction

Sparse convex optimization seeks a vector with few nonzero coordinates and
small objective value. Given a target sparsitykkand an accuracyε>0\varepsilon>0,
the bicriteria goal is to attain an objective value no greater than the bestkk-sparse value plusε\varepsilon, while allowing the output to use more thankkcoordinates. We study this question for the least-squares objectiveQ​(x)=∥A​x−b∥22,Q(x)=\lVert Ax-b\rVert_{2}^{2},(1.1)

Even the closely related task of finding the sparsest vector subject to a
prescribed residual tolerance is NP-hard[NAT95]. The
central question here is how much additional support suffices for an efficient
additive approximation.

The relevant structural parameter is the restricted condition number. For
least squares, letκr\kappa_{r}denote the ratio between the maximum and minimum
values of∥A​h∥22\lVert Ah\rVert_{2}^{2}overrr-sparse unit vectorshh. Suppressing the
sparsity level in the notation, earlier analyses gave either a support bound

the guarantee holds with high probability[AS21]. They later
gave a deterministic adaptive-regularization method with the sameO​(k​κ)O(k\kappa)support dependence and an improved
runtime[AS22].

Axiotis and Sviridenko discuss in Section 5.1 of their
paper[AS21]a construction that appears in Appendix B of the
full version of Foster, Karloff, and Thaler[FKT15]. They
interpret it as an obstruction matching the linear dependence onκ\kappafor
several greedy and hard-thresholding methods. The construction is
algorithm-specific and does not rule out a different polynomial-time
algorithm. Axiotis and Sviridenko therefore formulated the remaining question
as Conjecture 27[AS21]. Writingγ\gammafor the proposed

The occurrence ofsson both sides is intentional: the restricted condition

## 1.3Related work

For sparse convex optimization, early algorithms either retained a dependence
on the requested accuracy or gave accuracy-independent support bounds of orderk​κ2k\kappa^{2}[NAT95,SSZ10,JTK14].
Axiotis and Sviridenko introduced Adaptively Regularized Hard Thresholding and
obtained anO​(k​κ)O(k\kappa)support bound with high probability, removing both the
accuracy dependence and the extra factor ofκ\kappa[AS21]. Their later deterministic variant retains
theO​(k​κ)O(k\kappa)support guarantee, improves the runtime, and does not require
prior knowledge of the optimal value or sparsity[AS22].

Classical worst-case hardness results for sparse regression are not
parameterized by the restricted condition number. Natarajan proved that it is
NP-hard to find the sparsest vector attaining a prescribed residual
tolerance[NAT95]. Foster, Karloff, and Thaler introduced a
bicriteria(g,h)(g,h)-sparse-regression formulation that simultaneously relaxes
support and prediction error[FKT15]. For anm×pm\times pdesign, under the assumptionNP⊈BPTIME​(Npolylog⁡N)\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{BPTIME}(N^{\operatorname{polylog}N}), whereNNdenotes the input
length, their main result shows that for any fixed positive constantsδ,C1,C2\delta,C_{1},C_{2}there are functionsg​(p)∈2Ω​(log1−δ⁡p)g(p)\in 2^{\Omega(\log^{1-\delta}p)}andh​(m,p)∈Ω​(pC1​m1−C2)h(m,p)\in\Omega(p^{C_{1}}m^{1-C_{2}})for which no quasipolynomial-time
randomized algorithm exists. Under the Biregular Projection Games Conjecture
together withNP⊈BPP\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{BPP}, they obtain analogous
hardness against randomized polynomial-time algorithms withg​(p)∈pΩ​(1)g(p)\in p^{\Omega(1)}, and they establish corresponding results for a noisy
statistical formulation. These theorems give strong general bicriteria
inapproximability, but do not calibrate the hardness toκ\kappa.

Separate from those complexity-theoretic results, Appendix B of the full
version of Foster, Karloff, and Thaler gives a concrete bad instance for
forward stepwise selection[FKT15]. In Section 5.1,
Axiotis and Sviridenko interpret this construction as a linear-in-κ\kappabarrier for OMP, OMPR, IHT, and Partial Hard Thresholding, and note that the
same example applies to ARHT and Exhaustive Local
Search[AS21]. Their discussion also lists LASSO; the Foster,
Karloff, and Thaler full version itself states that the example thwarts LASSO
but does not include that proof. For the OMP-like methods, the mechanism is
explicit: the gradient criterion, when initialized outside the optimal
support, favors distractor coordinates and never enters that support. This is
an algorithm-specific obstruction, rather than a complexity lower bound for
arbitrary polynomial-time algorithms.

Conjecture 27 of Axiotis and Sviridenko asks whether the exponent ofκ\kappacan be improved by any polynomial-time algorithm. Our result addresses this
remaining question for least squares: conditional onSection2.1, it
rules out the support boundO​(k​κs+k1−γ)O(k\kappa_{s+k}^{1-\gamma})for arbitrary
randomized polynomial-time algorithms.
Thus the lower bound is directly parameterized by the same restricted
condition number that appears in the upper bounds.

The Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis arose from work relating graph expansion to
the Unique Games Conjecture[RS10]. Reductions among
exact-volume and robust expansion problems were developed by Raghavendra,
Steurer, and Tulsiani[RST12]. The hypothesis used
here concerns randomized hardness of this exact-volume problem on rational
weighted regular inputs. Our main soundness step converts expansion at one
prescribed cardinality into a bound on the nonexpanding core of an arbitrary
relaxed support.
