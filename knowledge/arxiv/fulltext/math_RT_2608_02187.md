## Introduction

Let(W,S)(W,S)be a Coxeter system, and letℍ:=ℍ​(W,S)\mathbb{H}:=\mathbb{H}(W,S)be the corresponding Iwahori–Hecke algebra.
AHecke categoryis a graded additive monoidal categoryℋ\mathcal{H}which categorifiesℍ\mathbb{H}.
Such categories (and their categorical actions) are ubiquitous in Lie theory.
In characteristic0their behavior is often determined by Kazhdan–Lusztig theory, which gives insight into how Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials control the categories they act on (e.g. the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures for the BGG category𝒪\mathcal{O}).
In positive characteristic, Hecke categories are used todefinepp-Kazhdan–Lusztig theory, providing a replacement for Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials with applications to modular representation theory.

As with the Hecke algebra itself, the most general construction of a Hecke category is via a (diagrammatic) presentation in terms of generators and relations due to Elias–Williamson[10].
The fundamental input data for such a presentation is arealizationof the Coxeter system(W,S)(W,S).
Realizations generalize the notion of a reflection representation (specifically that of areflection faithfulrepresentation) with labeled roots and coroots.
Such representations are the foundation for Soergel’s original algebraic categorification of the Hecke algebra[18].
In the diagrammatic setting, realizations were first introduced by Elias in[8].

In many applications the underlying realization is the action of a (finite or affine) Weyl group on a Cartan subalgebra, which is almost always well behaved. However, finding the correct completely general definition is surprisingly subtle.
For example, 7 years after introducing general realizations Elias–Williamson showed that the original definition contained an incorrect inference[11]; this error was subsequently corrected by the author in[13].
Moreover, in the literature the relatively weak condition ofDemazure surjectivityis often freely assumed to ensure that the corresponding diagrammatic category actually categorifies the Hecke algebra.
Foranti-sphericalquotients of Hecke categories, Libedinsky–Williamson require an additional strong assumption (theparabolic property) which often fails in positive characteristic[16].
Both of these conditions add complexity to an already complicated theory.

The goal of this paper is to simplify the theory of diagrammatic Hecke categories by showing that these assumptions are broadly unnecessary.
Our main results are as follows.

## Theorem A(Corollary3.7).

The diagrammatic category constructed by Elias–Williamson always admits a quotient which categorifies the Hecke algebra.

## Theorem B(Corollary4.5).

The anti-spherical quotient of a Hecke category (à la Libedinsky–Williamson) always categorifies the anti-spherical module for the Hecke algebra.

In addition, we provide an easier way to check forbalancedness, another assumption on the realization which simplifies some of the notation in the diagrammatic Hecke category.

## Theorem C(Corollary2.5).

A realization is balanced if and only if for all distincts,t∈Ss,t\in Sfor whichs​tsthas odd ordermm, the(s,t)(s,t)th entry of the Cartan matrix is a root of the minimal polynomial of−2​cos⁡(π/m)-2\cos(\pi/m)overℤ\mathbb{Z}.

This follows from a calculation of the general rotational scalar[m−1]s[m-1]_{s}in Theorem2.3, which has applications to unbalanced realizations.

## 2.Unbalanced realizations without quantum numbers

Recall that a realization of a Coxeter system(W,S)(W,S)is calledbalancedif for all distincts,t∈Ss,t\in Swithms​t<∞m_{st}<\infty, the corresponding two-colored quantum numbers[ms​t−1]s[m_{st}-1]_{s}and[ms​t−1]t[m_{st}-1]_{t}are both11.
The majority of the literature on diagrammatic Hecke categories solely treats balanced realizations, as they are notationally more straightforward to work with.

## 3.Demazure surjectivity and cinched Hecke categories

An extremely common assumption on realizations in the literature is Demazure surjectivity, which we recall (and slightly generalize) below.

## Definition 3.1.

LetVVbe a realization of(W,S)(W,S)over𝕜\Bbbk.
- •

## Remark 3.5.
- (1)

We have included the term “cinched” here only to avoid potential confusion with the original Elias–Williamson construction.
Once we have shown thatℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}is better behaved, and in particularalwayscategorifies the Hecke algebra, it is reasonable to redefine the terms “diagrammatic Bott–Samelson category” and “diagrammatic Hecke category” so that they are always “cinched” (i.e. always include the cinching relation) (cf.[4]).
- (2)

Many results forℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}carry over without change.
For example, it is easy to see that the construction of the cinched Bott–Samelson category is functorial in the realization, and that localization is still well defined whenever the roots of the underlying realization are all non-zero.

Recall thelight leavesanddouble leavesconstructions for𝒟BS\mathcal{D}_{\rm BS}[10, §6.1, §6.3].
These diagrammatic constructions carry over identically to the cinched Hecke category. The main result in this section is the following.

## Theorem 3.6(cf.[10, Theorem 6.11, Proposition 7.6]).

The light leaves and double leaves constructions yield bases for theHom\operatorname{Hom}-spaces of the cinched Bott–Samelson categoryℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}.

As in𝒟BS\mathcal{D}_{\rm BS}this immediately implies the following.

## Remark 3.10.

The situation for diagrammaticsingularHecke categories is completely different.
In this setting the invariant ringsRIR^{I}for allfinitaryI⊆SI\subseteq S(i.e. for whichWIW_{I}is finite) play a key role.
For all finitaryI⊂JI\subset J, the diagrammatic relations themselves posit the existence of a Demazure operator∂JI:RI→RJ\partial_{J}^{I}:R^{I}\rightarrow R^{J}which givesRJ⊂RIR^{J}\subset R^{I}a Frobenius extension structure (see e.g.[9, §24.2.1]. Unlike in the regular setting this condition naturally places restrictions on the base ring𝕜\Bbbkand the Cartan matrixAA.
To see this, supposeVVis a realization for which the Demazure operators{∂JI:I⊂J​finitary}\{\partial_{J}^{I}:I\subset J\text{ finitary}\}are all surjective.
There is a natural morphismϕ:V→VA,sc\phi:V\rightarrow V_{A,{\rm sc}}by Lemma1.7, which extends to a morphismϕ:R→RA,sc\phi:R\rightarrow R_{A,{\rm sc}}between the corresponding symmetric algebras.

The problem of determining precisely when this can happen dates back to Demazure’s original paper introducing Demazure operators[7]!

It also shows that the local intersection forms (see e.g.[9, Definition 11.74]) for the two categories coincide.
The second part follows because local intersection forms determine the decompositions of the Bott–Samelson objects into direct sums of indecomposable objects[9, Corollary 11.75]∎

We can use this to say a little more about the relationship between the cinched Bott–Samelson categoryℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}and the Elias–Williamson diagrammatic category𝒟BS\mathcal{D}_{\rm BS}.

## Proposition 4.2.

IfVVis coroot surjective, thenℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}is equivalent to the Elias–Williamson diagrammatic category𝒟BS\mathcal{D}_{\rm BS}.
More generally, letV↪V′V\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}be an embedding ofVVinto a coroot surjective realizationV′V^{\prime}, which extends to an embeddingR↪R′R\hookrightarrow R^{\prime}of their symmetric algebras.
Let𝒟BS′\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}denote the Elias–Williamson diagrammatic category constructed fromV′V^{\prime}.
Then there is an embeddingℋBS→𝒟BS′\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}which induces an isomorphism𝒟BS′≅R′⊗RℋBS\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}\cong R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}.

In other words, the cinched Bott–Samelson categoryℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}is equivalent to the usual Elias–Williamson diagrammatic category𝒟BS\mathcal{D}_{\rm BS}whenever the latter is well behaved.
MoreoverℋBS\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}is always a monoidal subcategory of a well-behaved Elias–Williamson diagrammatic category𝒟BS′\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}.

## Proof.

IfVVis coroot surjective, then the cinching relation already holds in𝒟BS\mathcal{D}_{\rm BS}[10, (5.14)–(5.15)].

For generalVV, letV′V^{\prime}be a coroot surjective realization with an embeddingV↪V′V\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}, and letℋBS′\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}denote the corresponding cinched Bott–Samelson category.
The composition of the induced functorℋBS→ℋBS′\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}with the equivalenceℋBS′→∼𝒟BS′\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}gives theHom\operatorname{Hom}-formula above.
∎

Next, we recall that a consequence of Soergel’s categorification theorem is that the indecomposable objects give rise to acanonical basisof the Hecke algebra.
For example, thepp-canonicalorpp-Kazhdan–Lusztig basisarises from the Hecke category for a Cartan realization of a finite or affine Weyl group over a field𝕜\Bbbkof characteristicpp.
In principle such canonical bases depend on the realization, and indeed there are numerous examples which showcase dependency on the characteristicppor the Cartan matrixAA.
In fact, these essentially turn out to be the only things the canonical basis can depend on.
This has been observed before for thepp-canonical basis[17, Remark 10.17(2)].
The only potential difficulty in formulating a more general result is describing precisely what it means for two realizations over different base rings to have “the same” Cartan matrix.

## Proposition 4.3.

Suppose𝕜\Bbbkis a henselian local ring with residue field𝔽\mathbb{F}.
LetAAdenote the Cartan matrix of the realizationVV, and let𝔽′\mathbb{F}^{\prime}be the subfield of𝔽\mathbb{F}generated by its prime subfield and the images of the entriesas​ta_{st}ofAAin𝔽\mathbb{F}.
The canonical basis of the Hecke algebra arising fromℋ\mathcal{H}depends only on𝔽′\mathbb{F}^{\prime}and image ofAAin𝔽′\mathbb{F}^{\prime}.

## Proof.

IfBBis an indecomposable object inℋ\mathcal{H}, then its image in𝔽⊗𝕜ℋ\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{H}is also indecomposable by the usual idempotent lifting argument.
The latter is the cinched Hecke category for the𝔽\mathbb{F}-realization𝔽⊗𝕜V\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}V, so without loss of generality we may assume that𝕜\Bbbkis a field.

Let𝕜′\Bbbk^{\prime}be the subfield of𝕜\Bbbkgenerated by the prime subfield and the images of the entries of the Cartan matrix, and letV′V^{\prime}denote the restriction ofVVto a𝕜′\Bbbk^{\prime}-module.
ClearlyV′V^{\prime}is a𝕜′\Bbbk^{\prime}-realization, and for the corresponding cinched Bott–Samelson categoryℋBS′\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}^{\prime}we haveℋBS=𝕜⊗𝕜′ℋBS′\mathcal{H}_{\rm BS}=\Bbbk\otimes_{\Bbbk^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\rm BS}, and similarly for the local intersection forms of the two categories.
Recall that the decomposition of each Bott–Samelson object into a direct sum of indecomposable objects is determined by the ranks of these forms.
As the rank of a matrix does not change after field extension, this shows that indecomposable objects inℋ′\mathcal{H}^{\prime}remain indecomposable inℋ\mathcal{H}.
So without loss of generality we may further assume that𝕜=𝕜′\Bbbk=\Bbbk^{\prime}.

To complete the proof we consider the morphismVA,ad→VV_{A,{\rm ad}}\rightarrow V, which gives rise to a monoidal functorℋA,ad→ℋ\mathcal{H}_{A,{\rm ad}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}by Lemma4.1.
This functor maps indecomposable objects to indecomposable objects, so the canonical bases arising from both cinched Hecke categories must coincide.
∎
