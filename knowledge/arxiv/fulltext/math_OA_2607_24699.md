## Abstract

We show that finiteness of diagonal dimension, in the sense of Li--Liao--Winter, passes to intermediate sub-C*-algebras. More specifically, we obtain an upper bound for the diagonal dimension of the pair of an intermediate sub-C*-algebra in a C*-diagonal together with the diagonal, which we estimate in terms of the diagonal dimension of the ambient pair and the covering dimension of the diagonal's spectrum. This generalizes a theorem of Archbold and Kumjian stating that intermediate sub-C*-algebras of AF-diagonals are AF C*-algebras.

## Introduction

Dimension theories are omnipresent across all subjects of mathematics, with various notions of dimension recording different types of data associated to each class of objects. For topological spaces, among the most prominent notions of dimension is that ofcovering dimension: a space has covering dimension at mostd∈ℕd\in\mathbb{N}when every finite open cover can be refined to one such that at mostd+1d+1of its sets can simultaneously intersect[12]. With the view ofC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras as noncommutative topology via Gelfand duality, the notion of covering dimension was extended to the noncommutative setting with the seminal notions ofnuclear dimensionanddecomposition rankintroduced by Winter and his collaborators[17,39](cf.[35]). Roughly speaking, just as covering dimension of a space measures its complexity through the least number of overlapping open sets in a refining cover, the nuclear dimension of aC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra measures the least number of finite-dimensional summands needed to approximate the identity map with a sum of completely positive contractive (c.p.c.), orthogonality-preserving (i.e.order zero) maps – see[37, Definition 1.5]for the precise definition.

Along with𝒵\mathcal{Z}-stability (i.e. tensorial absorption of the Jiang–Su algebra𝒵\mathcal{Z}), nuclear dimension plays a key role in Elliott’s classification programme: unital, simple, separable, nuclear, UCTC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras with finite nuclear dimension fall within the scope of the celebrated Classification Theorem[34, Theorem 5.3]. With such a powerful result at hand, establishing finiteness of the nuclear dimension of variousC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras is an imperative task to ensure that they areclassifiable, i.e. they lie in the domain of the Classification Theorem. Some notions of dimension whose theory was developed with one of the main goals being to obtain upper bounds for the nuclear dimension of associatedC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras include those ofRokhlin dimension[15,33,31], (fine)tower dimension[16], anddynamic asymptotic dimension[14,5].

After X. Li’s breakthrough[23]on Cartan subalgebras (in the sense of Renault[29]) andC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-diagonals (in the sense of Kumjian[21]; seeDefinition1.1) in classifiableC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras, K. Li, H. Liao and W. Winter introduced thediagonal dimension of sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras[24], a definition akin to that of the nuclear dimension of the ambientC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra that further aims to keep track of the positioning of the sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra – seeDefinition1.2. In the non-degenerate case, the diagonal dimension of aC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-pair encodes information about the normalizers and the (partial) homeomorphisms that these induce on the spectrum of the abelian sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra: when it is finite, the sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra is a diagonal, and for diagonals arising from dynamics or groupoids, its value is tightly linked respectively to the fine tower dimension or the dynamic asymptotic dimension of the underlying object (cf.[24, Theorems B, E]), thus making it a prominent definition for a potential (classification and) regularity theory forC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-diagonal pairs (cf.[30, Problem XLVIII]).

Nuclear dimension enjoys many permanence properties (e.g. it is non-increasing with respect to hereditary sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras), but it is not monotone with respect to arbitrary sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras. This is rather natural since aC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra can contain much wilder sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras, with Kirchberg’s𝒪2\mathcal{O}_{2}-embedding andM2∞M_{2^{\infty}}-subquotient theorems[4, Theorem IV.3.4.18]being prime examples of this phenomenon – in particular, an arbitrary sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra of a nuclearC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra need not be nuclear. Nevertheless, Archbold and Kumjian showed in[2]that some well-positioned sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras retain nuclearity: more precisely, they proved that sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras of uniformly hyperfinite (UHF) algebras that contain anapproximately finite-dimensional(AF)diagonal, are not only nuclear, but even AFC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras (see also[1]). Recall that a diagonal(D⊂A)(D\subset A)is anAF-diagonalwhen it is the inductive limit of a system of masas(DFi⊂Fi)(D_{F_{i}}\subset F_{i})in finite-dimensionalC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebras with normalizer-preserving connecting maps. Every AF algebra admits an AF-diagonal[32], and conceptually understanding when a sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra is an AF-diagonal is a problem of current interest[30, Problem XLVII].

Just as nuclear dimension zero is equivalent to theC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra being an AF algebra, diagonal dimension zero of a (non-degenerate) sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra is equivalent to the pair being an AF-diagonal[24, Theorem 4.1]. This allows us to interpret the theorem of Archbold and Kumjian as a statement for dimension zero, which brings us to the main result of this paper. In what follows we use the notationdim+1(.)\dim^{+1}(.)as shorthand fordim(.)+1\dim(.)+1.

## Remark 4.2.

It is not clear at the moment whether the upper bound of (4.1) can be improved by removing the factordim+1D^\dim^{+1}\widehat{D}. More precisely, we have no example of aC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-diagonal(D⊂A)(D\subset A)with spectrum of covering dimension at least one (since for zero-dimensional spectra this discussion is vacuous) and an intermediate sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebraBBsuch that the diagonal dimension of(D⊂B)(D\subset B)is greater than that of(D⊂A)(D\subset A).

The diagonal dimension is strongly related to the dynamic asymptotic dimension of the underlying Weyl groupoid (in the sense of[29, Definition 4.2]), but the precise relation remains unclear (cf.[24, Remarks 6.8 and 6.9, Question 6.11]). Since by[7,8]an intermediate sub-C∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebraBBin our case is the (possibly twisted) groupoidC∗\mathrm{C}^{\ast}-algebra of a wide,333A subgroupoid is calledwidewhen it has the same unit space as the ambient groupoid.open subgroupoid of the Weyl groupoid of(D⊂A)(D\subset A), and since dynamic asymptotic dimension seems to be monotone with respect to wide open subgroupoids[5], obtaining a precise relation between the diagonal dimension of a pair and the dynamic asymptotic dimension of the underlying twisted groupoid could possibly shed more light on this. To the best of my knowledge, this topic will be further explored in upcoming work of C. Bönicke and K. Li.
