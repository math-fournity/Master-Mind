## Abstract.

We use families of generalized Seifert matrices to define algebraic concordance groups for links with non trivial Alexander polynomial. We then use these groups to recover the invariance by concordance of the signature, the Fox-Milnor condition for concordant links and the invariance by concordance of the Witt class of the Blanchfield pairing for links. Finally, we use these invariants to investigate the isomorphism types of these groups.

## 1.Introduction

## 1.1.Historical background and motivation

Two knots inS3S^{3}are calledtopologically concordantif they cobound a locally flat annulus inS3×[0,1]S^{3}\times[0,1]. Concordance is an equivalence relation, and the set of concordance classes of knots forms a group, called theknot concordance group, for the operation induced by connected sum. The neutral element is given by the class ofsliceknots, i.e. knots that bound a (locally flat) embedded disc inB4B^{4}. This group was introduced in 1966 by Fox and Milnor[13]. One way to study the knot concordance group is via its algebraic version, which is defined as follows. A Seifert matrixAAfor a knot is calledmetabolic, or equivalentlyalgebraically sliceif it is congruent overℤ\mathbb{Z}to a block matrix of the form(0CDE)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&C\\
D&E\end{smallmatrix}\right), withCC,DDandEEsquare matrices half the size ofAA. Two Seifert matricesAAandBBare calledWitt equivalent, or equivalentlyalgebraically concordantif the direct sumA⊕−BA\oplus-Bis metabolic. Witt equivalence is an equivalence relation on the set of Seifert matrices, and the set of Witt equivalence classes form a group, called thealgebraic concordance group, for the operation induced by the direct sum. The neutral element is given by the class of metabolic Seifert matrices.

In[20], Levine showed that slice knots have algebraically slice Seifert matrices. This property ensures that associating to the concordance class of a knot the algebraic concordance class of its Seifert matrices yields a well defined homomorphism from the knot concordance group onto the algebraic concordance group. Therefore, any invariant of the algebraic concordance group defines an invariant of knot concordance. These are calledalgebraic concordance invariants of knots, and knots with algebraically concordant Seifert matrices are called algebraically concordant. In the early days of knot concordance, all concordance invariants were algebraic concordance invariant. Among these, let us mention the Levine-Tristram signature[20,28], studied by Murasugi[24], the Arf invariant[26], the Fox-Milnor condition on the Alexander polynomial[13], the Blanchfield pairing[1], and invariants coming from Witt groups of finite fields. By combining these invariants, Levine showed that the algebraic concordance group is isomorphic toℤ∞⊕ℤ2∞⊕ℤ4∞\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\infty}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{\infty}[19].

Let us mention that, in higher dimensions, i.e. knotted spheresSnS^{n}inSn+2S^{n+2}forn>1n>1, this completely classifies concordance of knots. Indeed, Levine[19]showed that in this case, the homomorphism associating to the concordance class of a knot the algebraically concordance class of its Seifert matrix is an isomorphism. However, this does not holdn=1n=1. Indeed, the existence of elements in the kernel, i.e. algebraically slice knots which are not slice was established in 1976 by Casson and Gordon[2,3].

Concordance of links is defined in a similar manner : two links inS3S^{3}aretopologically concordantif they cobound disjoint locally flat annuli properly embedded inS3×[0,1]S^{3}\times[0,1], each intersectingS3×{0}S^{3}\times\{0\}andS3×{1}S^{3}\times\{1\}. A link is calledsliceif it bounds disjoint locally flat embedded discs inB4B^{4}. Even though concordance is an equivalence relation on the set of links, there is no natural group structure on the set of equivalence classes. This is because for more than one component, the connected sum operation depends on the choice of a component, and therefore does not yield a well defined operation. However, analogues of Seifert matrices do exist for links. These are calledgeneralized Seifert matrices, introduced by[11,7], and can be used to compute several link invariants, such as the multivariable Levine-Tristram signature[5], the multivariable Alexander-Conway polynomial[7], or the multivariable Blanchfield pairing[8,10].

Event though the analogue of Seifert matrices for links is well defined, there is so far no construction of an algebraic concordance group for links. The present work is an attempt to fill this gap. Using generalized Seifert matrices of links with non trivial Alexander polynomial, we construct algebraic concordance groups for links, and use them successfully to recover known results such as the invariance by concordance of the signature[5,9], the Fox-Milnor condition[17]and the invariance by concordance of the Witt class of the Blanchfield form, see e.g.[16].

## 1.2.Paper outline and statement of the results

Throughout this note, we work in the setting of colored links. Aμ\mu-colored linkis an oriented link together with a surjective map assigning to each component a color in{1,…,μ}\{1,\ldots,\mu\}. This framework is a natural setting which allows to consider all at once oriented links, corresponding to the caseμ=1\mu=1, and ordered links, which correspond to the case whereμ\muequals the number of components. All the necessary background on colored links and generalized Seifert matrices necessary for this note is contained in Section2.

Section3is our main section, devoted to the construction of algebraic concordance groups forμ\mu-colored links. Our idea is to mimic the construction of the classical algebraic concordance group to links, using generalized Seifert matrices. There are three main differences compared to knots. First, generalized Seifert matrices forμ\mu-colored links come in families of2μ2^{\mu}matrices, depending on asignε∈{±}μ\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}^{\mu}. Even though in the case of oriented links one of the two Seifert matrices determines the whole pair (via transposition), this is not the case forμ≥2\mu\geq 2. Hence, we consider families of matrices to define algebraic concordance. More precisely, we consider the setℳμ\mathcal{M}_{\mu}of all families of integral matrices{Aε}ε∈{±}μ\{A^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}^{\mu}}of the same size, satisfyingA−ε=(Aε)TA^{-\varepsilon}=(A^{\varepsilon})^{\operatorname{T}}for allε\varepsilon.

The second difference is that ifAAis a Seifert matrix for a knot, the matrixA−ATA-A^{\operatorname{T}}is non degenerate (in fact non singular). This property is key in proving that Witt equivalence is an equivalence relation. Analogues of this property do not always hold for families of generalized Seifert matrices, as illustrated in Example3.12. To circumvent this issue, we restrict to families of matrices withnon trivial Alexander polynomial. In the following definition,Λ:=ℤ​[t1±1,…,tμ±1]\Lambda:=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{\mu}^{\pm 1}]denotes the ring ofμ\mu-variable Laurent polynomials.

## Remark 1.6.
- (i)

As a metabolic family necessarily consists of matrices of even size, matrices of Witt equivalent families must have size of the same parity.
- (ii)

In the caseμ=1\mu=1, by Remark1.4, two pairs of matrices{A,AT}\{A,A^{\operatorname{T}}\}and{B,BT}\{B,B^{\operatorname{T}}\}are Witt equivalent if and only ifA⊕−BA\oplus-Bis a metabolic matrix, which is the classical definition of Witt equivalence of Seifert matrices, see[20, Section 1]or[22, Definition 3.4.3]

The third and last difference is that in the case of knots, any two Seifert surfaces for the same knot give Witt equivalent Seifert matrices. Equivalently, this means that S-equivalence implies Witt equivalence. This fails forμ≥2\mu\geq 2, as illustrated in Example3.7. Therefore, to get a notion of algebraic concordance which only depends on links rather than C-complexes, we are forced to enlarge the equivalence relation by including generalized S-equivalence. Postponing the technical definition of generalized S-equivalence to Section2.2(see Definition2.4), we define algebraic concordance of families of matrices.

## Definition 1.7.

Two families of matricesAAandBBinℳμ∗\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{\ast}arealgebraically concordantif there exists a sequence of families of matricesA=A0,A1,…,An=BA=A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}=B, each having non trivial Alexander polynomial, and such that for all0≤i<n0\leq i<n,AiA_{i}andAi+1A_{i+1}are either generalized S-equivalent, or Witt equivalent.

## Remark 1.8.

In the caseμ=1\mu=1, we recover the classical notion of algebraic concordance for Seifert matrices. Indeed, two pairs of Seifert matrices of knots{A,AT}\{A,A^{\operatorname{T}}\}and{B,BT}\{B,B^{\operatorname{T}}\}lie inℳ1∗\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\ast}by Remark1.2. By definition these are algebraically concordant as pairs if and only if they are related by a sequence of Witt equivalence and generalized S-equivalence. Asμ=1\mu=1, generalized S-equivalence implies Witt equivalence, and it follows that{A,AT}\{A,A^{\operatorname{T}}\}and{B,BT}\{B,B^{\operatorname{T}}\}are algebraically concordant pairs if and only they are Witt equivalent pairs. By Remark1.6, they are Witt equivalent pairs if and only ifAAandBBare Witt equivalent matrices, which is the classical definition of algebraically concordant Seifert matrices.

Using this notion, we state our main result.

## Theorem 1.9.

For any positive integerμ\mu, algebraic concordance is an equivalence relation on the set of families of matricesℳμ∗\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{\ast}. Moreover, direct sum yields a well defined abelian group structure on the quotient.

Using Theorem1.9, we formulate our main definition.

## Definition 1.10.

We call the group of Theorem1.9theμ\mu-colored algebraic concordance group, denoted by𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔.\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}.

## Remark 1.11.

We emphasize the fact the the group𝒞1𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathit{alg}}does not coincide with the classical algebraic concordance group of knots. Indeed, the group𝒞1𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\mathit{alg}}contains the classes of Seifert matrices of all oriented links with arbitrary number of components and non trivial Alexander polynomial. In particular, it contains classes of pairs of matrices of odd size. On the other hand, the classical algebraic concordance group only contains the classes of Seifert matrices of knots, an thus only classes of pairs of matrices of even size.
As the parity of the size of a pair of matrices is preserved by S-equivalence and Witt equivalence, it is an invariant of the algebraic concordance class of a pair of matrices. Thus, the two groups do not coincide.

Of course, the original motivation for the introduction of the classical algebraic concordance group is the fact that concordant knots have algebraically concordant Seifert matrices. Our construction satisfies the analogue for colored links, at least in the smooth category. This is the content of the following theorem, which is proved in Section4.

## Theorem 1.12.

Smoothly concordant colored links with non trivial Alexander polynomial have algebraically concordant families of generalized Seifert matrices.

Note that the notion ofsmoothconcordance might be unsettling for the reader used to Seifert matrices and abelian invariants. Indeed, Seifert matrices are classically considered in the topological category, yielding topological concordance invariants. However, our actual proof heavily relies on the smooth setting and all our attempts to prove an analogue of Theorem1.12in the topological category have been unsuccessful. In particular, we were not able to adapt the classical proof from[21, Chapter 8], details are given in[27].

In Section5, we turn toward the extension of several known concordance invariants of colored links to the groups𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}. More precisely, in Section5.1, we show that the multivariable signature from[5]gives a invariant of theμ\mu-colored algebraic concordance group. Using this invariant, we obtain a three dimensional smooth proof of the following result, classically obtained in the topological category via four dimensional considerations[5,9].

## 1.3.Other versions of algebraic concordance

We conclude this introduction by mentioning other approaches to the definition of algebraic concordance of links. In the case of knots, there exists several equivalent criterion for two knots to be algebraically concordant. Indeed, as explained in the introduction of[4], algebraic sliceness of a knotKKcan also be defined by considering equivariant intersection forms of spin fillings of the0-surgery alongKK, or via its Blanchfield pairing. Therefore, one could also try to define algebraic concordance of links starting from each of these two approaches. This is done in[4], yielding so calledhomology surgery invariantandBlanchfield invariant.

Note that for two components,22-colored links with non trivial Alexander polynomials and equal linking numbers, algebraic concordance defined via generalized Seifert matrices (see Definition4.1) implies the vanishment of the homology surgery invariant and of the Blanchfield invariant. Indeed, if two such links are algebraically concordant, then their families of generalized Seifert matrices are algebraically concordant, and thus the matricesHFH_{F}andHF′H_{F^{\prime}}of[4, Theorem 1.1 (5)]are Witt equivalent. It follows that the homology surgery invariant is represented by a metabolic matrix, and thus vanishes. The vanishment of the Blanchfield invariant follows from[4, Corollary 1.7].

## Organization

## 3.2.Algebraic concordance of families of matrices

We now show that the property of having non trivial Alexander polynomial is invariant by generalized S-equivalence. Before we do so, let us recall that the Alexander polynomial of a link is well defined only up to multiplication by units ofΛ\Lambda. In our case, when dealing only with generalized Seifert matrices and not with C-complexes, we loose some information. Indeed, in order to compute the Alexander polynomial of a colored link, one needs the generalized Seifert matrices together with an information concerning the genus of the C-complex, and of its underlying colored surfaces, see[7]. Even though this information is encoded in the size of the matrix forμ=1\mu=1, this is not the case forμ≥2\mu\geq 2. ## Proof.

The fact that algebraic concordance is an equivalence relation is immediate since Witt equivalence and generalized S-equivalence are equivalence relations.

Furthermore, direct sum yields a well defined operation on the quotient, as it preserves generalized S-equivalence and Witt equivalence. Indeed, ifA∼A′A\sim A^{\prime}andB∼B′B\sim B^{\prime}are Witt equivalent (respectively generalized S-equivalent) families of matrices, thenA⊕BA\oplus BandA′⊕B′A^{\prime}\oplus B^{\prime}are Witt equivalent (respectively generalized S-equivalent). It remains to verify that this well defined operation yields an abelian group structure. Associativity and commutativity are immediate as direct sum is associative and commutative up to congruence (and congruent matrices are generalized S-equivalent).

The zero element is given by the class of metabolic families of matrices, which contains the family of empty matrices. Indeed, any metabolic family of matrices is Witt equivalent to the family of empty matrices, and taking direct sum with a family of empty matrices does not change an element.

## 5.Algebraic concordance invariants of links

The aim of this section is twofold. First, in Sections5.1through5.3, we review the following known invariants of colored links : the signature, the Alexander polynomial and the Blanchfield pairing. We show that these invariants naturally extend to families of matrices inℳμ∗\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{\ast}, and use them to define algebraic concordance invariants. In Section5.4, we show that these invariants can be combined to unveil part of the structure of𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}, proving Theorem1.16. Finally, Section5.5briefly discusses the potential existence of aℤ4∞\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{\infty}summand of𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}.

## 5.1.Signatures

In this section, after briefly recalling the Levine-Tristram signature of knots and its extension to colored links, we define the signature of families of matrices inℳμ∗\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{\ast}and show that it is an algebraic concordance invariant. We then apply this result to links, proving Corollary1.13.

The Levine-Tristram signature of a knot is a well known algebraic concordance invariant, defined as follows. For a knotKK, with a pair of Seifert matrices{A,AT}\{A,A^{\operatorname{T}}\}and a complex numberω∈S1∖{1}\omega\in S^{1}\setminus\{1\}, the signatureσK​(ω)\sigma_{K}(\omega)ofKKatω\omegais the signature of the hermitian matrixH​(ω):=(1−ω)​A+(1−ω¯)​AT.H(\omega):=(1-\omega)A+(1-\overline{\omega})A^{\operatorname{T}}.

A direct computation shows that the signature is invariant under classical S-equivalence, and therefore is a well defined invariant ofKK. The fact that it is an algebraic concordance invariant (forω\omeganot a root of the Alexander polynomials) is immediate from the purely algebraic fact that the signature of a non degenerate hermitian matrix is unchanged by Witt equivalence.

The signature of a colored link was defined by Cimasoni and Florens in[5], using C-complexes and generalized Seifert matrices. Their techniques extended previous work of Cooper, which considered the case of two component,22-colored links, see[11]. Similarly as in the classical case of knots, Cimasoni and Florens proved that this signature does not depend on the choice of generalized Seifert matrices by showing that it is invariant under generalized S-equivalence, (see[12]for a completed proof). In their original paper, they also showed that, when restricted to suitable complex numbers, the signature is a topological concordance invariant of colored links. Their proof is based on the consideration of four dimensional intersection forms. This result was later strengthened in[9, Corollary 1.3], also by using four dimensional techniques.

Our goal is now to apply the strategy from the knot case to the signature defined by Cimasoni and Florens. More precisely, we observe that the definition of Cimasoni and Florens makes sense for any family of matrices inℳμ∗\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{\ast}, and that, by their computations, it is invariant by generalized S-equivalence. We then use the algebraic fact that that the signature of a non degenerate hermitian matrix is invariant under Witt equivalence to show that the signature is an algebraic concordance invariant, except at zeros of the Alexander polynomial. In particular, we recover a variation of[5, Theorem 7.1]and[8, Corollary 1.3]as Corollary1.13, in the smooth category.

The Blanchfield pairing of a knot, introduced by Blanchfield in[1], is a non singular hermitian pairing on the Alexander module of the knot. ## 5.5.Invariants from Witt groups of finite fields

In the classical case of knots, Levine[19]showed that the algebraic concordance group contains a furtherℤ4∞\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{\infty}summand, by considering invariants coming from Witt group of finite fields. In this section, we give a reason why the extension of these invariants to𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}does not seem straightforward. This is an obstacle to showing the existence of aℤ4∞\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}_{4}summand of𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}.

In the classical case of knots, these invariants are defined as follows. Start with a Seifert matrixAAfor a knot, and consider the non singular symmetric formA+ATA+A^{\operatorname{T}}. This defines an element in the Witt groupW​(ℚ)W(\mathbb{Q}), and thus defines a homomorphismhhfrom the classical algebraic concordance group intoW​(ℚ)W(\mathbb{Q}). Then for any prime numberpp, there are well defined map∂p:W​(ℚ)→W​(𝔽p)\partial_{p}\colon W(\mathbb{Q})\to W(\mathbb{F}_{p}), and the latter Witt group of a finite field is isomorphic either toℤ2\mathbb{Z}_{2}orℤ4\mathbb{Z}_{4}depending on the prime numberpp. The composition∂p∘h\partial_{p}\circ hthen produces aℤ2\mathbb{Z}_{2}orℤ4\mathbb{Z}_{4}invariant of the classical algebraic concordance group.

Extending this construction to𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}does not seem straightforward, as the sum of generalized Seifert matrices (even considered with some signs) is not necessarily non singular. Hence, one does in general not get an element inW​(ℚ)W(\mathbb{Q}). Furthermore, even in the case where one would get an element inW​(ℚ)W(\mathbb{Q}), one would in general not obtain a well defined map𝒞μ→W​(ℚ)\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\to W(\mathbb{Q}). Indeed, the Witt class might depend on the choice of the representative of the generalized S-equivalence class. This issue does not appear in the case of knots, as in this classical case S-equivalence implies Witt equivalence.

## 6.Questions

We conclude this work by gathering a few questions.

Our first question concerns the presence of thesmoothhypothesis in Theorem1.12. This hypothesis might be unsettling for the reader used to Seifert forms and abelian invariants of links. Indeed, Seifert matrices are classically considered in the topological category, yielding topological invariants. For instance, it is known that the signature of colored links is a topological concordance invariant[5,9], as well as the Fox-Milnor condition[17]and the Witt class of the Blanchfield pairing, see for example[16]. Moreover, in the classical case of knots, the analogue of Theorem1.12holds in the topological category. Thus, we ask the following question.

## Question 1.

Do topologically concordant links have algebraically concordant generalized Seifert matrices ?

Our second question is about which elements of the groups𝒞μ𝑎𝑙𝑔\mathcal{C}_{\mu}^{\mathit{alg}}come from generalized Seifert matrices. In the classical case, it is well known that an integral matrixAAis a Seifert matrix of a knot if and only ifdet(A−AT)=1\det(A-A^{\operatorname{T}})=1. It is therefore very natural to ask whether such a criterion exists for families of generalized Seifert matrices. Our attempts have so far been unfruitful, and we are left with the following question.
