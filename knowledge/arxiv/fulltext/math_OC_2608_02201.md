## Abstract

In this paper, we propose a proximal gradient method with adaptive linesearch for multiobjective optimization problems whose objective functions are weakly smooth, i.e., they have Hölder continuous gradients. The proposed method is parameter-free as we do not require prior knowledge of parameters related to the weak smoothness of the objective function; the proposed linesearch finds an appropriate step-size that adapts to the weak smoothness. The complexity guarantee analyzed in this paper for the non-convex case is compatible with related works and our algorithm accepts coercer stationarity measure compared to existing methods. We also establish a novel complexity result for the convex case which improves the one in non-convex case.

## Abstract

In this paper, we propose a proximal gradient method with adaptive linesearch for multiobjective optimization problems whose objective functions are weakly smooth, i.e., they have Hölder continuous gradients. The proposed method is parameter-free as we do not require prior knowledge of parameters related to the weak smoothness of the objective function; the proposed linesearch finds an appropriate step-size that adapts to the weak smoothness.
The complexity guarantee analyzed in this paper for the non-convex case is compatible with related works and our algorithm accepts coercer stationarity measure compared to existing methods.
We also establish a novel complexity result for the convex case which improves the one in non-convex case.

Keywords:Multiobjective optimization; Proximal gradient method; Backtracking linesearch; Hölder continuous gradient.

## 1Introduction

Multiobjective optimization is a problem that involves multiple objective functions to be optimized simultaneously. A major optimality concept for this problem is the Pareto optimality identifying points on which any single objective function cannot be improved without degrading other objective functions.
Multiobjective optimization has wide applications in problems involving multi-decisions such as economics, finance, robust optimization, and many others (see, e.g.,[5,8,9,13]).

## Related works

Over a couple of decades,descent methodsin multiobjective optimization have been developed as extensions of the ones from single objective case, which includes, for instance, the steepest descent method[6], the projected gradient method[4,7], quasi-Newton method[12], etc.
Such methods aim to find a suitable search direction along which the next iterate is updated.
For multiobjective optimization with the composite form (2), a proximal gradient method (PGM) was proposed by Tanabe et al.[14]. Subsequently, the same authors[15]provided convergence rate results of the PGM where the functionsfif_{i}are assumed to have Lipschitz gradients.
The convergence rate analyses were performed in terms of themerit functionsu0​(x)u_{0}(x)andwℓ​(x)w_{\ell}(x)proposed in[16]that measure the concepts of weak Pareto optimality and Pareto stationarity at a pointxx, respectively.
Under Lipschitz continuity of∇fi\nabla f_{i}’s, the PGM proposed in[15]guarantees to find an approximate Pareto stationary pointxxsatisfyingw1​(x)≤εw_{1}(x)\leq\varepsilonwithinO​(ε−1)O(\varepsilon^{-1})iterations. They also showed that, when allfif_{i}are convex, the same PGM finds an approximate weak Pareto optimal pointxxsatisfyingu0​(x)≤εu_{0}(x)\leq\varepsiloninO​(ε−1)O(\varepsilon^{-1})iterations.

Beyond the Lipschitz continuity of gradients, parameter-free PGMs[11,1]were recently developed for the problem (1) when the componentsfif_{i}areweakly smooth, i.e., the gradients∇fi\nabla f_{i}are Hölder continuous. These methods are parameter-free in the sense that they do not need for knowing constants related to Hölder continuity such as the (worst) Hölder exponentνmin∈(0,1]\nu_{\min}\in(0,1]. In general non-convex setting, these PGMs guarantee a convergence rate ofO​(ε−1+νmin2​νmin)\displaystyle O(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1+\nu_{\min}}{2\nu_{\min}}})to achieveℓk2​‖dk‖2≤ε\ell_{k}^{2}\|d^{k}\|^{2}\leq\varepsilonfor a search directiondkd^{k}and a linesearch parameterℓk\ell_{k}(which is fixed to11in[11]).
Although the convergence rates for weakly smooth and non-convex setting were analyzed by aforementioned studies,
the ones in weakly smooth and convex case have not been specifically addressed.

## 4Conclusion

In this paper, we demonstrated parameter-free proximal gradient methods for multiobjective optimization problems with weakly smooth components.
In the non-convex setting, an iteration complexity was obtained consistent to existing results and the convergence analysis is performed in terms of the criterionℓk​wℓk​(xk)≤ε\ell_{k}w_{\ell_{k}}(x^{k})\leq\varepsilonwhich is coercer than the ones in previous works[1,11,15].
In the convex setting, we established a novel complexity result for weakly smooth problems, improving the complexity guarantee in non-convex case.

Although the proposed method ensures to obtain anε\varepsilon-approximate solution, the tolerance parameterε>0\varepsilon>0is fixed as an input of the algorithm, from which a global convergence property of the merit function is not guaranteed. This limitation is also seen in the work[1], and even in the work[10]in a single objective and convex case, while[11]relaxes it for constrained problems. It would be valuable to develop PGMs with convergence guarantee of the merit function.
