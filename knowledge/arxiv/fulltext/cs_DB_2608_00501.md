## Abstract

After a crash, a delivery process faces a question its own database cannot answer: did the other side already receive the effect? Transactional outboxes and change data capture remove the application's dual write, but the relay they introduce delivers and records its own progress as two separate durable acts, so the same decision reappears one stage later.   Practitioners have handled this boundary for a decade with retries, checkpoints, idempotency keys, and fencing, and the operational advice is largely sound. What has been missing is a precise account of when it works: the exact event the guarantees refer to, the evidence they require, and how long that evidence lasts. This paper supplies the missing account as a machine-checked theory, developed in Isabelle/HOL.   At its heart is an information bound. Two reachable post-crash states can agree on everything the crashed side durably knows and still differ in what the sink accepted, so any recovery decision computed from that side must duplicate a delivery or leave one owed. This is not a story about sloppy bookkeeping: a single deterministic deliver-then-checkpoint protocol, its own durable cursor included in what recovery reads, is defeated by crash timing alone. Reading the sink's accepted record escapes the bound exactly, under stated premises. The answer can then go stale -- an old request still in flight, a second recoverer racing the first -- and each hazard has its own proved fence at the sink's acceptance boundary, the cost of fencing itself a theorem. Finally, the guarantee has a lifetime: bounded deduplication memory and truncated source history each void it in a proved way. The resulting test for any exactly-once recovery claim is short: what did the sink accept, what can still change that answer, and how long will the evidence survive?

## Machine-Checked Dual-Write Recovery from a Committed LogAndreas Andreakis

## Abstract.

After a crash, a delivery process faces a question its own database
cannot answer: did the other side already receive the effect? An order
is committed and a message is sent, and the process dies between the
send and the checkpoint that was meant to record it. The database can
be repaired and every store-side audit can pass, yet recovery still
cannot tell whether re-sending will duplicate the effect or skipping
will abandon it. Transactional outboxes and change data capture remove
the application’s dual write, but the relay they introduce delivers and
records its own progress as two separate durable acts, so the same
decision reappears one stage later.

Practitioners have handled this boundary for a decade with retries,
checkpoints, idempotency keys, and fencing, and the operational advice
is largely sound. What has been missing is a precise account of when it
works: the exact event the guarantees refer to, the evidence they
require, and how long that evidence lasts. Formal verification has
settled the neighboring problems—transaction isolation, single-store
crash safety, replicated consensus—while leaving this boundary out.
This paper supplies the missing account as a machine-checked theory,
developed in Isabelle/HOL.

At its heart is an information bound. Two reachable post-crash states
can agree on everything the crashed side durably knows and still differ
in what the sink accepted, so any recovery decision computed from that
side must duplicate a delivery or leave one owed. This is not a story
about sloppy bookkeeping: a single deterministic deliver-then-checkpoint
protocol, its own durable cursor included in what recovery reads, is
defeated by crash timing alone. Reading the sink’s accepted record
escapes the bound exactly, under stated premises. The answer can then go
stale—an old request still in flight, a second recoverer racing the
first—and each hazard has its own proved fence at the sink’s
acceptance boundary, the cost of fencing itself a theorem. Finally, the
guarantee has a lifetime: bounded deduplication memory and truncated
source history each void it in a proved way. The resulting test for any
exactly-once recovery claim is short: what did the sink accept, what can

## 1.Introduction

An order service commitsshippedto its database, and a relay
delivers a confirmation email for each committed order, checkpointing
its progress after every send. One night the process crashes. When it
restarts, the checkpoint reads one order behind the commit log, so
recovery does the obvious thing and re-sends the last confirmation. By
morning a customer has two copies of the same email, and the on-call
engineer is staring at a database in which nothing is wrong: every row
correct, the checkpoint plausible, the store-level audit green. The
question that incident leaves behind—we checkpoint after every
send; how were we not exactly-once?—is the question this paper
answers.

The answer is uncomfortable. The crash landed in the window between a
send and the checkpoint write that would have recorded it. Inside that
window there are two possible worlds: one where the mail provider had
already durably accepted the send, one where it had not—provider
acceptance is the event at issue, not what the mail does afterwards on
its way to an inbox. Everything the service can read on
restart—its database, its write-ahead log, its outbox, its
acknowledgement log, its recovery generation,and the checkpoint
itself—is byte-identical in both. Re-sending duplicates in one world;
skipping abandons committed work in the other. The checkpoint was not
buggy. It was asked to testify about an event it never observed:
whether the other side had accepted the effect. Only the receiving
side’s own record can distinguish the two worlds, and this paper proves
that, along with what it takes—and what it costs—to act on that
record correctly.

## Dual writes.

The incident is an instance of adual write: related facts
become durable under independent authorities—a source database and a
mail provider, a search index, a broker, a cache, another
service—without one commit spanning both. The name suggests two
databases, but neither side owes it that shape: the second authority
can be anything that durably accepts work, and the source need not be
relational or even transactional beyond the single write that makes a
fact durable—Section2says what little each side must
provide. The standard remedy is to stop writing the second copy
from application code and instead derive it from the source’s committed
log, through change data capture (CDC) or a transactional
outbox(Kleppmann,2015,2017; Kleppmann et al.,2019). The
remedy is real, and Section9proves exactly what it
buys: at the store tier, crash safety is equivalent to keeping the
derived image faithful to the committed log. But the pipeline still has
to deliver. A relay that writes a sink and separately records its own
progress performs, per event, two durable acts with no shared
commit—which is, by this paper’s definition, another dual write.
Derivation does not dissolve the recovery question; it moves it to a
stage with better tools: a replayable log behind it and a sink whose
acceptance rules it can negotiate.

## Why formalize now.

The operational lore around this boundary is a decade old and largely
correct: Kleppmann’s dual-writes argument(Kleppmann,2015,2017), Treat’s essay on exactly-once delivery(Treat,2015), and the
log-centric architecture literature(Kleppmann et al.,2019)all say,
in different words,derive from the log, and be careful about
re-delivery. But the recovery half of that advice has never had a
precise statement, and the imprecision has consequences. Exactly-once
claims circulate without saying which event they count, what evidence
they consume, or how long that evidence remains valid; incident
retrospectives blame checkpoint bugs for what is actually an
information limit; and the phrase “exactly-once is impossible” is
repeated in settings where a conditional guarantee is, in fact,
achievable and deployed. Meanwhile the verification literature settled
the surrounding territory—transaction isolation(Ghasemirad et al.,2025),
single-store crash recovery(Chajed et al.,2021,2019), distributed
protocols with a shared commit(Sergey et al.,2018)—without touching the
cross-store recovery decision, whose subject, what the outside world
already received, is precisely what store-centric models erase. The
nearest formal artifacts are bounded model checks of outbox
designs(Masternak and Pobiega,2020; Roohitavaf et al.,2019). To our knowledge, no deductive,
machine-checked account of dual-write recovery existed before this one.

## What the theory says.

The results organize themselves around three questions that turn out to
have exact answers.

What did the sink accept?No answer computed from the crashed
side’s durable state can be trusted. We construct two reachable
post-crash states that agree on everything a recovery procedure can
read locally—store, control plane, recovery generation—yet differ
in what the sink accepted, and show that every recovery policy that
cannot see the difference duplicates a delivery in one world or leaves
one owed in the other (Sections3and4). The strongest form needs no external
adversary at all: one deterministic deliver-then-checkpoint protocol,
its durable cursor part of the state recovery reads, produces the
confusable pair from crash timing alone. What escapes the bound is
reading the sink’s own durable record of accepted work; the escape is
exact, and its premises are stated, not assumed
(Section5).

What can still change that answer?A sink read is a snapshot,
and two hazards can invalidate it after the fact: an old request from
the crashed incarnation still in flight, and a second recoverer acting
on the same crash. Each defeats a natural class of policies, and each
has a proved escape that works by changingacceptancerather
than by reading more: an arrival fence that makes the sink reject
superseded incarnations, and a claim fence that serializes competing
recoverers at the fire point (Sections6and7). Fencing is not free, and its cost is itself a
theorem: a fence trades the chance that a straggler completes your work
for certainty at the frontier you chose.

How long does the evidence last?Exactly-once claims are
usually implemented as at-least-once delivery plus deduplication, and
we prove the exact sense in which that equation holds—and that its
premise is permanence of memory. A deduplication window that expires,
or a source history truncated before recovery replays it, each voids
the guarantee along a concrete run, in one case leaving recovery unable
to distinguish fabricating work from abandoning it
(Section8).

## What this paper is.

Everything stated as a theorem in this paper is machine-checked in
Isabelle/HOL, and the paper is written to be read without the formal
artifact: models, statements, premises, and proof ideas are all in the
text. The negative results are constructions—each exhibits reachable
states or schedules of a specific machine and defeats every policy in a
named class on them; none claims that every system fails. The positive
results are conditional on premises the paper displays and discusses.
No broker, connector, mail provider, or deployed recovery
implementation is verified, and Section10lists the
model’s deliberate absences. One more boundary is the configuration
itself: everything here studies a single one-way pair—one
record-bearing authority, one accepting endpoint, one direction—and
the shapes it leaves out are named alongside those absences. We
believe the theory is most useful
precisely at that grade: it will not certify a product, but it will
tell a designer exactly which question their next retry has to answer,
and which mechanisms can and cannot answer it.

## What each side must provide.

The model is deliberately agnostic at both ends. On the source side,
everything in this paper needs only a durable, ordered, append-only
committed history: each fact either is inHH, with a stable
coordinate, or is not. How a fact became durable is below the
model—no relational store, multi-object transaction, or isolation
discipline is assumed anywhere; a write-ahead log, an event store, a
replicated queue, or a store limited to single-document writes each
yields anHH. Strictly ascending coordinates are load-bearing twice
over (premise P4, Section5): they keep the escape’s own
re-drive batch duplicate-free, and they lift the counting results from
set level, by payload, to per instance. A source that can
lose committed content is not left unmodeled: this section’s machines
never shrinkHH, and truncation gets its own regime in
Section8. And a business fact with no single
committing write has no place inHHuntil one write is designated as
its durability event; a fact spread across independent writes with no
such point reproduces this paper’s problem one level down, inside the
source.

On the sink side, the judged event isdurable acceptance: the
moment the receiving authority durably admits the operation—a
broker’s acknowledged append, a mail provider accepting a send
request, a store committing a put. Nothing in this paper reaches past
acceptance—not into internal processing, onward delivery, or what a
user interface eventually shows. What separates sinks is not their
kind but what they let recoveryread. A sink exposing its own
durable, complete per-operation accepted record gives the escape
results ahead their raw material—their premises are stated where
they are proved, starting in Section5. A sink readable
only as current contents gets strictly less, and what that read can
and cannot separate is itself proved (Sections3and4). A sink that lets recovery read nothing
leaves it inside the negative results, where the honest act is
choosing which failure to prefer
(Section12)—and no acknowledgement the caller
kept changes that. One directional fact frames both lists: the
obligation runs one way, from the source’s record to the sink’s
acceptance. Obligations running both ways are reached here only one
direction at a time, and if even the source keeps no such record, the
question cannot be posed at all.

## Polarity, stated once.

Every negative recovery result in this paper is a construction: it
exhibits reachable states or schedules of a specific machine and
defeats every policy of a named class on them. That is an existence
claim about the boundary—such situations are reachable, and no policy
of the class survives them—never a claim that every deployed system
fails. Positive results are conditional on premises displayed where
they are used. The one result quantifying over all implementations of
its tier is the store-tier equivalence of Section9, and
it is flagged there. The paper keeps three quantifier layers separate
throughout: machines are fixed by construction; policy classes are
defeated by exhibiting states on which every member fails; and
schedules range inside families the theory itself inhabits, so no
defeat is scheduled on an empty stage.

## Two slogans, revisited.

Read against these results, the two slogans that circulate about this

Keep checkpoints and cursors for throughput; do not present them

## 4.The Checkpoint Window

values of a reconcile parameter—chosen, as it were, from outside the

from wherever the cursor points. Perhaps the confusion of

Exactly-once is two conjuncts, and operations sees them asymmetrically.

honest re-drive push the original delivery out of the window, so the
sink processes the re-driven copy as new—the windowed count reads
two while permanent memory still reads one. For the constructed run
the threshold is sharp between window sizes one and two, and the
window-sufficiency condition that protects a run is proved sufficient
andnotnecessary. The operational shape of the hazard is
familiar: Stripe’s idempotency keys become eligible for removal after
at least 24 hours(Stripe,2026), and Kafka drops idempotent-producer
state after about a day of inactivity by default(Apache Kafka documentation,2026a). An
outage that outlives the deduplication horizon is the incident’s
quiet return: recovery is honest, the keys are gone, and the re-drive
lands as new work.

## Deduplicating by key.

Deployed sinks rarely compare payload bytes; they compare keys—an
LSN, an offset, an idempotency key(Stripe,2026). The development
proves the keyed policy equal to the payload policy under exactly two
premises: strictly ascending source coordinates, which is what makes
the coordinate a non-aliasing key, and every delivered key tracing to
a committed record. Each premise has its own isolating
counterexample, and the failure direction is the treacherous one: the
keyed batch is a sub-filter of the payload batch, so a broken premise
starves replay silently toward loss—the direction no hazard audit
sees—never toward duplication. Gating on payload content instead is
unsound the other way, because two equal payloads can be a legitimate
pair that only their instances distinguish. Derive the key once, at
commit time, from the record’s stable source coordinate.

## Undo instead of dedup.

Sinks with reversing entries invite a different escape: compensate,
then redo. The development examines three selected compensation
observations—matched cancellation, signed net, and truncation—and
the verdicts split. Under the matched-cancellation view, order-aware
compensate-then-redo normalizes both outcomes of the designed pair:
the same batch succeeds on both members, an escape by changing the
observation under which outcomes are judged. Netting to a signed
aggregate falls to the same batch-agreement defeat as everything else,
and truncated views escape nothing. Throughout, the underlying record
stays append-only—“undo” never un-sends; it appends a matching
reversing emission, in order. One realizability hedge attaches: the
matched result is proved against the abstract re-drive relation, not
as an execution of this machine’s suffix-only reconcile; realizing it
requires a compensation-capable transition, or a refinement proof for
an implementation that has one.

## Compaction that keeps presence.

On the sink’s own record the safe boundary ispresence.
Rewrites that preserve a record’s presence—compaction, tombstoning
that keeps the key’s entry—are provably invisible to the escape,
which needs no more than window-local presence. Presence-destroying
retention fabricates duplicates at a reachable state, and the
retention-facing escape never loses—its whole error budget is the
duplicate horn. This bounds what the landed policy needs to read; that
no policy could do better with bounded memory is not claimed
(Section10).

Idempotency keys are a guarantee with a shelf life measured by
the sink’s memory: size the deduplication horizon from the longest

duplicate-admission event, not a cleanup. Upstream, preserve the
journal’s content for every frontier recovery may revisit—a cursor
and a length are not an owed set. When the evidence is gone on both
sides, no honest recovery exists: only a choice between fabricating
and abandoning.

## The prover and the trust base.

Every numbered result in this paper is a machine-checked theorem in
Isabelle/HOL(Nipkow et al.,2002), checked in a mode where an unfinished
proof fails the build. The trusted base is thin: stock Isabelle2025-2
and, beyond the standard prelude, exactly two standard-library
theories—finite multisets and subsequence orderings. There are no
global axioms and no proof oracles. Theorems carry their premises as
explicit hypotheses, so a statement is its own interface; the one
named-assumption context—the acceptance interface—has its two
obligations discharged on both of its instances.
The full development rebuilds from cold in a few minutes on a laptop.

## Checking that can fail.

Machinery earns trust by being falsifiable, and four controls did real
work here. Before landing, scratch-side gate sessions probed the
headline proof terms for unproved dependencies, with a
planted-failure control confirming the probe bites; those sessions
were then stripped and are not part of the landed tree. Both sides of
every bound carry closed witnesses—designed pairs and defeating
schedules on the negative side, escape runs and disciplined traces on
the positive—so no theorem here is true of an empty class. The
modeling choices are exercised from both directions: the wipe-twin
and early-arrival runs show the straggler defeat needs exactly the
wire semantics it names, the negative results re-prove on a
small-step variant under interleaving, and the concurrency schedules
carry their own non-triviality bounds. And one premise was refuted
honestly along the way: the keyed-deduplication rule was designed
with one premise, the development disproved that version, and the
surviving rule carries two. The intended rule was false; that is what
checking is for.

## Executables.

Certified executables accompany the theory—chief among them an
exported, theorem-correct store/hazard decider and the core store
machine’s trace validator. Both are point evaluators, and their limits matter
more than their existence: a clean hazard verdict is not
exactly-once—completeness at the frontier is the second conjunct,
and no hazard audit sees it—and the validator certifies label
structure, not history wellformedness. In the other direction, the
decider sharpens the wall from below: provably, no function of the
durable core alone computes even the hazard verdict.

## The studied shape.

The absences above are mechanics; one more is shape. Every result in
this paper concerns a single one-way pair: one authority holding a
durable, ordered, per-operation record that defines the owed work, one
endpoint whose durable acceptance defines the done work, one delivery
direction between them. That shape covers the log-derived pipelines
this paper grew out of, and it excludes real configurations by design:
obligations running both ways; several sources feeding one sink, or
one source feeding several sinks bound together rather than held each
to its own pair; chains judged end to end rather than hop by hop;
payloads transformed in flight rather than delivered verbatim; and
pairs in which even the source keeps no committed record of what it
wrote—nothing left to define the owed work, so the model cannot pose
the question. The shape is not an arbitrary cut: its capabilities are
the same ones every result turns on—each escape theorem names the
capability that buys it, and each bound is proved against a named
class of policies that lack it. A theory that treats both endpoints as
capability-graded nodes of one kind—and recovers these results as
the one-way, record-bearing instance—is the generalization this
boundary map points toward.

## The worked instance.

A pipeline in the style of DBLog mixes an initial snapshot or
backfill with live log records, and the theory covers the mixture at
safety grade: justification is append-stable across the machine,
mixed-discipline runs stay hazard-free, and the log-only fragment
recovers the delivery hazards exactly as stated. Two boundaries are
explicit. The snapshot modality carries no exactness law—safety
only. And the watermark algorithm itself is not re-proved here: the
instance consumes our prior snapshot-replay development by
citation(Andreakis,2026b), through a proved bridge, and that
development’s own disclosed assumptions remain its own. One fact
about process belongs in the same breath: at the time of writing, no
independent expert has cold-read the development; the machine check
and the controls above are the evidence on offer.

## 11.Related Work

## The advice this paper formalizes.

The operational rule has been public for a decade, in places
peer-reviewed: Kleppmann’s dual-writes argument and its book
form(Kleppmann,2015,2017), the log-centric architecture
literature(Kleppmann et al.,2019), and Treat’s essay on
exactly-once delivery(Treat,2015). We are not correcting this
lineage; we are completing it. The derivation half of the advice was
always the tractable half. The recovery half—what a re-drive may do
after a crash—resists informal statement because its subject is
what the world received, the one record store-centric reasoning
erases. The theorems here give that half its exact shape, premises
attached.

## The nearest formal artifacts.

Closest to our subject are bounded model checks of outbox designs:
Masternak and Pobiega model-check an exactly-once outbox in
TLA+(Masternak and Pobiega,2020), and LogPlayer model-checks in-order
exactly-once delivery to backend shards(Roohitavaf et al.,2019); both predate
this work. The boundary between us is method, and it matters here: a
bounded check certifies the states it enumerates, while the wall of
Sections3–4is a higher-order claim
overarbitrary policy functions, which is exactly the kind of
statement finite enumeration cannot reach. We are aware of no prior
deductive, mechanized account of this boundary; that narrow claim is
the paper’s novelty claim, and its evidence is this section.

## Impossibility neighbors.

The classical results fence our scope rather than supply our
theorems. FLP(Fischer et al.,1985), in mechanized form
too(Bisping et al.,2016), concerns asynchronous consensus liveness;
CALM(Hellerstein and Alvaro,2020)characterizes coordination-freeness;
CAP(Gilbert and Lynch,2002)trades availability under partition.
Nothing here asks processes to agree, assumes partition, or claims
kinship in depth: the mathematics is elementary by design, and the
contribution is exactness at a boundary practitioners operate daily.
The genre precedent is certified impossibility work such as
Pactole(Auger et al.,2013)—mechanizing known boundaries until
they are exact.

## Atomic commitment.

The classical way to remove a separable completion window is a shared
commit(Gray,1978), and atomic commitment across autonomous stores
has its own theory and impossibility
results(Mullen et al.,1992; Guerraoui,1995). Our failure
class is deliberately the other one: source committed, delivery owed,
no coordinator and no votes. Where a real shared commit spans both
effects, the window this paper studies never opens; where the sink
cannot join one—the usual reason the pattern exists—these
theorems apply. Nothing here bears on commit-protocol correctness.

## Verified data systems.

Mechanized systems work has settled adjacent problems that each
exclude this one. VerIso(Ghasemirad et al.,2025)and mechanized
serializability(Traytel,2025)treat isolation inside a database,
with no crash-fed second store. GoJournal and
Perennial(Chajed et al.,2021,2019)verify one store’s crash
safety, with no independently written copy. Disel(Sergey et al.,2018)proves
distributed protocols, two-phase commit included, where a shared
commit exists by construction. The cross-store recovery
question—what may be re-sent, after a crash, to a sink that keeps
its own record—steps to the side of all of them.

## Deployed guarantees, located.

The model gives deployed exactly-once machinery a vocabulary for
where its guarantees stop. Kafka’s idempotent producer and
transactions(Apache Kafka community,2017)deduplicate broker-side by producer id,
epoch, and sequence—the epoch is a deployed cousin of our
generation, consulted at acceptance—and the producer state behind
it expires after about a day of inactivity by
default(Apache Kafka documentation,2026a)—a finite horizon of exactly the kind
Section8’s windowed control voids.
Flink’s two-phase-commit sink(Apache Flink documentation,2026; Nowojski and Winters,2018)places
transactional acceptance at the sink—fencing’s role in our terms.
Kafka Streams’ exactly-once guarantee covers processing from source
topics to output topics and state stores(Apache Kafka documentation,2026b); that is
a scope boundary, not a defect, and outside it sits the boundary this
paper studies. No broker, connector, or runtime is verified here; the
map says where each guarantee stops, in the model’s terms.

## Fencing lineage.

Fencing by monotonic token is established engineering: Kleppmann’s
fencing-token argument(Kleppmann,2016), Kafka’s producer
epoch(Apache Kafka community,2017), and mechanized lease reasoning in
Grove(Sharma et al.,2023). We claim the theorems, not the mechanism: the
defeat of every channel-blind policy on a designed pair, the premised
fenced escape with its cost stated as a corollary, and the
claim-fence guarantee—to our knowledge the first mechanized
separation of that information bound from a fenced acceptance escape.
The corpus models no clocks and no leases, so it neither supports nor
refutes lock-service designs.

## Our own substrate.

The snapshot-replay development the worked instance consumes is our
prior study of DBLog(Andreakis,2026b). It is a substrate cited by the

step-ordering alone converts duplicates into losses. Use the full
claim discipline (Thm1, Lem.2,
Thm3).Can dedup or journal evidence expire before replay ends?The guarantee ends with the evidence: expiry admits duplicates, and
lost journal content forces fabricate-or-abandon
(§8; Thm2).

## The base case.

Suppose one recoverer acts, no old send can arrive after its read, and
the sink exposes an authoritative, complete, current durable accepted
record at the decision frontier. Then the recovery batch is committed
obligations minus that record, and Theorem1makes it
exact at the frontier under four premises: no hazard already on the
record, a crashed run, an in-range frontier, and strictly ascending
source coordinates—the last is what upgrades payload counting to
per-instance counting. This is the theorem-backed core of “read the
sink.” It is not permission to read a source checkpoint, a relay
progress flag, a stale replica, or a paginated projection under a
different name, and it is not a whole-system claim.

## When old sends outlive the process.

A retry queue or network buffer can hold a valid send from the
crashed incarnation, and if it arrives after the read, it duplicates
an honest recovery (Theorem2). Reading longer does not
fix this—the missing fact is in flight. Change acceptance instead:
one atomic act that heals, computes the delta from the accepted
record, lands it, and fences to the successor generation
(Theorem3). Two costs come with it. The atomicity is a
specification boundary—a crash inside the composite is outside the
model, so an implementation must realize it transactionally at one
authority or bring its own argument. And the fence spends the wire’s
possible rescue: a below-fence straggler that would have completed
later work is dropped so that exactness at the chosen frontier is
certain (Corollary4). Choose the frontier with that
trade in view.

## When recoveries overlap.

Concurrent recovery is its own regime. Two honest reads go stale
against each other (Theorem1); merely ordering each
recoverer’s steps trades the double-fire for a loss; the discipline
that survives is the atomic claim—fence to your generation and arm
the batch read under the claim, then fire while the fence still
admits you (Theorem3). Its guarantee is exactly as
strong as its premises: safety needs the full grammar, not the
claim/fire order alone; exactness needs window freshness and
completion at the fence; and the verdict is graded against what
retention kept. If retention can expire undelivered work, close the
gap explicitly—journal-grade exactness holds exactly when retention
drops only already-accepted obligations
(Corollary4)—or operate knowing your exactly-once
is relative to the retained history. Finally, this paper proves the
two fence mechanisms on different machines and transports only safety
between the regimes; a deployment facing both hazards at once carries
a composition obligation the theory does not discharge for it.

## Make the premises real.

Four implementation questions decide whether the positive theorems
describe your system rather than a neighboring one.Is
acceptance authoritative, complete, durable, and current when
recovery reads it?The model makes acceptance and its readable
record one event; a sink that acknowledges first and persists later,
or serves recovery a stale or partial projection, is outside the
proved delta argument.Is event identity stable?Derive the
idempotency key once, at source commit, from a non-aliasing source
coordinate; content-derived identity merges legitimate pairs, and
ungrounded keys starve replay toward silent loss
(Section8).Are the fence and claim atomic at
the stated boundaries?“Eventually fence” and “read, then claim”
are not relaxations of the proved disciplines but refuted
reorderings.Can recovery still read the evidence that defines
what was owed?Lengths, cursors, and high-water marks are extent,
not content; Theorem2is what the difference costs.

## Budget evidence, not slogans.

Define the replay horizon as the greatest age of an accepted effect
that recovery may legitimately send again—outage time, operator
delay, retry backlog, and replay lag included. As guidance: keep
sink-side deduplication evidence beyond that horizon with margin, and
preserve journal content upstream for every frontier recovery may
revisit. Theorem1does not certify a sizing formula;
it fixes the permanent-memory premise, Section8’s
windowed control establishes the consequence of forgetting, and
Theorem2the consequence of forgetting upstream.
Stripe’s at-least-24-hour keys and Kafka’s roughly one-day default
producer-state expiry(Stripe,2026; Apache Kafka documentation,2026a)are finite horizons
that exist in the wild, not recommended targets. Size from the outage
you promise to survive.

## Monitor both halves.

Exactly-once has a loud half and a quiet half. Duplicates and
premature effects are visible on the accepted record; omissions are
absences, and a hazard audit stays green through them
(Section5). As guidance, expose: committed obligations
not yet on the accepted record; the current generation and fence; the
oldest generation still present in transport or retry state; the
active claim and its read frontier; the effective deduplication
horizon and journal-retention floor; and a completeness alarm
separate from every duplicate alarm. These signals do not prove the
premises; they make premise violations observable. “No duplicates
detected” is not evidence that nothing was lost.

## Exercise the dangerous schedules.

Testing cannot prove quantified results, but it can catch an
implementation that fails to instantiate their premises. Force at
least five schedules: an old send arrives after the sink read; two
recoverers read before either fires; deduplication state expires
during an outage; a truncating crash leaves only length-like journal
evidence; and a crash lands between the sub-operations implementing
the fenced re-drive—the last probes a declared model boundary, not
a guarantee. Run the controls too: an old send arrivingbeforethe read should be absorbed by the delta, and a deliberately lost
delivery should trip the completeness alarm while the hazard audit
stays green.

## The incident, as a runbook.

For the shipping email: locate the durable source event and the
provider’s accepted record. If the provider exposes no durable
acceptance evidence, no source-side checkpoint can manufacture
it—choose explicitly between tolerating duplicates and tolerating
loss, and write the choice down. If old requests can survive in a
queue, attach a generation the sink checks at acceptance, and make
the fenced re-drive atomic at your chosen frontier. If the
orchestrator can overlap recoveries, serialize them through the claim
discipline, window freshness included. If both hazards exist, treat
the composition as an engineering obligation, not a theorem you own.
Set deduplication and journal horizons from the longest outage you
support, and alert on committed-but-unaccepted work. That is the
runbook the original checkpoint was trying, and structurally unable,
to be.

## 13.Conclusion

Dual-write incidents get blamed on bad checkpointing, and the blame
misses the point. A checkpoint records where the caller believes it
stopped; recovery needs to know what the other side accepted; and
between a delivery and the checkpoint that would have recorded it,
those are different facts. We built machines where that difference is
exact and proved it cannot be reasoned away: two reachable post-crash
states, indistinguishable to everything the crashed side durably
knows—its store, its control plane, its own faithfully maintained
cursor—that require opposite recovery decisions. Any re-drive
computed from that side duplicates a delivery in one world or leaves
one owed in the other, and this holds not just for abstract
bookkeeping but for a deterministic deliver-then-checkpoint protocol
defeated by crash timing alone.

What survives is specific, and its premises are the design. The
sink’s durable accepted record supplies the missing fact, and the
delta against it is an exact re-drive—provided the record is
authoritative, complete, and current when read. That answer stays
good only while nothing can change it: an old send still in flight
calls for a fence at arrival; a competing recoverer calls for a claim
at the fire point; each is proved on its own machine, each escapes by
changing what the sink accepts rather than by reading more, and the
arrival fence’s price—rescues traded for certainty—is a theorem,
not a footnote. And every guarantee inherits a lifetime from its
evidence: deduplication memory that expires re-admits honest
duplicates, and a source truncated below its journal leaves recovery
choosing between fabricating work and abandoning it.

For the systems this paper grew out of, the practical shift is where
scrutiny belongs. Deriving downstream state from the committed
log—CDC, outboxes—is exactly right at the store tier, and we
prove precisely what it guarantees there. But it relocates the hard
question to the relay’s delivery loop, so that is where review should
concentrate: which durable record says an effect was accepted, which
actors can still act on the crash, and how long the evidence on both
sides outlives the outage. Those three questions have exact answers
now, machine-checked end to end, with their premises in print. The
duplicate email was never a database bug. Recovery asked the database
a question only the sink could answer—and the fix begins with
asking it of the right party.
