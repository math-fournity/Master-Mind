## Abstract

We construct unambiguous DNFs having width $O(n)$ but $0$-certificate complexity $Ω(n^2)$. By utilizing the special structure of these DNFs, we prove a lifting theorem with a constant-sized gadget that lifts the DNF to a communication problem, while losslessly translating the separation in certificate complexity to a separation in communication complexity. This leads to an optimal refutation of the Alon-Saks-Seymour conjecture, as well as an optimal communication lower bound for the Clique versus Independent Set problem, improving the previous results of Balodis, Ben-David, Göös, Jain and Kothari (FOCS 2021, SICOMP 2023) by several doubly logarithmic factors. As further applications of our construction to query complexity and learning theory, we exhibit: (a) a family of Boolean functions that has an optimal quartic separation between certificate complexity and approximate degree, and (b) a sample compression lower bound of $Ω(\sqrt{\log c})$ for multiclass concept classes over $c$ labels.

## 1.2Application: Alon-Saks-Seymour and Clique versus Independent Set

As mentioned above, Puzzle I is motivated (among other reasons) by its application to the Alon-Saks-Seymour conjecture and the Clique versus Independent Set problem. The Alon-Saks-Seymour conjecture, first referenced inKahn (1991), is concerned with two combinatorial quantities of a graphGG: the chromatic numberχ​(G)\chi(G)and biclique partition numberbp​(G)\mathrm{bp}(G). The chromatic number ofGGis the smallest number of colors that can be assigned to the vertices inGG, such that no two adjacent vertices are assigned the same color. The biclique partition number ofGGis the smallest number of bicliques that the edge set ofGGcan be partitioned into. Alon, Saks and Seymour conjectured that these quantities are related asχ​(G)≤bp​(G)+1\chi(G)\leq\mathrm{bp}(G)+1. WhileGao, McKay, Naserasr, and Stevens (2016)confirmed this conjecture for all graphs havingbp​(G)≤9\mathrm{bp}(G)\leq 9, in a celebrated result,Huang and Sudakov (2012)disproved it by constructing a family of graphs satisfyingχ​(G)≥Ω​(bp​(G)6/5)\chi(G)\geq\Omega(\mathrm{bp}(G)^{6/5}). This separation has since been improved in a series of works, namely:Göös (2015), who showedχ​(G)≥exp⁡(Ω​(log1.12⁡(bp​(G))))\chi(G)\geq\exp(\Omega(\log^{1.12}(\mathrm{bp}(G)))),Ben-David, Hatami, and Tal (2017), who showedχ​(G)≥exp⁡(Ω​(log1.22⁡(bp​(G))))\chi(G)\geq\exp(\Omega(\log^{1.22}(\mathrm{bp}(G)))), and finallyBalodiset al.(2023), who showedχ​(G)≥exp⁡(Ω~​(log2⁡(bp​(G))))\chi(G)\geq\exp(\widetilde{\Omega}(\log^{2}(\mathrm{bp}(G)))). In particular, the precise bound shown byBalodiset al.(2023)(which is made explicit inCheunget al.(2023, Theorem 1.11)111At first glance, Theorem 1.11 inCheunget al.(2023)appears to imply the weaker separationχ​(G)≥exp⁡(Ω​(log2⁡(bp​(G))(log⁡log⁡(bp​(G)))8))\chi(G)\geq\exp\left(\Omega\left(\frac{\log^{2}(\mathrm{bp}(G))}{(\log\log(\mathrm{bp}(G)))^{8}}\right)\right). However, combining their Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 gives an additionalΩ​(log⁡n)\Omega(\log n)factor inlog⁡Cov0\log\operatorname{{Cov}}_{0}beyond the bound stated in their Corollary 3.4, yielding the bound shown in (4)., and to our knowledge, is the best-known separation so far) wasχ​(G)≥exp⁡(Ω​(log2⁡(bp​(G))(log⁡log⁡(bp​(G)))7)).\displaystyle\chi(G)\geq\exp\left(\Omega\left(\frac{\log^{2}(\mathrm{bp}(G))}{(\log\log(\mathrm{bp}(G)))^{7}}\right)\right).(4)

Importantly, the bound in (4) is optimal up to the doubly logarithmic factors, since on the upper bound side, it is known thatχ​(G)≤exp⁡(O​(log2⁡(bp​(G))))\chi(G)\leq\exp(O(\log^{2}(\mathrm{bp}(G))))for all graphs(Mubayi and Vishwanathan,2009; Fox,2026).

The way thatBalodiset al.(2023)obtain the near-optimal separation in (4) is by applying alifting theoremofGöös, Lovett, Meka, Watson, and Zuckerman (2016)to the family of functions they construct that witnessesC0​(f)≥Ω~​(UC1​(f)2)\mathrm{C}_{0}(f)\geq\widetilde{\Omega}(\mathrm{UC}_{1}(f)^{2}). The lifting operation converts a Boolean function into a two-party communication problemhhby composing through a gadgetgg. The classical result ofGööset al.(2016)constructs a gadget of sizek=Θ​(log⁡n)k=\Theta(\log n)that translates a lower bound on the0-certificate complexityC0​(f)\mathrm{C}_{0}(f)of any Boolean functionffinto a lower bound on the 0-monochromatic rectangle covering numberCov0⁡(h)\operatorname{{Cov}}_{0}(h)of the lifted communication problemhh(seeSection4for a definition ofCov0⁡(h)\operatorname{{Cov}}_{0}(h)). While the lifting theorem ofGööset al.(2016)is extremely powerful, in that it applies toeveryBoolean function, the fact that it uses a gadget of sizek=Θ​(log⁡n)k=\Theta(\log n)is precisely what leads to the superfluous doubly logarithmic factor in the final separation between chromatic number and biclique partition number. The proof ofTheorem4is based on thecheat-sheetframework ofAaronsonet al.(2016), which was also used byBalodiset al.(2023)to establish their cubic separation between certificate complexity and approximate degree.

## Sample Compression.

Our proof ofTheorem5is largely inspired by the sample compression lower bound for multiclass concept classes shown inPabbaraju (2024), which translates an Alon-Saks-Seymour refutation into a multiclass concept class that is hard to compress. The strategy is as follows: first, a partial concept class (whose range is{0,1,∗}\{0,1,*\}) having VC dimension 1 is constructed from a graph witnessing a separation between chromatic number and biclique partition number. The class has a partial concept corresponding to every vertex in the graph. Then, a total multiclass concept class thatdisambiguatesthe partial concept class is constructed by having a total concept for every partial concept, where the total concept fills in the∗*values with a unique label that is distinct from all other total concepts. Because the labels used for disambiguation are unique, the Natarajan dimension of this total concept class is equal to the VC dimension of the partial class. Since there is one disambiguating total concept that uses a unique label per partial concept in the class, and the number of partial concepts is equal to the number of vertices in the graph, the number of labels in the disambiguating class is on the order of the number of vertices in the graph. We thus apply this strategy to our small-sized graph fromTheorem3. Finally, we use the observation that a sample compression scheme for the total class implies a coloring of the vertices of the graph, where the number of colors scales with the size of the sample compression; because the chromatic number of the graph is lower-bounded in terms of the number of labels, we infer the desired lower bound.

We note that applying the construction above to the graph given by Theorem 1.11 inCheunget al.(2023)would already have given a weaker sample compression lower bound ofΩ~​((log⁡c)1/4)\widetilde{\Omega}((\log c)^{1/4}); our improvement on the size of the graph inTheorem3is crucial for the stronger lower bound ofTheorem5.
