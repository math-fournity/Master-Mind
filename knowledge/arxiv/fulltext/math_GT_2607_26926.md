## Abstract

We survey recent advancements on the realisation problem for mapping degree sets. In particular, we explain that any finite set containing zero is the mapping degree set between some $n$-manifolds, for each $n\geq3$. Our building factors for the construction of the realising manifolds will be aspherical manifolds. ## 1.Introduction

LetM,NM,Nbe two closed orientednn-manifolds.
Thedegreeof a continuous mapf:M→Nf\colon M\to N, denoted bydeg⁡(f)\deg(f), is the integer given byHn​(f)​([M])=deg⁡(f)​[N]H_{n}(f)([M])=\deg(f)[N], whereHn​(f)H_{n}(f)is the induced homomorphism in top degree homology and[X][X]denotes the fundamental class. The purpose of this survey is to give a concise account of the following realisation problem:

## Problem 1.1(Realisation Problem).

Given a setA⊆ℤA\subseteq\mathbb{Z}, are thereMMandNNsuch thatD​(M,N)=AD(M,N)=A?

While one quickly observes that such a setAAmust contain zero, Problem1.1is highly non-trivial. In this survey, we will outline some recent advancements, including a complete solution in the case of finite sets. From now on, we assume that anyAAas in the Realisation Problem1.1contains zero.

## Theorem 1.2.
- (a)

[NWW23]Not all sets are mapping degree sets.
- (b)

[NWW23]All finite arithmetic sequences are mapping degree sets between33-manifolds.
- (c)

[CMV24]All finite sets are mapping degree sets between33-manifolds.
- (d)

[NS+24,NS+25]All finite sets are mapping degree sets betweennn-manifolds, for eachn≥4n\geq 4.

As we shall see, part (a) of Theorem1.2follows from a quick enumeration argument. Part (b) – in fact, a much more general and involved statement (see Theorem3.1) – encompasses the topology needed and part (c) follows by applying the argument for part (b) to a combinatorial description of any given finite set. Part (d) forn≥6n\geq 6follows by part (c) together with a result which roughly says that mapping degree sets in lower dimensions are mapping degree sets in higher dimensions. The casesn=4,5n=4,5of part (d) require a different approach to be able to apply parts (b) and (c).

Unless otherwise stated, all manifolds are closed, oriented and connected.
