## Abstract.

We construct noetherian rings admitting local cohomology modules with nonclosed support, or equivalently
with infinitely many minimal primes; this answers a question of Huneke–Lyubeznik.

## 1.Introduction

For an idealIIin a commutative ringRR, Grothendieck’s 1961 seminar[HAR67]introduced local cohomologyHI∗​(−)H^{*}_{I}(-)ofRR-modules. The
construction provided a non-graded cohomological counterpart of Serre’s
dictionary[SER55]relating coherent sheaves on projective varieties to
graded modules; it was developed further in SGA2[GRO68], where it was
applied to Lefschetz theorems for Picard groups and fundamental groups. Since then,
local cohomology has become a central tool in the study of cohomological dimension
and vanishing theorems, (e.g., from the early work[HAR68,OGU73]to the recent paper[MP22]).

TheRR-modulesHI∗​(R)H^{*}_{I}(R)are seldom finitely generated, even in nice
situations. Nevertheless, beginning with Grothendieck’s work, much effort has
been expended on finding “hidden finiteness” properties of these modules,
extending Serre’s finiteness theorems for coherent cohomology of projective
varieties. Some of Grothendieck’s early conjectures in this direction were
disproved by Hartshorne[HAR69]. On the positive side, Faltings[FAL78,FAL81]proved the existence of a nonzero annihilator ideal for
local cohomology modules in certain situations. In[HUN92], Huneke asked
whether local cohomology modules always have finitely many associated prime
ideals. The answer is affirmative for regular local rings in positive
characteristic[HS93], characteristic zero[LYU93], and
unramified111Very recently, the second author has shown that the
statement fails for ramified regular local rings[MA26].mixed
characteristic[LYU00], and also for smoothℤ\mathbb{Z}-algebras[BBL+14]. The proofs in these regular settings are “topological”:
they rely crucially on a𝒟\mathcal{D}-module orFF-module structure.

Outside the regular case, examples of Singh, Katzman, and Singh–Swanson showed
that the set of associated primes may be infinite[SIN00,KAT02,SS04].
These examples still left open the following extremely natural question,
usually attributed to Huneke–Lyubeznik:

## Question 1.1.

Fix a noetherian ringRR, idealI⊂RI\subset R, and integerii. IsSupp⁡HIi​(R)\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)Zariski closed? Equivalently, sinceSupp⁡HIi​(R)\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)is closed under specialization, doesSupp⁡HIi​(R)\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)have finitely many minimal primes?

Question1.1was studied in depth in[HKM09]; in
particular, they gave an affirmative answer whendim(R)≤4\dim(R)\leq 4andRRis
local, or whenIIhas cohomological dimension at most22. Other positive
cases, mostly in characteristicpp, include hypersurface and finiteFF-representation type settings[HN17,KZ17,DQ20,QUI24], and more
recently certain complete intersections[GZ25]. It is tempting to
speculate that a sufficiently robust𝒟\mathcal{D}-module/FF-module theory for
singular varieties might extend the argument in the regular case to the
general case. This note shows that the simplest form of this expectation
fails as Question1.1has a negative answer, even for normal domains:

## Remark 1.4(A comment on dimension).

The counterexamples in Theorem1.2(1) and
Theorem1.3have dimension55. On the other hand, ifRRis a noetherian ring of
dimension at most33, thenHIi​(M)H^{i}_{I}(M)has only finitely many minimal primes
for every idealI⊂RI\subset R, every finitely generatedRR-moduleMM, and
everyii. The casesi=0,1i=0,1are elementary[MAR01, Proposition 1.1(c)], and the remaining cases follow from[MAR01, Proposition 2.3 and Corollary 2.4]and[HKM09, Corollary 3.3]. Thus dimension44is the only unresolved case
without further hypotheses. Since finiteness is known for local rings of
dimension at most44[HKM09], any44-dimensional counterexample to
Question1.1would necessarily be nonlocal; we were unable to find one.

In the local case, Theorem1.2(2) gives a66-dimensional example, so the main remaining question is to find a55-dimensional local example.

Besides the precise question in Remark1.4, it would be interesting know if local cohomology modules of noetherian rings enjoy some yet-to-be-articulated finiteness property.
