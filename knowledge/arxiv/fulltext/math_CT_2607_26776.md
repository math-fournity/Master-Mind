## Abstract

In this paper we prove that Dubois--Prade's eight possibilistic operators in Formal Concept Analysis arise canonically from Kan extensions of the underlying boolean profunctor. This provides a conceptual explanation for the result that $NΠ$-pairs are the formal concepts of the complement context. ## 1Introduction

Formal concept analysis (FCA) is a technique for structuring data by associated attributes[13], and has during recent years seen a wide array of applications in various contexts[17]. Given a list of objects, a list of attributes, and a relation describing which object has which attribute, FCA constructs a set offormal conceptswhich are naturally structured into a complete lattice.

Formal concepts are constructed as fixedpoints of derivation operations(−)′(-)^{\prime}on the set of objects and attributes. More formally, given a set of objectsGG, a set of attributesMMand a relationR⊆G×MR\subseteq G\times M, one defines for a subset of objectsA⊆GA\subseteq GitsintentA′={m∈M∣∀g∈G,(g∈A⟹g​R​m)},A^{\prime}=\{m\in M\mid\forall g\in G,(g\in A\implies gRm)\},

In[5,8], the authors take inspiration from possibility theory – see[6,7,9]– to give a new reading of additional power-set operators onP​(G)P(G), previously studied in relation to qualitative data analysis[10]and rough set theory[18].

Third, it shows how general machinery can identify new closure operators on formal contexts. ## 2Category theory and FCA

Formal concept analysis has attracted interest by category theorists for being a natural and useful application of their abstract tools. Category theory was introduced by Eilenberg and MacLane in[11], and is an abstract mathematical theory for understanding compositional structure – see[16]for a comprehensive introduction.

A category consists ofobjectsandmorphisms, which are abstract relationships between objects. Category theory can then be thought of as studying objects by understanding how they relate to all other objects of the same defined type. We will assume that the reader is familiar with basic categorical constructions.

For a category theorist, the objectsGGand attributesMMcan be viewed as discrete categories. Any relation can be described as a𝟚={0,1}\mathbbm{2}=\{0,1\}-valued profunctor, also calledbooleanprofunctors. ## 3.1Idempotency and closure

The standard FCA operator(−)ΔΔ(-)^{\Delta}_{\Delta}is a so-called closure operator, defined as follows.

## Definition 3.1.

A functorF:𝒞⟶𝒞F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}on a poset𝒞\mathcal{C}is aclosure operatorif it is extensive, monotone and idempotent, meaning it satisfies the following axioms:
- 1.

A⩽F​(A)A\leqslant F(A),
- 2.

A⩽A′⟹F​(A)⩽F​(A′)A\leqslant A^{\prime}\implies F(A)\leqslant F(A^{\prime}), and
- 3.

F​F​(A)=F​(A)FF(A)=F(A).

The FCA adjunction coming from a formal context(G,M,R)(G,M,R)satisfies all three of these: by[14, 2.1.11], the monadR∗​R∗R_{*}R^{*}is idempotent, and the two other properties follow formally by the adjunction unit and the fact that it is a functor on posets. Thus, on a poset, any monadic functor determines a closure operator, and conversely, any closure operator is the monad of an adjunction – see[3, Chapter 7]for details.

Consequently, in order to be recognized as a closure operator onsomeformal context, these three axioms have to be fulfilled. Otherwise the resulting fixedpoints have no chance of being formal concepts, or concepts of any type. In light of this discussion, the following proposition is rather trivial, but we state it for reference later.

## Proposition 3.2.

LetL:𝒞⟶𝒞L\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}be an endofunctor on a poset. IfLLfails to be extensive, monotone or idempotent, then it cannot be the closure operator of a formal context.

This result will help us quickly identify which of the asymmetric compositions later possibly could give rise to alternative formal concepts on some related formal context. However, satisfying these three properties does not, as far as we know, guarantee that the fixedpoints form the formal concept of some formal context.

## Remark 3.3.

Dually, given an adjoint pairL⊣RL\dashv R, the compositionL​RLRis a comonad. On posets, this comonad satisfies dual properties of the closure operators, called being aninterioroperator. In the case of formal concepts, instead of completing a subsetAAto a formal concept, i.e., constructing the smallest formal concept containing the set, the interior operator finds the largest formal conceptinsideAA.

## 4Conclusion

This paper shows that the eight possibilistic operators studied in[5,8]arise canonically as Kan extensions of profunctors. We used this to identify some of their properties as arising from standard categorical arguments, as well as classified exactly which binary compositions, asymmetric or symmetric, which give closure operators. Finally we introduced infinitely many new closure operators onP​(G)P(G)by using ideas from category theory. We are not aware of any studies on these operators.

The categorical approach opens up a new avenue of research, and a new way of understanding existing results. In[4], the authors extend the eight possibilistic operators in formal concept analysis to its fuzzy sibling. These results can be obtained in this categorical framework by replacing the boolean category𝟚\mathbbm{2}by a fuzzy latticeLL. More generally, by enriching in an epistemic uncertainty framework, as introduced in our previous work[1], one can obtain a plethora of extensions to this paper into epistemically uncertain settings.
