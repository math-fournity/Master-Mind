## Abstract

Let $ω$ be a concave modulus of continuity that is weaker than Lipschitz, meaning $ω(t)/t$ diverges as $t$ approaches $0$. We construct a diffeomorphism of the circle with irrational rotation number, in the regularity class $C^{1+ω}$, with a wandering interval. This construction implies that Denjoy's 1932 theorem is sharp in regularity, unless additional restrictions are imposed on the rotation number. The construction in the special case $ω(t) = t\log(1/t)$ settles an open problem dating back to Herman's 1979 work on circle diffeomorphisms, which gave constructions for $ω(t) = t\log(1/t)^{1+\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$. Our examples arise as limits of periodic circle diffeomorphisms with rapidly converging rotation numbers.

## 1.1Background

Let𝕊1:=ℝ/ℤ\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}denote the circle. In the 1880s, Poincaré[18]sought to classify homeomorphisms of𝕊1\mathbb{S}^{1}. To do so, he introducedrotation numbersof circle homeomorphisms. Whenf:𝕊1→𝕊1f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}is a circle homeomorphism with irrational rotation numberα\alpha, Poincaré proved the following dichotomy:
- •

- •

In 1932, Denjoy[3]showed that ifffis adiffeomorphismof the circle with irrational rotation number, of classC1C^{1}, such that the derivativeD​fDfhas bounded variation, thenffcannot admit a wandering interval. Then, by Poincaré’s dichotomy, anyffin the regularity classC1+BVC^{1+\text{BV}}with irrational rotation number must be topologically conjugate to a rotation. In the present day, circle diffeomorphisms that exhibit irrational rotation number and a wandering interval are referred to as “Denjoy examples”.

In the 1970s, Denjoy’s work was revisited by Herman[8]. Among many other results, Herman improved the known regularity range of Denjoy examples. To state Herman’s result, we need to define the appropriate function spaces. ## 1.3Conditions on the rotation number

It is important to notice that, unlike the theorems of Herman and Kim–Koberda, the construction of Theorem1does not work for any irrational rotation number, and one cannot expect it to. We do not explicitly state or prove it, but the construction only works ifα\alphais highly Liouvillean, which means that it is approximated very quickly by rational numbers. Another theorem of Herman improves Denjoy’s theorem under arithmetic assumptions on the rotation number[8, § X.4.4]. Supposeα\alphais Diophantine of bounded type (and so not very quickly approximated by rational numbers),ε\varepsilonis a constant in(0,1)(0,1), andω\omegais a concave modulus of continuity such thatω​(t)=t​(log⁡log⁡log⁡(1t))ε\omega(t)=t\Big(\log\log\log\Big(\frac{1}{t}\Big)\Big)^{\varepsilon}

forttnear0. Letffbe aC1+ωC^{1+\omega}-diffeomorphism of the circle with rotation numberα\alpha. Then, Herman showed thatffis topologically conjugate to a rotation. Determining a precise relationship between arithmetic conditions and the optimal regularity for Denjoy’s theorem remains a significant open problem, which for example was recently advertised in[14, Question22].

## 1.4The Zygmund scale

In the 1990s, Hu–Sullivan[9]ruled out wandering intervals forC1C^{1}circle diffeomorphismsffunder regularity assumptions that are not expressible in terms of the modulus of continuity ofD​fDf. Their first theorem rules out wandering intervals wheneverD​fDfsatisfies theZygmund conditionsupt,s|D​f​(t+s)−2​D​f​(t)+D​f​(t−s)||s|<∞.\sup_{t,s}\frac{|Df(t+s)-2Df(t)+Df(t-s)|}{|s|}<\infty.

It seems plausible that the methods of this paper could be used to construct Denjoy examples for whichD​fDfsatisfies theω\omega-Zygmund conditionsupt,s|D​f​(t+s)−2​D​f​(t)+D​f​(t−s)|ω​(|s|)<∞,\sup_{t,s}\frac{|Df(t+s)-2Df(t)+Df(t-s)|}{\omega(|s|)}<\infty,

whereω\omegais any concave modulus of continuity that is weaker than Lipschitz, but we leave this to follow-up work. Hu–Sullivan’s strongest theorem rules out wandering intervals wheneverD​fDfhas “bounded Zygmund variation” and “bounded quadratic variation”. The sharpness of this condition is an even more interesting question, since it is implied by both bounded variation and the Zygmund condition. This question could also be investigated from the viewpoint of this paper.
