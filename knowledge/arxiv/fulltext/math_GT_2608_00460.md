## Abstract

Multisections were defined by Ben Aribi--Courte--Golla--Moussard as a way to decompose closed manifolds into $1$-handlebodies, which is a generalization of Heegaard splittings and trisections. Previously, their existence was only known in dimensions up to $5$. We show that multisections exist for closed manifolds in arbitrary dimensions. We also show that submanifolds of codimension at least $2$ in multisected manifolds can always be put into an appropriate bridge position, generalizing the existence result on bridge trisections in dimension $4$.

## Abstract.

Multisections were defined by Ben Aribi–Courte–Golla–Moussard as a way to decompose closed manifolds into11-handlebodies, which is a generalization of Heegaard splittings and trisections. Previously, their existence was only known in dimensions up to55. We show that multisections exist for closed manifolds in arbitrary dimensions. We also show that submanifolds of codimension at least22in multisected manifolds can always be put into an appropriate bridge position, generalizing the existence result on bridge trisections in dimension44.

## 1.Introduction

The notion of multisections of smooth manifolds was introduced in[2]as a generalization of Heegaard splittings[5]in dimension33and of trisections in dimension44[3]. Roughly speaking, an(n−1)(n-1)-section(ormultisectionfor short) of a closed smoothnn-manifoldXXis a decompositionX=X1∪⋯∪Xn−1X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{n-1}such that for every index set∅≠I⊊{1,⋯,n−1}\emptyset\neq I\subsetneq\{1,\cdots,n-1\}, the intersectionXI=∩i∈IXiX_{I}=\cap_{i\in I}X_{i}is a smooth manifold (with corners) built only out of0,10,1-handles of the appropriate dimension, and∩i=1n−1Xi\cap_{i=1}^{n-1}X_{i}is a closed surface. A22-section of a (closed, smooth)33-manifold is called aHeegaard splitting, a33-section of a44-manifold is called atrisection, and a44-section of a55-manifold is called aquadrisection. The existence of multisections for arbitrary closed oriented smooth manifolds of dimensions3,4,53,4,5is proved in[5],[3, Theorem 4],[2, Theorem 7.3], respectively, but higher dimensional cases were previously unknown. The first goal of our paper is to prove the existence of multisections in arbitrary dimensions.

## Theorem 1.1.

Every closed smoothnn-manifold admits an(n−1)(n-1)-section.

A key step in the proof of Theorem1.1is related to the notion ofbridge position. Roughly speaking, we say that a closed submanifoldSSin a multisectionX=X1∪⋯∪Xn−1X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{n-1}is inbridge positionif it is transverse to every sector (and subsector), and, for every index set∅≠I⊊{1,⋯,n−1}\emptyset\neq I\subsetneq\{1,\cdots,n-1\}, the submanifoldSI:=S∩XI⊂XIS_{I}:=S\cap X_{I}\subset X_{I}is a collection of∂\partial-parallel disks. WhenX=S3X=S^{3}with the genus-0Heegaard splitting anddimS=1\dim S=1, this recovers the classical notion of bridge position for links. WhendimX=4\dim X=4,dimS=2\dim S=2, this recovers the notion of bridge trisections for surface knots in44-manifolds as introduced by Meier–Zupan[9,10], whose existence was carefully established there. Existence was also proved forX=S5X=S^{5}with the genus-0quadrisection anddimS=3\dim S=3by Aranda–Blackwell–Kim–Naylor–Pongtanapaisan[1]. The second goal of our paper is to prove the existence of bridge position in arbitrary dimensions.

## Theorem 1.2.

IfX=X1∪⋯∪Xn−1X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{n-1}is a multisection andS⊂XS\subset Xis a closed submanifold of codimension at least22, thenSScan be isotoped to be in bridge position.

In fact, we will also prove the existence of bridge positions in a relative setup, whereXXmay have boundary and is multisected in an appropriate sense, and whereSSis a properly embedded submanifold with codimension at least22. The condition on codimension is necessary, as can already be seen in dimension33.

In particular, in dimensions44and55, Theorem1.1and Theorem1.2yield alternative proofs of the existence of trisections[3], quadrisections[2], bridge trisections[9,10], and bridge quadrisections inS5S^{5}[1]. In contrast to the proofs in the cited works, our argument is symmetric with respect to the sectors, lending it a certain aesthetic appeal.
Moreover, our proof is very general: it works in every dimension, for smooth or PL manifolds, orientable or not.

The paper is organized as follows. In the preliminary section2, we give the definitions of multisections and bridge positions for closed manifolds and submanifolds. In Section3, we prove Theorem1.1using an inductive argument assuming Theorem1.2. The proof of Theorem1.2is an analogous inductive argument, presented in Section5, but which requires relaxing our setup to the case of manifolds with boundary and properly embedded submanifolds, which we introduce in Section4. Section5ends with a proof that the handle decompositions we use also exist for PL manifolds. In Section6, partly as an illustration of our proof method, we reprove a result by Lambert-Cole–Miller which asserts that55-manifolds admit trisections that agree with a given trisection on the boundary. The reader may read Section6before Section3for the flavor of the proof of Theorem1.1. Finally, Section7proposes some perspectives about uniqueness of multisections and bridge positions, and generalizations of multisections.
