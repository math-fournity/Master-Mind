## Abstract

We give a new proof that algebraic K-theory increases chromatic height by at most one, originally established by Clausen–Mathew–Naumann–Noel. Our argument proceeds by descent from the Lubin–Tate spectrum, for which the required vanishing follows from Hahn–Wilson’s Quillen–Lichtenbaum result.

## Introduction

Two of the foundational results that have uncovered the relationship between algebraic K-theory and the chromatic filtration are Mitchell’s theorem[Mit90]and the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture proved by Voevodsky–Rost[Voe03,Voe11].
The first says that the algebraic K-theory of any ordinary ringRRvanishes at chromatic heights above11, namelyLT​(m)​K​(R)=0for allm≥2.L_{\mathrm{T}(m)}\mathrm{K}(R)=0\quad\text{for all $m\geq 2$}.

The second, as reinterpreted by Waldhausen[Wal84], says that for suitable ringsRR, the mapK​(R)(p)→L1f​K​(R),\mathrm{K}(R)_{(p)}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1em]{}}L_{1}^{f}\mathrm{K}(R),

which approximates K-theory by chromatic heights0and11, has bounded above fiber.
We note that the latter implies the former for these suitable rings, sinceT​(m)\mathrm{T}(m)-localization vanishes on bounded above spectra.

Based on these and computational evidence from height11, Ausoni–Rognes have outlined the far-reaching redshift conjectures, generalizing these phenomena to higher chromatic heights[Rog00,AR02,AR08].
These conjectures have seen remarkable progress in recent years.
In particular, the higher height analogue of Mitchell’s theorem was proved by Clausen–Mathew–Naumann–Noel[CMNN24], using the purity theorem of Land–Mathew–Meier–Tamme[LMMT24], giving the following redshift upper bound.

## Theorem A([CMNN24, Theorem C],˜13).

If𝒞∈Catperf\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{perf}}isLnfL_{n}^{f}-local thenLT​(m)​K​(𝒞)=0for allm≥n+2.L_{\mathrm{T}(m)}\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{C})=0\quad\text{for all $m\geq n{+}2$}.

Almost concurrently, Hahn–Wilson proved a higher height analogue of the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture[HW22], later revisited by Angelini-Knoll[AK26].
They constructed a certainE3\mathord{\text{E}}_{3}-form of the truncated Brown–Peterson spectrumBP​⟨n⟩\mathrm{BP}\langle n\ranglefor which they proved the following.

## Theorem 1([HW22, Theorem B]).

The following map has a bounded above fiberK​(BP​⟨n⟩)(p)→Ln+1f​K​(BP​⟨n⟩).\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{BP}\langle n\rangle)_{(p)}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1em]{}}L_{n{+}1}^{f}\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{BP}\langle n\rangle).

Yuan proved the redshift lower bound for the Lubin–Tate spectrumEn\mathrm{E}_{n}[Yua26].
Burklund–Schlank–Yuan subsequently extended this to all commutative ring spectra using their chromatic nullstellensatz, which expresses one sense in which Lubin–Tate spectra behave like algebraically closed fields[BSY24].

In this paper, we give a new proof ofTheorem˜Aby descent fromEn\mathrm{E}_{n}.
The vanishing for the Lubin–Tate spectrum follows from the Quillen–Lichtenbaum result of˜1, and the passage to the general case relies on the following more familiar sense in whichEn\mathrm{E}_{n}is algebraically closed.
The pioneering work of Devinatz–Hopkins[DH04], together with subsequent work of Rognes, Baker–Richter, and Mathew[Rog08,BR08,Mat16], shows thatEn\mathrm{E}_{n}is the maximal Galois extension of theK​(n)\mathrm{K}(n)-local sphereSSK​(n)\SS_{\mathrm{K}(n)}.
Recent unpublished work of Burklund–Clausen–Levy shows thatSST​(n)\SS_{\mathrm{T}(n)}has the same Galois theory asSSK​(n)\SS_{\mathrm{K}(n)}, and that its algebraic closure coincides withEn\mathrm{E}_{n}as well, which in particular gives the following.

## Theorem 2(Burklund–Clausen–Levy).

TheT​(n)\mathrm{T}(n)-local filtered colimit of theT​(n)\mathrm{T}(n)-local finite Galois extensionsRRofSST​(n)\SS_{\mathrm{T}(n)}isEn\mathrm{E}_{n}, that is, there is an isomorphismLT​(n)​(colimR/SST​(n)R)→∼En∈CAlg⁡(SpT​(n)).L_{\mathrm{T}(n)}(\operatorname*{colim}_{R/\SS_{\mathrm{T}(n)}}R)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1em]{\smash{\raisebox{-2.41112pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}}}\mathrm{E}_{n}\quad\in\quad\operatorname{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{T}(n)}).
