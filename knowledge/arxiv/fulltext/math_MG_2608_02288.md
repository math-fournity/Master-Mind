## Abstract

We study the reconstruction of planar polygonal domains from non-local integral-geometric invariants, namely the interpoint distance distribution and the Riesz energy function (Brylinski beta function), together with the chord length distribution in the convex case. We show that a generic polygonal domain is uniquely determined, up to Euclidean isometry, by its Riesz energy function or, equivalently, by its interpoint distance distribution. For convex domains this also yields reconstruction from the chord length distribution, extending Waksman's classical generic reconstruction theorem beyond the convex setting.   The proof uses a boundary interpoint distance distribution weighted by the scalar product of the outer unit normals. This weighted distribution is equivalent to the Riesz energy function. By analyzing jumps and blow-up terms of its second and third derivatives at critical lengths, we recover the side lengths, their cyclic incidence, and the exterior angles of the polygon. In an appendix we obtain complementary identification results for regular polygonal domains when the competing domain is convex or has the same number of sides.

## 1Introduction

Inspired by inverse problems such as Kac’s celebrated question “Can one hear the shape of a drum?”, we consider a distance-based reconstruction problem: to what extent can a planar domain be recovered from non-local invariants defined by pairwise distances? The invariants considered here are the Riesz energy function and the interpoint distance distribution, and, for convex bodies, the chord length distribution.
(Here, abodyin Euclidean space refers to a compact subset equal to the closure of its interior.) In a recent work, the author proved that generic finite metric spaces are uniquely determined by their magnitude functions ([13]) or by the eigenvalues of their similarity matrices ([14]). The present paper is a natural continuation of this line of research, focusing on planar polygonal domains.

Let us recall the definitions of these invariants. LetXXbe either a compact bodyΩ⊂ℝ2\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}or its boundaryΓ=∂Ω\Gamma=\partial\Omega.
Theinterpoint distance distributionΨX​(r)\Psi_{X}(r)maps eachr>0r>0to the product measure of the set of pairs(x,y)∈X×X(x,y)\in X\times Xwhose distance|x−y||x-y|is at mostrr.
For a convex bodyΩ\Omega, thechord length distributionΦΩ​(r)\Phi_{\Omega}(r)assigns tor>0r>0the measure of the set of lines meetingΩ\Omegain a chord of length at mostrr, where we use a measure on the space of affine lines inℝ2\mathbb{R}^{2}that is isometry-invariant.
TheRiesz energy function(orBrylinski beta function)BX​(z)B_{X}(z)is defined for a complex variablezzas the integralBX​(z)=∫X∫X|x−y|z​𝑑x​𝑑yB_{X}(z)=\int_{X}\int_{X}|x-y|^{z}\,dx\,dy

This integral converges and defines a holomorphic function forℜ⁡𝔢​z>−dimX\Re\mathfrak{e}\,z>-\dim X. WhenXXis smooth or polygonal, the meromorphic continuation to the complex plane yields a meromorphic function with only simple poles. This function was introduced for knots by Brylinski[4]and extended to compact bodies by O’Hara and Solanes[16].

As established in[16], the interpoint distance distributionΨX​(r)\Psi_{X}(r)and the Riesz energy functionBX​(z)B_{X}(z)are equivalent to each other via the Mellin transform. Moreover, ifΩ\Omegais a convex body, the chord length distributionΦX​(r)\Phi_{X}(r)is also equivalent to these functions, as noted by Matérn[9].

The question of whether a domain is determined by these functions can be viewed as a generalization of Blaschke’s problem: whether a convex body is uniquely determined by the moment integral of the chord length (which is equivalent to the chord length distribution via the Mellin transform). While Mallows and Clark[8]constructed non-isometric convex polygons with the same chord length distribution, Waksman[20]demonstrated that such counterexamples are exceptional by proving that a generic closed planar convex polygon is uniquely determined up to isometry by its chord length distribution. The main theorem of this paper extends this result to non-convex polygons. Namely, we prove that anygenericplanar polygon (under a genericity condition different from Waksman’s) is uniquely determined up to isometry by its Riesz energy function, or equivalently, by its interpoint distance distribution (and by its chord length distribution if it is convex).

The key steps of the proof are as follows. First, applying Stokes’ theorem yields a boundary integral representation of the Riesz energy for a compact body, expressed as a boundary Riesz energy weighted by⟨νx,νy⟩\langle\nu_{x},\nu_{y}\rangle, whereν\nudenotes the unit outer normal vector[16]. Via the Mellin transform, this is equivalent to theν\nu-weighted boundary interpoint distance distribution. While thisν\nu-weighted distribution is smooth nearr=0r=0, itsC2C^{2}or higher regularity breaks down at specific values ofrr, calledcritical lengths, namely, inter-vertex distances and theinterior height, which are the lengths of perpendiculars dropped from vertices to interiors of sides.
The residue information and the genericity conditions uniquely identify the actual side lengths among the critical lengths.
By computing the jumps (the differences between the left and right limits) in the second and third derivatives at these critical lengths, we recover local geometric information, such as the angles between the corresponding sides. The genericity condition is introduced precisely to prevent these jump contributions from interfering with one another, ensuring that the geometric parameters can be recovered unambiguously.
The polygon is then reconstructed by matching the extracted side lengths and angles, using the genericity conditions.

Furthermore, at the opposite extreme from generic polygons, we show in the Appendix that regular polygons, which possess the highest degree of symmetry, can also be uniquely identified by their Riesz energy functions (or equivalent invariants) under a certain condition. Although the overlapping of critical lengths causes geometric information to blend together, we demonstrate that unique identification remains achievable by combining residue information with the singular behavior of the weighted boundary distance distribution.

Regarding the unique determination of domains from non-local invariants, several related results are worth noting. Highly symmetric domains, such as balls, are known to be uniquely determined by their Riesz energy functions owing to the isoperimetric inequality[11]. Additionally, Matheron’s conjecture asks whether a convex bodyKKis uniquely determined up to translation and reflection by itscovariogramgK​(x)=Vol⁡(K∩(K+x))g_{K}(x)=\operatorname{Vol}(K\cap(K+x)). This conjecture was answered affirmatively for planar convex polygons by Nagel[10]and for general planar convex bodies by Averkov and Bianchi[1].
