## Abstract

We construct two explicit countable discrete groups $Γ_1$ and $Γ_2$ that are both ICC and have Kazhdan's property (T). Although $Γ_1$ and $Γ_2$ are not isomorphic as groups, their group von Neumann algebras are isomorphic: $L(Γ_1)\cong L(Γ_2)$. This provides a counterexample to Connes' rigidity conjecture for ICC property (T) groups. ## Abstract.

We construct two explicit countable discrete groupsΓ1\Gamma_{1}andΓ2\Gamma_{2}that are both ICC and have Kazhdan’s property (T). AlthoughΓ1\Gamma_{1}andΓ2\Gamma_{2}are not isomorphic as groups, their group von Neumann algebras are isomorphic:L​(Γ1)≅L​(Γ2){L}(\Gamma_{1})\cong{L}(\Gamma_{2}). This provides a counterexample to Connes’ rigidity conjecture for ICC property (T) groups. This result was obtained with assistance from GPT-5.6 Sol, independently of and concurrently with work by OpenAI[19].

## 1.Introduction

In 1980, Alain Connes proved that ifΓ\Gammais a countable ICC (infinite conjugacy classes) group with property (T), then both the outer automorphism groupOut⁡(L​(Γ))\operatorname{Out}(L(\Gamma))and the Murray–von Neumann fundamental groupℱ​(L​(Γ))\mathcal{F}(L(\Gamma))are countable[7]. This was one of the first manifestations of the property (T) as a rigidity phenomenon for von Neumann algebras and suggested thatL​(Γ)L(\Gamma)might retain substantial information aboutΓ\Gamma.

At that time, the main known examples of infinite discrete groups with property (T) came from lattices in semisimple Lie groups, with higher rank lattices forming the central motivating class. For higher rank lattices, the strong rigidity theorems of Mostow and Margulis[18,17]show that ifΓi<Gi\Gamma_{i}<G_{i},i=1,2i=1,2, are irreducible higher rank lattices in connected semisimple real Lie groups with trivial center and no nontrivial compact factors, then an isomorphismΓ1≅Γ2\Gamma_{1}\cong\Gamma_{2}forces an isomorphismG1≅G2G_{1}\cong G_{2}. Thus higher rank lattices provided a natural model for the idea that a sufficiently rigid analytic invariant might retain the underlying group.

Against this background, Connes proposed the following bold conjecture[8].

## Connes’ rigidity conjecture for ICC
property (T) groups.

Every countable ICC property (T) groupΓ\GammaisW∗-superrigid: ifΛ\Lambdais a countable discrete group such thatL​(Λ)≅L​(Γ)L(\Lambda)\cong L(\Gamma), thenΛ≅Γ\Lambda\cong\Gammaas groups.

An early rigidity result illustrating the information retained by group factors was obtained by Cowling–Haagerup[9]. They proved that ifΓ\Gammais a lattice inSp⁡(n,1)/{±1}\operatorname{Sp}(n,1)/\{\pm 1\}, withn≥2n\geq 2, thennnis determined by the group factorL​(Γ)L(\Gamma). Beginning in the early 2000s, Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory produced decisive advances in the classification of group and group measure space von Neumann algebras[20,21]. This program also yielded W∗-superrigidity results for group actions, notably Ioana’s theorem for Bernoulli actions of property (T) groups[15]. At the level of group factors themselves, the first W∗-superrigid groups were constructed by Ioana–Popa–Vaes[14]. Later, Chifan–Ioana–Osin–Sun constructed the first W∗-superrigid groups with property (T)[4]. Subsequent work developed this circle of ideas in several directions, including rigidity of outer automorphisms and embeddings, cocycle-twisted and virtual W∗-superrigidity, and W∗-superrigidity for groups with infinite center[5,10,6,3,11,2].

In the decades after Connes formulated the conjecture, the class of known property (T) groups expanded far beyond higher rank lattices, and the original conjecture came to be regarded as too broad in its full generality. At the same time, positive W∗-rigidity results typically relied on substantial additional algebraic, geometric, or deformation-theoretic structure. It therefore became natural to distinguish the original conjecture, formulated for arbitrary ICC property (T) groups, from the more geometric rigidity problem for higher rank lattices. This motivates the following more focused version of Connes’ conjecture, formulated by Houdayer[13].

## Connes’ rigidity conjecture for higher rank lattices.

Fori=1,2i=1,2, letGiG_{i}be a connected simple real Lie group with trivial center andrkℝ⁡(Gi)≥2\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{R}}(G_{i})\geq 2, and letΓi<Gi\Gamma_{i}<G_{i}be a lattice. IfL​(Γ1)≅L​(Γ2)L(\Gamma_{1})\cong L(\Gamma_{2}), thenG1≅G2G_{1}\cong G_{2}as Lie groups.

This version is weaker than the restriction of Connes’ original conjecture to higher rank lattices, but it retains the geometric rigidity suggested by the theorems of Mostow and Margulis. The original conjecture should therefore be viewed not merely as a prediction awaiting a yes-or-no answer, but as a guiding problem that led to increasingly precise forms of W∗-rigidity.

The present work starts from the possibility that property (T), once separated from the geometric structure of higher rank lattices, may be insufficient by itself to enforce W∗-superrigidity. Our main result confirms this possibility by disproving Connes’ original conjecture.

## Theorem A.

There exist explicitly defined countable discrete groupsΓ1\Gamma_{1}andΓ2\Gamma_{2}such that:
- (1)

bothΓ1\Gamma_{1}andΓ2\Gamma_{2}have Kazhdan’s property (T);
- (2)

both groups are ICC;
- (3)

Γ1≇Γ2\Gamma_{1}\not\cong\Gamma_{2};
- (4)

there is a∗*-isomorphismL​(Γ1)≅L​(Γ2){L}(\Gamma_{1})\cong{L}(\Gamma_{2})

between their group von Neumann algebras.

## Independent and concurrent work.

On August 1, 2026, OpenAI publicly announced a disproof of Connes’ rigidity conjecture produced by an internal version of Astra, its next major model[19, Chapter 4]. The present construction was developed with the assistance of GPT-5.6 Sol, and a preliminary proof manuscript had been assembled by July 27, 2026. The two works were developed independently and concurrently, as is also recorded in[19, Chapter 4, p. 110, Acknowledgments]. The OpenAI binary-carry construction produces a countably infinite family of pairwise nonisomorphic, mutually commensurable ICC property (T) groups with isomorphic group von Neumann algebras.

Both constructions exploit the same general principle. LetHHbe a countable discrete group, letDiD_{i}be a countable discrete abelian group fori=1,2i=1,2, and letθi:H↷Di\theta_{i}:H\curvearrowright D_{i}be an action by automorphisms fori=1,2i=1,2. Denote byαi:H↷(D^i,μD^i)\alpha_{i}:H\curvearrowright(\widehat{D}_{i},\mu_{\widehat{D}_{i}})the induced action on the Pontryagin dual equipped with its Haar probability measure. Fourier transform givesL​(Di⋊θiH)≅L∞​(D^i)⋊αiH.L(D_{i}\rtimes_{\theta_{i}}H)\cong L^{\infty}(\widehat{D}_{i})\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}H.

Consequently, the semidirect productsDi⋊θiHD_{i}\rtimes_{\theta_{i}}Hmay have isomorphic group von Neumann algebras wheneverαi:H↷(D^i,μD^i)\alpha_{i}:H\curvearrowright(\widehat{D}_{i},\mu_{\widehat{D}_{i}})are conjugate as nonsingular probability-space actions, even if the conjugating map does not preserve the compact abelian group structures. This is the common mechanism underlying both counterexamples.

In the OpenAI construction, the acting groupHHis kept fixed while the discrete abelian kernelsDiD_{i}are changed. The basic pair is distinguished by torsion:D1D_{1}has exponent two, whereasD2D_{2}contains elements of order four. By takingHHto be torsion-free, every finite-order element ofDi⋊θiHD_{i}\rtimes_{\theta_{i}}Hbelongs toDiD_{i}. HenceD1⋊θ1HD_{1}\rtimes_{\theta_{1}}Hhas no elements of order four, whereasD2⋊θ2HD_{2}\rtimes_{\theta_{2}}Hdoes, so the two groups are not isomorphic. Shifted versions of the binary-carry construction, together with an additional finite-orbit invariant, distinguish the resulting countably infinite family.

In the present construction, by contrast, both the quotient groupHHand the elementary abelian normal subgroupDDare kept fixed. We change only the two actionsθi:H↷D\theta_{i}:H\curvearrowright Ddefining the semidirect productsΓi=D⋊θiH\Gamma_{i}=D\rtimes_{\theta_{i}}H. The induced actions onD^\widehat{D}are conjugate as nonsingularHH-actions through a quadratic fiber shear, but the twoHH-module structures onDDremain different. The nonisomorphism ofΓ1\Gamma_{1}andΓ2\Gamma_{2}is detected by a semisimple–nonsemisimple distinction between these twoHH-module structures ofDD.
