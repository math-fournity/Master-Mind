## 1.Introduction

The notion ofalgebraically coherent categoryfirst appeared in[13]as an algebraic version of the notion ofcoherent category[22]. The parallelism stems from the fact that a coherent category𝒞\mathcal{C}is a regular category such that every change-of-base functor of the codomain fibrationcod:Arr​(𝒞)⟶𝒞\mathrm{cod}:\mathrm{Arr}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}is coherent, that is, preserves finite limits and jointly strongly epimorphic pairs of arrows. Analogously, an algebraically coherent category is a finitely complete category𝒞\mathcal{C}for which the coherence is required of every change-of-base functor of the fibration of pointscod:Pt​(𝒞)⟶𝒞\mathrm{cod}:\mathrm{Pt}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}.

The property of being algebraically coherent for a category𝒞\mathcal{C}has some interesting consequences, particularly when the category𝒞\mathcal{C}is semi-abelian. Indeed, in this case the category𝒞\mathcal{C}satisfies the so-calledSmith is Huqcondition (SH), isstrongly protomodularand satisfies thenormality of Higgins commutator of normal subobjectscondition (NH). Knowing that a category is not only semi-abelian, but satisfies these additional conditions is crucial for many results in categorical algebra, in particular to applications in (co)homology theory.

Semi-abelian categories were introduced by Janelidze, Márki and Tholen[21], building on Bourn’s earlier notion of protomodular category[8]. A finitely complete category with a zero object is protomodular if and only if it satisfies the Split Short Five Lemma. Semi-abelian categories are defined as exact (in the sense of Barr[3]), protomodular categories with finite coproducts and a zero object. These categories are suitable to develop several basic aspects of homological algebra of groups and rings[6], as well as an abstract theory of commutators and of ideals[7].

There are several examples of semi-abelian categories which are algebraically coherent, such as the categories of groups, rings (not necessarily unitary), Lie algebras and cocommutative Hopf algebras over a field[16].

Skew braces were introduced in[18]generalizing braces[24], in connection with the study of set-theoretic solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation. A skew brace is a triple(A,+,∘)(A,+,\circ), where(A,+)(A,+)and(A,∘)(A,\circ)are groups such that the compatibility conditiona∘(b+c)=a∘b−a+a∘ca\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ cholds for alla,b,c∈Aa,b,c\in A. Skew braces form a variety ofΩ\Omega-groups in the sense of Higgins[19], hence in particular a semi-abelian category. They share many interesting properties with the category of groups. For instance, the category𝖲𝖪𝖡\mathsf{SKB}of skew braces satisfies the (SH) condition[9]and is strongly protomodular[4]. However, in[1], it was recently shown that it is not action accessible.

In these notes, we further prove another difference between𝖲𝖪𝖡\mathsf{SKB}and𝖦𝗋𝗉\mathsf{Grp}. More precisely, we show that𝖲𝖪𝖡\mathsf{SKB}(as well as braces) is not algebraically coherent. This is done by showing that theThree Subobjects Lemmadoes not hold for a (skew) brace of dimension 16 and for an infinite family of (skew) braces of dimensionp4p^{4}, withp≠2p\not=2prime. The same counterexample shows that also (NH) is not satisfied. Since any locally algebraically cartesian closed category in the sense of[10]is algebraically coherent, we deduce that𝖲𝖪𝖡\mathsf{SKB}(as well as braces) is not locally algebraically cartesian closed. Therefore, also the category of cocommutative Hopf braces is not algebraically coherent, hence it is not locally algebraically cartesian closed.

Any algebraically coherent semi-abelian category is strongly protomodular[13, Theorem 6.24]and satisfies the ”Smith is Huq” condition (SH): two equivalence relations on a given object always centralise each other (i.e. commute in the sense of Smith[25,23]) as soon as their normalisations commute in the sense of Huq[20]. Any algebraically coherent semi-abelian category also satisfies the condition (NH) of normality of Higgins commutators of normal subobjects[11,12], see[13, Theorem 6.18]: the Higgins commutator of two normal subobjects of a given object is again a normal subobject, so that it coincides with the Huq commutator.

Hence, in such a category𝒞\mathcal{C}, the Higgins commutator and the Huq commutator of two normal subojectsX,YX,Yof an objectAAare the same, and are usually denoted by[X,Y][X,Y].
