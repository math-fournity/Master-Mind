## 1.Introduction

To motivate the discussion, let us examine a certain relationship between dynamics and topology in group theory. LetGGbe a finitely generated group andμ\mube a symmetric probability measure onGGwhose support generates the group. Some of the dynamical properties of the associated random walk can be arranged in the following hierarchy.
- (a)

- (d)

GGisLiouville, i.e. every boundedμ\mu-harmonic function is constant. For example, ifGGis virtually nilpotent then it is Liouville for any such measureμ\mu.
- (e)

GGisamenable, i.e. the spectral radius is strictly less than one.

The relevant topological invariant is the space of endsℰ​(G)\mathcal{E}\left(G\right)of the groupGG. By Stallings theorem|ℰ​(G)|∈{0,1,2}|\mathcal{E}\left(G\right)|\in\{0,1,2\}orℰ​(G)\mathcal{E}\left(G\right)is the Cantor space. Ifℰ​(G)\mathcal{E}\left(G\right)is a Cantor space then the groupGGis non-amenable, which implies the negation of case (e). The remaining (and in some sense the most abundant) case|ℰ​(G)|=1|\mathcal{E}\left(G\right)|=1is harder to categorize.

We move on to another related classical setting of regular covers.

Our goal will be to extend this relationship going beyond regular covers. We immediately run into two problems. First, a general hyperbolic manifold is not associated to any particular covering group. We resolve this by replacing the covering group with a weaker topological invariant, which still retains some useful dynamical and geometric information, i.e. the space of ends of the manifold. For regular covers of compact manifolds, this is the same invariant as the space of ends of the group of deck transformations. However, in the presence of cusps we need to be more careful and distinguish the subspaceℰ∞​(N)\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\left(N\right)of infinite-volume ends of the manifoldNN.

The second problem is that, for an arbitrary manifoldNN, we cannot expect to be able to say much based solely on its space of (infinite-volume) endsℰ∞​(N)\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\left(N\right). For example, consider the funnelF=⟨γ⟩\ℍ2F=\left<\gamma\right>\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}, whereγ\gammais some loxodromic isometry of the hyperbolic planeℍ2\mathbb{H}^{2}. Then|ℰ∞​(F)|=2|\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\left(F\right)|=2buth​(F)>0h(F)>0. At the other extreme, by gluing pairs of pants with rapidly shrinking cuff lengths, one may construct a hyperbolic surfaceSSsuch thatℰ∞​(S)\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\left(S\right)a Cantor space buth​(S)=0h(S)=0.

We point out that the recurrence question (i.e. property (B) above) for hyperbolic surfaces is a very delicate and classical one, admitting numerous equivalent characterizations and reformulizations, see e.g.[BHŠ22]and the references therein. Some authors call it thetype problem[AS15]. Recurrent surfaces are said to be ofparabolic type. The class of all parabolic type surfaces is denotedOGO_{G}.
On its own, the space of ends is a hopelessly weak invariant for resolving the type problem. Interesting such examples, where the recurrence question boils down to the converge of certain series involving lengths of closed geodesics on the surface, include the two-ended flute surfaces[BAS93]or infinitely-ended Cantor trees[MCM98,ŠAR24,PAN24,BDŠ26].

## 1.1.Unimodular random hyperbolic manifolds

To deal with this issue, we move from a deterministic to a random setting, and introduce a powerful additional assumption called unimodularity[AB22]. A random pointed hyperbolic manifold is a probability measure on the Gromov–Hausdorff space of hyperbolic manifolds. Such a random object is calledunimodularif, roughly speaking, its distribution“looks the same from all points”(see §2for the formal definition). Unimodular hyperbolic random manifolds generalize regular covers. Our point of departure is an extension of Stallings’ result to the unimodular setting by Biringer and Raimbault.

## Theorem([BR17]).

Letμ\mube a unimodular random hyperbolic manifold. Thenμ\mu-almost every manifoldMMhas|ℰ∞​(M)|∈{0,1,2}|\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\left(M\right)|\in\{0,1,2\}orℰ∞​(M)\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\left(M\right)is a Cantor space.

We remark that a unimodular hyperbolic manifold is essentially the same thing as a discrete invariant random subgroup of the group of isometriesSO​(n,1)\mathrm{SO}(n,1), see[BT17, Theorem 1.4]and[AB22, Theorem 2.8], but we will not be pursuing the latter viewpoint here. Another remark is that the result of[BR17]continues to hold for stationary random hyperbolic manifolds[LS26].

Our first main result is the analogue of the above-mentioned fact that a two-ended finitely generated group is recurrent:

## Theorem 1.1.

Letμ\mube a unimodular random hyperbolic manifold. Ifμ\mu-almost every manifold has two infinite-volume ends thenμ\mu-almost every manifold is recurrent.

While the implications between the properties (A)–(E) are not true in complete generality for arbitrary manifolds, they do carry over to unimodular random manifolds (and more generally, to stationary random graphs[BC12]or manifolds[LES16]). For example, it is a consequence of our Theorem1.1that a unimodular random hyperbolic manifold with two infinite-volume ends isamenable, i.e. has zero Cheeger constant in the sense of property (E) above (this consequence can also be obtained directly).

Our second main result is the analogue of the fact that an infinitely-ended finitely generated group is non-Liouville:

## Theorem 1.2.

Letμ\mube a unimodular random hyperbolic manifold. Ifμ\mu-almost every manifold has infinitely many infinite-volume ends thenμ\mu-almost every manifold is non-Liouville. In particular, it has positive drift and positive volume growth.

The conclusion of Theorem1.2certainly implies thatμ\mu-almost every manifold is transient (i.e. non recurrent), in distinction from the two-ended case considered in Theorem1.1. We note that unlike the more strict case of regular covers (or finitely generated groups), a unimodular random hyperbolic manifold with infinitely many infinite-volume ends may almost surely be either amenable or non-amenable.

As part of the proof of both Theorems1.1and1.2we are led to consider the interesting notion of anexit map. This is a natural mapζ:T1​M→ℰ​(M)\zeta:T^{1}M\to\mathcal{E}\left(M\right)defined from the unit tangent bundle of a giventransienthyperbolic manifold to its space of ends. The properties of this map in the unimodular random setting are studied §7.

## Remark 1.3.

For unimodular random graphs with finite expected root degree, the analogues of Theorem1.1and Theorem1.2were obtained
in[AHN+16]and[AHN+18, Proposition 7.6]respectively.

## 1.2.Delaunay graphs and point processes

For general unimodular random hyperbolic manifolds, including the ubiquitous one-ended ones, the situation is more complicated and not much can be said in general using our methods.
However, we do provide a criterion for transience (which can be regarded as a certain solution of the type problem) that applies in both the deterministic and the random setting:

Given a hyperbolic manifoldMMand a discrete point configurationmmon it, we study the associated Delaunay graph𝒟​𝒢​(M,m)\mathcal{DG}(M,m). This is a graph whose vertices represent the Voronoi cells at the points of configurationmmand whose edges are geodesic arcs corresponding to neighboring Voronoi cells. We call the point configurationmmgood(Definition4.8) if its Delaunay graph enjoys certain geometric properties, most notably, that the angle between any pair of incident edges of the graph𝒟​𝒢​(M,m)\mathcal{DG}(M,m)is uniformly bounded away from zero.

We now state a deterministic result relating the transience of a hyperbolic manifold with the transience of a Delaunay graph on it.

## Theorem 1.4.

LetMMbe annn-dimensional hyperbolic manifold andmma good point configuration onMM. Equip the Delaunay graphG=𝒟​𝒢​(M,m)G=\mathcal{DG}(M,m)with transition probabilities proportional to the(n−2)(n-2)-th powers of the distances inMM. Namely, for each vertexv∈V​(G)=mv\in V(G)=mand edge{v,u}∈E​(G)\{v,u\}\in E(G)set the transition probabilityp​(v,u)=dM​(v,u)n−2∑{v,w}∈E​(G)dM​(v,w)n−2.p(v,u)=\frac{d_{M}(v,u)^{n-2}}{\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E(G)}d_{M}(v,w)^{n-2}}.

Then the manifoldMMis a transient if and only if the Delaunay graphGGis transient with respect to the random walk with transition probabilities given bypp.

Theorem1.4is based on an analysis of capacities of graphs and of manifolds. It is inspired to a large extent by the ideas of[GNS17], where a similar statement is obtained for planar surfaces (in dimension two). Theorem1.4allows us to reduce the transience problem for manifolds to an analogous one for graphs, which is typically easier to settle.

We address the problem of constructing good point configurations on hyperbolic manifolds and show that these always exist (Theorem5.9). The construction of a good point configuration is probabilistic (even for a given deterministic hyperbolic manifold). The idea is to make use of so-calledcontrolled point processes(Definition5.1). Roughly speaking, these are point processes whose intensity is comparable to the injectivity radius function on the manifold. A controlled point process is not hard to construct by a rather explicit probabilistic metric thinning method, and is sufficiently well-behaved geometrically to produce good point configurations (§5).

## 2.Unimodular metric measure spaces

In this preliminary section we introduce some key notions, including metric measure spaces, which is a natural framework to define and study unimodular random objects. We also discuss the space of ends associated to a metric space.

For a much more detailed introduction to unimodular metric measures spaces, their properties and various examples, we refer the reader to[KHE23].

## 2.1.Metric measure spaces

A metric measure space is essentially a space equipped with both a metric and a measure in a compatible manner.
More precisely, we use the following definition from[BOW15, §3].

## 6.Transience for unimodular random manifolds via graphs

## 7.The geodesic flow and the exit map

For the purpose of this section, we find it more convenient to work with the dynamics of the geodesic flow rather than Brownian motion. These two notions are geometrically related. First, the Brownian motion is transient if and only if the geodesic flow is[SUL81]. Second, a generic sample path of Brownian motion sublinearly tracks a geodesic ray inℍn\mathbb{H}^{n}[BHM11]. Lastly, the hitting measure induced by Brownian motion on the Gromov boundary∂ℍn\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure[QUI06, Appendix B]. Hence, in the transient case, the Brownian motion and and the geodesic flow starting at a given base point induce equivalent hitting measures on the boundary at infinity.

We remark that by the classic Hopf–Tsuji–Sullivan dichotomy[HOP71,TSU59], the geodesic flow on a given hyperbolic manifold is recurrent if and only if it is ergodic.
