## Abstract

We investigate the holographic dual of the extended bulk thermodynamics of dyonic dilaton AdS black holes by allowing both the cosmological constant and Newton's gravitational constant to vary. In the dual CFT thermodynamics, the central charge $C$ and chemical potential $μ$ as its conjugate enter the thermodynamic relations, in addition to $(\tilde{T},\tilde{S})$, $(\tilde{Q},\tildeΦ)$, $(\tilde{P},\tildeΨ)$, $(\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\tilde{V})$. We consider sixteen different ensembles related to those five pairs of thermodynamic variables which we separate into fixed volume and pressure ensembles. Due to the symmetry on ($\tilde{Q},\tilde{P}$), we only need to analyze five ensembles each in fixed volume and pressure ensembles. In the fixed volume ensembles, it is found that the fixed ($\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{V},μ$) and ($\tildeΦ,\tilde{P},\tilde{V},μ$) exhibit a zeroth-order phase transition. Interestingly, the fixed ($\tildeΦ,\tildeΨ,\tilde{V},C$) ensemble shows the zeroth- and first-order phase transitions at above and below critical potential $\tildeΥ_c$, respectively. In the fixed pressure ensembles, it is found that richer structures appear where the sign of $μ$ heavily influences phase space for several ensembles. For fixed ($\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},μ$) and ($\tildeΦ,\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},μ$), they show the zeroth-, first-, and second-order phase transitions. For the fixed ($\tildeΦ,\tildeΨ,\tilde{\mathcal{P}},μ$) ensemble, there is only a zeroth-order phase transition occurs. The other ensembles which we do not mention eventually do not show any phase transition. These findings provide deeper insight into how bulk gravitational variations, particularly regarding the cosmological and gravitational constants, translate to exact critical phenomena and richer phase structures within CFT.

## 1Introduction

Since Hawking’s seminal work on black hole radiation[1,2], the study of black hole thermodynamics has grown into one of the most attractive areas of research in gravitational physics. It is now widely viewed as a promising route toward understanding the quantum nature of gravity. Over the past few decades, the thermodynamic properties of many black hole solutions have been explored in detail, leading to several important developments. These include the connection between the laws of gravitation and the laws of thermodynamics[3,4], for instance, the study of black hole thermodynamics from a geometric perspective[5,6,7,8,9,10,11,12,13], the use of black holes as holographic models in condensed matter physics[14,15]and quantum chromodynamics[16].

Recently, several studies show that the thermodynamics of black holes in asymptotic anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime are quite distinct from those in flat or de Sitter (dS) space. In particular, since the emergence of “black hole chemistry”[17,18,19], the study of black hole thermodynamics has undergone a significant paradigm shift that follows the recognition that the cosmological constantΛ\Lambdashould be treated as a thermodynamic pressure
in an extended thermodynamic phase space that includes
its conjugate volume[20,21,22,23,24,25,26]. This framework has revealed a rich landscape of critical phenomena in bulk gravity, such as first-order transition from radiation
to a black hole (or vice-versa)[27], which is related to confinement (deconfinement) transition of the dual quark gluon plasma[28],
and can be understood as a solid-liquid phase transition[17].
Further investigations have led to the discovery of
Van der Waals-like phase transitions[29,30,31,32], superfluid phase transitions[33], re-entrant phase transitions[34,35], triple points[36,37], and multicritical points[38,39,40].

Currently, the AdS/CFT correspondence provides a powerful dictionary for exploring the dual boundary Conformal Field Theory (CFT) thermodynamics. Recently, this correspondence has been extended by treating Newton’s gravitational constantGGas a dynamical thermodynamic variable in addition to the extended bulk thermodynamics[41,42,43,44,45,46], or alternatively varying the conformal factor of the boundary metric[47].
This formulation was raised when one considers the mapping of bulk pressure and thermodynamic volume to CFT thermodynamics, since both are not dual to the pressure and spatial volume in CFT thermodynamics, respectively. Moreover, the interpretation of the bulk pressure seemed incompatible with the holography[10,11,42,48,49,50]. Currently, this correspondence, known as holographic thermodynamics, has been successful in mapping the bulk thermodynamics of charged and charged rotating AdS black holes in Einstein-Maxwell system[51,52]. This formulation helps to correspond the bulk pressure and thermodynamic volume in bulk thermodynamics to the quantities in CFT thermodynamics. Concretely, varying the cosmological constant simultaneously with varying Newton’s gravitational constant corresponds to the varying of the central chargeCCor the number of colorsNNin the dual gauge theory. To bridge the bulk and CFT thermodynamics, interestingly one can construct such mixed thermodynamics by allowing the central chargeCCof the dual boundary theory to emerge as a thermodynamic quantity with a corresponding chemical potentialμc\mu_{c}as its conjugate[53]. By applying the AdS/CFT dictionary, the standard first law of the bulk thermodynamics can be mapped to the first law of the CFT thermodynamics via mixed thermodynamics. Furthermore, the generalized Smarr formula for black holes can be shown also that this is dual to the Euler formula in the CFT[43,45]. Not only in Einstein-Maxwell system, this formulation is succesfully applied also to beyond this theory such as in Born-Infeld theory[54,55]and Gauss-Bonnet theory[56,57]to investigate the phase transitions.

Our purpose here is to systematically investigate the holographic dual of the extended thermodynamics for dyonic dilaton AdS black holes by varying both the cosmological constant and Newton’s gravitational constant. We find that presence of the dilaton and axion fields provides a much richer structure to the phase phase compared to that of charged AdS black holes[51]. We map bulk parameters to their dual boundary CFT variables which are temperature-entropy(T~,S~)(\tilde{T},\tilde{S}), electric charge-potential(Q~,Φ~)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\Phi}), magnetic charge-potential(P~,Ψ~)(\tilde{P},\tilde{\Psi}), pressure-volume(𝒫~,V~)(\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\tilde{V}), and central charge-chemical potential (C,μC,\mu), enabling us to explore the thermodynamic phase space of sixteen different ensembles. Due to the symmetry on (Q~,P~\tilde{Q},\tilde{P}), we only need to analyze five ensembles each in fixed volume and pressure ensembles. We aim to explore the phase transitions and stability of the CFT states, which are
dual to dyonic dilaton AdS black holes, providing a new perspective on how bulk gravitational variations translate to exact critical phenomena in boundary field theories.

After the Introduction in Section1, we carry out the dyonic dilaton AdS black hole from its rotating version by settinga=0a=0in Section2. In Section3, we focus on the construction generalized mass/energy formulas of dyonic dilaton AdS black holes in extended thermodynamics, mixed thermodynamics, and CFT thermodynamics. In Section4, we delve into the phase transitions of the CFT thermodynamics for fixed spatial volume ensembles. Afterward, we will explore phase behaviors of the CFT states in the fixed pressure ensembles in Section5. We then present the summary in the last section.

## 6Summary

We have systematically investigated the holographic thermodynamics of static dyonic dilaton AdS black holes. The metric is obtained by setting the spin parameter to be zero for dyonic Kerr-Sen-AdS black holes, which are an exact solution of Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton-Axion theory. The static black hole has a root atr0=p2/mr_{0}=p^{2}/mand another root at a larger value ofrr. However, whenr0=p2/mr_{0}=p^{2}/m,Σ​(r0)=0\Sigma(r_{0})=0, and the spacetime is singular, which also corresponds to divergent gauge and dilaton fields. Since physical spacetime terminates atr0r_{0}, the inner horizonr<r0r<r_{0}is physically non-existent. The metric functionΔ​(r)\Delta(r)possesses only one single physical root located at a larger radiusr>r0r>r_{0}, which defines the event horizon of the black hole. Because the curvature singularity is located exactly where the inner root ofΔ​(r)\Delta(r)would be, the inner and outer horizons can never be brought into coincidence. Therefore, the dyonic dilaton AdS black hole cannot possess an extremal counterpart.

We first presented explicit mass/energy formulas for the extended black hole thermodynamics. By leveraging the holographic dictionary in an extended thermodynamic framework where Newton’s gravitational constantGGand AdS lengthllare treated as a dynamical variable for the dyonic dilaton AdS black hole, we mapped the bulk parameters to their dual boundary CFT variables. This allowed us to explore the thermodynamic phase space featuring sixteen distinct ensembles defined under conditions of either fixed boundary volumeV~\tilde{V}or fixed boundary pressure𝒫~\tilde{\mathcal{P}}. However, due to the symmetry between the electric chargeQ~\tilde{Q}and magnetic chargeP~\tilde{P}, as well as their potentialsΦ~\tilde{\Phi}andΨ~\tilde{\Psi}, several ensembles share qualitatively identical behaviors. This symmetry narrows the parameter space down to ten distinct thermodynamic classes split evenly between fixed boundary volumeV~\tilde{V}and fixed boundary pressure𝒫~\tilde{\mathcal{P}}frameworks. The detailed results and phase properties extracted from every single one of these 16 ensembles are summarized below.

## 6.1Fixed Spatial VolumeV~\tilde{V}Ensembles

The fixed(Q~,P~,V~,C)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{V},C)ensemble with Helmholtz free energyF1F_{1}(4.1) serves as the baseline for fixed-boundary volume systems. The temperature behavior mirrors the standard U-shaped Schwarzschild-AdS black hole’s curve which behaves like(T~∼1/S~+S~)(\tilde{T}\sim 1/\sqrt{\tilde{S}}+\sqrt{\tilde{S}}). On the other hand, the temperature increases for small and large entropy. It features a sharp bifurcation point (cusp) at a minimum temperatureTminT_{\text{min}}that separates an unstable small black hole branch from a stable large black hole branch. However, since the electromagnetic charges are held fixed, there is not a Hawking-Page phase transition.

Then we considered fixed(Q~,P~,V~,μ)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{V},\mu)ensemble with free energyF2F_{2}(4.2) by holding the chemical potential fixed instead of the central charge. The system yields positive free energy curves, which strictly forbid the emergence of first-order phase transitions. However whenμ>0\mu>0, the temperature curve is double-valued and exhibits a minimum threshold leading to a zeroth-order phase transition from a low-entropy state to a high-entropy state.

The ensemble with fixed(Φ~,P~,V~,C)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{P},\tilde{V},C)is linked symmetrically by electromagnetic duality with the ensemble with fixed(Q~,Ψ~,V~,C)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{V},C). Both ensembles correspond with the free energyF3F_{3}(4.3) andF7F_{7}(4.7), respectively. These ensembles allow the CFT to freely discharge its electric or magnetic sectors because its corresponding gauge potential is fixed. Despite this freedom, the system is prevented from completely transitioning into a pure thermal AdS space because one of the electromagnetic charge parameters remains conserved and fixed. A small parameter space window ofΦ~\tilde{\Phi}(orΨ\Psi) exists whereTminT_{\text{min}}occurs at negative values of entropy.

The ensemble with fixed(Φ~,P~,V~,μ)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{P},\tilde{V},\mu)which is symmetric to the ensemble with fixed(Q~,Ψ~,V~,μ)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{V},\mu). These ensembles keep both gauge and chemical potentials fixed which correspond with the free energyF4F_{4}(4.4)andF6F_{6}(4.6), respectively. Whenμ>0\mu>0, the magnitude of the electric potentialΦ~\tilde{\Phi}(or magnetic potentialΨ~\tilde{\Psi}) is bounded. The system exhibits a distinct zeroth-order phase transition where a high-entropy state terminates at a critical temperatureT1T_{1}, jumping directly into a low-entropy state as heating occurs toT2T_{2}. Forμ<0\mu<0, this zeroth-order phase transition vanishes unless the gauge potential exceeds a specific threshold.

The fixed(Φ~,Ψ~,V~,C)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{V},C)ensemble withF8F_{8}holds both the electric and magnetic potentials fixed alongside the central charge. The phase behavior depends on a critical combined potentialΥ~=Φ~2+Ψ~2\tilde{\Upsilon}=\sqrt{\tilde{\Phi}^{2}+\tilde{\Psi}^{2}}. At the exact critical valueΥ~c=2​π/V~\tilde{\Upsilon}_{c}=\sqrt{2\pi/\tilde{V}}, the system has a single-valued free energy and completely stable. AboveΥ~c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{c}, a zeroth-order phase transition emerges with a finite free energy jump atTminT_{\text{min}}from thermal AdS space to high-entropy state. BelowΥ~c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{c}, it produces a first-order (de)confinement phase transition where the system changes sign atF8=0F_{8}=0, successfully mapping the dual representation of a bulk Hawking-Page transition between large AdS black hole and AdS spacetime with thermal radiation.

The last ensemble in fixed spatial volume groups is the fixed(Φ~,Ψ~,V~,μ)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{V},\mu)ensemble which corrresponds withF5F_{5}(4.5). Because all intensive conjugate variables are held constant simultaneously, the underlying Euler scaling laws dictate that the thermodynamic potential identically vanishesF5=0F_{5}=0. Consequently, this configuration represents a trivial unconstrained boundary system that does not warrant thermodynamic stability analysis.

## 6.2Fixed Pressure𝒫~\tilde{\mathcal{P}}Ensembles

The first ensemble in the fixed pressure ensemble group is the ensemble with fixed(Q~,P~,𝒫~,μ)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\mu)which corresponds with the free energyF10F_{10}(5.2). This ensemble is globally unstable but it features significantμ−\mu-dependent characteristics. Whenμ<0\mu<0, the free energy curve develops a characteristic inverted swallowtail architecture bounded by two unstable phases and a stable phase(𝒞>0)(\mathcal{C}>0). At lower temperatures, a zeroth-order phase transition occurs from the cold end of the inverted swallowtail to the stable branch. Forμ=0\mu=0, the system contains only two thermodynamic phases—an unstable high-entropy phase𝒞<0\mathcal{C}<0and a locally stable low-entropy phase𝒞>0\mathcal{C}>0meeting at a maximum temperature. Forμ>0\mu>0, the phase structure features both local minimum and maximum temperatures, generating double-valued triangle-like loops for𝒫~>𝒫~c\tilde{\mathcal{P}}>\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{c}where for the given parameters, the critical pressure is𝒫~c=0.426\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{c}=0.426.
When𝒫~>𝒫~c\tilde{\mathcal{P}}>\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{c}, it has been observed that the zeroth- and first-order phase transition occurs simultaneously. At the critical pressure, the inverted swallowtail disappears and second-order phase transition emerges. There is no phase transition occurs below the critical pressure.

For the ensemble with fixed(Φ~,P~,𝒫~,μ)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\mu)which is symmetric to the ensemble with fixed(Q~,Ψ~,𝒫~,μ)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\mu), they are governed by free energyF12F_{12}andF14F_{14}, respectively. These ensemble exhibit behavior highly analogous to the fixed(Q~,P~,𝒫~,μ)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\mu)ensemble. The main distinctions are that forμ<0\mu<0, the inverted swallowtail is present only above a distinct critical pressure𝒫~c\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{c}denoting the presence of zeroth-order and first-order phase transitions while at the critical pressure, there occurs only second-order phase transition. Forμ=0\mu=0, the unstable phase separates two distinct stable phases and the zeroth-order phase transition occurs from unstable phase to the stable phase. Forμ>0\mu>0, above the critical potentialΦ~c\tilde{\Phi}_{c}(orΨ~c\tilde{\Psi}_{c}) dictates the appearance of the inverted swallowtail denoting the presence of similar phase transitions asμ>0\mu>0in the previous ensemble.

In the ensemble with fixed(Φ~,Ψ~,𝒫~,μ)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\mu), we observed the absence of inverted swallowtails portrayed by free energyF13=𝒫~​V~F_{13}=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}\tilde{V}. A single cusp characterizes the system whenμ≠0\mu\neq 0, and no cusps appear whenμ=0\mu=0. Stable configurations (𝒞>0\mathcal{C}>0) exist strictly at low temperatures. There occurs only a zeroth-phase order transition forμ>0\mu>0.

The remaining ensembles are(Q~,P~,𝒫~,C)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},C),(Φ~,P~,𝒫~,C)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},C),(Φ~,Ψ~,𝒫~,C)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},C)which correspond to free energiesF9,F11F_{9},F_{11}, andF16F_{16}, respectively. The ensemble with(Φ~,P~,𝒫~,C)(\tilde{\Phi},\tilde{P},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},C)is symmetric to the ensemble with fixed(Q~,Ψ~,𝒫~,C)(\tilde{Q},\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{\mathcal{P}},C). All these ensembles do not exhibit any interesting phase transition. The ensembles which correspond toF9F_{9}andF16F_{16}are unstable observed from the specific heat plots while the ensembleF11F_{11}(orF15F_{15}) is stable only in small temperature regime.

Generally, comparing the results from this dyonic dilaton AdS black hole to the charged AdS black hole in Einstein-Maxwell system[51], we observe that the phase transition that occurred in each ensemble (for fixed spatial volume only; fixed pressure ensembles have not been studied) are quite different. For instance, in the ensemble with Helmholtz free energy, we do not observe any phase transition whereas for a charged AdS black hole, first- and second-order phase transitions occur. Evidently the presence of dilaton and axion fields is the main factor for the interesting behaviors we observe. One cannot obtain the charged AdS black holes from dyonic dilaton AdS black hole because of the distinct interdependence of the electromagnetic, dilaton, and axion charges.

It is worth noting that in the charged AdS black hole, the fixed pressure ensembles do not exhibit any critical behavior[51]. In contrast, our results show that the fixed pressure ensembles possess richer structures and exhibit distinct phase transitions, especially due to the dependence on the chemical potentialμ\mu. Furthermore, for the rotating charged AdS black hole some interesting phase transition behaviour has been observed[52]. For future endeavors, we would like to consider the rotating version of the dyonic dilaton AdS black hole to see the role of angular momentum in the phase space of holographic thermodynamics.
