A fundamental theme in arithmetic combinatorics is the interplay between addition and multiplication. Classical manifestations include Ostmann’s inverse Goldbach problem[6](which predicts that the set of primes cannot be written, up to finitely many exceptions, as a nontrivial sumset) and Erdős’s conjecture on additive decompositions of small perturbations of the set of perfect squares[10]. Both conjectures remain open.

In this paper, we study a finite-field analogue of this theme.
A celebrated conjecture of Sárközy[8]asserted that, for every sufficiently large primepp, the subgroupℛp\mathcal{R}_{p}of nonzero quadratic residues in𝔽p{\mathbb{F}}_{p}has no nontrivial additive decomposition, that is,ℛp\mathcal{R}_{p}cannot be written as a sumsetA+B={a+b:a∈A,b∈B}A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}

with|A|,|B|≥2|A|,|B|\geq 2.
The problem has seen remarkable progress in recent years.
Hanson and Petridis[1]established the conjecture for almost all primes using a Stepanov-type polynomial method, and Kalmynin[2]subsequently resolved Sárközy’s conjecture.
Related additive and multiplicative decomposition problems have also been studied extensively; see, for example,[8,14,9,11,12,5,13,1,3,15,2,4,16,18].

In particular, our approach avoids Kalmynin’s fractional-linear transformations and residue calculations, as well as the reciprocal-transfer identities used by Rudnev and Tyrrell.
