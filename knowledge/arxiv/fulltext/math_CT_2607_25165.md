## Abstract.

We show that several categories of hyperstructures, including the mosaics of Nakamura and Reyes and the hypergroups of Marty, are realizable as categories of objects with extra structure within the category of complete atomic Boolean algebras (CABAs).

## 1.Introduction

The fundamental objects of study in any elementary algebra course, such as monoids, groups, rings, and modules, are expressible as objects endowed with extra structure in an appropriate base category—for example, an abelian group is a commutative monoid with inverses in the category of sets, and a ring is a monoid object in the category of abelian groups. Since hypersemigroups and hypermonoids—analogues of semigroups and monoids in which a binary operationX×X→XX\times X\to Xon a setXXis replaced with a functionX×X→𝒫​(X)X\times X\to\mathcal{P}(X)into the power set ofXX—are algebraic structures, it seems natural to wonder whether one can view them as “objects with extra structure” in an appropriate category.

In[3, N], it is shown that we can view hypersemigroups and hypermonoids in this way, in the sense that there is a bijective correspondence between, for example, hypersemigroup structures on a setXXand semigroup structures on𝒫​(X)\mathcal{P}(X), viewed as an object of the category of suplattices.

Below, we prove analogous results for the mosaics of[7]and the hypergroups of[6], and extend these bijections to equivalences of categories. In particular, letting𝖬𝗌𝖼\mathsf{Msc}denote the category of mosaics, and letting𝒪𝖬𝗌𝖼​(𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠)N\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{Msc}}(\mathsf{CABA})_{N}denote the category of unital residuated complete atomic Boolean algebras (CABAs) with non-singular morphisms between them, we prove several results of the following type:

## Theorem 1.1.

There is an equivalence of categories𝖬𝗌𝖼≅𝒪𝖬𝗌𝖼​(𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠)N\mathsf{Msc}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{Msc}}(\mathsf{CABA})_{N}, and a pseudomonic embedding𝒪𝖬𝗌𝖼​(𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠)N↪𝒪𝖬𝗌𝖼​(𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠).\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{Msc}}(\mathsf{CABA})_{N}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{Msc}}(\mathsf{CABA}).

By asuplattice, we mean a partially ordered set admitting arbitrary joins, including the nullary join⊥\bot. Note that any such lattice is in fact complete, i.e., it also admits arbitrary meets. By amorphism of suplattices, we mean a join-preserving, but not necessarily meet-preserving, monotone set function from the underlying set of one lattice to the underlying set of another. We denote the category of suplattices and suplattice morphisms by𝖲𝗎𝗉𝖫𝖺𝗍\mathsf{SupLat}.

By anatomof a suplatticeLL, we mean an elementx∈Lx\in Lsuch that⊥≠x\bot\neq x, and such that the downsetx↓x^{\downarrow}ofxxis{x,⊥}\{x,\bot\}. ABoolean algebrais a complemented distributive lattice: A lattice(L,∧,∨,⊥)(L,\land,\lor,\bot)equipped with a unary operation(−)c(-)^{c}, calledcomplementation, such thatxc∧x=⊥x^{c}\land x=\botandxc∨x=Lx^{c}\lor x=Lfor anyx∈Lx\in L, and such that finitary∧\landand∨\lordistribute over one another. ## Definition 2.2.
