## 1.Introduction

We study some invariants of symplectic manifolds under the general namesymplectic cohomology. Roughly speaking, for a suitable symplectic manifold(M,ω)(M,\omega)and a subsetKKofMM, there exists a ringS​HM∗​(K,ω)SH^{*}_{M}(K,\omega). The first such definition is given by Floer-Hofer[9], whereM=ℂnM=\mathbb{C}^{n}andKKis a bounded open subset. Over the past thirty years, it has been developed intensively and played a central role in the research of symplectic topology, Hamiltonian dynamics, and mirror symmetry[28,23,22,5]. Here we are interested in how it is sensitive to the symplectic structureω\omega.

From now on, letKKbe a compact subset of a closed symplectic manifold(M,ω)(M,\omega). An acceleration datum𝒟\mathcal{D}forKKis a collection of suitable Hamiltonian functions and almost complex structures onMM. It produces a telescopetel∗​(𝒟)\mathrm{tel}^{*}(\mathcal{D}), which is a chain complex whose generators are Hamiltonian orbits, and the differentials are given by counting solutions to the Floer equations. The Hamiltonian action functional gives an action filtration ontel∗​(𝒟)\mathrm{tel}^{*}(\mathcal{D}). For any real numbersa<ba<b, we writetel∗​(𝒟)[a,b)\mathrm{tel}^{*}(\mathcal{D})_{[a,b)}as the sub-quotient complex whose generators have actions in[a,b)[a,b). These telescopes form a direct system inaaand an inverse system inbb, asa→−∞a\to-\inftyandb→+∞b\to+\infty. The symplectic cohomology with support onKKdefined by Varolgunes[27,4]can be computed in the following wayS​HM∗​(K,ω)=H​(lim←b⁡lim→a⁡tel∗​(𝒟)[a,b))=H​(lim←b⁡tel∗​(𝒟)(−∞,b)).\displaystyle SH^{*}_{M}(K,\omega)=H(\varprojlim_{b}\varinjlim_{a}\mathrm{tel}^{*}(\mathcal{D})_{[a,b)})=H(\varprojlim_{b}\mathrm{tel}^{*}(\mathcal{D})_{(-\infty,b)}).

The resulting homology groups are independent of the choice of acceleration data𝒟\mathcal{D}. It is an interesting question how they depend onω\omega. In this article, we focus on the following situation.

Let𝒥​(M)\mathcal{J}(M)be the space of all almost complex structures on a smooth manifoldMM. Given a symplectic formω\omegaonMM, we write𝒥τ​(M,ω)\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(M,\omega)as the space of almost complex structures tamed byω\omega, that is,𝒥τ(M,ω):={J∈𝒥(M)∣ω(⋅,J⋅)>0}.\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(M,\omega):=\{J\in\mathcal{J}(M)\mid\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)>0\}.

## Definition 1.1.

Letω,ω′\omega,\omega^{\prime}be two symplectic forms onMM. They are called adjacent if𝒥τ​(M,ω)∩𝒥τ​(M,ω′)≠∅.\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(M,\omega)\cap\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(M,\omega^{\prime})\neq\emptyset.

Since the tameness is an open condition, we have that𝒥τ​(M,ω)\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(M,\omega)is an open subspace of𝒥​(M)\mathcal{J}(M). Therefore, ifω,ω′\omega,\omega^{\prime}are sufficiently close, then they are adjacent. In particular, if we have a smooth family of symplectic forms{ωt}t∈[0,1]\{\omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}, then we can connectω0,ω1\omega_{0},\omega_{1}by a finite sequence of adjacent symplectic forms.

## Definition 1.2.

LetUUbe an open subset ofMM. Two symplectic formsω,ω′\omega,\omega^{\prime}onMMare calledUU-comparable ifω=ω′\omega=\omega^{\prime}onUU, and for any sequence of mapsuk:(D2,∂D2)→(M,U)u_{k}:(D^{2},\partial D^{2})\to(M,U), we have∫uk∗​ω→+∞\int u^{*}_{k}\omega\to+\inftyif and only if∫uk∗​ω′→+∞\int u^{*}_{k}\omega^{\prime}\to+\infty.

Our main result is the following.

## Theorem 1.3.

LetKKbe a compact subset of a closed manifoldMM. Suppose thatω\omegaandω′\omega^{\prime}are two adjacent aspherical symplectic forms onMMwhich areUU-comparable for some neighborhoodUUofKK. Then there are group isomorphismsS​HM∗​(K,ω)≅S​HM∗​(K,ω′).\displaystyle SH^{*}_{M}(K,\omega)\cong SH^{*}_{M}(K,\omega^{\prime}).

Recall that a symplectic form is aspherical if it vanishes onπ2​(M)\pi_{2}(M). Without this condition, the above statement is not true.

Let(S2,ω)(S^{2},\omega)be the two sphere with an area form. LetKKbe a disk inS2S^{2}with more than half of the total area. ThenS​HS2∗​(K,ω)≠0SH^{*}_{S^{2}}(K,\omega)\neq 0. When the integral ofρ\rhois large enough, the setKKbecomes displaceable in(S2,ω′)(S^{2},\omega^{\prime}), which implies thatS​HS2∗​(K,ω′)=0SH^{*}_{S^{2}}(K,\omega^{\prime})=0.

TheUU-comparable condition is satisfied under certain topological assumptions.

## Example 1.5.

Letω\omegaandω′\omega^{\prime}be two aspherical symplectic forms onMMwithω=ω′\omega=\omega^{\prime}onUU.
- •

IfUUis incompressible inMM, meaning that the inclusion-induced mapπ1​(U)→π1​(M)\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(M)is injective, thenω\omegaandω′\omega^{\prime}areUU-comparable.
- •

In the particular case whereMMis a Riemann surface with positive genus, one can verify the above conditions by using surface topology. This matches the known results that symplectic cohomology with support on a positive genus surface is topological; see[25, Theorem 1.31]and[6, Theorem 1.48]. We also remark that theUU-comparable condition is on someUU, not onKK.

## Remark 1.6.

We state Theorem1.3for closed manifolds, while the proof works for open manifolds with bothω,ω′\omega,\omega^{\prime}being convex at infinity andω−ω′\omega-\omega^{\prime}having compact support. For open manifolds, certain (non-)invariance results are known; for example, see[12,11,21,3].

## 1.1.Outlook

Now we provide more motivation for Theorem1.3.

## 1.1.1.Heaviness

In[7], Entov-Polterovich introduced the notion of heaviness, which reveals a remarkable hierarchy of rigidity among compact subsets of symplectic manifolds. It is known that symplectic cohomology with support determines the heaviness of a compact set[19,16]. Therefore, we expect the following.

## Corollary 1.7.

Under the setup of Theorem1.3, we have thatKKisω\omega-heavy if and only if it isω′\omega^{\prime}-heavy.

## Remark 1.8.

Strictly speaking, our Floer theory uses the Hamiltonian action filtration while[1,16]uses a Novikov filtration. The above corollary depends on a modification of[1]to the action filtration case, which should be much easier under our aspherical condition.

The theory of heavy sets has many geometric and dynamical applications; see[8]for a nice survey. In view of Corollary1.7, these consequences are preserved under a suitable change of symplectic forms. For example,[15, Proposition 6]gives a topological description of heavy sets in positive genus surfaces. Furthermore, we are interested in how the Hofer and spectral geometry of Hamiltonian groups change when we change the symplectic form. This will be pursued elsewhere.

## Remark 1.9.

In[30], Zhang studied how Hamiltonian spectral invariants change under the change of symplectic structures. It would be interesting to use the parametrized Floer continuation maps therein to deduce semi-continuity of symplectic cohomology with support on non-aspherical manifolds.

## 1.1.2.Relative Floer theory

Let(M,ω)(M,\omega)be a closed symplectic manifold andDDbe a suitable symplectic divisor that supports the Poincaré dual of[ω][\omega]. The complement ofDDinMMcan be made into a Liouville domainWW. The symplectic topology and Floer theories betweenWWandMMprovide an effective method[22]to study the homological mirror symmetry conjecture. In particular, Perutz-Sheridan[20]defined a relative Fukaya categoryℱ​(W⊂M)\mathcal{F}(W\subset M). If one varies the symplectic form outsideWW, the category has several nice invariance properties. One of our motivations is to realize similar properties for symplectic cohomology with support.

In this article, we work on the aspherical case and consider Floer theory over the integers. It should be fruitful to study symplectic cohomology with support over (several-variable) Novikov rings and establish a change of variable formula under the perturbation of symplectic structures; see[18,20]. Specializing to a particular symplectic form gives a map from the several-variable Novikov ring to the universal Novikov ring. This should be related to the semi-continuity phenomenon in Remark1.9.

## 1.1.3.Sketch of proof

In order to compareS​HM∗​(K,ω)SH^{*}_{M}(K,\omega)withS​HM∗​(K,ω′)SH^{*}_{M}(K,\omega^{\prime}), we will use a fixed acceleration datum𝒟=(Hs,t,Js,t)\mathcal{D}=(H_{s,t},J_{s,t})for bothω\omegaandω′\omega^{\prime}. The adjacent condition implies that such a family of almost complex structuresJs,tJ_{s,t}exists. However, the Hamiltonian vector fields forHs,tH_{s,t}depend on the symplectic structure. To solve this, we assume that the functions are constant onM∖UM\setminus U. Then there is a canonical correspondence between the Hamiltonian vector fields forHs,tH_{s,t}with respect toω\omegaandω′\omega^{\prime}. The main difficulty in using such functions is that they have many degenerate constant orbits. We will use action cut-offs to ignore these degenerate orbits, motivated by[18,5,24]. This gives a correspondence between generators of action-truncated telescopes.

Next, by the special choice of Hamiltonian functions, the Floer equations are the same with respect to bothω\omegaandω′\omega^{\prime}. In particular, the part of a Floer solution outsideUUbecomes pseudo-holomorphic; see Figure1. This provides a correspondence between moduli spaces of Floer solutions to define differentials in the telescopes.

Therefore, we have obtained two action-truncated telescopes containing a common group of generators with the same differentials, but with different action filtrations. Finally, theUU-comparable condition is used to compare these two filtrations and to show this common group of generators captures the whole homology, after taking inverse limits. This produces the unfiltered isomorphisms in Theorem1.3.

A useful condition for the vanishing ofcoker⁡(f)=lim←1\operatorname{coker}(f)=\varprojlim\nolimits^{1}term is the Mittag-Leffler condition[29, Definition 3.5.6]. For example, an inverse system with surjective structural maps satisfies the Mittag-Leffler condition.
