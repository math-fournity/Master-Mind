## 1.Introduction

The classical Postnikov tower decomposes a spectrum into layers controlled by its homotopy groups.
By Bott-periodicity,
the spectrum representing topological real K-theoryKO\mathrm{KO}is eight-periodic with homotopy groups given by𝐙\mathbf{Z},𝐙/2\mathbf{Z}/2,𝐙/2\mathbf{Z}/2,0,𝐙\mathbf{Z},0,0, and0.
In motivic homotopy theory, the presence of two possible candidates for a circle, theTatecircle𝐆m\mathbf{G}_{m}and the simplicial circleS1S^{1}, gives rise to at least two distinct Postnikov-type filtrations called the (effective) slice filtration[34](𝐆m\mathbf{G}_{m}-Postnikov type filtration) and the generalized (or very effective) slice filtration[32](a𝐏1\mathbf{P}^{1}-Postnikov type filtration).
We are interested in studying the motivic analogue ofKO\mathrm{KO}; this motivic spectrum is called the Hermitian111The underlying theory is called Grothendieck-Witt theory.K-theory motivic spectrum𝐊𝐐\mathbf{K}\mathbf{Q}.
It was first constructed in[13]over fields of characteristic not equal to22, and in[4]over any base scheme without assuming that22is invertible.
Motivic Bott-periodicity holds for𝐊𝐐\mathbf{K}\mathbf{Q}[4, Prop. 1.0.4], which is likewise four-periodic222This four-periodicity should be interpreted as an eight-periodicity in topology, since as we previously mentioned𝐏1\mathbf{P}^{1}is an analogue ofS2S^{2}, i.e.,𝐊𝐐≃𝐊𝐐⊗(𝐏1)⊗4\mathbf{K}\mathbf{Q}\simeq\mathbf{K}\mathbf{Q}\otimes(\mathbf{P}^{1})^{\otimes 4}.

Better behavior is expressed by the generalized slices, as proved by Bachmann[2]over a field of characteristic not equal to22, and by Kolderup-Röndigs-Østavær[19]over essentially smooth schemes over a Dedekind scheme.
The generalized effective slices of𝐊𝐐\mathbf{K}\mathbf{Q}are four-periodic up to twists.
