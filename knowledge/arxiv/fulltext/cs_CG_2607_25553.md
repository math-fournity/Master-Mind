## Abstract

Motivated by this observation, in this work, we provide bounds on the slope number of biconnected $1$-planar graphs when two bends are allowed along each edge. ## 1Introduction

The problem of computing drawings of graphs in which the edges use only a limited number of slopes has received considerable attention in Graph Drawing, due to both its theoretical significance and practical relevance in areas such as network visualization and VLSI design[BattistaETT99,Juenger04,2013gd].
Given a graph, algorithmically one seeks to minimize (or to compute bounds on) itsslope number, that is, the number of distinct slopes used by its edge segments, taken over all polyline drawings of the graph with a prescribed number of bends per edge. Among the first to investigate this problem were Wade and Chu[WadeChu1994], who proved that the slope number ofKnK_{n}in the straight-line setting isnn. Since then, the problem has been extensively investigated with several results known for graphs of bounded degree[DujmovicSW07,KeszeghPPT06,KeszeghPPT08,MukkamalaP11,MukkamalaSzegedy2009,PachP06].

For planar input graphs, one usually seeks to find corresponding crossing-free drawings.
Several results have established tight or near-tight bounds on the planar slope number, depending on the maximum degree of the graph and on the number of bends per edge allowed[BekosGDLM22,KlawitterM22,KlawitterZ21,KlawitterZ23,ChaplickLGLM21,ChaplickLGLM24,DiGiacomoLM2014,GiacomoLM18,GiacomoLM16,GiacomoLM20,DujmovicESW07,JelinekJKLTV09,JelinekJKLTV13,KPP2010GD,KPP2013,KnauerMW12,KnauerMW14,LenhartLMN23,BrucknerKM19,BrucknerKM22,KnauerW16]. Additionally, trade-offs between the number of slopes, the number of bends, and drawing area have been proposed, yielding efficient algorithms to produce corresponding drawings[BekosKKP26,BiedlKant1998,BekosGKK14,BekosGKK15,Tamassia1987]. By now, the required number of slopes in combination with other parameters in the planar setting is fairly well understood.

In the non-planar setting, Pach and Pálvölgyi[PachP06]showed that graphs of maximum degree55can have arbitrarily large slope number, while if one bend per edge is allowed
Dujmović, Suderman, and Wood[DujmovicSW07]proved that every graph of maximum degreeΔ\Deltaadmits a drawing usingΔ+1\Delta+1slopes. However, if the input graph belongs to a particular graph class, then the output drawing does not necessarily respect the defining properties of the class. A prominent example in this regard is the class of 1-planar graphs, which have a drawing where each edge is crossed at most once.
Introduced by Ringel[Ringel1965], 1-planar graphs
have been the subject of extensive research[DidimoLM19,KobourovLM17]. However, their slope number has been studied to a limited extent. To the best of our knowledge, the only non-trivial bounds on their slope numbers concern subclasses. Kindermann, Montecchiani, Schlipf, and Schulz[KindermannMSS18,KindermannMSS21]showed that triconnected cubic 1-planar graphs admit 1-planar drawings with one bend per edge using four slopes. Di Giacomo, Liotta, and Montecchiani[GiacomoLM14,GiacomoLM15]showed that outer 1-planar graphs of degreeΔ\Deltaadmit outer 1-planar drawings with one bend per edge usingO​(Δ)O(\Delta)slopes. Argyriou, Cornelsen, Förster, Kaufmann, Nöllenburg, Okamoto, Raftopoulou, and Wolff[ArgyriouCF0NORW18]presented an algorithm to draw 1-plane graphs with maximum degree four orthogonally (i.e., with two slopes) and with at most three bends per edge.
Most existing results, however, in non-planar settings focus either on dense graphs, such as complete and complete bipartite graphs[WadeChu1994,DujmovicSW07], or on graphs of small degree[KeszeghPPT06,KeszeghPPT08,MukkamalaP11]. As a consequence, sparse yet structured non-planar graph families remain largely unexplored.

This lack of results highlights a gap in the literature:while the slope number problem is well studied in the planar case, its extension to beyond-planar graph classes is still in its infancy. Understanding how different crossing restrictions affect the number of slopes, the required number of bends per edge, and the drawing area is a challenging and largely unexplored research line, which we seek to investigate. To this end, we leverage a central technique for computing drawings with few slopes and few bends per edge that was introduced in[AngeliniBLM19]by extending previous techniques for graphs of low degree[BiedlKant1998,BekosGKK14,BekosGKK15]. Initially, it was applied to planar graphs of degreeΔ\Delta[AngeliniBLM19], but later it was extended to produce corresponding drawings for directed planar graphs[BekosGDLM18], for triconnected cubic11-planar graphs[KindermannMSS18,KindermannMSS21], and recently to study the interplay between thekk-bend planar slope number and the area requirements[BekosKKP26].

## Our contribution.

We extend the aforementioned technique and adapt it to the22-bend setting for11-planar graphs of arbitrary degree.
InSection˜3, we consider33-connected11-planar graphs.
Given such a graph of maximum degreeΔ\Deltaand a 1-planar embedding of it, we construct a22-bend11-planar drawing using an arbitrary (prescribed) set ofΔ\Deltaslopes, not necessarily maintaining the given embedding.
The drawing is obtained via an incremental algorithm based on a canonical ordering[Kant96]of a planarization of the input11-planar graph.
InSection˜4, the result is extended to biconnected11-planar graphs by exploiting SPQR-tree decompositions[BattistaT90]. Our main result is as follows.

The22-bend slope number of22-connected11-planar graphs with degreeΔ\Deltais at mostΔ\Delta.

## 5Conclusions

In this work, we studied the22-bend slope number of11-planar graphs, thereby providing the first investigation of the problem for a beyond-planar graph class of
arbitrary degree.
We showed that every biconnected11-planar graph of maximum degreeΔ\Deltaadmits a22-bend drawing using any given set ofΔ\Deltaslopes.
We strongly believe that our result extends to simply connected11-planar graphs using a similar approach as the one in[AngeliniBLM19]by ensuring that the biconnected components are drawn with enough consecutive ports available at the cut vertices.
We also believe that our algorithm can be extended to drawkk-planar graphs withΔ\Deltaslopes and2​k2kbends per edge, fork>1k>1.

Several directions, however, remain unexplored.
A key open problem is whether every11-planar graph admits a11-bend drawing where the slope number is bounded by a function ofΔ\Delta.
Another important question concerns the area requirement. Although our algorithm uses a bounded number of slopes and at most two bends per edge, it is unclear whether the output drawings fit on polynomial-area grids.
A different direction is to study trade-offs between the number of slopes and the number of bends per edge, in particular, whether similar results can be obtained with fewer bends (even for subclasses of11-planar graphs).
Finally, as a first step towards other beyond-planar graph classes, optimal 2-planar[DBLP:conf/compgeom/Bekos0R17]and framed graphs[DBLP:conf/compgeom/BekosLGGMR20]appear to be particularly promising.
