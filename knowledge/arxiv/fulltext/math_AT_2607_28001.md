## Abstract.

We compute the homotopy type of the classifying space for commutativity introduced by Adem, F. Cohen and Torres-Giese for the groupSL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]), both as a
discrete topological group and with the subspace topology fromSL2⁡(ℝ)\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}). Doing so requires compiling detailed information on the maximal abelian subgroups ofSL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]), which turn out to be precisely the centralizers of the non-central elements of the group, for which we employ methods from geometric group theory.

## 1.Introduction

LetGGbe a topological group. For everyn≥0n\geq 0, consider the subspace ofGnG^{n}given byHom⁡(ℤn,G)≅{(g1,…,gn)∈Gn∣gi​gj=gj​gi​for all​i,j}.\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)\cong\{(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in G^{n}\mid g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}\text{ for all }i,j\}.

These spaces assemble into a simplicial subspace of the usual bar construction forB​GBG: the face maps multiply adjacent entries or delete the first or last entry, and the degeneracy maps insert the identity element. The geometric realization of this simplicial space is called theclassifying space for commutativity[ACT12]ofGGand is denoted byB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}G.

The inclusion of commuting tuples into all tuples induces a a natural map fromB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}GtoB​GBG. The homotopy type of the spaceE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}Gcan be described as the homotopy fiber of this map, but there is also a more precise definition ofE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}Gup to homeomorphism, which we will recall in Section2. There is a notion oftransitionally commutative principalGG-bundleclassified byB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}G, andE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}Gis the total space of the universal such bundle.

So far most of the work on the classifying space for commutativity and the corresponding total space of the universal bundle has been carried out for Lie groups and discrete groups, and indeed most often the group is even assumed to be a compact and connected Lie group. Our goal in this paper is to test the waters outside of those extremely well-behaved topological groups and so we compute the homotopy type ofB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}GandE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}Gfor the groupG=SL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]), which is topologically less well-behaved, being a dense subgroup ofSL2⁡(ℝ)\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

More precisely, ifGδG^{\delta}denotesGGequipped with the discrete topology, we first computeB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδB_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}andE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδE_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}up to homotopy equivalence, and then discuss what one obtains forB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}GandE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}GwhereGGcarries its usual topology. We argue that the definition ofB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}Gshould probably be modified to use the fat geometric realization for groups with not-so-nice topologies, and show that for totally disconnected groups likeSL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])the modified definition will produce spaces that are weakly equivalent toB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδB_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}andE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδE_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}. We show that forG=SL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])the fat version ofB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}Gis merely weakly equivalent toB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδB_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}, not also homotopy equivalent to it; and we speculate on what would be obtained from the unmodified definition using geometric realization.

We now outline the contents of the paper. As mentioned above, Section2has some background information on classifying spaces for commutativity and on total spaces of the corresponding universal bundles. In particular it also cites the main results about them that we will use in our calculation.

Computing the classifying space for commutativity of a group requires detailed knowledge of its abelian subgroups and how they intersect. We provide the first ingredient for this in Section3, where we show that for an integral domainRR, the centralizers of non-central elements inSL2⁡(R)\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)are abelian. Consequently, the maximal abelian subgroups ofSL2⁡(R)\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)are precisely the centralizers of non-central elements, and any two distinct maximal abelian subgroups intersect in the center. Using this information we prove that for any infinite integral domainRR, we have:E𝖼𝗈𝗆SL2(R)δ≃⋁ℕS1.E_{\mathsf{com}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)^{\delta}\simeq\bigvee_{\mathbb{N}}S^{1}.

and similarly, thatB𝖼𝗈𝗆SL2(R)δB_{\mathsf{com}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)^{\delta}is aK​(ΓR,1)K(\Gamma_{R},1)for a groupΓR\Gamma_{R}depending on the ringRR, specifically,ΓR\Gamma_{R}is the amalgamated product of all centralizers of non-central elements, the product being amalgamated over the center of the group.

Of course, that general description still leaves open the question of what these centralizers are exactly and the answer does depend on the specific ringRR. We carry out the classification of the centralizers in the case ofR=ℤ​[1/p]R=\mathbb{Z}[1/p]in Section5. These centralizers are organized according to the type of the element: unipotent, split overℚ\mathbb{Q}, real irreducible, or imaginary irreducible. In the irreducible cases the rank depends on the splitting behavior ofppin the associated quadratic field generated by an eigenvalue. These types are described in Section4, prior to the aforementioned centralizer computation, and the consequences forE𝖼𝗈𝗆E_{\mathsf{com}}andB𝖼𝗈𝗆B_{\mathsf{com}}are collected in the short Section6. Namely, as already stated,E𝖼𝗈𝗆SL2(ℤ[1/p])δE_{\mathsf{com}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])^{\delta}(theδindicates we use the discrete topology) has the homotopy type of a countably infinite wedge of circles, andB𝖼𝗈𝗆SL2(ℤ[1/p])δB_{\mathsf{com}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])^{\delta}is aK​(Γp,1)K(\Gamma_{p},1), where the groupΓp\Gamma_{p}is is the amalgamated product of countably many copies of each of the following groups, amalgated over the obviousC2C_{2}subgroup coming form the first factors:C2×ℤ​[1/p]C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}[1/p],C2×ℤC_{2}\times\mathbb{Z},C2×ℤ2C_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2},C2C_{2},C4C_{4},C6C_{6},C4×ℤC_{4}\times\mathbb{Z},C6×ℤC_{6}\times\mathbb{Z}.

## 2.Classifying spaces for commutativity

As we said in the introduction, the classifying space for commutativity in a topological group is defined as the geometric realization of the simplicial space whosenn-simplices are thenn-tuples of pairwise commuting elements ofGG, that is,B𝖼𝗈𝗆G=|[n]↦Hom(ℤn,G)|.B_{\mathsf{com}}G=|[n]\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)|.

The mapsHom⁡(ℤn,G)→Gn\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},G)\to G^{n}recording the values of a homomorphism on the standard basis form a simplicial map and they geometrically realize to a mapB𝖼𝗈𝗆​G→B​GB_{\mathsf{com}}G\to BG, whose homotopy fiber isE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}G.

To defineE𝖼𝗈𝗆E_{\mathsf{com}}up to homeomorphism, again one uses a simplicial model: it is the geometric realization of the simplicial space whosennsimplices consist of all(n+1)(n+1)-tuples of elements ofGGthatcommute affinely, that is, tuples(g0,…,gn)(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})such that the elements{gi−1​gj:0≤i,j≤n}\{g_{i}^{-1}g_{j}:0\leq i,j\leq n\}commute pairwise; or, equivalently, that allgjg_{j}are contained in a single coset of some abelian subgroup. The face maps simply delete entries and the degeneracy maps insert identity elements, as in one of the two usual simplicial models forE​GEG. The mapE𝖼𝗈𝗆​G→B𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}G\to B_{\mathsf{com}}GexhibitingE𝖼𝗈𝗆​GE_{\mathsf{com}}Gas the homotopy fiber of the map toB​GBGis given at the level of simplices by(g0,…,gn)↦(g0−1​g1,g1−1​g2,…,gn−1−1​gn)(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})\mapsto(g_{0}^{-1}g_{1},g_{1}^{-1}g_{2},\ldots,g_{n-1}^{-1}g_{n}).[AG15,AC07].

## 4.The geometry of elements ofSL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])

The purpose of this section is to explain the geometric information that will be used in the computation of centralizers. There are three ingredients. First, we fix the two ambient actions ofSL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]): the Archimedean action on the hyperbolic plane and the non-Archimedean action on the Bruhat–Tits treeTpT_{p}. Second, we classify elements by the factorization of their characteristic polynomial overℚ\mathbb{Q}. Third, we translate that algebraic classification into fixed points, axes, and min-sets inℍ2×Tp\mathbb{H}^{2}\times T_{p}. The central point is that an element of the centralizer ofggmust preserve the geometric objects naturally associated togg, and the number of translation directions in those objects will determine the free abelian rank of the centralizer.

Recall that given a group elementggand pointxxin a metric space acted on by the group, we calld​(x,g⋅x)d(x,g\cdot x)thedisplacement ofxx, and we let thedisplacement ofggbe the infimum of the displacements of all pointsxx. Themin-setofggis the set of all points achieving the infimum, that is, the set of points whose displacement is the displacement ofgg. The min-set ofggmay very well be empty.

The definitions and basic facts concerning the Bruhat–Tits tree, its boundary, vertex stabilizers, and the elliptic/hyperbolic dichotomy for elements ofSL2⁡(ℚp)\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})are standard; we follow the treatments in Serre’s book on trees and in Bass’s account of tree lattices[SER80].

## Corollary 7.9.

LetG=SL2⁡(ℤ​[1/p])G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]), endowed with the topology induced fromSL2⁡(ℝ)\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}). Then the spacesB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδandB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{\mathsf{com}}G

are weakly homotopy equivalent, but they are not homotopy equivalent.

## Proof.

By7.2, the groupGGis totally disconnected. ThereforeProposition7.4implies that the natural comparison mapB𝖼𝗈𝗆​Gδ⟶B𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}\longrightarrow B_{\mathsf{com}}G

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

On the other hand,Corollary7.6shows thatπ1qtop​(B𝖼𝗈𝗆​Gδ)\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{qtop}}(B_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta})

is discrete, whileProposition7.7shows thatπ1qtop​(B𝖼𝗈𝗆​G)\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{qtop}}(B_{\mathsf{com}}G)

is not discrete. IfB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GδB_{\mathsf{com}}G^{\delta}andB𝖼𝗈𝗆​GB_{\mathsf{com}}Gwere homotopy equivalent, then the theorem of Brazas–Fabel recalled above would imply that these two quasi-topological fundamental groups are homeomorphic. ∎
