## Abstract

We study the category $\mathsf{AtoMon}$ of atomic monoids and atom-preserving homomorphisms. We prove that $\mathsf{AtoMon}$ is locally finitely presentable by exhibiting a strong generator consisting of compact objects. We show that $\mathsf{AtoMon}$ admits (regular epi, mono)-factorizations but that it is not a regular category: we construct a regular epimorphism which is not pullback-stable. We also establish adjunctions for the group of units and explicitly construct the ``cofree atomic monoid'' over an arbitrary monoid. Finally, we exhibit a way to lift torsion theories of $\mathsf{Grp}$ to pretorsion theories of $\mathsf{AtoMon}$ and extend this construction to a more general setting.

## 1.Introduction

LetHHbe a monoid. A non-unita∈Ha\in His anatomif it cannot be written as a product of two non-units, andHHisatomicif every non-unit is a finite product of atoms. Factorization theory studies factorizations into atoms and the arithmetic invariants that measure their possible non-uniqueness. We refer to the monograph[9]for deep insight into this subject.

The category𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}was introduced in[3]to bring categorical methods into this setting. Its objects are atomic monoids, and its morphisms are atom-preserving monoid homomorphisms. One of the main motivations for introducing𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}was to study the arithmetic behavior of universal categorical constructions. In[3], products and coproducts were constructed explicitly[3, Theorems 4.6 and 5.4], and their sets of factorization lengths, together with related invariants, were computed[3, Theorems 4.10 and 5.7]. These constructions provide flexible ways to produce new atomic monoids while controlling their arithmetic, and thus offer useful tools for addressing realization problems in factorization theory.

More generally, all small limits and colimits in𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}were constructed in[3]. It is worth noting that colimits in𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}are computed as in the category𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{Mon}of monoids, whereas limits generally are not[3, Theorems 6.3 and 6.6]. It turns out that𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}is a complete and cocomplete non-full subcategory of𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{Mon}, with a categorical structure that reflects the distinction between units, atoms, and reducible non-units. These results show that𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}is not only a natural setting for factorization-theoretic questions, but also a category with a rich structure of its own. It is therefore natural to ask which other standard properties of categorical algebra it satisfies. The present paper addresses this question.

We first prove in Section2that𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}is locally finitely presentable (Theorem2.6) by exhibiting a strong generator consisting of compact objects. We also show that the compact objects of𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}are precisely the atomic monoids that are finitely presented as ordinary monoids (Theorem2.7).

In Section3, we investigate the regularity of𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}. In Subsection3.1, we show that every morphism in𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}admits a canonical (regular epi, mono)-factorization, and we characterize regular epimorphisms in terms of their kernel in𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{Mon}. However, regular epimorphisms are not stable under pullback: in Subsection3.2, we explicitly construct a regular epimorphism whose pullback is not regular. Consequently,𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}is not a regular category and, in particular, is not equivalent to a variety of universal algebras. We conclude this part by considering the regular completion of𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}in the sense of Carboni and Vitale[4]; see Subsection3.3.

In Section4, we study some natural functors associated with atomic monoids. We prove in Subsection4.1that the “group-of-units functor”(−)×:𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇→𝖦𝗋𝗉(-)^{\times}\colon\mathsf{AtoMon}\to\mathsf{Grp}has both a left and a right adjoint. Moreover, we construct in Subsection4.2the “atomization functor”𝖠:𝖬𝗈𝗇→𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{A}\colon\mathsf{Mon}\to\mathsf{AtoMon}, and prove that it is the right adjoint to the inclusion functorU:𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇→𝖬𝗈𝗇U\colon\mathsf{AtoMon}\to\mathsf{Mon}.

Finally, in Section5, we exhibit a way to lift torsion theories of𝖦𝗋𝗉\mathsf{Grp}to pretorsion theories of𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}. In particular, we show that the pair(𝖦𝗋𝗉,𝖱𝖾𝖽𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇)(\mathsf{Grp},\mathsf{RedAtoMon})is a pretorsion theory in𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}, where𝖱𝖾𝖽𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{RedAtoMon}is the full subcategory of𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}consisting of atomic monoids with trivial group of units (see Subsection5.1). From the above construction we deduce in Subsection5.2a general criterion to lift pretorsion theories along a coreflection (Theorem5.6).

## 3.Failure of regularity

Let𝒞\mathcal{C}be a category. Following[10], a morphism in𝒞\mathcal{C}is called aregular epimorphismif it is the coequalizer of two parallel arrows. A morphismf:X→Yf\colon X\to Yadmits a(regular epi, mono)-factorizationif there exist a regular epimorphismp:X→Ip\colon X\to Iand a monomorphismm:I→Ym\colon I\to Ysuch thatf=m​pf=mp. A category with finite limits isregularif (1) every morphism admits a (regular epi, mono)-factorization and (2) the pullback of a regular epimorphism along any morphism is again a regular epimorphism. Examples of regular categories are the category of sets, the category of (abelian) groups, and more generally any variety of universal algebras. In particular, the category𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{Mon}of monoids is regular.

The aim of this section is to address the natural question of whether𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}is also a regular category. We show that morphisms in𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}admit (regular epi, mono)-factorizations. Nevertheless, we construct an explicit example in which the pullback of a regular epimorphism is not regular, showing that𝖠𝗍𝗈𝖬𝗈𝗇\mathsf{AtoMon}is not a regular category. Its regular completion will be discussed in the last subsection.
