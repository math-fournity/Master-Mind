It provides that when a seller accepts payment from a buyer for goods or services ordered online, the seller must, within 30 days, either deliver the goods or services, provide a full refund, or send a written delay notice offering a full refund option. Failure to do so is unlawful.

3. FTC Enforcement Precedent: AI Services That Do Not Perform as Claimed

The Federal Trade Commission has established clear precedent that AI services which claim capabilities they do not actually possess constitute deceptive practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act. In the matter of DoNotPay, Inc. (2024-2025), the FTC charged that DoNotPay's "AI lawyer" failed to live up to claims that it could substitute for a human lawyer, and the company was required to pay monetary relief and notify all affected subscribers.

The principle established in DoNotPay is directly applicable here. ChatGPT is marketed as an AI capable of performing complex research and development tasks. 4. The Distinction Between Token Consumption and Service Delivery

Your response states that "work performed while analyzing, generating, testing, or revising code can count toward usage even when the resulting implementation does not ultimately meet the intended objective." I do not dispute this as a description of how tokens are counted. However, token consumption is a measure of resource usage, not a measure of service delivery. A service that consumes resources without delivering the promised result is not a service that has been "provided" within the meaning of consumer protection law.

To use an analogy: if a contractor consumes materials and labor hours building a structure that cannot bear weight and must be demolished, the homeowner is not obligated to pay for the labor simply because labor was expended. The question is whether the service was delivered as described, not whether resources were consumed in the attempt.
