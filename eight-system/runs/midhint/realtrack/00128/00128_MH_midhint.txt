We are restarting the elevator of problem F. 2280 in the ten-story building. It starts again from the ground floor, and during its journey, it will stop exactly once on every floor, just as before. However, this time we also ensure that after stopping at an even-numbered floor, it stops at an odd-numbered floor, and after an odd-numbered floor, it stops at an even-numbered floor. (We consider the ground floor as the 0th floor.) How many meters can it travel at most under these conditions, if the height difference between two adjacent floors is 4 meters?

1. (Setup) The journey is a sequence $0, s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_{10}$ where $(s_1, \ldots, s_{10})$ is a permutation of $\{1, 2, \ldots, 10\}$. The parity condition forces the parities to alternate throughout, including from the start: $s_1$ is odd, $s_2$ is even, $\ldots$, $s_{10}$ is even. The quantity to maximize is $D = |s_1 - 0| + \sum_{i=1}^{9} |s_{i+1} - s_i|$.

2. (Local structure of the differences) Every term $|s_{i+1} - s_i|$ is the absolute difference of an even and an odd number, hence every one of the 10 terms is odd. Therefore $D$, being a sum of ten odd numbers, is even.

3. (Unconstrained reference) Without the parity condition, the journey $0, 10, 1, 9, 2, 8, 3, 7, 4, 6, 5$ achieves $D = 55$.
