[00] I need to prove the Fibonacci gcd formula for arbitrary nonnegative m and n.
[01] First I try Binet's formula and hope common divisors can be read from the powers of phi.
[02] This becomes unusable modulo an arbitrary divisor, so I abandon the Binet route. It did remind me of the addition identity F_(a+b)=F_(a-1)F_b+F_a F_(b+1).
[03] I return to the original gcd goal, now looking for a Euclidean reduction rather than a closed form.
[04] Assume m>n. The addition identity makes F_m congruent, up to a unit/sign factor, to F_(m-n) modulo F_n, so gcd(F_m,F_n)=gcd(F_n,F_(m-n)).
[05] Repeating this reduction follows the Euclidean algorithm on the pair of indices until the pair reaches (d,0), where d=gcd(m,n).
[06] Separately I check the terminal and symmetry cases: F_0=0, gcd(F_d,0)=F_d, and swapping m,n does not change the gcd.
[07] The terminal-case branch also uses the addition identity left over from the failed Binet branch to justify each subtraction step uniformly.
[08] Combining the Euclidean reduction with the independently checked terminal cases gives gcd(F_m,F_n)=F_d.
[09] Since d=gcd(m,n), this is exactly the desired formula.
